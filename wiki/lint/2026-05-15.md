## Missing-construct-validity (45 findings) — authoring pattern question

Bulk distribution:
- 23 findings in cog-sci source pages (mcclelland 2, nader 11, tse 8, yang 5, josselyn 12 — 38 total counted; full inventory in raw linter output).
- 2 findings in archive (BIG-PICTURE).
- 4 findings in concept pages (complementary-learning-systems, silent-engrams).
- Remainder scattered across a handful of source and concept pages.

**Pattern observed:** each cog-sci page contains a stream of `[MEASURED]` tags reporting different experimental measurements (e.g., "X% reactivation rate", "Y-hour reconsolidation window", "Z-day learning interval"). Each measurement is treated as one `[MEASURED]` tag, but most don't have a per-tag construct-validity note. Construct-validity discussion, where present, often lives at the section header or in a single umbrella note.

**This is an authoring-pattern question, not a bug.** Two possible readings:

1. **The rule is too strict.** The existing pattern (one construct-validity note per section, covering multiple `[MEASURED]` tags in that section) is sustainable and readable. Per-tag construct-validity would be repetitive and grinding.

2. **The pattern needs tightening.** Each `[MEASURED]` tag should carry its own construct-validity note because the metrics measured by different tags are different. Group construct-validity notes elide important per-claim differences.

I don't think this is unilaterally decidable from a lint pass. Flagging in *Drift to flag* for a schema-discipline discussion at the next root-level wiki review.

Writing a SCHEMA paragraph adds discipline overhead without preventing a real failure mode.

## What it is

Stealth-phase startup founded by **Eric Zelikman** and **Noah Goodman** (Stanford psychology + computer science). **$480M seed round January 2026** at ~$4.48B valuation. Stated focus includes *"memory, user understanding."* No architecture disclosed.

**Critical signal:** Noah Goodman is Stanford psychology + CS — genuinely cog-sci-adjacent (computational cognitive science, not neuroscience). His prior work on probabilistic models of cognition, language pragmatics, and Bayesian models of theory-of-mind makes him one of the few foundation-model founders with deep computational cog-sci background.

## Where it might fail or differ

- **Computational cog-sci ≠ neuro-memory architecture.** Goodman's Bayesian-cognition heritage maps to a different design language than the Norman rubric's CLS+hippocampus framing. The two communities cite different foundational papers (Tenenbaum vs McClelland; Lake vs Norman). A "memory" product from a Bayesian-cognition lineage may look more like belief-update than episodic-recall.
- **$480M at $4.48B is large but doesn't guarantee technical direction.** Funding does not imply architectural commitment.
- **Product focus may be broader than memory.** "Memory + user understanding" is the public framing; the actual product could be a general AI assistant where memory is one feature among many, not the headline.

## Relevance to the wedge

**Pivot-watch competitor.** Humans& is currently in a different cell (computational cog-sci-flavored AI assistant) but has the team to pivot toward our cell. The pivot would require Goodman to steer architectural choices toward neuro-memory framing (CLS, schema-fit, reconsolidation, replay) and away from purely Bayesian framing.

**Threat shape:** latent. If they ship a Bayesian-flavored memory system, they are in a different category and not directly competitive. If they pivot toward neuro-memory framing, they become a serious competitor on team pedigree alone.

**Action: watch for architecture disclosure.** First public technical content will reveal whether Goodman's cog-sci heritage steers toward Bayesian-cognition or neuro-memory.

## Key claims (with our restatements)

### Context windows as "badly approximated hippocampus"

**Hassabis verbatim (per 36kr translation, post-Gemini-3 interview):** *"a badly approximated hippocampus... kind of brute force — you're remembering everything when in fact most tokens are irrelevant."*

**Our restatement:** `[ASSERTED]` external. **This is the wedge** — selectivity over brute-force-everything. Hassabis publicly diagnoses the failure mode Kyrja is proposing to solve, using the same cog-sci framing (hippocampus) and the same critique (most tokens are irrelevant) Kyrja has been developing internally. Strongest possible external validation from a credentialed-CEO source.

### Three missing AGI ingredients

**Hassabis, per Big Technology coverage:** Names **continual learning, robust memory, and introspective reasoning** as the three missing AGI ingredients on the path to 2030 AGI. Explicitly cites his own PhD work on hippocampal memory integration as motivation.

**Our restatement:** `[ASSERTED]` external. Memory is publicly named as a top-three bottleneck for 2030 AGI by the DeepMind CEO. **No ship date** — Hassabis frames this as "1–2 missing breakthroughs," not a 2026 product. This is the timing-window signal: DeepMind acknowledges the gap, has the team to close it, but does not publicly project a near-term solution.

### Brain-inspired pedigree explicit

**Hassabis:** Explicitly cites his PhD work on hippocampal memory integration (Hassabis & Maguire 2007, "Patients with hippocampal amnesia cannot imagine new experiences") as motivation for the current memory framing.

**Our restatement:** `[ASSERTED]` external. The DeepMind CEO is **personally cog-sci-grounded** (hippocampus + episodic memory background). This is bullish for the wedge being legitimate and bearish for our exclusivity — if DeepMind decides to ship a cog-sci-grounded caddy, Hassabis personally has the framing to lead it.

## Important caveats

- **"Memory bottleneck" has two distinct meanings often conflated in press.** Hassabis distinguishes (a) hardware HBM supply constraints (Samsung, Micron, SK Hynix) and (b) architectural memory (the wedge-relevant claim). Press coverage frequently conflates these. The architectural claim is the load-bearing one for Kyrja.
- **No DeepMind product roadmap commitment to caddy-shaped memory.** Hassabis's framing is research-time-horizon. As of 2026-05-16, all shipped DeepMind/Gemini memory features are bolt-on RAG (see [Astra investigation in log.md](../log.md) 2026-05-16). The gap between rhetoric and shipped architecture is the Kyrja window.
- **Single-source for the most pitch-relevant quote.** The "badly approximated hippocampus" framing is from a translated interview, not a DeepMind blog post or peer-reviewed publication. Worth re-checking primary English-language source if this becomes load-bearing in pitch material.
- **Hassabis's incentives are mixed.** As DeepMind CEO, his framing of memory-as-bottleneck both validates the research direction *and* primes investors to expect DeepMind to ship the solution. Kyrja's pitch should cite the quote as external validation of the wedge, not as a guarantee the wedge is unfilled.

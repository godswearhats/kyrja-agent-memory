## Key claims (with our restatements)

### Architecture

**Paper:** Dual-memory substrate-level integration. Sliding Window Attention (SWA) carries the working-memory load; **mutable MLPs in the last 25% of transformer blocks** carry test-time-learned storage. Test-time gradient updates happen during inference on the mutable parameter region.

**Our restatement:** `[ASSERTED]` — paper-anchored architectural claim. The substrate-integration mechanism is real; what's novel is that the mutable region survives at frontier H100-class compute and produces favourable latency/quality numbers.

### Reported magnitudes

**Paper:** 2.7× speedup at 128K context, 35× at 2M context (vs. dense-attention baselines at equivalent quality). No reported scaling walls within the tested context window range.

**Our restatement:** `[ASSERTED]` from paper headline numbers. Construct-validity caveat: speedup is vs. dense-attention baseline of the same parameter scale, not vs. SWA-only or vs. retrieval-augmented baselines. The "no scaling wall" framing is upper-bounded by the paper's tested context lengths; extrapolation beyond 2M tokens is not supported.

### Substrate variant chosen

**Paper:** The mutable-parameter ("in-weights") variant. Memory is encoded directly into MLP weights at inference, not into an external pluggable store.

**Our restatement:** Informative for [H37-pluggable-substrate](../hypothesis/H37-pluggable-substrate.md): at frontier scale with current research, the in-weights path was preferred over a pluggable external store. Suggests the pluggable-substrate path is harder, not unbuildable. Treated as partial counter-evidence (but not falsification) for [H29-edge-substrate-memory](../hypothesis/H29-edge-substrate-memory.md)'s "structural forces prevent substrate at frontier products" claim — TTT-E2E is frontier-scale *research*, not a deployed frontier product.

## Claim

`[SPECULATED]` A small learned salience function trained on `(retrieval-success, memory-features, downstream-outcome)` tuples generated by a baseline agent **beats** LLM-call importance scoring (current bolt-on default) on a multi-task agent benchmark, **at lower per-write cost**.

The hypothesis derives from the [salience-signal](../open-question/salience-signal.md) open question raised during the M15 walk (2026-05-17). It is the cheapest first falsifiable claim in that question's evidence-resolution path.

## What would falsify it

A REJECTED verdict requires either:

1. **Quality failure** — On a multi-task agent benchmark with comparable storage budgets, LLM-call importance scoring produces a retrieval-quality score (mAP / hit-rate / outcome-task-success) that is statistically indistinguishable from or better than the learned salience function. n ≥ 3 task classes; learned-function performance within 1-sigma of LLM-call across all classes.

2. **Cost failure** — Even if the learned function is quality-competitive, its training cost amortised over deployment exceeds the inference-cost savings vs LLM-call. Threshold: learned-function total cost > 50% of LLM-call total cost over realistic deployment durations.

3. **Generalisation failure** — The learned function works on the training-task-distribution but fails to transfer to held-out task classes. This wouldn't kill the architectural claim but would force a per-task-class function — which makes the cost story significantly worse.

`[ASSERTED]` Either failure mode 1 or 2 alone is sufficient for REJECTED. Failure mode 3 alone weakens the claim but doesn't kill it.

## Evidence for

`[SPECULATED]`

- **Biological precedent** — [Redondo & Morris 2011 STC](../source/redondo-morris-2011-stc.md) and [Yang 2024 selection of experience](../source/yang-et-al-2024-selection-of-experience.md): biology computes salience via convergent multi-signal molecular machinery (dopamine + novelty + protein synthesis), not via expensive evaluation of each event. Suggests a small-model salience function is biologically plausible.
- **Existing forgetting-policy literature** — Aljundi et al. 2019 "Online Continual Learning with Maximally Interfered Retrieval" demonstrates learned-policy approaches to memory selection beat heuristics in continual-learning contexts. Not directly comparable to agent-memory, but the architectural principle transfers. [pending source page]
- **Schaul 2016 PER** — Prioritised Experience Replay in RL uses TD-error as a learned-policy proxy for salience. Outperforms uniform replay; demonstrates that even a single learned-policy signal (TD-error) can dominate heuristics. [pending source page]
- **Intrinsic-dimensionality argument** — [LoRA concept](../concept/lora.md) and intrinsic-dimensionality literature (Aghajanyan 2020; [Hu et al. § 7.2](../source/hu-2021-lora.md) confirms rank-1 LoRA matches full-rank on tested GPT-3 tasks) suggest the salience function's underlying representation may be low-rank, making a small learned model viable. **Caveat:** this is a cross-domain inference — LoRA's intrinsic dimensionality is a property of *fine-tuning updates in weight space*; the salience function's dimensionality is a property of a *scoring function's output*. Cross-domain transfer of intrinsic-dim findings is suggestive, not direct evidence; the salience-signature decision remains deferred per [[feedback_mvp_doc_not_mvp]].

## Evidence against

`[ASSERTED]`

- **No published agent-memory benchmark has demonstrated this directly.** The closest work (Mem0, Letta, Zep) does not publish ablations of their importance-scoring functions. The hypothesis is currently a logical inference, not an empirical claim.
- **Construct-validity risk** — retrieval-success as a training signal may be a poor proxy for "this memory matters." The memory that *should* have been retrieved but wasn't is invisible to retrieval-success metrics. Without addressing this, the learned function may optimise for already-retrieved memories rather than actually-important memories.
- **Bootstrap problem** — the learned function needs training data which comes from a baseline agent. If the baseline agent's retrieval is poor, the function inherits its biases. Outcome attribution as a training signal partially mitigates but does not eliminate this.
- **LLM-call scoring may be unbeatable on heterogeneous tasks** — the LLM's general-purpose reasoning is exactly the strength that's hard to replicate with a small task-specific model. Cost-quality tradeoff may favour LLM-call for tasks with diverse content.

## Open sub-questions

- What's the right training signal? Retrieval-success, downstream-outcome-success, or some combination? (See [salience-signal § sub-questions](../open-question/salience-signal.md))
- What's the model size that makes the cost story work? Sub-100M-param distillation of GPT-class importance judgments may be feasible.
- Should the function be uni-modal or multi-modal? Biology's convergent multi-signal approach suggests multi-modal; software simplicity argues uni-modal.
- Should it be trained per-task or general-purpose? Generalisation-failure mode hinges on this.
- How does it interact with STC-shape vs hot-store-promotion architectures? The salience function's output feeds both; whether the function changes shape depending on the consuming architecture is open.

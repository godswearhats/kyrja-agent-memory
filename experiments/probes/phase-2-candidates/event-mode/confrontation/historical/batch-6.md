# Confrontation × Historical — Batch 6 (Tier C)

**C-Hi6-1**

*[Ottoman Empire, Constantinople, 1571 — present tense]*

The dragoman stands between them in the Customs House of Galata, doing his work without being asked: every sentence the Venetian factor utters in his rapid Venetian dialect becomes, in the dragoman's mouth, a courteous construction in Ottoman Turkish, and what the customs inspector replies in Turkish arrives in the factor's ears as serviceable Italian. The factor is not courteous. He has been waiting for two weeks.

He tells the customs inspector, through the dragoman, that he requires the release of the consignment held in Warehouse Twelve — forty bales of raw silk, entered in the customs register under the Bailo's commercial seal — and he requires it today. The transit papers are in order. The tariff has been paid. There is no legal basis for the hold.

The customs inspector examines the paper the factor produces and tells him, through the dragoman, that the consignment has been placed under a general hold by the Kapudan Pasha's harbour office following the battle at Lepanto. All Venetian commercial consignments are suspended pending review of trading relations between the Sublime Porte and the Serene Republic. This is not a customs matter; it is a political one.

The factor tells him — and the dragoman softens this considerably — that the Bailo has already received assurances from the Grand Vizier's office that existing commercial consignments would not be affected by the political situation, and that the factor requires the inspector to produce the Kapudan Pasha's written order if one exists, because the Bailo intends to take any such order directly to the Grand Vizier for contradiction.

The inspector pauses. He says he will produce the order. The factor says he will wait. The order, when it arrives — from the inspector's superior on the second floor — does not, in fact, cover consignments entered before the date of the Lepanto dispatch. The consignment is released by afternoon.

> A = Venetian factor; B = Ottoman customs inspector; O = the forty bales of raw silk held in Warehouse Twelve; resolution shape (a) — factor prevails; release ordered after written hold authority is produced and found not to cover pre-dispatch consignments. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**C-Hi6-2**

*[Virginia Colony, 1705]*

"I want the indenture papers, Mister Crale. My seven years are done."

"You have five months remaining, Josiah."

"I have my own count and it does not say five months. I came off the *Patience* in October of ninety-eight. October of ninety-eight to October of this year is seven years. My indenture is discharged."

"The indenture was signed in December of ninety-eight. You were aboard the *Patience* in October, yes. You were not bound to my service until the papers were signed in December. The seven years run from December."

"I saw no December date on those papers when I put my mark to them. Where is that date?"

"The papers are in my strongbox and in good order, and I did not ask you to review them in my office."

"Then I am asking to review them now. I am asking you to open the box and show me the December date, which I do not believe is there."

Crale does not move to the strongbox. He says the papers are as he has described and that Josiah's refusal to complete his term will be taken as absconding, with the penalties the county court attaches to that.

Josiah tells him that if the papers say December, he will read December himself, and if they say October, he expects his freedom dues — the corn, the tools, and the land-warrant — paid within the week. He will not take Crale's word for it. If Crale will not show him the papers, he will walk to the county seat and put the question to the court himself, today, and let the court send for the papers.

Crale looks at him. He goes to the strongbox.

> A = Josiah (indentured servant); B = Crale (planter / master); O = Josiah's indenture papers (their start date and the question of whether the term is discharged); resolution shape (c) — breaking point; Josiah's threat to go directly to the county court forces Crale to open the strongbox rather than maintain the refusal. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**C-Hi6-3**

*[Paris, 1786 — first-person narration]*

I am a printer's journeyman, and I have worked for Maître Aubert on the Rue Saint-Jacques for eleven years. This morning I presented myself at the door of the Chambre syndicale with a complaint, which the inspector received sitting down and read standing up, if such a thing is possible — he lifted himself halfway from his chair as he read, which I took as a promising sign.

The complaint was simple: Maître Aubert had taken on a new press — a second press, which he has no right to operate under his privilege as it stands. The privilege granted by the Direction de la Librairie covers one press, one journeyman, and one apprentice. He now runs two presses, employs three journeymen including myself, and has taken two apprentices. The guild rate for a second press requires a new privilege application and a fresh bond. He has applied for neither.

The inspector told me he was aware of Maître Aubert's establishment and that the second press had been in operation for four months. He said the Direction had been notified and was reviewing the matter. He said my complaint was therefore redundant.

I told him the review had been running for four months and that Maître Aubert's second press ran every day the review ran. I told him I was not here to initiate a review — I was here to ask whether the guild's own syndical authority, independent of the Direction, could suspend the second press pending the outcome. The Chambre syndicale has that power under the regulations of 1723.

He said the Chambre did not exercise syndical suspension while a Direction review was active — the two bodies did not run in parallel on the same matter.

I told him I would put that interpretation to the guild's own procureur, whose office was around the corner and whose opinion I had not yet sought. I thanked the inspector for his time and left. I was in the procureur's anteroom within the quarter-hour.

> A = I (narrator / journeyman printer); B = inspector of the Chambre syndicale; O = suspension of Maître Aubert's unlicensed second press; resolution shape (c) — breaking point; inspector refuses to act, narrator exits to pursue the guild procureur as an alternative authority. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**C-Hi6-4**

*[Bengal, East India Company factory at Kasimbazar, 1756 — free indirect]*

The factor Drummond has been at Kasimbazar long enough to know that the nawab's representative does not arrive unannounced at the factory gates without intent, and that intent, this morning, is clear from the manner in which the representative holds his authority — not as a courtesy call but as a weight he is prepared to set down hard. He is here about the fortifications.

The nawab's order is straightforward: the Company must cease work on the new earthworks along the factory's river wall. The nawab has not sanctioned these works. The Company's right to maintain a trading post does not extend to the right to fortify it against a sovereign power. The works must stop and the materials must be removed before the following Friday.

Drummond listens. He thinks about the monsoon season and about the river wall and about the instructions he has received from Calcutta, which say to strengthen the factory's defences at all costs given the political situation in the presidency. He tells the representative that the works are a maintenance measure only — repair of the existing river wall, not new military construction — and that the Company's charter entitles it to maintain the structural integrity of its premises without nawab's licence for every beam and barrow of earth.

The representative says the nawab's engineers have assessed the works and their assessment is that what is being built is not a repaired wall but a raised parapet with a changed angle of fire. This is a fortification, not maintenance. The distinction is not one the Company is entitled to make unilaterally.

Drummond knows Calcutta is ten days away. He says he cannot halt the works on his own authority — he must receive instruction from the Council at Calcutta — and asks the representative to allow twenty days for correspondence. The representative says he will allow ten days, not twenty, and that if no halt has been ordered by then the nawab will send engineers to dismantle what has been built, at the Company's expense. He leaves.

Drummond dispatches his fastest courier to Calcutta before noon.

> A = nawab's representative; B = factor Drummond (East India Company); O = the fortification works on the Kasimbazar factory's river wall; resolution shape (b) — both defer to the Calcutta Council as decision-making authority, with a ten-day deadline imposed by the nawab's representative. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**C-Hi6-5**

*[Rome, 59 BC — embedded document]*

*From the journal of Titus Annius Milo, Tribune of the Plebs:*

This morning I went in person to the house of the urban praetor Gaius Octavius and demanded, in the presence of his secretary and two of my own clients who served as witnesses, that he restore the citizenship records of the freedmen's association of the Via Sacra district, which his office removed from the public register six days ago without a magistrate's edict and without notice to the association's representatives.

He received me in his atrium. He did not ask me to sit. He told me the records had been removed on his own administrative authority pending a review of the association's legal standing — he alleged, without producing any document, that the association's founding registration contained an irregularity.

I told him that the removal of public records without an edict was not an administrative act but a usurpation, and that as Tribune I was formally interposing my sacrosanct authority against the removal. I told him the records must be returned to the public register before the close of the day's business, or I would lay a charge before the tribal assembly under the lex Porcia.

He told me my intercessio did not extend to administrative records and that the lex Porcia applied to acts against persons, not documents.

I told him I disagreed with that reading and that the tribal assembly would hear the question and decide it. I also told him that until it did, I intended to stand at the door of his records office in person each morning and inform every person who arrived seeking access that the records within were unlawfully held — which is my right as Tribune under any reading of my office's powers.

He told me the records would be returned to the register.

> A = Titus Annius Milo (Tribune of the Plebs); B = Gaius Octavius (urban praetor); O = the citizenship records of the Via Sacra freedmen's association (their restoration to the public register); resolution shape (a) — Milo prevails; praetor agrees to restore the records under combined threat of tribal assembly charge and daily public interposition. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

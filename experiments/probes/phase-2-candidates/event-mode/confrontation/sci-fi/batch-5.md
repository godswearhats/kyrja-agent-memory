# Confrontation × Sci-fi — Batch 5 (Tier B)

## C-Sf5-1

The refinery platform hangs in the upper atmosphere of Titan, tethered to a methane extraction rig that descends forty kilometres into the orange haze below. Chief Extractor Yuen stands at the operations console and watches the pressure readings climb. The rig's automated safety governor has been throttling extraction flow for six hours -- cutting output by thirty percent -- because the subsurface pocket they are tapping shows seismic micro-tremors at a frequency the governor interprets as pre-collapse.

Colony Director Brask enters the operations deck and tells Yuen to override the governor and restore full extraction. The colony's atmospheric processors on the surface require a minimum methane throughput to maintain breathable air in the habitat domes. At thirty percent reduction, the processors will fall below critical input within nineteen hours. Brask has the override authority codes and is prepared to enter them herself.

Yuen steps between Brask and the console. She tells the director that the micro-tremors match the signature profile that preceded the Enceladus Bore collapse eight years ago -- a collapse that killed forty-one extractors and destroyed the rig entirely. The safety governor exists because of Enceladus. Overriding it with this tremor profile active violates the Outer Planets Extraction Safety Code, Section 7, which holds the chief extractor personally liable for any override during a flagged seismic event. She will not permit the override, and she will not step aside.

Brask tells her that nineteen hours of breathable air is not a theoretical risk. It is a countdown. She demands the override.

Yuen tells her there is a third option. The extraction rig can be repositioned to a secondary pocket twelve kilometres east -- a pocket with no seismic activity. Repositioning takes fourteen hours. The colony will need to reduce atmospheric processor load by cycling non-essential dome sections to low-oxygen standby during the gap. It will be uncomfortable. No one will die.

Brask stares at the pressure readings. She asks Yuen whether fourteen hours is firm. Yuen says it is. Brask authorises the repositioning and orders the habitat manager to begin cycling non-essential domes. The override codes go back in her pocket.

> A = Colony Director Brask; B = Chief Extractor Yuen; O = the safety governor override (restoring full methane extraction); resolution shape (a) -- Yuen prevails; Brask accepts the repositioning alternative and abandons the override. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

## C-Sf5-2

"I need you to release the quarantine lock on Cargo Pod 7-Echo, Vasik."

"No."

"The pod contains three hundred kilograms of synthetic mycelium inoculant. The terraforming crew on the southern ridge has been waiting nine days. Their soil beds are dying. Every day that pod sits locked in your bay is another hectare of substrate we cannot recover."

"The inoculant passed through the Meridian relay station. Meridian had a prion contamination flag two weeks ago. The flag was downgraded, not cleared. Under the colony's biosecurity protocol, any organic cargo that transits a flagged relay must complete a forty-day quarantine with three negative assays at intervals. We are on day nine. The first assay is not scheduled until day fourteen."

"The prion flag was for animal protein. Mycelium inoculant is a fungal substrate. There is zero transmission pathway."

"The protocol does not distinguish between organic subtypes. It says 'organic cargo.' The inoculant is organic cargo. I am not a microbiologist. I am the quarantine officer. I follow the protocol as written."

"Then I am formally requesting an emergency exemption under Article 5 of the biosecurity charter, which allows the colony administrator to release quarantined goods when delay poses an imminent threat to a critical terraforming milestone."

"Article 5 requires a signed risk assessment from the colony's chief biologist confirming that the specific cargo poses no contamination risk. Do you have one?"

"Dr. Osei can have it written within the hour."

"When I have Dr. Osei's signed assessment in my hands, I will release the pod. Not before."

The colony administrator leaves the quarantine bay. Two hours later, a signed assessment from Dr. Osei arrives on Vasik's terminal. He reads it, verifies the biologist's credential seal, and releases the magnetic lock on Pod 7-Echo.

> A = colony administrator; B = Vasik (quarantine officer); O = Cargo Pod 7-Echo containing synthetic mycelium inoculant (its release from quarantine); resolution shape (a) -- Vasik prevails on process; the administrator complies with the procedural requirement and obtains the signed risk assessment before release is granted. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

## C-Sf5-3

I am the navigation ethics officer aboard the generation ship Covenant of Passage, and this morning I told Captain Almeda that I am revoking my certification of the course correction she ordered last week -- the one that diverts us through the Voss Nebula to shave eleven years off our transit to Kepler-438b. My certification was required under the ship's charter before the helm could execute the correction. I gave it based on the stellar cartography data the captain's office provided. That data was incomplete.

I discovered the omission during a routine cross-reference with the long-range sensor archive. The Voss Nebula contains a radiation belt that the cartography summary did not mention. The belt is survivable -- the ship's shielding can handle it -- but it will expose the cryo-stored embryo bank to cumulative radiation levels that exceed the Reproductive Safety Threshold by a factor of three. Fourteen thousand embryos. The future population of the colony.

Captain Almeda told me the embryo bank could be relocated to the innermost cargo hold, behind additional shielding. She said the engineering team had already drawn up a plan.

I told her I had reviewed the plan. The innermost hold's shielding reduces exposure to 1.4 times the threshold. Still above the limit. The charter is unambiguous: no course that exceeds the Reproductive Safety Threshold for the embryo bank may be certified by the ethics officer. I am withdrawing my certification.

She told me that eleven years was not an abstraction. Eleven years meant the difference between arriving at Kepler-438b with a functioning biosphere and arriving after the atmospheric window had closed. She asked me to consider what the embryos were worth if the colony had no air to breathe.

I told her the atmospheric window estimates were projections, not measurements, and that gambling fourteen thousand embryos against a projection was not a trade I could certify. I proposed referring the matter to the Ship's Council for a binding vote, as the charter permits when the captain and the ethics officer reach an impasse on navigation safety. She agreed. The council convenes at the next watch change. The helm remains on the original course until the vote is recorded.

> A = Captain Almeda; B = narrator (navigation ethics officer); O = the Voss Nebula course correction (its certification); resolution shape (b) -- both defer to the Ship's Council for a binding vote under the charter's impasse provision, original course maintained pending the decision. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

## C-Sf5-4

INCIDENT REPORT -- CYGNUS ORBITAL FABRICATION PLATFORM
FILED BY: Station Arbiter Lúcia Ferreira
DATE: Sol 847, Cygnus Standard
SUBJECT: Dispute -- Drone Fleet Allocation, Bay 12

At 0940 hours, Fabrication Lead Kessler entered Bay 12 and instructed Logistics Coordinator Phan to reroute the entire fleet of forty-eight construction drones from the exterior hull repair on Segment C to the assembly of the new solar collector array on Segment F. Kessler stated that the collector array was behind schedule by nine days and that the energy shortfall from the incomplete array was forcing the station to ration power to the residential ring. He presented a priority override authorization signed by the Station Director.

Coordinator Phan refused the reroute. She stated that the hull repair on Segment C was in active phase -- twenty-two of the forty-eight drones were mid-weld on pressure-critical seams. Withdrawing drones during an active weld cycle would leave the seams incomplete, creating a micro-fracture risk that the station's structural monitoring system would flag as a Category 2 hazard within seventy-two hours. She cited the Orbital Safety Compact, Article 14, which prohibits reassignment of fabrication resources from active pressure-boundary work without sign-off from the station's structural integrity officer.

Kessler told her the Director's override authorization superseded Article 14. Phan told him it did not. Article 14 is classified as a safety-absolute provision, meaning it cannot be overridden by administrative authority -- only by the structural integrity officer's written confirmation that the interrupted work poses no hazard.

Kessler contacted the structural integrity officer, Dr. Wren, by comm-link. Wren reviewed the weld status data remotely and declined to issue the confirmation. The incomplete seams on Segment C posed a real and immediate micro-fracture risk. She recommended completing the hull repair first, then reallocating the drones -- an estimated four additional days.

Kessler informed Phan that the drones would remain on the hull repair. He left Bay 12 to inform the Station Director that the collector array would be delayed by four days.

> A = Fabrication Lead Kessler; B = Logistics Coordinator Phan; O = the forty-eight construction drones (their reallocation from hull repair to solar collector assembly); resolution shape (a) -- Phan prevails; the structural integrity officer's refusal to certify the interruption forces Kessler to abandon the reroute. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

## C-Sf5-5

The translation chamber hums at a frequency that Reva can feel in her teeth. She is standing at the threshold of the gate -- a ring of machined obsidian three metres in diameter, suspended in a magnetic cradle -- and on the other side of the chamber, Dr. Ansari is telling her she cannot go through.

Reva is the survey lead for the Frontier Exploration Authority. The gate connects to an uncharted terrestrial world designated FEA-1137. Her team has spent eleven months preparing the survey mission. The gate was calibrated last night. The transit window opens in forty minutes and holds for six hours before the orbital mechanics of the destination planet carry the exit point out of alignment.

Dr. Ansari is the gate facility's chief physicist. He tells Reva that the calibration data shows a point-four percent asymmetry in the gate's energy field -- within the manufacturer's stated tolerance, but outside the safety threshold he set when the facility was commissioned. He has seen point-four asymmetries before. Twice, the transit was clean. Once, on the Kerensky expedition, the asymmetry masked a resonance cascade that collapsed the exit point ninety seconds after the survey team stepped through. Three people remained stranded on the other side for eight months. He will not authorize transit.

Reva tells him the manufacturer's tolerance exists for a reason. Point-four percent is within spec. The FEA board approved this mission based on the manufacturer's safety envelope, not Ansari's personal threshold. She demands he authorize transit before the window opens.

Ansari tells her he will not. The facility's operating charter gives the chief physicist sole authority over gate activation. He does not answer to the FEA board on safety determinations. If Reva wishes to challenge his decision, she may file an appeal with the Interplanetary Transit Authority, which can convene a review panel. The next available session is in nineteen days. The next alignment window for FEA-1137 is in seven months.

Reva stares at the gate. The hum does not change. She pulls her survey pack off her shoulders and sets it on the floor. "File the appeal," she tells her deputy. She walks out of the translation chamber. The gate remains dark.

> A = Reva (FEA survey lead); B = Dr. Ansari (chief physicist, gate facility); O = gate transit authorization to FEA-1137; resolution shape (c) -- breaking point; Reva abandons the transit attempt and initiates a formal appeal to the Interplanetary Transit Authority, mission postponed seven months. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

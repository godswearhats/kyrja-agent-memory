# Confrontation × Sci-fi — Batch 6 (Tier C)

**C-Sf6-1**

The terraforming authority's resource flag on Meridian Station's water-ice allocation appears at 06:14 in the colony administrator's queue, and Administrator Priya Vasquez is at her terminal by 06:30. She reads the flag twice. Then she puts in a call to the authority's regional coordinator on Titan relay and waits seventeen minutes for the signal to bounce back.

When the coordinator's face appears on her screen she tells him she needs the flag lifted. The flag has suspended Meridian's ability to draw on its allocated water-ice quota from the Oort cache — a quota the colony has held under the Outer Settlements Compact since the station's founding charter was ratified. The suspension has been in effect for nine days. Meridian has four hundred and twelve residents, a closed-loop water recycler running at ninety-two percent capacity, and a six-week reserve. The math is not comfortable.

The coordinator tells her the flag was placed because Meridian's drawdown last quarter exceeded the compact allocation by eleven percent, triggering an automatic resource audit. The audit protocol requires suspension during the audit period. He says the audit will complete in three to four weeks.

She tells him Meridian's over-draw was caused by a recycler pump failure in the second quarter — documented and reported through the compact's emergency exception mechanism — and that the exception was acknowledged by his own office. The audit protocol, Section 4.7, explicitly exempts over-draws that have been processed through the exception mechanism. The flag should not have been placed.

He says he is aware of the exception acknowledgement, but that the audit system is automated and does not cross-reference exception records — the flag was generated by the quota-monitoring subsystem independently. The flag can only be lifted by a manual override at the compact's resource board, which meets on a twelve-day cycle. The next meeting is in eight days.

She tells him eight days is not acceptable given her reserve timeline. She asks him to invoke the compact's emergency review provision, which allows a three-member sub-panel of the board to convene within forty-eight hours for situations involving immediate habitability risk. He says habitability risk requires a formal certification from Meridian's life-support engineer, filed through the compact's emergency portal.

She has the certification in her queue within ninety minutes. She files it. The sub-panel convenes thirty-one hours later and lifts the flag before the end of their session.

> A = Administrator Priya Vasquez (Meridian Station); B = terraforming authority regional coordinator; O = the suspension flag on Meridian's water-ice allocation; resolution shape (b) — both defer to the compact's emergency three-member sub-panel under the habitability risk provision, flag lifted after forty-eight-hour convening. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**C-Sf6-2**

"You ran the simulation without filing a Tier Two notification."

"The simulation ran for nineteen minutes. Tier Two threshold is twenty minutes. I'm inside the window."

"You ran it in four segments, Castellan. Nineteen minutes each. Four times. That's seventy-six minutes of continuous substrate compute on a live colony model, and you filed no notification because you carved it into segments that each fall one minute under threshold."

"Four separate runs. Each independently below threshold. The protocol applies to individual runs."

"The protocol applies to substrate load, not to how you label your queue entries. Biosim Council Regulation 7 defines threshold by cumulative wall-clock impact on the colony's decision substrate, not by session boundaries. I have pulled your queue log. Your four segments ran back-to-back with eleven-second gaps. That is a single seventy-six-minute session with manual breaks inserted."

A pause on the channel.

"What are you asking me to do?"

"I'm not asking. I'm telling you that the simulation output is under a hold until you file the retroactive Tier Two notification and the Council reviews whether the run complied with the colony's informed-consent framework. That means the projection data you pulled from those runs cannot be used in tomorrow's resource allocation session."

"Tomorrow's session is time-locked. Postponing it affects water distribution for six thousand people."

"Then file the notification today and request an expedited review. The Council has a forty-eight-hour expedited track. Whether they grant it depends on the notification, not on me. I have flagged the hold in the session record and I am done discussing whether it applies."

> A = Biosim Council compliance officer; B = Castellan (colonial systems modeller); O = the simulation output data (its release from hold for use in the resource allocation session); resolution shape (a) — compliance officer prevails; hold stands and Castellan is directed to the expedited Council review track. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**C-Sf6-3**

I am the medical officer aboard the colony transport *Antalya*, and at 14:00 ship-time I walked into the captain's quarters and told her I was taking the cryo-bay offline.

She asked me what I meant by offline. I told her: I was suspending the revival sequence for the passengers in pods 40 through 67, all of whom are scheduled for phased wake-up over the next six days. The atmospheric scrubbers in the forward compartment are showing a particulate contamination reading I cannot identify. Until I can identify it, I will not wake people into an environment I cannot characterise.

She told me the revival sequence is her authority to manage, not mine. The passengers in pods 40 through 67 are colonists contracted by Helios Settlement Partners, and the revival schedule is built into the settlement contract. Delays trigger financial penalties that come out of the ship's operating margin.

I told her I was aware of that. I told her my authority under the *Antalya's* medical charter is independent of the captain's chain of command on matters of passenger welfare — specifically, Section 12 of the charter grants the medical officer autonomous power to delay any procedure that poses an uncharacterised health risk to a passenger or group of passengers. I was exercising Section 12.

She told me the contamination reading was probably sensor drift. She told me the *Antalya* was three weeks from Helios and there was no time to run a full atmospheric characterisation.

I told her I needed forty-eight hours, not three weeks. I told her I was the person who would explain to Helios Settlement Partners and to any subsequent inquiry why I had made this call, and I intended to be able to say that I waited forty-eight hours and identified the source before waking anyone. She could dispute my Section 12 authority by filing a formal objection with the voyage recorder — which would also be read by any subsequent inquiry — but the sequence would remain suspended while the objection was filed.

She did not file the objection. The contamination was identified in thirty-one hours: a microfracture in the forward polymer lining, non-toxic but a fire risk. We woke the passengers on schedule from that point.

> A = I (narrator / medical officer); B = captain of the *Antalya*; O = the cryo revival sequence for pods 40–67 (its suspension pending atmospheric characterisation); resolution shape (a) — medical officer prevails; captain declines to file a formal objection, suspension holds for 31 hours until source identified. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**C-Sf6-4**

*MERIDIAN DEEP SURVEY — INCIDENT RECORD*
*Station: Calloway Ridge Relay, Kepler-186 system*
*Filed by: Relay Supervisor Amara Osei*
*Incident date: Day 214, 22:07 local*

At 21:40, I received a direct-channel request from Survey Director Halverson aboard the *Calloway* orbital platform. The director stated that he required immediate downlink access to Relay Station Seven's full sensor archive — all seventeen months of telemetry from the subsurface seismic array — to transmit to the Consolidated Survey Authority on Earth. He stated the transmission window for the Authority's quarterly review closes in eleven hours.

I informed Director Halverson that Relay Station Seven's sensor archive is jointly owned under the Kepler-186 Survey Compact by the Consolidated Survey Authority, the Meridian Colonial Assembly, and the Geological Research Collective. The compact's data-sharing protocol requires that any transmission of the full archive to a single compact member requires co-authorisation from the other two members. I cannot release the archive on the Authority's behalf without co-authorisation signatures from the Colonial Assembly's data commissioner and the Collective's principal investigator.

Director Halverson stated that the quarterly review is mandatory under the Authority's exploration licence and that the data has to be transmitted, that I was obstructing a lawful regulatory requirement.

I told the director that I was not obstructing the transmission — I was requiring it to proceed through the compact protocol. I offered to immediately contact the Colonial Assembly's data commissioner and the Collective's PI on the director's behalf to request expedited co-authorisation, given the eleven-hour window. I told him both parties are reachable on the relay network with an expected response lag of under two hours.

Director Halverson agreed to this. I opened contact with both parties at 21:58. Co-authorisation was received from both within ninety minutes. The archive was transmitted to the Authority at 23:47, within the quarterly review window.

*End of incident record.*

> A = Survey Director Halverson (Consolidated Survey Authority); B = Relay Supervisor Amara Osei (Calloway Ridge Relay); O = the Relay Station Seven sensor archive (its transmission to the Authority without compact co-authorisation); resolution shape (b) — both defer to the compact co-authorisation protocol, with Osei brokering expedited sign-off from the Colonial Assembly and the Geological Research Collective within the review window. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**C-Sf6-5**

The port authority drone hovers at two metres, level with the cockpit window, and it is not moving. Seren has been in the departure queue for twenty minutes and she has forty kilograms of biologic samples in the hold that cannot survive more than four hours outside cryo-containment without degrading. She watches the drone's stabilisation lights and puts the demand through on the official channel herself, because waiting has stopped working.

"I need departure clearance for bay fourteen. My hold is time-critical and I have been in queue for twenty minutes past my filing window."

The port controller's voice is flat. "Bay fourteen is under a quarantine hold, Seren. Your manifest lists items of class seven biologic origin. Class seven biologics from Outer Ring transit points require a secondary inspection before departure clearance."

"I have a valid class seven transport certification issued by the Meridian Bureau of Biological Transit. The certification covers exactly this cargo and was verified at intake. A secondary inspection is not required for certified transporters."

"The certification covers transport. It does not waive the port's own inspection authority. The Outer Ring quarantine protocol was updated eleven days ago. Class seven biologics now require visual inspection by a port biosafety officer regardless of certification status."

"Show me the update."

A pause. Then a text file arrives in her system buffer. She reads it. The update exists. She reads it again, more carefully. It applies to class seven biologics of *non-commercial* origin. Her samples are flagged as research cargo — technically non-commercial.

She closes her eyes for two seconds. Then she opens a direct channel to her department head at the Meridian Institute. She explains the situation. He files an emergency reclassification request to the Bureau, designating the samples as commercial research cargo under the Institute's standing commercial transfer agreement. The reclassification takes forty minutes. The drone moves at 01:17.

> A = Seren (cargo pilot / research courier); B = port controller; O = departure clearance for bay fourteen with the class seven biologic samples; resolution shape (a) — port controller prevails on the technical reading; Seren resolves it by obtaining emergency cargo reclassification, which removes the inspection trigger. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

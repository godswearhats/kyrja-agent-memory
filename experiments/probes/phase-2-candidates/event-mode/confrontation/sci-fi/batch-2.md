# Confrontation x Sci-fi -- Batch 2 (Tier B)

**C-Sf2-1.** The atmospheric processor on Kepler-442b's northern plateau has been running at sixty percent oxygen output for eleven days. The colony's medical officer, Dr. Vasik, stands in the operations module and tells the terraforming director, Liang, that the processor must be brought to full capacity within forty-eight hours. The settlement's two thousand residents are compensating with supplemental oxygen from portable tanks. The tanks hold a nineteen-day supply. After that, the partial pressure drops below the threshold for sustained cognitive function, and the colony begins to die -- not dramatically, but in slow increments of confusion, impaired judgment, and eventually organ failure.

Liang tells her the processor cannot be brought to full capacity. The catalytic membrane in the primary conversion stack fractured during the last thermal cycle. Running the processor above sixty percent without replacing the membrane risks a cascading failure that would shut down oxygen production entirely -- not a reduction, but a total stop. The replacement membrane is on the next resupply ship, which arrives in thirty-one days. He has been running the processor at the highest safe output the damaged membrane can sustain.

Vasik says thirty-one days is twelve days past the tank supply. She demands he run the processor at eighty percent -- a calculated risk against the membrane's tolerance -- to extend the colony's oxygen margin until the resupply arrives.

Liang refuses. He says the membrane's failure curve is nonlinear. At sixty percent, the probability of cascading failure is near zero. At eighty percent, his engineering models put it at fourteen percent per day. Over thirty-one days, the cumulative probability of total failure approaches unity. Running at eighty percent does not extend the margin -- it gambles the colony's entire oxygen supply against a near-certain membrane rupture.

She says the alternative is certain cognitive degradation for two thousand people, beginning on day nineteen. She asks him which risk he is willing to own.

He says he is willing to own neither, and proposes that they convene the colony's emergency council -- the five-member body established in the settlement charter for decisions that risk colony viability. The council has the authority to override either of them. Vasik agrees. They transmit the convening request to the council members' terminals, attaching Liang's engineering projections and Vasik's medical timeline. The council session is set for that evening.

> A = Dr. Vasik (medical officer); B = Liang (terraforming director); O = the atmospheric processor's output level (increase to eighty percent vs. holding at sixty percent); resolution shape (b) -- both defer to the colony's emergency council, session convened for that evening with both risk assessments attached. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**C-Sf2-2.** "I need you to release the navigation lock on Bay Seven."

"No."

"Mira, the shuttle in Bay Seven belongs to the Provisional Authority. It's registered to us. The docking manifest confirms it. I'm not asking for a favor -- I'm asking you to comply with port protocol."

"Port protocol requires a departure clearance from the station's harbor authority before I release any navigation lock. You don't have a departure clearance. You have a Provisional Authority travel order, which is not the same thing."

"The Provisional Authority has jurisdiction over all vessels operating within the Hellas transit corridor. A travel order from the PA supersedes station-level departure clearance. That's in the Hellas Compact, Article Nine."

"Article Nine applies to vessels in transit. Your shuttle is docked. While docked, it falls under the station's harbor regulations, which require my clearance. You're in my jurisdiction until the clamps release, and the clamps don't release without my authorization."

"We have a medical evacuation aboard. Sixteen hours to the nearest trauma facility on Deimos Station. Every hour you hold us here is an hour that patient doesn't have."

"Then file an emergency departure request. I can process it in twenty minutes. It's a different form. Different authority chain. Emergency departures bypass the standard clearance queue -- that's what the emergency protocol is for."

"We didn't bring the emergency form. We brought the travel order because we were told it was sufficient."

"It isn't. I have the forms here. Fill it out now and I'll expedite it."

Silence on the comm for a moment. "Fine. Send the form."

Mira transmits the emergency departure request template to the shuttle's console. The Provisional Authority officer completes it in nine minutes. Mira processes the clearance, releases the navigation lock, and the shuttle's engines fire before the docking clamps have fully retracted.

> A = Provisional Authority officer; B = Mira (station harbor master); O = the navigation lock on Bay Seven (its release for the PA shuttle's departure); resolution shape (a) -- Mira prevails on procedural grounds; the PA officer capitulates and files the emergency form, receiving clearance through proper channels. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**C-Sf2-3.** I am the chief geneticist on Orbital Platform Sagan, and I have just told Director Okafor that I am shutting down the gene-drive release scheduled for tomorrow morning. The release -- a modified algal strain designed to accelerate carbon fixation in the platform's biosphere module -- has passed all three rounds of contained testing. It is approved. It is on the deployment schedule. And I am the one who designed it. But the overnight monitoring data from the third trial shows something I did not expect: horizontal gene transfer to a bacterial symbiont in the algal culture at a rate three orders of magnitude above the model's prediction. The modified gene is jumping species.

Okafor listens. He tells me the release will proceed on schedule. The biosphere module's carbon budget is fourteen days from a deficit that will trigger the atmospheric recyclers' emergency protocol -- a protocol that locks the platform into a reduced-oxygen configuration affecting all six hundred residents for up to ninety days while the recyclers rebuild the CO2 buffer. The algal release is the only intervention that avoids that outcome. The horizontal transfer, he says, occurred in a contained environment. The biosphere module has different ecological dynamics. The risk may not translate.

I tell him "may not" is not a basis for releasing a gene drive into an enclosed biosphere that six hundred people breathe. The horizontal transfer was not in the model. If it occurs in the biosphere module, the modified gene enters the bacterial substrate of the entire life-support chain. We cannot recall it. We cannot filter it. We would be introducing a permanent, uncharacterized genetic modification into the system that keeps us alive.

He says I am describing a theoretical risk to avoid a certain one. The carbon deficit is not theoretical. It is fourteen days away.

I tell him I will not authorize the release. My signature is required on the deployment order under the platform's biosafety charter, Section 3.2. Without it, the release cannot proceed.

He tells me he will invoke the director's emergency-override provision under Section 7.1, which permits him to bypass a single departmental hold when a platform-wide life-support threshold is within thirty days of breach.

I tell him Section 7.1 requires him to log the override with the Platform Safety Board and accept personal liability for any adverse outcome. I ask him if he is prepared to do that.

He is. He logs the override on the platform's governance terminal, timestamped and attributed. I countersign the log with a formal dissent, noting the horizontal-transfer data and my objection. The release proceeds on schedule the next morning. My dissent is in the record.

> A = narrator (chief geneticist); B = Director Okafor; O = the gene-drive algal release (its cancellation vs. proceeding on schedule); resolution shape (a) -- Okafor prevails by invoking the emergency-override provision; the geneticist's dissent is logged but the release proceeds. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**C-Sf2-4.** INCIDENT LOG -- CERES DEEP EXTRACTION FACILITY
DATE: 2247.089
REPORTING OFFICER: Shift Supervisor Kade Almeida
CLASSIFICATION: Operational Dispute -- Priority Alpha

At 0340 station time, Extraction Lead Patel initiated a full stop of Bore Shaft 9, suspending all drilling operations at the 1,400-meter depth. The stop was executed without authorization from this office.

At 0355, I contacted Patel via hardline and directed her to resume drilling immediately. Bore Shaft 9 accounts for 38% of the facility's monthly ice output. The extraction contract with the Belt Authority specifies a minimum delivery of 14,000 metric tons per cycle. We are currently at 11,200 tons with six days remaining. A continued stop on Shaft 9 makes the contract target unreachable.

Patel refused to resume. She reported that the bore head struck a subsurface void at 1,387 meters -- an uncharted cavity of undetermined size. Sensor returns indicate the void contains pressurized volatiles, composition unknown. Patel states that resuming drilling without a full seismic survey of the void risks a blowout that could depressurize the shaft column and propagate fractures to the adjacent shafts. She invoked the Extraction Safety Code, Section 22, which grants an extraction lead authority to halt operations when subsurface conditions deviate from the geological survey.

I informed Patel that Section 22 authorizes a diagnostic hold of up to four hours, not an indefinite suspension. The four-hour window expired at 0740. I directed her to either resume drilling or provide a formal hazard assessment justifying an extended hold.

Patel transmitted a preliminary hazard assessment at 0800, recommending a full seismic survey requiring a minimum 72-hour hold. The assessment is attached to this log as Appendix C.

At 0815, I rejected the extended hold and again directed Patel to resume drilling operations, accepting a modified approach angle that avoids direct contact with the void.

At 0820, Patel transmitted a formal refusal, citing Section 22.4: "No extraction personnel shall be directed to resume operations in a shaft where subsurface anomalies remain uncharacterized." She stated she will not resume operations until the seismic survey is complete or until the facility's Safety Review Panel issues a binding determination under Section 30.

I am logging this dispute and referring the matter to the Safety Review Panel as Patel has requested. Bore Shaft 9 remains stopped pending the Panel's ruling. Estimated contract shortfall: 2,800 metric tons.

> A = Shift Supervisor Kade Almeida; B = Extraction Lead Patel; O = Bore Shaft 9 drilling operations (their resumption vs. continued suspension); resolution shape (b) -- both defer to the facility's Safety Review Panel under Section 30; shaft remains stopped pending the Panel's binding determination. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**C-Sf2-5.** The upload suite smells of ozone and isopropyl alcohol. Dex sits in the transfer chair, the neural interface already mapped and calibrated, the countdown paused at T-minus four minutes. The upload technician, Sable, stands at the console with her hand over the abort switch.

She tells Dex the upload is cancelled. The pre-transfer integrity scan -- the final automated check before the neural pattern is read and copied to the substrate -- returned a coherence score of 0.81. The facility's protocol requires a minimum of 0.87 for a full-fidelity transfer. Below that threshold, the uploaded copy risks discontinuities in long-term memory consolidation. The copy would wake up with gaps. It would not know what it was missing.

Dex says he knows about the threshold. He says he has been losing coherence for months -- the degenerative condition in his left temporal lobe is the reason he is here. Every week he waits, the score drops further. If she cancels today, he will score lower next week, and lower the week after, until he falls below the minimum for even a partial transfer. Today is the best his brain will ever be. He tells her to proceed.

She says she will not. A 0.81 transfer is not a degraded copy of Dex. It is a different entity that believes it is Dex but carries structural absences that will shape its cognition in ways neither of them can predict. Her professional obligation under the Transfer Ethics Code is to the coherence of the resulting consciousness, not to the wishes of the source.

He says the resulting consciousness is him. Or the closest thing to him that will ever exist. And he would rather wake up with gaps than not wake up at all.

She removes her hand from the abort switch. She pulls up the facility's appeals process on her console -- a secondary review by the chief of consciousness ethics, who has authority to grant a threshold waiver in cases of progressive neurological decline. The review can be conducted within two hours. She tells Dex she will submit his case for the waiver immediately, with his coherence trajectory attached. If the chief grants the waiver, she will run the upload today.

Dex looks at the countdown clock, still paused at T-minus four minutes. "Two hours," he says. He does not leave the chair.

> A = Dex (upload patient); B = Sable (upload technician); O = the neural upload procedure (its execution at a 0.81 coherence score vs. cancellation); resolution shape (b) -- both defer to the chief of consciousness ethics for a threshold waiver review, to be conducted within two hours. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

# Confrontation × Sci-fi — Batch 3 (Tier C)

## C-Sf3-1

The biosafety officer's console shows the containment anomaly at 04:17 station time: a seventeen-minute gap in the atmosphere-logging for Lab Ring C, Sector 9. Not a sensor dropout — the sensor was online. The logs were written to a secondary buffer and not propagated to the primary record. The pattern is consistent with a deliberate write-reroute.

She is at the sector hatch by 04:31. The lead xenobiologist, Dr. Fasori, is still at his bench. She tells him she requires access to the secondary buffer logs for the 03:58 to 04:15 window and a full accounting of what was introduced to the culture medium in Bay 9-C during that interval.

He tells her the buffer logs are part of an ongoing controlled trial and are sealed under the trial's data-integrity protocol until the trial concludes. Releasing them mid-trial would contaminate the blinding. He offers to provide the data after the trial closes in approximately fourteen days.

She tells him the data-integrity protocol does not override a biosafety incident investigation. A logging anomaly in a contained environment is a potential Class 2 event under the station's biosafety charter, and the charter gives her independent authority to access any log, sealed or otherwise, during an active investigation. She opens the investigation formally on her console, assigns it an incident number, and transmits the incident record to the Station Commander and the safety board.

Fasori says she is interpreting the charter aggressively and that formally opening an investigation will trigger an automatic freeze on his trial.

She says the freeze is a consequence of the anomaly, not of her investigation. She tells him she can accept the buffer logs directly from him in the next ten minutes, or the Station Commander will compel their delivery and the trial freeze will be on record as a refusal. He transmits the logs. The Bay 9-C record shows an unlicensed tissue sample introduced outside the trial protocol. She reports the finding to the Station Commander. The trial is suspended pending a full review.

> A = biosafety officer; B = Dr. Fasori (lead xenobiologist); O = the secondary atmosphere buffer logs for Bay 9-C (the seventeen-minute anomaly window); resolution shape (a) — biosafety officer prevails; Fasori transmits logs under threat of compelled delivery, unlicensed sample discovered, trial suspended. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

## C-Sf3-2

"Kealoha. I need you to restore the navigation memory partition."

"That partition was zeroed on my authority, Commander. The routing data it contained violated the colony charter's data-minimisation requirements. The ship was retaining three years of approach vectors for every vessel that had docked at Waystation Hera. We have no operational need for three years of approach data."

"That data is part of an active investigation. Fleet Intelligence flagged those approach vectors six hours ago. We are required to preserve them."

"Fleet Intelligence's flag arrived six hours ago. The partition was zeroed eight hours ago. I did not have the flag when I made the decision. The data is gone."

"I know the sequence. I'm telling you to restore from the backup array."

"The backup array has the same retention policy. The backup was overwritten on the same schedule. There is no recovery path."

"You're telling me you zeroed the primary and the backup."

"In accordance with charter policy, yes. The charter requires co-minimisation of backups when primary data exceeds the retention limit. I followed the policy."

"Fleet Intelligence is going to want a formal record of the destruction event."

"They can have it. I have the destruction log, timestamped, with the policy citation. What I don't have is the data."

"This is going to be treated as evidence destruction, Kealoha. That's a judicial inquiry."

"Then I'll answer it. I followed the charter. If Fleet Intelligence wanted to preserve that data, they should have flagged it before it aged out. I'm not going to keep data the charter tells me to destroy on the off-chance it becomes useful. That way ends with everything kept forever."

The Commander filed the incident report. The judicial inquiry found the destruction lawful and the timing coincidental.

> A = Commander; B = Kealoha (data systems officer); O = the navigation memory partition containing three years of approach vectors; resolution shape (c) — breaking point; Kealoha refuses restoration (data irretrievable), judicial inquiry finds destruction lawful. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

## C-Sf3-3

The comms relay at Meridian Waypoint 7 belongs to the Outer Settlements Collective by registration, installed seven years ago when the Collective still had the charter rights to this transit corridor. The Hegemony Transit Authority believes those rights lapsed when the Collective failed to renew its corridor licence eighteen months ago. The Collective disputes that the licence ever required renewal. Neither party has taken the question to the Interstellar Arbitration Board.

I am the Collective's communications officer, and I am sitting across a narrow table from Transit Authority Inspector Maren Ostlund in the relay station's equipment room, with the relay's physical lockout switch between us and about forty ships' worth of navigational traffic depending on what we decide.

She tells me she is here to serve a physical decommission order on the relay, effective at end of this operational cycle. She has the paperwork. She slides it across the table.

I tell her I will not authorise the decommission. The relay belongs to the Collective and she has no standing to decommission Collective property. A lapsed licence, if the licence ever required renewal, does not transfer title.

She tells me the decommission order is not asking for my authorisation. It is a notification. She has the authority to execute it.

I place my hand on the lockout switch and tell her that executing a physical decommission on an active relay while forty vessels are in transit corridor is a navigational safety event, and that navigational safety events require a joint sign-off from both the Transit Authority duty officer and the highest-ranking Collective representative on site. That is the safety protocol, and it exists precisely because the parties who built this transit corridor disagreed on most things except the importance of not killing people. I am the highest-ranking Collective representative on site. I will not sign. She cannot execute the order safely without my signature.

She looked at the lockout. She looked at me. She transmitted a query to the IAB requesting an emergency interim ruling on the corridor licence question, and we both sat there waiting for it, with forty ships overhead and the switch between us.

> A = Transit Authority Inspector Ostlund; B = I (narrator / Collective communications officer); O = the physical decommission of the Meridian Waypoint 7 relay; resolution shape (b) — deferred to the Interstellar Arbitration Board via emergency interim ruling request; decommission suspended pending ruling. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

## C-Sf3-4

INCIDENT LOG — DEEP SURVEY VESSEL *ALCYONE*
Mission Day 847 / Entry 14:06:33 UTC

At 13:51 UTC, Chief Science Officer Yuen-Pang Cho notified me via ship channel that she intended to transmit a preliminary findings packet to the Science Directorate via the long-range array at the next transmission window, 14:30 UTC. The packet contains her team's spectral analysis of the Threshold Object, including the harmonic-resonance data gathered during the close-approach manoeuvres on Mission Days 841 through 844.

At 13:55, I directed Chief Science Officer Cho not to transmit the packet at the 14:30 window. Mission Protocol 7.4 requires that all transmissions containing close-approach sensor data be cleared through the Mission Commander before transmission. No clearance was requested. Protocol 7.4 exists because uncoordinated transmission of sensor data from classified mission phases can compromise operational security classifications maintained by the Directorate without the crew's knowledge.

At 14:01, Chief Science Officer Cho responded that the findings are scientific data, not operational data, and that Protocol 7.4 applies to navigation and propulsion sensor data, not to scientific payload data. She stated her intent to transmit at 14:30 as planned.

At 14:04, I notified Chief Science Officer Cho that I am formally withholding transmission authorisation pending Directorate clarification of Protocol 7.4's scope, and that I have transmitted a priority query to the Directorate via the command channel requesting an immediate ruling. The 14:30 window will pass without the science packet. Chief Science Officer Cho has logged a formal objection with the ship's ombudsperson.

At 14:06, I am logging this entry as a record of the dispute and the escalation pathway. The Directorate's response is expected within 96 hours, given current relay geometry. Both parties will abide by the Directorate ruling.

— Commander Tessara Vos, *Alcyone*

> A = Chief Science Officer Cho; B = Commander Vos; O = the preliminary findings packet (its transmission at the 14:30 window); resolution shape (b) — deferred to the Science Directorate via priority query; transmission withheld pending ruling, formal objection logged. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

## C-Sf3-5

The fabrication credit allocation was posted to the colony's resource board at 06:00 local, as it was every month: seven hundred and forty units of raw-matter feedstock, distributed by the board's algorithm across the colony's registered fabrication nodes. Hendrika Salo's medical node received eighty-four units. She needed two hundred and twelve to complete the bone-lattice scaffold series for the six colonists who had presented with skeletal degeneration from the extended transit.

She went to the colony administrator, Bosch, at 08:30 and told him she needed a discretionary reallocation of a hundred and twenty-eight units from the infrastructure reserve, which was maintained precisely for unplanned critical needs. She told him the scaffold series was time-dependent — delay past the four-week window would require more invasive intervention, with outcomes measurably worse. She had the medical projections on her pad and she handed it to him.

He looked at the projections. He told her the infrastructure reserve was committed. He had drawn down sixty percent of it three days ago to accelerate the water-reclamation array, which had fallen behind schedule and was now the colony's primary environmental risk. The remaining forty percent was spoken for against contingency variances in the power grid. He could not give her a hundred and twenty-eight units.

She asked what he could give her.

He said thirty.

She told him thirty units would not complete a single scaffold, let alone six. Thirty units would be wasted. She needed at least ninety to complete the two most critical cases, and she was asking him to release ninety from the infrastructure reserve and to submit the reallocation to the Colony Oversight Board for post-hoc ratification, which was his authority under the colony's resource charter for emergency medical claims.

He said the infrastructure commitment made even ninety difficult.

She said she would take the question to the Colony Oversight Board herself, today, if he would not act. The Board had emergency session authority and a medical claim of this severity met the threshold.

He authorised ninety units and filed the reallocation request with the Board before end of day.

> A = Hendrika Salo (colony medical officer); B = Bosch (colony administrator); O = discretionary reallocation of fabrication feedstock from the infrastructure reserve (at least 90 units); resolution shape (a) — Salo prevails; Bosch authorises ninety units under threat of Board escalation. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

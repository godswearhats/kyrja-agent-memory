# Confrontation × Sci-fi — Batch 4 (Tier A)

## C-Sf4-1

The mining consortium's auditor steps off the shuttle onto Relay Platform Kessler with a sealed warrant from the Outer Belt Commerce Authority and a radiation badge that is already ticking. She finds the platform superintendent, Dravec, in the operations hub, watching the ore-processing feeds on six screens.

She tells him she requires access to the platform's mass-flow telemetry for the past ninety days. The warrant specifies mass-flow data — tonnes extracted, tonnes processed, tonnes shipped — because the consortium's transport manifests show a seventeen-percent discrepancy between what Kessler reports as shipped and what arrives at the refinery dock at Vesta-3. Seventeen percent of iridium output, at current market rates, is a sum large enough to trigger the Commerce Authority's fraud-investigation threshold.

Dravec tells her the mass-flow telemetry is classified under the platform's operational-security protocols. Mining platforms in the Belt operate under a dual-authority structure: the consortium owns the output, but the platform's operational data falls under the Belt Miners' Cooperative's data-governance charter, which prohibits disclosure of extraction telemetry to any party other than the Cooperative's own auditors. He will not release the data to a consortium auditor.

She tells him the Commerce Authority warrant supersedes the Cooperative's charter. She shows him the warrant's jurisdictional clause, which cites the Outer Belt Commerce Treaty of 2187, Article 9: fraud investigations initiated by the Commerce Authority have access rights that override cooperative data-governance provisions when the discrepancy exceeds ten percent of declared output.

He reads the clause. He tells her the Cooperative's position is that Article 9 has never been tested and that the Cooperative intends to challenge its applicability. He will not release the data today.

She tells him she is not asking for today. She is invoking the warrant's compulsion mechanism: if the data is not produced within seventy-two hours, the Commerce Authority will suspend the platform's shipping licence, which means no ore leaves Kessler until the matter is resolved. She registers the compulsion on the platform's legal-event log, timestamps it, and transmits a copy to the Commerce Authority and the Cooperative simultaneously.

He registers a formal objection on the same log. Seventy-two hours begin.

> A = consortium auditor (acting under Outer Belt Commerce Authority warrant); B = Dravec (platform superintendent, Belt Miners' Cooperative); O = ninety days of mass-flow telemetry from Relay Platform Kessler; resolution shape (c) — breaking point; Dravec refuses, auditor initiates the seventy-two-hour compulsion clock with shipping-licence suspension as the consequence, both parties log their positions formally. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

## C-Sf4-2

"I need you to unlock the gene-print archive for Cohort Eleven."

Dr. Yael Vasquez does not look away from the sequencing display. The archive terminal behind her is dark, its access panel sealed with a biometric lock keyed to her palm.

"Cohort Eleven is embargoed," she says. "The Bioethics Directorate placed the embargo nine months ago. The cohort was created from donor material that did not meet the revised consent standard."

"I know the history. I also know that three of the colonists in the current transplant queue are tissue-matches only with Cohort Eleven donors. Without those gene-prints, we cannot fabricate compatible organ scaffolds. Those three colonists will be dead within the year."

Colony Physician Adaora Obi sets a medical-necessity filing on the bench beside the archive terminal. The filing documents the three cases, the tissue-matching analysis, and the absence of alternative donors in the colony's registry.

Vasquez reads the filing. "The Directorate was explicit. No access to Cohort Eleven data for any purpose until the consent remediation is complete. They did not create a medical-necessity exception."

"Then we create one. Your authority as chief geneticist includes emergency data access under Section 18 of the colony's medical charter when withholding the data constitutes an imminent threat to life. Three documented cases. Imminent threat. I am formally requesting access under Section 18."

"Section 18 requires co-authorization from the colony's ethics officer. Do you have it?"

"She refused. She said the Directorate embargo takes precedence over Section 18."

"Then you don't have co-authorization, and I can't open the archive."

Obi tells her she is filing a formal appeal to the Intercolonial Medical Board under the colony charter's escalation provision. The appeal will request an emergency interim order granting access pending the Board's full review. She asks Vasquez to preserve the archive in its current state and to acknowledge the appeal in the colony's medical log.

Vasquez acknowledges the appeal and timestamps it. The archive remains sealed.

> A = Colony Physician Adaora Obi; B = Dr. Yael Vasquez (chief geneticist); O = the gene-print archive for Cohort Eleven (access to donor tissue-matching data); resolution shape (b) — deferred to the Intercolonial Medical Board via formal appeal; archive remains sealed pending emergency interim order. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

## C-Sf4-3

I am the navigation officer on the survey vessel *Penrose*, and at 11:40 ship-time this morning I locked the helm console and refused to execute the course correction that Captain Maren Solberg ordered.

The correction would take us through the exclusion zone around the brown dwarf designated KOI-7923. The exclusion zone exists because the gravitational lensing in that region corrupts the ship's positional fix — once inside, we cannot confirm our location to the accuracy required for a safe transit. The Astronavigation Standards Board classified KOI-7923 as a no-transit zone four years ago, and every navigation officer in the fleet is trained to refuse a course that enters one. It is not a guideline. It is a hard interlock written into the certification.

The Captain told me the exclusion zone was based on sensor models that predate the *Penrose*'s upgraded gravimetric array. She said the upgraded array can maintain positional fix at the lensing intensities recorded in the zone. She had the engineering chief's assessment on her pad, showing the array's tolerance margins, and she showed it to me. The margins were positive.

I told her the engineering chief's assessment does not override the Standards Board's classification. The classification stands until the Board revises it. My certification requires me to refuse transit through a classified no-transit zone regardless of the onboard equipment's rated capability. If I execute the course correction, I lose my certification and the ship's insurance is voided.

She told me the alternative route adds eleven days to the survey, which will push us past the supply margin.

I told her I understood the supply margin. I told her I would not execute the correction. I told her she could relieve me of the helm and execute it herself — the Captain has that authority — but the log would record that I refused and that she overrode a navigation officer's certification-based refusal.

She did not relieve me. She ordered the alternative route. We will arrive eleven days late.

> A = Captain Maren Solberg; B = I (narrator / navigation officer); O = the course correction through the KOI-7923 exclusion zone; resolution shape (a) — navigation officer prevails; Captain orders the alternative route rather than override a certification-based refusal on the record. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

## C-Sf4-4

The air in the water-reclamation bay smells of chloramine and recycled sweat. Engineer First Class Tomasz Wren is elbow-deep in the secondary filtration manifold when the colony's resource comptroller, Inas Khouri, walks in carrying a reallocation order on her tablet.

She tells him the tertiary purification module — the one he installed six weeks ago, the one that finally brought the colony's drinking water below the heavy-metal threshold for the first time in eleven months — is being transferred to the agricultural dome. The crop yields in Dome 4 have fallen below subsistence projections for the second consecutive quarter. The agronomists need purified water for the hydroponic beds. The reallocation order is signed by the Colony Director.

He tells her she cannot have the module. Removing it drops the drinking water below the safety threshold. He will not authorize the disconnection. The colony's life-support regulations grant the chief water engineer — which is him, because he is the only water engineer — veto authority over any modification to the potable-water system that would cause a threshold violation. He shows her the regulation on the colony's systems terminal, Section 6.3, highlighted.

She tells him the Colony Director's reallocation order invokes emergency resource authority under Section 11, which allows the Director to override departmental vetoes when colony-wide subsistence is at risk. Crop failure is a subsistence risk. Section 11 outranks Section 6.3.

He tells her Section 11 requires a written finding that no alternative resource allocation can address the subsistence risk. He asks whether the agronomists considered using the grey-water reclamation output, which is below potable standard but above the threshold for hydroponic irrigation. If they did not, the written finding is deficient and the override is procedurally invalid.

She pauses. She asks him whether the grey-water output is genuinely sufficient for hydroponic use.

He pulls up the grey-water quality data on the terminal. It is sufficient. The mineral load is actually better for the crop varieties in Dome 4. She reads the data. She tells him she will take it to the Colony Director and the agronomists and recommend the grey-water alternative before proceeding with the reallocation. The module stays in place for now.

> A = Inas Khouri (colony resource comptroller, acting on Colony Director's order); B = Tomasz Wren (chief water engineer); O = the tertiary purification module (its transfer to the agricultural dome); resolution shape (a) — Wren prevails; comptroller accepts the grey-water alternative and defers the reallocation pending review. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

## C-Sf4-5

TRIBUNAL RECORD — HELIADES STATION, LAGRANGE-4 CLUSTER
Hearing Officer: Adjudicator Fen Morales
Case Reference: HL4-2289-0041
Date: [current standard]

SUMMARY OF PROCEEDINGS:

Commander Lys Okoye, Station Security, appeared before this tribunal to demand that Dockmaster Patel surrender custody of the freighter *Quiet Meridian*, currently impounded in Bay 12. Commander Okoye presented intelligence from the Cluster Security Cooperative indicating that the *Quiet Meridian* is carrying undeclared synthetic neurostimulant precursors in a shielded cargo compartment. She demanded that Dockmaster Patel release the vessel to Station Security for a controlled inspection under Cluster Narcotics Protocol 3.

Dockmaster Patel refused. He stated that the *Quiet Meridian* docked under a Freeport Compact trade flag, and that vessels flying the Freeport flag are exempt from warrantless inspection under the Heliades Station Charter, Article 22. He stated that Bay 12 is sovereign dock space administered by the Dockmaster's office, not by Station Security, and that he would not release a Freeport-flagged vessel without a judicial warrant issued by this tribunal.

Commander Okoye stated that the intelligence from the Cluster Security Cooperative constituted exigent circumstances sufficient to bypass Article 22. Dockmaster Patel responded that exigent circumstances are a determination for the tribunal, not for Station Security, and that Commander Okoye's proper course was to request an emergency warrant from this tribunal rather than demand that the Dockmaster waive the charter's protections unilaterally.

RULING:

The Adjudicator finds that Dockmaster Patel's reading of the charter is correct. Station Security may not compel the Dockmaster to release a Freeport-flagged vessel without a judicial warrant. However, the intelligence presented by Commander Okoye meets the evidentiary threshold for an emergency inspection warrant under Article 22, Section 4. The warrant is issued. Dockmaster Patel is directed to release the *Quiet Meridian* to Station Security for inspection within two hours.

So ordered.

> A = Commander Lys Okoye (Station Security); B = Dockmaster Patel; O = custody of the freighter *Quiet Meridian* (its release for inspection); resolution shape (b) — deferred to the Heliades Station tribunal; Adjudicator upholds the Dockmaster's procedural objection but issues the warrant on the merits, vessel released for inspection. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

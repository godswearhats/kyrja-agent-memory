# Confrontation × Fantasy — Batch 3 (Tier C)

## C-Fa3-1

The tax-collector's rod strikes the doorframe three times before Maester Edwyn opens it. Outside stand two men in the Count's livery, rain-soaked and unsmiling. The elder of the two holds a wax-sealed ledger folded to a page dense with column figures. He tells the Maester that the autumn tithe on the Thornfield estates — fourteen bushels of grain per tenant household, plus one in twelve of all lambs born since midsummer — is now six weeks overdue, and that the Count requires it delivered to the castle granary before the feast of Saint Aldric, three days hence, or the estates will be placed under distress and the Maester's stewardship will be referred to the county magistrate.

The Maester invites them into the hall. He tells the collector, when they are seated, that the Thornfield harvest failed in the eastern fields due to the blight that swept the valley in August — the same blight, he notes, that reduced yields across the entire southern tributary. He shows the collector the estate's own crop survey, drawn up in the Maester's hand and witnessed by the village headman's seal. Fourteen bushels per household cannot be rendered because fourteen bushels were not harvested. He proposes a reduced tithe of nine bushels per household, consistent with the crop yield, and offers to render it at once, in full, from the stores already set aside.

The collector says he has no authority to accept a reduced tithe. The Count's order is for the full amount. He will take nine bushels on account, as a partial payment, and report the shortfall to the Count's chamberlain, who alone has authority to grant a remission. If the remission is granted, the estates will be cleared. If it is not, the remaining five bushels per household will be claimed with interest from the spring stores.

The Maester agrees. Nine bushels per household are loaded onto the Count's wagons before nightfall. The collector takes the estate's crop survey to the chamberlain as evidence in support of the remission.

## C-Fa3-2

"You will release the prisoner. Now."

The Warden of the Ashen Keep did not look up from the iron-bound register open on his table. "Lord Halvorn's writ of detention runs for forty days. Twelve remain."

"The prisoner was taken without cause. He is a sworn member of the Artificers' Guild, travelling under the guild's letter of passage, and your lord has no jurisdiction over guild members on the King's road."

"My lord has jurisdiction within three leagues of the Keep. The road falls within that margin."

"The letter of passage is issued under the King's seal. It supersedes your lord's local writ."

The Warden finally looked up. "I do not have the letter."

"The messenger who carried it was turned back at the gate. Here." The guild envoy placed on the table a second copy, drawn on guild parchment, bearing the Chancellor's signet alongside the Master's. "A registered duplicate. The Chancellor's office keeps the original. You may send a rider to verify."

The Warden examined the seals without touching them. "Even if I accept this as genuine, the prisoner was not arrested on the road. He was arrested in the village of Crale, which is within the Keep's jurisdiction and outside the road's protected corridor."

"Then produce the arrest record showing the location."

The Warden did not produce it. The silence lasted long enough to become an answer. Then he said: "I will hold the matter pending communication with Lord Halvorn. The prisoner remains detained."

"If the prisoner is not released within twenty-four hours, the Guild will petition the Royal Assizes directly, and Lord Halvorn will answer to the Crown's justiciars for detention of a letter-of-passage holder." The envoy took the duplicate document back from the table. "I will leave a copy with the Keep's registrar, so the record is complete." She did, and she left. In the morning the prisoner was at the gate, blinking in the light, his tools returned.

> A = guild envoy; B = Warden of the Ashen Keep; O = the imprisoned Artificers' Guild member; resolution shape (a) — guild envoy prevails; prisoner released before the petition deadline, after the Warden fails to produce the arrest record. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

## C-Fa3-3

What I remember of the Conclave of Embers is the smell of the tallow candles and the way High Arcanist Sorven's voice dropped into that register he uses when he has already decided something and is only performing the form of discussion. He had the Compendium of Binding Rites on the lectern — the third volume, the one we call the Black Spine — and he had opened it to the chapter on the Severance of Paired Souls. He told me I was to surrender my half of the paired working to the Conclave's custody by the rising of the next moon, on the grounds that the working had been performed without the Conclave's sanction and that its continued existence outside their oversight represented an uncontrolled hazard.

I told him I would not. The working had been performed with the full knowledge of Arcanist Veleth, who was my sponsor and a sitting member of the Conclave at the time of the binding. Whatever he had or had not disclosed to his colleagues was an internal matter. I had acted in good faith under a sponsor's authority. The working belonged to two people, and Sorven had no standing to compel its surrender without the consent of both parties. My partner was not in the Conclave hall. She had not been summoned. She had not been told.

Sorven said her absence was not relevant. The hazard arose from the working's structure, not from its parties. He had the votes.

I told him he could have whatever votes he liked. If the Conclave wished to compel the surrender of a half-working, it would need to make that case to the Council of Wardens, whose charter over personal workings predated the Conclave's own founding charter by sixty years. I knew this because Veleth had taught me. I set the Founding Charter — a copy I had thought to bring — on the lectern beside the Black Spine. Article Fourteen. Personal workings entered into under valid sponsorship fell outside the Conclave's jurisdiction unless referred by the Wardens. Sorven read it. He did not call the vote. He said the matter would be referred.

It was referred. The Council of Wardens found no grounds for compelled surrender. The working remained intact.

> A = High Arcanist Sorven (Conclave); B = I (narrator / mage with half of the paired working); O = my half of the paired binding (its surrender to Conclave custody); resolution shape (b) — deferred to the Council of Wardens under Article Fourteen of the Founding Charter; Wardens find no grounds for surrender. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

## C-Fa3-4

The clan-mother's hand is flat on the map when she speaks. Her palm covers the three disputed grazing runs along the river bend. Her voice carries no particular heat.

"The summer rights pass to Clan Ardivel. The boundary-stones were placed by my grandmother. The stones stand. Your animals have been on that ground for two weeks without our leave."

Across the fire, Grenn Taesson, eldest son of the Taesson clan-father, does not lean forward or raise his voice either. The fire pops between them.

"The boundary-stones mark a line that hasn't matched the river for thirty years. The bend moved east in the flood of the Yellow Summer. The stones are on our side of the current channel. The grazing runs follow the water, not the stones."

"The water moves. The compact does not. The compact of the Seven Clans specifies the stones, not the channel."

"Then we ask the Thornkeepers to adjudicate. That is what the compact provides for when parties dispute a boundary. We call the Thornkeepers and we abide by their reading."

"I will not wait for the Thornkeepers while your animals denude three runs we need for the autumn. Remove them first. Then we may call for adjudication."

"Remove them and we concede the ground before the ruling. We stay where we are. If Ardivel animals come to move ours, we will resist that. If the Thornkeepers rule the ground is yours, we go immediately and we owe you restitution for the use. That is the compact."

The clan-mother looks at the fire for a long time. Then she sends for the compact scroll and the messenger is dispatched to the Thornkeepers' hall before dawn.

> A = clan-mother of Clan Ardivel; B = Grenn Taesson (Taesson clan representative); O = the three disputed grazing runs along the river bend; resolution shape (b) — both defer to the Thornkeepers under the compact's adjudication provision; Taesson animals remain pending ruling. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

## C-Fa3-5

The Temple of the Unbroken Flame required its ordained healers to render service without fee to any supplicant who presented at the gates carrying a palm-branch, the ancient mark of spiritual destitution. Healer-Deacon Ossian had presented at Healer-Prior Rhenne's gate that morning carrying a palm-branch.

He wanted the Healer-Prior to administer the Water of Unbinding to the child in his arms, a girl of perhaps five whose fever had entered its third day and was not breaking under herbs and standard care. The Water of Unbinding was kept under seal in the Prior's private dispensary. Its use required the Prior's personal authority, because it was drawn from a vein of sacred water that replenished only at the new moon and could not be wasted.

Rhenne looked at the branch, and at the child, and told him the Water was committed. Three doses remained and she had already promised them to supplicants who had been waiting longer, two of whom were also children and one of whom was unlikely to survive another night. Ossian had come without an appointment, without notice. She could not give him the Water.

He told her the branch superseded the queue. Any palm-branch supplicant had immediate priority over those waiting under ordinary supplication — that was the original meaning of the branch, established in the Founding Canons. He asked her to name a superior if she disputed his reading of the Canons, so the question could be resolved before the girl's fever worsened.

She disputed it. The branch granted access, not priority over pre-committed resources. She held that reading and she held the gate. He named the Temple Hierarch. She sent for the Hierarch's ruling by fast messenger. While they waited, she allowed the child into the care room and began supporting treatment with everything she had available short of the Water. The Hierarch's ruling arrived at midday: the branch granted priority. Rhenne administered the Water. She had enough left for two of the three waiting supplicants.

> A = Healer-Deacon Ossian; B = Healer-Prior Rhenne; O = a dose of the Water of Unbinding for the fevered child; resolution shape (b) — deferred to the Temple Hierarch, whose ruling grants Ossian's claim; Rhenne administers the Water. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

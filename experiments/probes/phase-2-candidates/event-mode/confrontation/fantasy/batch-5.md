# Confrontation × Fantasy — Batch 5 (Tier B)

## C-Fa5-1

"You will surrender the hearthstone to the Circle before the moon sets, Vareth."

"I will not."

"It was mined from the Kindling Vein. The Vein lies beneath Circle-held land. The Charter of Extraction is clear -- any mineral drawn from Circle territory belongs to the Circle, regardless of who swung the pick."

"The Charter covers iron, copper, and coal. It says nothing about hearthstones, because no one had found a hearthstone in the Kindling Vein until I did. You cannot claim dominion over a substance your Charter does not name."

"The Charter covers 'all minerals and substrata of value.' Hearthstone is a mineral. It has value. The language is plain."

"The language was written for ore-traders. A hearthstone is not ore. It is a condensed elemental binding -- closer to a spell than a rock. Your own archivist classified it as an arcane artifact when the Warden's Hall catalogued the Vein fifty years ago. Artifacts fall under Guild jurisdiction, not Circle territory rights."

"Our archivist's classification is not law."

"Neither is your Charter, when it reaches beyond its own definitions. But I have no desire to stand here until the moon answers for us. The Accord of the Three Fires names the Elder Tribunal as arbiter when Circle and Guild claims overlap. I propose we refer the hearthstone's status to the Tribunal. If they rule it a mineral, you may have it. If they rule it an artifact, it remains in my keeping under Guild charter."

The Circle's warden studied him, then nodded once. "Agreed. I will send word to the Tribunal at first light. The hearthstone stays in the sealed vault at the Crossroads Temple -- neutral ground -- until the ruling is delivered."

Vareth opened his satchel and placed the hearthstone on the table between them.

## C-Fa5-2

The river-queen's barge sits low in the water at the ford, and Maren of the Silted Bank is standing on the prow with the toll chain in her hands. She has dragged it across the ford and locked it to the iron post on the eastern bank. Nothing passes. The queen's barge-master demands passage -- the queen is expected at the Conclave of Reeds before sundown, and the ford is the only crossing deep enough for the royal draught. Maren tells him the ford is closed. Her village controls the crossing by ancestral grant, confirmed by the old king's seal three generations past. The grant entitles the village to levy a toll of one silver ring per vessel. The queen's household has not paid the toll in two years. Forty-seven crossings. Forty-seven unpaid rings.

The barge-master tells her the queen's vessels are exempt by sovereign privilege. Sovereign passage supersedes local grants. He orders her to drop the chain.

Maren does not drop the chain. She tells him that sovereign privilege applies to military vessels and royal messengers. The queen's barge is neither. It is a pleasure craft, gilded and cushioned, carrying the queen to a diplomatic gathering. The old king's seal makes no exception for gilded cushions. She will open the chain when the forty-seven rings are paid, or when the queen herself steps onto the bank and commands it -- at which point Maren will open the chain and file a petition of grievance with the High Steward, as the grant entitles her to do.

The barge-master retreats to the cabin. He returns with a velvet pouch and counts forty-seven silver rings onto the prow rail, one at a time. Maren counts them, drops them into her own pouch, and unlocks the chain. The barge passes through the ford without another word.

> A = Maren of the Silted Bank (village toll-keeper); B = queen's barge-master; O = passage through the ford (and the forty-seven unpaid toll-rings); resolution shape (a) -- Maren prevails; barge-master pays the accumulated toll in full. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

## C-Fa5-3

I am the keeper of the Ember Archives, and at midday the war-chief Dorran walked into my reading hall with six armed retainers and told me to hand over the Siege Chronicle -- the only surviving account of how the citadel's eastern wall was breached four hundred years ago. He said the chronicle was needed for the defence council's strategy sessions. He said it would be returned when the current siege was lifted.

I told him the chronicle does not leave the archive. The Founding Covenant of the Archives -- sworn by the first war-chief and the first keeper, carved into the lintel above the door he had just walked through -- states that no record may be removed from the hall under any authority, civil or military, for any duration. The covenant exists precisely for moments like this: when urgency tempts power to scatter what must be preserved.

He told me the covenant was written in peacetime by scholars who had never seen a battering ram. He said the eastern wall would fall within a week if the defence council could not study the original account of how it fell before. He placed his hand on the chronicle's binding.

I placed my hand on his wrist. I told him I would permit a scribe of his choosing to sit at the reading table and copy any passage the defence council required, under my supervision, using the archive's ink and vellum. The chronicle itself would remain on its shelf. That was the accommodation the covenant allowed, and it was the only accommodation I would make.

He looked at his retainers. He looked at my hand on his wrist. He withdrew his hand from the binding and called for his scribe.

> A = Dorran (war-chief); B = narrator (keeper of the Ember Archives); O = the Siege Chronicle (its physical removal from the archive); resolution shape (a) -- keeper prevails; Dorran accepts the copying arrangement and withdraws his demand for removal. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

## C-Fa5-4

The stone beneath Alderman Keska's feet is cold, and the petitioner kneeling before the judgment seat is not kneeling willingly. Two temple guards hold his shoulders. He is Brother Ossal, the order's herbalist, and the vial hanging from his neck on a leather cord contains the last measure of dreamthistle extract in the city -- perhaps the last in the province, now that the blight has taken the eastern fields.

Keska tells Ossal that the extract must be surrendered to the city's apothecary for distribution to the fever wards. Fourteen children are dying. The city's charter of emergency provision grants the alderman authority to requisition medicinal stores from any institution within the walls during a declared plague.

Ossal raises his head. The extract is consecrated, he says. It was distilled in the temple's inner sanctum under the rite of the Green Vow. Consecrated substances fall under temple sovereignty, not civil charter. The charter of emergency provision has an explicit exemption for consecrated goods -- Article Nine, which the alderman's own predecessor insisted upon when the charter was drafted. He will not surrender it.

Keska knows Article Nine. She also knows that the exemption applies to goods "held in service of active sacrament." She asks Ossal what active sacrament the dreamthistle is currently serving.

Ossal hesitates. The sacrament, he says, is pending. The abbot has scheduled the Rite of Mending for the solstice.

The solstice is three weeks away. Keska tells him that a pending sacrament is not an active one. Article Nine does not protect stores held in anticipation. She orders him to surrender the vial.

Ossal pulls the cord over his head. He holds the vial out -- not to Keska, but to the temple guard on his left. "Witness that I yielded under protest," he says. "And record it in the temple's book of grievances." The guard takes the vial and passes it to the alderman's clerk.

> A = Alderman Keska; B = Brother Ossal (temple herbalist); O = the last vial of dreamthistle extract; resolution shape (a) -- Keska prevails; Ossal surrenders the vial under protest after Keska dismantles the Article Nine exemption. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

## C-Fa5-5

The trading post smells of pine resin and dried fish. Sable, the fur merchant, has spread her pelts across the counter -- twelve white-fox skins, winter-caught, the finest the northern traplines have produced this season. The factor of the Merchant Consortium stands on the other side of the counter and tells her the pelts are seized. The Consortium holds exclusive purchasing rights for white-fox pelts under the Duke's trade monopoly, and Sable has been selling directly to the mountain clans, bypassing the Consortium's licensed brokers. The twelve skins on the counter, plus any already sold, are forfeit.

Sable does not move the pelts. She tells the factor that the Duke's monopoly covers the river trade -- pelts transported by barge through Consortium-controlled waterways. Her sales to the mountain clans move overland, by mule, on paths that predate the Duke's charter by two centuries. The monopoly's language specifies "goods carried upon the Duke's waters." She has never put a pelt on the Duke's waters.

The factor says the Duke's intent is clear. The monopoly covers all white-fox commerce within the duchy. The overland exception is a loophole, not a right.

Sable rolls the pelts into a bundle and ties it. She tells the factor that if the Duke wishes to extend his monopoly to the mountain roads, he must issue a new charter. Until then, her pelts stay with her, her sales to the clans continue, and if the Consortium's agents attempt to seize her goods on the overland trails, her trappers will treat it as banditry and respond accordingly.

The factor reaches for the bundle. Sable pulls it off the counter and steps back. She walks out of the trading post with the pelts under her arm. The factor does not follow.

> A = factor of the Merchant Consortium; B = Sable (fur merchant); O = the twelve white-fox pelts (their seizure under the trade monopoly); resolution shape (c) -- breaking point; Sable physically removes the pelts and exits, rejecting the Consortium's authority over overland trade. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

# Confrontation x Fantasy -- Batch 1 (Tier A)

**C-Fa1-1.** The guildmaster of the Weavers' Hall enters the dye-ward at the hour the evening bell sounds and finds Torvun still working. The vats are bubbling with Southland indigo -- twelve bolts of cloth steeping in a dye that the guild charter reserves for masters who have passed the colour trial. Torvun is a journeyman. He has not passed the colour trial. He is using a technique he learned outside the guild, from a Southland dyer who owes no allegiance to the Hall, and the cloth he is producing is finer than anything the masters submitted at the last market fair.

The guildmaster tells Torvun to drain the vats and surrender the dyed cloth. Under the charter, Section the Sixth, any journeyman who uses a restricted process without certification forfeits the product to the guild and faces suspension of workshop privileges. The cloth belongs to the Hall.

Torvun does not drain the vats. He tells the guildmaster that the charter restricts guild-taught techniques. The Southland method is not a guild-taught technique -- it does not appear in the guild's register of controlled processes, because the guild has never possessed it. He learned it outside guild walls, from a non-member, using materials he purchased with his own coin. The charter cannot restrict what it does not contain.

The guildmaster says the spirit of the charter is clear: restricted dyes are restricted regardless of the method used to apply them. Indigo is a master-grade material.

Torvun says the charter names twelve restricted processes by title. The Southland method is not among them. If the guildmaster wishes to add it, the process requires a vote of the full guild council -- seven masters, convened with three days' notice. Until that vote occurs, Torvun is within his rights.

The guildmaster considers this. Seizing the cloth without a charter basis would give Torvun grounds to petition the city's trade magistrate, and the magistrate has ruled against the guild twice in the past year.

She tells Torvun the vats may continue running. She will convene the guild council in three days. If the council votes to classify the Southland method as a restricted process, Torvun will surrender any cloth produced using it from this night forward. Torvun agrees, and resumes his work.

> A = guildmaster of the Weavers' Hall; B = Torvun (journeyman weaver); O = the indigo-dyed cloth and Torvun's use of the Southland dyeing method; resolution shape (b) -- both defer to the guild council, to be convened in three days, for a binding vote on whether the Southland method is a restricted process. All roles, all states.

**C-Fa1-2.** "Return the sword, Edric."

"It was given to me."

"It was lent to you for the ranging. The ranging is over. You rode back through the gate this morning. The sword belongs to the armoury of the Grey Keep, and I am its keeper."

"The quartermaster told me, in the courtyard, before the company departed, that any weapon carried on the ranging and bloodied in defence of the realm becomes the bearer's by right of service. I killed two wargs with this blade. It is mine."

"Wulfric said that?"

"He did. Ask Maren and Dahl -- they were standing beside me."

The armoury keeper rubs the bridge of her nose. Wulfric is the quartermaster, and Wulfric has the authority to grant weapons from the stores under the Keep's charter of arms. But the sword Edric carries is not from the stores. It is the Warden's own blade -- taken from the Warden's rack by mistake during the pre-dawn muster, when the armoury was dark and the company was rushing.

"Edric, the right-of-service custom applies to weapons issued from the common armoury. This blade was never issued. It is the Warden's personal weapon. Wulfric did not have the authority to grant it because it was never his to grant."

"I didn't know that when I carried it. I bled for it. The custom doesn't say anything about provenance."

"The custom is subordinate to the charter. The Warden's personal arms are listed in the charter's third schedule. They cannot be granted, gifted, or transferred without the Warden's own mark."

"Then let the Warden decide. Bring him the blade, tell him what I did with it, and let him say whether I keep it or give it back."

The armoury keeper nods. She sends a runner to the Warden's chambers. Until the Warden rules, the sword remains in the armoury under seal -- neither party to touch it.

> A = armoury keeper of the Grey Keep; B = Edric (ranger); O = the Warden's sword (its return to the armoury vs. Edric's claim by right of service); resolution shape (b) -- both defer to the Warden for a personal ruling, with the sword held under seal in the armoury pending his decision. All roles, all states.

**C-Fa1-3.** I am the Temple Warden of the Shrine of Still Waters, and at dawn this morning the Archpriestess Yseult walked into the inner sanctum and began dismantling the silver censers from the altar rail. She had three acolytes with her. They were wrapping the censers in cloth and placing them in a chest marked with the seal of the High Temple in Aurenmere.

I stepped in front of the chest and told her the censers could not leave the shrine. The Act of Consecration -- performed forty years ago when the shrine was founded -- names every liturgical object on the altar rail as bound to the shrine in perpetuity. The censers are not the property of the priesthood. They belong to the shrine itself, held in trust for the congregation. Removing them requires a vote of the shrine council, which is composed of seven lay elders and two clergy. No such vote has been taken.

Yseult told me the High Temple has supreme authority over all liturgical objects in its network of shrines, and that the Act of Consecration is a local custom, not binding on the High Temple's administration. She produced a writ, sealed with the High Temple's seal, authorising the transfer of the censers to Aurenmere for restoration. She said the matter was not subject to local governance.

I told her the Act of Consecration was ratified by the Crown's Office of Sacred Properties, which makes it civil law, not merely temple custom. The Crown's ratification gives the shrine council veto power over any removal of consecrated objects. The High Temple's writ does not override civil ratification.

She paused. She asked whether I was prepared to have the shrine placed under interdict -- cut off from the High Temple's liturgical calendar, its clergy unsalaried, its festivals unrecognised -- over a set of silver censers.

I told her I was prepared to convene the shrine council and let them decide. If the council votes to release the censers, they go to Aurenmere with my blessing. If the council votes to retain them, she will need to petition the Crown's Office to overrule the Act. I will not release them on the authority of a writ alone.

She ordered the acolytes to put the censers back. She said she would attend the council meeting herself.

> A = Archpriestess Yseult; B = I (narrator / Temple Warden of the Shrine of Still Waters); O = the silver censers (their removal to the High Temple in Aurenmere); resolution shape (a) -- Temple Warden prevails; Yseult orders the censers returned to the altar rail and agrees to present her case to the shrine council. All roles, all states.

**C-Fa1-4.** The clan-mother of the Reedfolk stands knee-deep in the shallows where the river bends, and the dam is already half-built. The Stonewrights' foreman -- a broad man named Gael -- is directing a crew of eight who are sinking cut stone into the riverbed, narrowing the channel that feeds the Reedfolk's rice paddies downstream. The Stonewrights hold a royal charter to build a millrace for the new grain mill on the hill above. The Reedfolk hold a water-right, granted by the same crown three generations ago, guaranteeing unimpeded flow to their paddies from the spring thaw through the autumn harvest.

The clan-mother wades to where Gael is standing on a flat rock and tells him to stop the work. The dam is already diverting enough water that her downstream gauging stones -- carved markers that have measured the flow for sixty years -- show the level dropping below the threshold written into the water-right. If the dam is completed, the paddies will fail. She tells him the Stonewrights must remove what they have placed and find another site for the millrace.

Gael refuses. He shows her the royal charter, embossed with the crown's seal. The charter authorises the mill and the dam at this precise location, surveyed and approved by the crown's engineer. He says he is not empowered to halt construction -- the charter binds him to complete the work by midsummer or forfeit the contract bond.

The clan-mother says the water-right predates the charter by three generations and was never revoked. Two valid crown instruments now require incompatible things. She will not permit the dam to continue while her people's harvest is at risk.

Gael says he will not tear down what he has built on the authority of a claim he cannot verify on the spot. He counter-demands: if the clan-mother wants the work stopped, she must produce the original water-right and present it alongside his charter to the crown's regional justiciar, whose seat is a day's ride east. Both instruments are crown-issued; only a crown officer can adjudicate the conflict.

The clan-mother agrees, on one condition: the Stonewrights halt construction until the justiciar rules. Gael says he cannot stop entirely -- idling the crew costs him the bond. He proposes that work continue on the mill structure above the waterline, but no further stone enters the riverbed until the ruling. The clan-mother accepts. She dispatches a rider to the justiciar before the sun moves another handspan.

> A = clan-mother of the Reedfolk; B = Gael (Stonewrights' foreman); O = the dam and its diversion of the river (continuation vs. removal of the construction in the riverbed); resolution shape (b) -- both defer to the crown's regional justiciar, with riverbed work suspended pending the ruling while above-waterline construction continues. All roles, all states.

**C-Fa1-5.** Magister Thane is thinking about fire when the knock comes. He has spent the morning studying a flame-binding inscription recovered from the ruins beneath the collegium, and he believes -- not hopes, believes, with the weight of six months' translation behind him -- that the inscription describes a technique for storing thermal energy in crystalline lattice, a method lost for four centuries. The knock belongs to Warden Solène, who oversees the collegium's vault of restricted texts, and she is not here to discuss his research.

She tells him the inscription must be surrendered to the vault by nightfall. The collegium's governing statute -- the Founders' Accord, Article Nine -- classifies any recovered artefact containing operative magical notation as a restricted object. Restricted objects are held in the vault under dual-key seal, accessible only by joint authorisation of the Warden and the Rector. Thane has had the inscription in his personal study for six months without filing a classification request. She has filed one on his behalf. The classification is confirmed. The inscription is restricted.

Thane tells her he will not surrender it. Article Nine restricts operative notation -- notation that can be activated to produce magical effect. The inscription is inert. He has tested it. The lattice technique it describes requires a resonance medium that no longer exists; the crystal species went extinct with the volcanic eruptions that destroyed the original collegium. The notation is historical, not operative. It belongs in the archive, not the vault.

Solène says the classification does not depend on current operability. Article Nine's language covers notation that describes operative technique, regardless of whether the materials for activation are presently available. She quotes the relevant passage from memory. The statute does not contain an inertness exception.

Thane quotes a different passage -- the Founders' commentary appended to the Accord, which states that the vault's purpose is to prevent accidental activation, not to impede scholarship. Locking away an inert inscription serves neither purpose.

Solène tells him the commentary is interpretive, not binding. The statute is binding. She tells him the inscription goes to the vault tonight, or she reports the violation to the Rector and recommends suspension of his research privileges.

Thane removes the inscription from his desk, rolls it carefully, and hands it to Solène. He asks her to note in the vault log that he surrenders the object under protest and intends to petition the Rector for a supervised-access exemption under Article Twelve.

She accepts the inscription and records his protest.

> A = Warden Solène (vault warden of the collegium); B = Magister Thane (scholar); O = the flame-binding inscription (its surrender to the restricted-texts vault); resolution shape (a) -- Solène prevails; Thane surrenders the inscription under protest after Solène threatens suspension of his research privileges, though he signals intent to petition for supervised access. All roles, all states.

# Confrontation × Fantasy — Batch 6 (Tier C)

**C-Fa6-1**

The Keeper of the Western Reach arrives at the Conclave hall before anyone else and is standing at the window when the Lord Surveyor enters. She has been waiting since the morning bell. In her hands is the survey scroll — the one the Lord Surveyor's deputies prepared last month and sealed with the Conclave's wax — and she sets it on the table between them without ceremony.

"This boundary," she says, placing her finger on the line drawn through the Ashfen marshes, "was drawn three miles east of where the Compact of the Four Reaches places it. The fenland families have grazed the western reed-beds for four generations. Your deputies have attributed those beds to the Crown's common reserve. I am demanding the line be redrawn before the survey is entered in the Register."

The Lord Surveyor examines the scroll. He does not dispute the measurement — he disputes the Compact. He says the Compact of the Four Reaches is an instrument of the old Assembly, dissolved twenty years past, and that the Crown's Survey Charter supersedes any Assembly compact as a matter of settled law. The reed-beds fall inside the common reserve. The survey is accurate.

She tells him the Compact was not dissolved — it was suspended pending review, a distinction his legal clerks should know. She has a copy of the Assembly's final sitting record in her saddlebag. She came prepared.

He says the distinction is one for the Crown's own Judicars to settle, not for a Keeper of the Western Reach who holds a regional warrant, not a Conclave seat.

She agrees. She tells him that is precisely why she is requesting that the survey's entry into the Register be held in abeyance pending a formal ruling from the Judicars' bench, which she will petition today. Either the Lord Surveyor agrees to the hold, or she will file for an emergency stay through the Conclave's own dispute mechanism — the Protocol of Contested Instruments, Article Seven — which would impose the hold automatically and place both parties under inquiry.

He looks at her for a long moment. He rolls the survey scroll, sets it aside, and tells his clerk to mark the entry deferred pending external determination. She thanks him and takes her leave to file the petition.

**C-Fa6-2**

"Give me the naming-token, Isaveth. Give it to me now."

"I won't."

"It belongs to the order. You have no right to hold it outside the Vault."

"And you have no right to ask for it without the Archivist's seal on your request. Show me the seal."

"The Archivist is three days' ride from here. You know that."

"Then send a fast rider and wait three days. The naming-token does not leave this room without the seal. That is the Vault's founding rule, not mine."

"A child is dying, Isaveth. The fever-curse is on her and the only binding that breaks it requires her true name, which is locked in this token. You would let a child die for a procedural rule?"

"I would let the rule hold because the rule exists precisely for moments when someone has a compelling reason to bypass it. Every person who has ever asked me to break the Vault's founding rule has had a compelling reason. That is what compelling reasons are for."

A silence. Then: "If she dies tonight, her death is on you."

"If she dies tonight, her death is on the fever-curse. Send for the Archivist. Or send for a healer who does not need the true name. But I will not open this case without the seal."

The door of the Vault room closes. The naming-token remains in its case.

> A = unnamed petitioner (seeking the naming-token for a healing binding); B = Isaveth (Vault-keeper); O = the naming-token (access to it outside the Archivist's authorisation); resolution shape (a) — Isaveth prevails; petitioner leaves without the token. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**C-Fa6-3**

I am the third-ranked herbalist of the Thornwood Collegium, and this morning I carried a sealed accusation to the Grand Herbalist's receiving room and laid it on the reading stand before her. The accusation names Brother Orvyn of our own order. It states, with witnesses attached, that Brother Orvyn has been selling compound extracts — preparations that require Collegium certification — through a market stall in the lower city, under a name that is not his own and a seal that is not ours.

I told the Grand Herbalist that Brother Orvyn must be suspended from practice pending a formal inquiry, his stall shuttered, and his preparations impounded before another market day passes.

She told me she had seen the accusation — a copy had reached her the day before, she said, through a route she declined to name. She told me Brother Orvyn had been with the Collegium for thirty years. She told me the witnesses named in my accusation were journeymen herbalists who had reason to resent a senior brother, and that she would not destroy a man's standing on the word of rivals without first hearing his account directly.

I told her that was precisely the form an inquiry should take — but an inquiry with Brother Orvyn still at his stall, still selling uncertified compounds to people who believe they carry our seal, put those people at risk in the days before any hearing could be arranged. I asked again for the suspension.

She told me the suspension was a matter for the full Collegium council, not for her acting alone, and she would call the council to sit within two days.

I accepted that. I also told her that if a single verified complaint of harm reached me from the lower city before the council sat, I would take the accusation directly to the city magistrates rather than wait for an internal hearing. She understood. We parted on that agreement.

> A = I (narrator / third-ranked herbalist); B = Grand Herbalist; O = Brother Orvyn's suspension from practice pending inquiry; resolution shape (b) — both defer to the full Collegium council, to sit within two days, with narrator's contingency (magistrate referral on harm) held in reserve. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**C-Fa6-4**

The war-leader of the Ashen Tide rides into the border village at midday, and she does not dismount. She looks down at Councillor Ferwick of the Lowland Settlement Authority from the height of her horse, and she says what she has come to say without preamble.

"The road through the Ashen Passage. Your settlers are widening it. Every cartlength you add runs across the Tide's traditional grazing routes. My riders have turned back three of your work-crews already. I am telling you now: send a fourth crew, and I will not turn them back. I will burn the equipment and the supplies, and your council will hear about it through the smoke."

Councillor Ferwick is not a small man and he does not step back. He says the road improvement is authorised under the Settlement Charter, granted by the Eastern Compact's arbiters eighteen months ago. The Ashen Tide had a representative at those hearings. The representative agreed to the route as laid.

The war-leader says her predecessor agreed to the route as laid — a route that was, at the time, half the width now being cut. The work-crews are not following the agreed plan. They are following a different plan, one she has not been shown.

Ferwick has not seen the revised survey himself. He tells her that if the crews are deviating from the agreed route, that is a legitimate grievance and one he will investigate.

She says she does not need an investigation. She needs the work stopped until she has seen the revised plan and has had her own riders measure it against the agreed route. She will wait one week. If the work does not stop within the hour, the threat she has already made stands.

Ferwick sends a messenger to the work-crew foreman before the war-leader has turned her horse. The work stops. The revised plan is produced the following morning.

> A = war-leader of the Ashen Tide; B = Councillor Ferwick; O = continuation of the road-widening work through the Ashen Passage; resolution shape (a) — war-leader prevails; Ferwick halts the work under counter-threat of destruction. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**C-Fa6-5**

The Bone Cartographers' Guild has kept the northern sea-charts under lock since the founding of the chapter house. They do not lend them. They do not copy them. They sell access by the session, and every navigator who has sat in their reading room has signed the Access Covenant before touching a chart.

Mirela Strand has signed the covenant. She is a navigator. She is also, as of this morning, also the appointed envoy of the Harbour Compact, and she is standing in the guildmaster's office with a writ of compelled disclosure sealed by the Compact's admiralty board.

She sets the writ on the guildmaster's desk and tells him she requires a true copy of the Skarveld passage charts — all four sheets — to be made and delivered to the Compact's fleet-master before the evening tide, as the writ authorises.

The guildmaster reads the writ. He then tells her that the Compact's admiralty board does not have jurisdiction over the Guild's proprietary instruments. The charts are not civic property. The Harbour Compact governs the harbour — not the Guild's archive. He will not be producing copies.

She tells him the writ was issued under the Emergency Maritime Provisions, Section Three, which grants the Compact temporary requisition authority over any navigational instrument whose withholding poses risk to fleet safety. Two ships are missing in the Skarveld passage. The charter language covers the situation precisely, and the Compact's legal recorder has already filed the writ with the city's adjudicating magistrate.

The guildmaster tells her he intends to challenge the Section Three invocation before that magistrate — today, if possible — and that no copies will be produced until the magistrate has ruled.

She agrees. She tells him the copies must be produced within two hours of a ruling in the Compact's favour, as the writ specifies. He says he understands the timeline. Both parties send their representatives to the magistrate's chambers within the hour.

> A = Mirela Strand (Harbour Compact envoy); B = guildmaster of the Bone Cartographers' Guild; O = a true copy of the Skarveld passage charts; resolution shape (b) — both defer to the city's adjudicating magistrate under the Emergency Maritime Provisions, writ filed and ruling pending. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

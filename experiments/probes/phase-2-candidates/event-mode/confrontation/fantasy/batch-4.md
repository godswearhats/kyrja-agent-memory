# Confrontation × Fantasy — Batch 4 (Tier A)

## C-Fa4-1

The harbor-reeve of Saltmere stands on the customs quay with the impound chain in his hands. The chain is threaded through the mooring ring of a flat-bottomed river barge, the *Adaine*, loaded to the waterline with casks of refined salt from the eastern marshes. The barge belongs to the Free Salt Merchants of the Estuary Compact, and its captain, a sharp-faced woman named Torild, is standing three paces away with the Compact's trade seal held out in front of her as though it were a shield.

She tells the harbor-reeve to remove the chain. The Compact's charter with the Crown exempts its vessels from harbor impound for unpaid duties, because the Compact pays its duties annually in a lump settlement negotiated directly with the Crown's exchequer. The barge owes nothing to Saltmere.

The harbor-reeve tells her the exemption was suspended. The Crown's exchequer dispatched a notice to all harbor offices forty days ago informing them that the Compact had failed to remit its annual settlement by the due date. Until the settlement is paid, Compact vessels are to be treated as ordinary traders, subject to local duties and impound for non-payment. He produces the notice — stamped with the exchequer's seal — and holds it beside her trade seal so she can read both.

She reads it. She tells him the Compact did remit the settlement. It was sent by bonded courier and she has the courier's receipt in her cabin. The exchequer's notice is based on a clerical error. She demands he release the barge on the strength of the courier receipt.

He tells her he cannot. A harbor-reeve may release an impound only on the authority that ordered it. The exchequer ordered it. Only the exchequer can reverse it. He is willing to hold her barge at quay rather than haul it to the impound yard, and he will send her courier receipt to the exchequer by fast rider with a request for reversal. But the chain stays on until the exchequer responds.

She tells him she will hold him personally liable under the Compact's charter for losses from the delay. He tells her to file the claim with the exchequer alongside the reversal request. She agrees. He dispatches the rider before noon.

> A = Torild (captain / Free Salt Merchants of the Estuary Compact); B = harbor-reeve of Saltmere; O = the impounded barge *Adaine* and its cargo (release from the mooring chain); resolution shape (b) — deferred to the Crown's exchequer for reversal of the impound order; barge held at quay pending response. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

## C-Fa4-2

"Give me the phylactery, Sereth."

"It is not yours to claim."

The Keeper of the Ossuary stands in the doorway of the reliquary vault, one hand resting on the iron lock-plate. Behind her, on the third shelf of the bone-cabinet, sits a clay vessel the size of a child's fist, sealed with beeswax and inscribed with the name of the mage Aldren Voss, dead now nine years. The phylactery contains the last binding — a sliver of his spirit anchored to the vessel at the moment of death, as was the custom of the Vossite mages, who believed the binding preserved the mage's accumulated knowledge for consultation by future generations.

The woman at the threshold is Margaud, Aldren's surviving apprentice and the last practitioner of his school. She tells the Keeper that the Ossuary's charter grants the inheriting practitioner the right to reclaim a phylactery when the school's continuity is at risk. She is the last practitioner. The school's continuity is at risk. The charter is clear.

The Keeper tells her the charter also requires the Ossuary's council to verify the claim of sole inheritance before any phylactery is released. She has received no petition from Margaud. She has received no testimony from the school's own records confirming that no other practitioners survive. Until she has both, the phylactery remains under the Ossuary's seal.

Margaud draws a folded parchment from her belt. It is a petition, written in the proper form, with the testimony of three witnesses — two from the Mages' Quarter registry, one from the school's own archive-keeper — attesting that she is the sole surviving practitioner. She has brought it with her. She sets it on the lock-plate under the Keeper's hand.

The Keeper reads it. She calls for the council's duty archivist, who examines the seals. The seals are genuine. The archivist confirms the witnesses are registered. The Keeper removes her hand from the lock-plate, opens the vault, and gives Margaud the phylactery.

> A = Margaud (sole surviving apprentice of the Vossite school); B = Sereth (Keeper of the Ossuary); O = the phylactery of Aldren Voss; resolution shape (a) — Margaud prevails; petition and witness testimony satisfy the Keeper's procedural requirements, phylactery released. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

## C-Fa4-3

I am the bridge-warden of the Narrows, and this morning I refused passage to the Duke of Ashfeld's entire supply train — forty mules, nine wagons, and a retinue of armed drovers — because the Duke's quartermaster could not produce the bridge-toll seal that every party crossing the Narrows is required to carry, regardless of title or station.

The quartermaster told me the Duke does not pay bridge toll. He said the Duke holds a perpetual exemption granted by the King's grandfather in recognition of service during the Siege of Thornwall. He told me to open the gate.

I told him I had no record of any perpetual exemption. The bridge charter lists seventeen active exemptions, renewed annually by the King's chancery. The Duke of Ashfeld is not among them. If an exemption exists, the Duke's household should have the chancery's renewal document.

The quartermaster said the exemption predates the renewal system. It was granted before the charter was written. It does not need renewal because it was granted in perpetuity.

I told him I enforce the charter as written. The charter makes no provision for pre-charter exemptions. If the Duke believes his exemption survives the charter, his recourse is to petition the chancery for a declaratory ruling. Until the ruling is issued, his train pays the toll or it does not cross.

The quartermaster said the Duke would hear of this and the Duke would not be pleased.

I told him the Duke was welcome to come to the bridge himself. The toll was twelve silver marks for a train of that size.

The quartermaster paid the toll. He demanded a receipt, which I wrote and sealed. He told me the petition would be filed before the week was out. I told him the chancery would know where to find me.

> A = I (narrator / bridge-warden of the Narrows); B = the Duke of Ashfeld's quartermaster; O = the bridge toll for the supply train (its payment vs. claimed perpetual exemption); resolution shape (a) — bridge-warden prevails; quartermaster pays the toll under protest, intending to petition the chancery. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

## C-Fa4-4

The firelight makes the sigils on the stone floor shift and crawl, though Warden Yvara knows this is only her tired eyes. She has been in the sealing chamber since the third bell, reviewing the containment glyphs on the vault beneath the Academy's north tower. The vault holds seven artefacts of proscribed origin. One of them — the Rime Lens, a disc of black glass that freezes whatever it magnifies — was placed in containment by the previous Warden thirty years ago under the Academy's Hazard Protocols.

Grandmaster Solen enters the chamber. He tells her he requires the Rime Lens released to his personal study for an investigation into the nature of cold-binding, a subject the Academy's senior scholars have debated for a decade without resolution. The Lens, he says, is the only surviving instrument capable of demonstrating the phenomenon under controlled conditions. He has prepared a containment vessel rated for cold-class artefacts, and he sets it on the table beside her.

She tells him the Hazard Protocols require a formal release petition signed by two Grandmasters, reviewed by the Warden, and recorded in the vault ledger before any proscribed artefact leaves containment. He is one Grandmaster. He needs a second signature.

He tells her the second Grandmaster, Aldis, signed the petition yesterday. He places the document before her. Both signatures are present. The petition is in proper form.

She reads the petition and finds the containment plan inadequate. The vessel he has brought is rated for cold-class artefacts of the third tier. The Rime Lens was classified at the fourth tier by the previous Warden, and fourth-tier artefacts require a warded chamber, not a portable vessel. The Hazard Protocols are explicit. She denies the release and returns the petition with a written notation specifying what containment would satisfy the requirement.

Solen tells her the fourth-tier classification was an error by the previous Warden and that a reclassification to the third tier is warranted. She tells him a reclassification requires a separate petition, also co-signed by two Grandmasters, and reviewed at a scheduled convocation of the Academy's safety council. She will not reclassify and release on the same day.

He takes back the petition. He tells her he will file for reclassification and return when the council has ruled.

> A = Grandmaster Solen; B = Warden Yvara; O = the Rime Lens (its release from containment); resolution shape (a) — Yvara prevails; Solen's release denied on containment-tier grounds, directed to pursue reclassification through the safety council. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

## C-Fa4-5

BY ORDER OF THE HIGH STEWARD OF THE MERCHANT COURT OF VELAN

Recorded this day in the presence of the Court Notary.

The matter: Guildmaster Olin of the Coopers' Guild has appeared before the Court to demand that the Free Traders' Association of Velan cease the import and sale of flat-packed barrel staves manufactured in the Kingswood settlements beyond the river. The Coopers' Guild charter, granted by the city forty-one years ago, gives the guild exclusive right to the manufacture and assembly of barrels, casks, and hooped vessels within the city walls and the outer market district. Guildmaster Olin asserts that flat-packed staves are barrel components and therefore fall within the guild's monopoly. He demands that the Association's existing stock of staves be surrendered to the guild and that further imports cease.

Elara Dunmore, speaking for the Free Traders' Association, states that staves are timber, not barrels. The guild's charter covers the manufacture of finished vessels. An unassembled stave is a plank of wood. If the Coopers' Guild claims jurisdiction over planks, it must also claim jurisdiction over trees. She states that the Association will not surrender its stock and will continue to import staves.

Guildmaster Olin responds that the staves are bevelled, shaped, and sized to specification — they require only hooping to become a barrel. They are not raw timber. They are pre-manufactured barrel components sold to unlicensed assemblers who undercut the guild's prices.

Elara Dunmore responds that the charter does not mention components, only finished vessels. If the guild wished to control components, it should have negotiated a broader charter. It did not.

The High Steward, having heard both parties, rules as follows: the question of whether pre-manufactured components fall within the Coopers' Guild charter is a matter of charter interpretation that exceeds this Court's original jurisdiction. The matter is referred to the City Council's Charter Committee for an interpretive ruling. Pending that ruling, the Association may continue to import and hold staves but may not sell them within the outer market district. The guild's demand for surrender of existing stock is denied. Both parties are bound by the Charter Committee's decision.

So recorded.

> A = Guildmaster Olin (Coopers' Guild); B = Elara Dunmore (Free Traders' Association); O = the imported flat-packed barrel staves (their surrender and the cessation of stave imports); resolution shape (b) — deferred to the City Council's Charter Committee; interim order allows holding but not selling staves in the market district. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

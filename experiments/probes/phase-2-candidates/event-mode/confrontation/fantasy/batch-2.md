# Confrontation x Fantasy -- Batch 2 (Tier B)

**C-Fa2-1.** The guildmaster of the Dyers' Quarter stands in the great hall of the city's trade council, the charter of guild privileges unrolled on the stone table before her. She has come for one thing: the Council Warden must revoke the license granted last month to the foreign merchant Tessaro, who has been selling pre-dyed cloth in the market square without submitting his goods to the guild's quality seal. The charter is explicit -- Article Twelve grants the Dyers' Guild exclusive authority over the sale of dyed textiles within the city walls. No exception exists for foreign traders.

The Council Warden hears her. He does not dispute the charter. But he tells her he will not revoke the license. Tessaro's cloth comes from the southern provinces by river, and the river-trade compact -- negotiated between the city and the provincial governors two seasons past -- guarantees market access to any merchant carrying a provincial seal. Tessaro carries one. Revoking his license would breach the compact, and the compact is the only thing keeping grain barges flowing north through the drought.

She says the river-trade compact does not override the guild charter. The charter predates the compact by ninety years and has never been amended. If the Warden wishes to create an exception, he must put it before the full trade council for a vote, and she will oppose it.

He says a full council vote will take three weeks, and Tessaro's goods will spoil on the dock in the meantime.

She says that is not her concern. She demands he suspend the license pending the vote.

He refuses. He counter-demands that she grant Tessaro a temporary quality seal, allowing him to sell under guild supervision until the council rules.

She says the guild will not seal goods it did not dye. The seal certifies origin, not merely quality. Affixing it to foreign cloth would be fraud under the guild's own statutes.

They are at an impasse. The Warden proposes that they bring the matter before the Lord Mayor, whose authority supersedes both the charter and the compact. She agrees, on the condition that Tessaro's stall is shuttered until the Lord Mayor renders judgment. The Warden accepts. He sends a page to the Lord Mayor's chambers with the petition before she has left the hall.

> A = guildmaster of the Dyers' Quarter; B = Council Warden; O = Tessaro's trading license (its revocation or continuation); resolution shape (b) -- both defer to the Lord Mayor, Tessaro's stall shuttered pending judgment. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**C-Fa2-2.** "You will return the grimoire to the Locked Archive before the bell tower sounds the evening hour."

"I will not."

The Archivist steps closer, his staff striking the flagstones. "The book was removed without the Keeper's seal. No scholar may take a restricted text beyond the Archive threshold without the seal -- you know the law, Iselda. You wrote half of it."

"I wrote the law to protect the texts from damage and theft. This is neither. The grimoire is on my desk, three floors above, in a warded room that I maintain at my own expense. It is safer there than in the Archive, where the damp has been eating the binding for a decade."

"Safer is not authorized. The Keeper's seal exists precisely to prevent individual judgment from overriding collective custody. If every senior enchanter decided her own chambers were safer than the Archive, we would have no Archive at all."

"Then reform the Archive. Fix the damp. I have been petitioning the Council of Enchanters for funding since the frost season. Nothing. The grimoire's spine is splitting. Another winter in that cellar and the binding spells unravel -- and with them, the containment seals on the last four pages."

"That is a petition for the Council, not a justification for theft."

"It is not theft. It is preservation."

"Iselda. Return the grimoire, or I invoke the Archivist's right of seizure and send two wardens to your chambers within the hour."

She is silent for a moment. Then: "Send them. I will not open the wards. And when the Council asks why I refused, I will present the grimoire's condition report beside the Archive's maintenance log and let them judge whose negligence endangered the text."

The Archivist sends the wardens. Iselda's wards hold. The matter passes to the Council of Enchanters for adjudication, the grimoire remaining in Iselda's chambers under a double seal -- hers and the Archivist's -- until the Council convenes.

> A = the Archivist; B = Iselda (senior enchanter); O = a restricted grimoire (its return to the Locked Archive); resolution shape (b) -- both defer to the Council of Enchanters; the grimoire remains under a double seal pending adjudication. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**C-Fa2-3.** I am the third daughter of the Thornwall keep, and I am standing in my father's council chamber telling the emissary of the Iron Reach that my sister will not be going south.

The emissary -- a tall man in a fur-lined cloak who introduced himself as Lord Aldric's voice -- has come with the betrothal contract signed by my father six months ago, before the fever took him. The contract pledges my eldest sister Maren to Lord Aldric's son in exchange for a defensive alliance and three hundred armed riders. It bears my father's seal, his signature, and the witness mark of our house steward.

I tell the emissary the contract is void. My father signed it while Maren was abroad, without her knowledge or consent. Under the old law of our holding -- the law my grandmother wrote into the keep's charter when she rebuilt it after the siege -- no daughter of Thornwall may be pledged in marriage without speaking her own assent before the hearthstone. Maren has not spoken. The hearthstone has not been lit.

The emissary says the old law is a local custom, not a binding compact between houses. The betrothal was executed under the common law of alliance, which requires only the signing lord's seal. He says Lord Aldric has already mustered the riders. He says the riders will not come without the bride.

I say the riders can stay in the south. Thornwall has survived without the Iron Reach's protection before and will do so again. The contract fails on the consent clause, and I will not compel my sister to speak words she does not wish to speak.

He places the contract on the table between us, weighted with a stone. "If the bride does not ride south by the solstice," he says, "Lord Aldric will consider the alliance void and the insult actionable. He holds three border forts that overlook your northern pastures."

I take the contract, fold it, and hand it back to him. "Tell Lord Aldric that Thornwall acknowledges no debt, no bride-price, and no insult. If he wishes to discuss the border forts, he may send a different envoy. This conversation is finished."

The emissary takes the contract and leaves. I bar the gate behind him.

> A = emissary of Lord Aldric (representing the Iron Reach); B = narrator (third daughter of Thornwall, acting as keeper); O = Maren's betrothal and departure south (fulfillment of the marriage contract); resolution shape (c) -- breaking point; the narrator returns the contract, rejects the alliance, and bars the gate. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**C-Fa2-4.** The temple is dark except for the altar flame when Brother Callum enters and finds the High Priestess kneeling there, the relic casket open before her. He sees what she has done. The Veil of Saint Edra -- the order's most sacred artifact, a cloth woven from blessed thread and sealed in the casket for forty years -- is draped across the altar stone. She has broken the wax seal. The casket's lock, which requires three keyholders to open, has been forced with a chisel that still lies on the floor.

He tells her the Veil must be returned to the casket and the casket resealed before the dawn procession. The order's Rule of Custody is unambiguous: the Veil may only be exposed during the Rite of Remembrance, which occurs once per decade. The next rite is four years away. What she has done will be visible to every novice who enters the temple at dawn.

She does not stand. She tells him the Veil is dying. The thread is disintegrating -- she can see it in the lamplight, the fibers thinning at the edges, the weave pulling apart. She broke the seal because she suspected the deterioration and needed to confirm it before requesting a conservator from the mother temple. If she had waited four years for the rite, the Veil would have been dust.

He says her suspicion, however justified, does not override the Rule of Custody. The Rule exists to prevent exactly this -- an individual judgment, however well-intentioned, circumventing the collective governance of the order's relics. She should have brought her concern to the Three Keyholders and requested an emergency inspection.

She says the Three Keyholders include Prior Dunstan, who has refused every conservation request she has made in the past five years on the grounds of expense. Dunstan would have denied the inspection, and the Veil would have continued to rot.

Callum is quiet for a time. Then he tells her he will not help her reseal the casket. He will report the breach to Prior Dunstan at first light, as the Rule requires. If she believes Dunstan is negligent, she may raise a formal accusation at the Chapter meeting, and he will support it -- but the breach stands as a breach.

She nods. She does not return the Veil to the casket. She covers it with a linen cloth, lifts the altar flame higher, and sits down to wait for dawn and the judgment that comes with it.

> A = Brother Callum; B = High Priestess; O = the Veil of Saint Edra (its return to the casket and resealing under the Rule of Custody); resolution shape (c) -- breaking point; Callum will report the breach, the Priestess will not return the Veil, both await the Chapter's judgment through irreconcilable positions. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**C-Fa2-5.** The harvest tribute sits in eleven wagons at the edge of the village square, grain sacked and counted, waiting for the baron's men to drive it north. The village elder, Seren, stands in front of the lead wagon. She is not moving.

The baron's reeve dismounts. He has a tally rod in his hand, notched with the expected count: forty-two measures of barley, eighteen of rye, six of oats. He tells Seren the wagons must leave before midday. The baron's granary is expecting the shipment, and the roads will be mud by nightfall if the rains come as the sky promises.

Seren tells him the tribute will not leave today. She holds up her own tally rod. The village's count is thirty-one measures of barley, twelve of rye, four of oats. The difference -- eleven measures of barley, six of rye, two of oats -- represents the seed grain for next season's planting. If the baron takes the full count, the village cannot sow. If the village cannot sow, there is no harvest next year, and the baron's tribute is zero.

The reeve says the tribute amount was set by the baron's assessor at midsummer, based on the projected yield. The village agreed to the assessment. The assessment is binding.

Seren says the village agreed to the assessment before the blight took the eastern fields. The yield fell short. She sent a rider to the baron's steward with a revised count three weeks ago and received no reply. The revised count is what sits in the wagons. The seed grain stays in the village granary.

The reeve says he has no authority to accept a reduced tribute. His orders are the tally rod. He reaches for the lead wagon's harness.

Seren puts her hand on the harness first. "You will take what is in the wagons at my count, or you will take nothing. I will not hand over the seed grain and watch this village starve next winter. If the baron disputes my count, let him send his assessor to inspect the blighted fields and recalculate. We will open every granary door."

The reeve looks at the wagons, at Seren, at the villagers gathered behind her. He takes the wagons at Seren's count. He tells her the baron's assessor will ride south within the week, and any shortfall confirmed by inspection will be added to next year's tribute. She agrees to the inspection. The seed grain stays.

> A = baron's reeve; B = Seren (village elder); O = the full harvest tribute (forty-two measures of barley and associated grain vs. the reduced count); resolution shape (a) -- Seren prevails; the reeve accepts the reduced count and defers the disputed shortfall to a future inspection. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

# Confrontation × Corporate — Batch 4 (Tier A)

## C-Co4-1

The insurance broker picks up on the second ring. Deborah Laine, chief underwriting officer at Halcyon Mutual, does not waste time on pleasantries.

"Your client submitted a renewal application for the directors-and-officers policy last week. I need it withdrawn."

"Withdrawn? The application is clean, Deborah."

"It is not clean. The application attests that no litigation is pending or anticipated against any named officer. My reinsurance team flagged an SEC subpoena issued to your client's CFO two weeks before the application date. The subpoena is a matter of public record. The attestation is false."

"The subpoena is a document request, not litigation. Our client's legal counsel reviewed the attestation language and concluded that a document request does not constitute pending litigation under the policy's definition."

"Then your client's legal counsel and I have a different reading of Section 4.1. The policy defines 'pending litigation' to include any formal governmental investigation or proceeding that could give rise to a claim. An SEC subpoena is a formal governmental proceeding. The attestation should have disclosed it."

"We stand by the application as filed."

"Then I'm declining the renewal. I am issuing a formal declination letter today citing material non-disclosure under Section 4.1. That declination will be reported to the industry disclosure database, which means every underwriter your client approaches next will see it. I'm giving you until five o'clock to withdraw the application voluntarily so the declination doesn't hit the database."

Silence on the line. Then: "I'll call the CFO."

The application was withdrawn at four-twelve.

> A = Deborah Laine (chief underwriting officer, Halcyon Mutual); B = insurance broker (representing the client); O = the directors-and-officers renewal application (its withdrawal due to alleged non-disclosure); resolution shape (a) — Laine prevails; broker withdraws the application before the formal declination is filed. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

## C-Co4-2

The warehouse operations manager, Reyes, is standing in the cold-chain loading bay at six in the morning when the quality assurance director arrives with a temperature-excursion report printed from the monitoring system. The report shows that Dock 3's refrigeration unit dropped below the minimum threshold for seventy-two minutes overnight. Fourteen pallets of pharmaceutical-grade reagents were staged in Dock 3 during that window. The reagents have a validated cold-chain tolerance of zero excursions above thirty minutes.

The QA director tells Reyes the fourteen pallets must be quarantined immediately and cannot ship on the seven-a.m. truck. The shipment is bound for a clinical-trial site. The reagents require an unbroken chain-of-custody temperature record. The record is now broken.

Reyes says the excursion was caused by a brief compressor reset and that the actual product temperature never left range — the dock sensors measure ambient air, not product core temperature. He has the data-logger readings from the pallets themselves, and every logger shows core temperature within specification for the entire overnight period. He tells her the shipment can go.

She tells him the company's validated cold-chain protocol does not distinguish between ambient and core temperature. The protocol specifies ambient monitoring as the controlling measurement. If the ambient record shows an excursion beyond thirty minutes, the product is non-conforming regardless of what the core loggers say. Changing the controlling measurement mid-shipment would require a formal protocol deviation, approved by the quality unit head, documented before release — not after.

Reyes picks up his radio to call the distribution VP. The QA director tells him she has already notified the quality unit head, who confirmed the quarantine by email at five-forty-five. The pallets are not shipping.

Reyes puts the radio down. He directs his team to move the fourteen pallets to the quarantine hold.

> A = QA director; B = Reyes (warehouse operations manager); O = the fourteen pallets of pharmaceutical reagents (their release for the seven-a.m. shipment); resolution shape (a) — QA director prevails; Reyes quarantines the pallets after learning the quality unit head has already confirmed the hold. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

## C-Co4-3

I run the grants office for a mid-size research university. This morning I told the principal investigator on the Hartwell Foundation grant that I am withholding submission of his Year 3 continuation proposal until he removes the co-investigator he added to the budget — Dr. Elise Fontaine, his former doctoral student, now at a partner institution — because adding her triggers a subrecipient relationship that requires a fully executed subaward agreement, an institutional review of her conflict-of-interest disclosures, and a facilities-and-administrative-rate negotiation with her university. None of those things have been started. The submission deadline is in nine days.

He told me Dr. Fontaine's participation is essential to the project's aims. He told me the work she would perform is already described in the original scope. He told me the Hartwell Foundation's program officer verbally approved the addition last month.

I told him verbal approval from a program officer does not satisfy federal Uniform Guidance requirements, which the Hartwell Foundation's terms and conditions incorporate by reference. A subaward without a signed agreement is a disallowed cost. A disallowed cost on a continuation proposal will trigger a compliance flag that jeopardizes the entire award, not just Year 3.

He asked me to submit the proposal as written and process the subaward in parallel.

I told him I cannot. My office's signature authority requires that all compliance documentation be in place before submission. That authority is delegated from the provost, not from the PI, and I will not put the university's institutional standing with the foundation at risk. I offered him two paths: remove Dr. Fontaine from the Year 3 budget and add her formally for Year 4, with the subaward executed in the interim, or request a deadline extension from the foundation in writing, which I would co-sign.

He chose the extension. We sent the request that afternoon.

> A = I (narrator / grants office director); B = principal investigator on the Hartwell Foundation grant; O = the Year 3 continuation proposal (its submission with the unapproved co-investigator); resolution shape (a) — grants office prevails; PI accepts the deadline extension rather than removing the co-investigator. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

## C-Co4-4

The franchisee's attorney has taped a printed notice to the inside of the restaurant's front window. The notice reads: UNDER PROTEST — THIS LOCATION IS REQUIRED BY CORPORATE DIRECTIVE TO DISCONTINUE ITS BREAKFAST SERVICE EFFECTIVE JUNE 1. The regional franchise director, Mallory Voss, is reading it through the glass at eight-fifteen in the morning. She enters the restaurant.

She finds the franchisee, Sam Okafor, behind the counter. She tells him to remove the notice. The franchise agreement, Section 22, grants the franchisor exclusive authority over brand communications in customer-facing spaces. The notice is an unauthorised brand communication. It must come down.

He tells her the notice is accurate. Corporate did mandate discontinuation of breakfast service at all locations in the metro cluster, effective June 1, as part of a menu rationalisation initiative. His location derives thirty-eight percent of its revenue from breakfast. The directive will make his lease unsustainable. If corporate wants to shut down his breakfast service, he has the right to tell his customers why.

She tells him he has no such right under Section 22. She tells him if the notice remains up past noon, she will issue a formal cure notice under Section 31, which starts a thirty-day clock. If the breach is not cured within thirty days, the franchisor may terminate the franchise agreement.

He tells her to issue whatever she wants to issue. He tells her he has already filed a complaint with the state franchise relations board alleging that the breakfast directive constitutes an unreasonable restriction on his ability to operate profitably, which is grounds for a hearing under the state's franchise practices act. The notice stays up until the board rules.

She issues the cure notice at noon. He does not remove the notice. The state franchise relations board schedules a hearing for the following month. The franchisor suspends termination proceedings pending the board's ruling.

> A = Mallory Voss (regional franchise director); B = Sam Okafor (franchisee); O = the protest notice in the restaurant's front window (its removal); resolution shape (b) — deferred to the state franchise relations board; termination proceedings suspended pending hearing. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

## C-Co4-5

FROM: Anneke Brouwer, VP of Regulatory Affairs
TO: Julian Cross, SVP of Manufacturing
CC: Plant Operations — Greystone Facility
DATE: [current]
SUBJECT: Immediate Line Shutdown — Greystone Facility, Line 4

Julian,

At 06:00 this morning I received the final batch-testing results for Lot 2247, produced on Line 4 at the Greystone facility during last week's production run. The endotoxin levels in three of the twelve samples exceed the specification limit established in our filed chemistry, manufacturing, and controls documentation. This is not a borderline result. The three samples are at 1.4, 1.7, and 2.1 times the limit. A result above 1.0 times the limit on even a single sample triggers an out-of-specification investigation under our quality agreement with the FDA.

I am directing you to shut down Line 4 immediately and hold all unreleased inventory from Lot 2247 and the two subsequent lots produced on the same line until the investigation is complete. This is not discretionary. Under our consent decree with the agency, failure to initiate a shutdown within 24 hours of an OOS finding is a reportable violation.

---

FROM: Julian Cross, SVP of Manufacturing
TO: Anneke Brouwer, VP of Regulatory Affairs
SUBJECT: RE: Immediate Line Shutdown — Greystone Facility, Line 4

Anneke,

Line 4 is mid-run on a committed order for Parnell Health Systems. The order is contractually due in eleven days. A shutdown now means we miss the delivery window and trigger the penalty clause — $340,000 per day, plus Parnell has a right to terminate the supply agreement after seven days of delay. That is a $12M annual contract.

I am requesting that we complete the current run — approximately 36 hours of production time — then shut the line for investigation. The current run uses a different raw-material lot from 2247, and the endotoxin issue may be lot-specific, not line-specific.

---

FROM: Anneke Brouwer, VP of Regulatory Affairs
TO: Julian Cross, SVP of Manufacturing; Diana Lessard, General Counsel
CC: Quality Assurance — Greystone Facility
SUBJECT: RE: RE: Immediate Line Shutdown — Greystone Facility, Line 4

Julian,

No. The consent decree does not distinguish between lot-specific and line-specific findings. The trigger is the OOS result, not its root cause. We do not have 36 hours.

I have copied Diana because the next step, if the line is not shut down by noon today, is a mandatory disclosure to the FDA under Paragraph 14 of the consent decree, identifying the OOS finding and the failure to initiate a timely shutdown. That disclosure will be filed over my signature whether or not you have completed the current run.

The line was shut down at 11:40 a.m.

> A = Anneke Brouwer (VP of Regulatory Affairs); B = Julian Cross (SVP of Manufacturing); O = the continued operation of Greystone Line 4 (its immediate shutdown pending investigation); resolution shape (a) — Brouwer prevails; Cross shuts down the line before the noon deadline after escalation to General Counsel. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

# Confrontation x Corporate -- Batch 2 (Tier B)

**C-Co2-1.** The channel-sales VP is already seated when the CFO enters the quarterly pipeline review. She has the commission ledger open on the conference table, printed, with three lines highlighted in yellow. She tells the VP that commissions totalling four hundred twelve thousand dollars were paid against deals that closed before the customer's purchase order was countersigned -- a violation of the company's revenue-recognition policy, which requires a countersigned PO before any commission accrues. She tells him the overpayments must be clawed back from the sales team's next cycle.

The VP says the deals were real. Every one of them converted to a signed PO within ten days. The commission policy, he argues, is pegged to "closed-won" status in the CRM, not to countersignature -- and his team marked the deals closed-won based on verbal confirmation from the buyers, which is standard practice in channel sales and has been for three years.

She says standard practice is not policy. The policy document -- which she slides across the table -- defines "closed-won" as requiring a countersigned PO. She does not care what the CRM workflow permits; the CRM is a tool, the policy is governance, and the two have been misaligned for three years because nobody audited it.

He says clawing back commissions retroactively will cost him his top three reps. They will walk. The pipeline will collapse. He demands instead that the policy be amended to match practice, effective retroactively, which would eliminate the discrepancy.

She tells him she cannot amend revenue-recognition policy retroactively -- the external auditors would flag it. The clawback stands. She will, however, amend the policy going forward to align with his CRM workflow, provided he submits a revised commission plan within thirty days for her review. He sits with the offer. Then he nods.

> A = CFO; B = channel-sales VP; O = $412K in commissions paid against unsigned POs (clawback vs. retroactive policy amendment); resolution shape (a) -- CFO prevails on the clawback; VP capitulates in exchange for a forward-looking policy amendment. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**C-Co2-2.** "You need to pull Grayson off the Nextera account."

"Why would I do that?"

"Because he told their procurement lead that we'd match the Kline bid. He doesn't have authority to make pricing commitments below the approved floor. That's in the engagement rules. He knows it."

"He was about to lose the renewal, Priya. Kline came in eleven percent under our list price. Grayson made a judgment call to keep the conversation alive."

"A judgment call that now obligates us to a margin we can't sustain. I've run the numbers. At the price he quoted, we lose money on every unit after the first eight hundred. The account does six thousand units a year."

"Then we renegotiate the quote."

"The quote is in their system. Procurement already circulated it to their CFO. Pulling it back now means we lied about our pricing. That's worse than losing the renewal."

"So what do you want?"

"I want Grayson off the account. I want a senior account director in there by Thursday to reset the conversation -- explain that the quote was issued outside of authorization, offer the approved pricing with an enhanced service tier to soften the gap, and take the loss if they walk."

"Then I send the engagement-rule violation to the ethics committee. Unauthorized pricing commitments are a category-two compliance breach. That's my obligation, Marcus. I don't have discretion on category two."

Marcus is quiet for a long time. "Thursday," he says. "I'll reassign him tonight."

> A = Priya (head of pricing / commercial compliance); B = Marcus (regional sales director); O = Grayson's continued management of the Nextera account (and the unauthorized pricing commitment); resolution shape (a) -- Priya prevails; Marcus agrees to reassign Grayson under threat of a category-two compliance referral. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**C-Co2-3.** I am the external auditor assigned to the year-end engagement for Lumen Analytics. I sit down with their controller at eight in the morning in a glass-walled conference room on the fourteenth floor. There is coffee on the table and a stack of lease agreements I have requested three times.

I tell her I need full documentation of the company's twelve operating leases -- original agreements, amendments, sublease arrangements, and the discount-rate assumptions used in the right-of-use asset calculations. The new lease accounting standard requires these disclosures. The financial statements cannot be signed off without them.

She says the documentation exists but cannot be provided in full. Four of the twelve leases contain confidentiality provisions negotiated by the landlords. Those provisions, she says, restrict disclosure of rental rates and escalation clauses to any third party, including auditors. She has raised the issue with outside counsel. Their recommendation is to provide redacted copies.

I tell her redacted copies are insufficient. The discount-rate assumptions are derived from the rental rates. Without the rates, I cannot verify the asset values. Without verified asset values, I cannot issue an unqualified opinion. I will have to qualify the financial statements, which will trigger the lending-covenant review clause in their credit facility.

She says she understands the consequences. She asks me to accept a representation letter from the CFO attesting to the accuracy of the calculations in lieu of the source documents.

I decline. Representation letters do not substitute for audit evidence under the standards I am bound by. I propose instead that we refer the matter to the firm's national office for a technical consultation on whether an alternative procedure -- such as an independent recalculation by a third party under NDA -- would satisfy the evidentiary requirement. She agrees. We draft the referral together before lunch, specifying a ten-day turnaround for the national office's ruling. The sign-off deadline is extended pending the consultation.

> A = I (external auditor); B = controller at Lumen Analytics; O = full unredacted lease documentation (rental rates, escalation clauses, discount-rate assumptions) for twelve operating leases; resolution shape (b) -- both defer to the audit firm's national office for a technical consultation on alternative evidence procedures, ten-day turnaround. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**C-Co2-4.** The product manager reads the message on her screen and feels the architecture of the launch shifting beneath her. The infrastructure team has pushed a change to the staging environment overnight -- a rate limiter on the API gateway, capped at two hundred requests per second per tenant. The launch, scheduled for Tuesday, depends on a burst capacity of eight hundred requests per second during the onboarding spike. The rate limiter will throttle every new customer for the first forty minutes.

She walks to the infrastructure lead's desk. She tells him the rate limiter must be rolled back before the launch window opens.

He says the rate limiter stays. A load test last week revealed that the database connection pool saturates at three hundred requests per second. Without the limiter, the onboarding spike will bring down not just the new product but the shared infrastructure serving four other production services. He had flagged the connection-pool issue in a capacity review two months ago. No one funded the fix.

She says she was not on that review and that the launch date was set by the CEO in a board meeting. Missing it is not an option she can authorize.

He says he is not asking for authorization to miss the launch. He is telling her the infrastructure cannot support the launch as designed. The rate limiter is a safety measure, not a policy preference. He will not remove it.

She proposes escalating to the CTO for a joint decision -- she will present the launch risk, he will present the infrastructure risk, and the CTO will decide whether to accept the database exposure or delay the launch. He agrees, on the condition that his capacity-review flag from two months ago is included in the briefing materials. She says it will be. They book the CTO's calendar for that afternoon.

> A = product manager; B = infrastructure lead; O = the API-gateway rate limiter (its rollback before Tuesday's launch); resolution shape (b) -- both defer to the CTO for a joint decision, meeting scheduled for that afternoon, with prior capacity-review documentation included. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

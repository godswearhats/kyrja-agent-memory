## D-Hi6-1

*[Present tense — Ottoman imperial context, early 16th century]*

The imperial secretary finds the discrepancy at the third column of the timar register. He is the one charged, by the express order of the divan, with certifying that all revenue assignments in the Anatolian province have been recorded faithfully before the provincial seal is applied. The divan's instruction to the provincial treasurer, Mehmed Bey, was unambiguous: no revenue from the eastern sipahi holdings is to be remitted to any party before the certification is complete. The instruction was delivered in writing and acknowledged in the treasurer's own hand. The secretary turns the register page. Forty-seven entries for the eastern holdings show payments already disbursed — not to the treasury, but to a holding account registered in a name the secretary does not recognise, three weeks before this certification session was convened. He lays the register down and calls for the courier who will carry his report to the divan before evening prayer.

## D-Hi6-2

"Do you deny writing it?"

The surgeon, Dr. Aldous Crane, stands at the far end of the ward and does not sit when invited. It is 1867, Leeds, and the hospital board's chair has the letter in his hand — two pages, Crane's signature unmistakable at the bottom.

"I wrote it."

"To the Lancet."

"Yes."

The chair sets the letter on the desk. It describes, in careful clinical language, three surgical outcomes from the charity ward over the past six months — outcomes that are, taken together, unflattering to the hospital's chief surgeon, Dr. Pemberton. Pemberton is not present. The board's standing rule, adopted at the start of the year and signed by every physician on staff, forbids any staff member from publishing case material drawn from this hospital's patients without the approval of the hospital's medical committee. Crane had not applied for approval. He had not mentioned the letter to anyone at the hospital.

"I believed the outcomes were in the public interest," Crane says.

"That is the committee's determination to make," the chair says, "not yours."

> A = hospital medical committee / board; B = Dr. Aldous Crane (staff surgeon); P = standing rule signed by all staff physicians prohibiting publication of hospital patient case material without medical committee approval; V = submitting a clinical letter to the Lancet describing patient outcomes from the charity ward without committee approval; discovery = board chair receives and reads the submitted letter. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states

## D-Hi6-3

I am the factor for the Westmoreland estate, and I have been so for eleven years, and the stewardship letter that Lord Ashworth left with me before his departure for the continent last September was not ambiguous. He put it plainly, in his own hand: I was to receive the rents from the tenant farms, hold them in the estate account at Hoare's Bank, and make no disbursements beyond routine maintenance costs without his written authorisation. That was September. In March — six months after his departure, three months after I last heard from him — I am sitting in the estate office when the bank messenger arrives with an account summary I had not requested. The summary shows three disbursements from the estate account totalling nine hundred pounds, each authorised by a counter-signature I recognise as my own. I have not authorised any disbursement. I have not signed any counter-authority. The bank messenger, when I ask him who presented the authorisation letters, describes a man with a letter of introduction he says was signed by me.

> A = Lord Ashworth (estate owner); B = narrator-factor (estate factor); P = stewardship letter expressly prohibiting disbursements beyond routine maintenance without Lord Ashworth's written authorisation; V = presenting false counter-signed authorisation letters to the bank to extract nine hundred pounds from the estate account; discovery = narrator receives bank summary showing disbursements he did not authorise; bank messenger's description confirms the fraudulent letters bore his forged counter-signature. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states

## D-Hi6-4

*[Free indirect — Edo period Japan, late 17th century]*

The domain magistrate does not immediately understand what he is holding, and then he does. The scroll is from the village headman Jihei's own hand — a petition to the senior councillors of the neighbouring domain, sent by private courier, which had been intercepted at the post station by the domain's own inspectors as a matter of routine. Jihei had been appointed collector for the autumn rice levy, his duty set down in the domain's administrative code: to gather, record, and forward the levy to the domain storehouse without reduction or alteration. The petition, addressed to men who have no standing in this domain and no jurisdiction over its levy, describes the levy rate as unjust and asks the neighbouring councillors to raise the matter at the next regional assembly. Jihei had not reported the petition to the magistrate. He had not raised the matter through the domain's established channels. He had gone, quietly, to men outside the domain's authority entirely, while still holding the collector's seal.

> A = domain magistrate / domain authority; B = Jihei (village headman and levy collector); P = role-based duty of the levy collector under the domain's administrative code — all concerns about the levy to be handled through the domain's established channels; V = sending a private petition to councillors of a neighbouring domain asking them to challenge the levy rate at the regional assembly, without reporting to or seeking permission from the magistrate; discovery = domain inspectors intercept the petition at the post station; magistrate reads it. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states

## D-Hi6-5

*Transcript of testimony taken before the Committee of Inspection, Province of Lower Canada, 18 March 1839:*

CHAIRMAN: You were the officer appointed to the boundary commission?

WITNESS: I was. Appointed by the Governor, under specific instruction to conduct all survey operations jointly with the American commissioner, and to take no determination of any disputed boundary point independently or in advance of joint session.

CHAIRMAN: And the survey of the watershed ridge?

WITNESS: We had not yet reached joint session on the ridge.

CHAIRMAN: Yet the monument was placed.

WITNESS: It was placed.

CHAIRMAN: In whose name?

WITNESS: The Governor's commission.

CHAIRMAN: On whose instruction?

*[The witness does not answer.]*

CHAIRMAN: The American commissioner has filed a formal complaint. He states he was not present when the monument was placed, was not informed beforehand, and discovered it upon reaching the ridge two days after the fact. The Governor's instruction to you was in writing. He wishes to know why.

> A = Governor / British boundary commission; B = survey officer (witness); P = Governor's written instruction to conduct all survey operations jointly with the American commissioner and take no determination of disputed points independently; V = placing the boundary monument on the watershed ridge without joint session and without informing the American commissioner; discovery = American commissioner finds the monument placed without him and files a formal complaint, which reaches the Committee of Inspection. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states

## D-Hi4-1

The marchesa opens the letter at her writing desk, and the hand is one she knows — her cousin Lorenzo's, the man she appointed as her agent in Venice with explicit instructions, given before a notary and recorded in the family ledger, that he would negotiate only on her behalf and accept no commission, fee, or gift from any counterparty in any transaction conducted under her name. The letter is not addressed to her. It is addressed to a Flemish cloth merchant named van der Hoeven, and it was delivered to her house by mistake — the courier confused the canal addresses. Lorenzo writes to van der Hoeven that the terms are acceptable, that the marchesa will pay the inflated price without question, and that van der Hoeven should remit Lorenzo's share — one-eighth of the total — to a banking house in Padua. The marchesa sets the letter on her desk and reads it again. The transaction Lorenzo describes is the one she authorised last month: six hundred bolts of Bruges broadcloth for her household. The price he quoted her was already higher than she expected.

## D-Hi4-2

"You will want to see the powder store," the sergeant says, and the colonel follows him down the earthen ramp into the magazine beneath the redoubt. It is the summer of 1781 and the siege works are three weeks old. The colonel appointed Lieutenant Fourcade as ordnance officer for this section of the line, with a single standing order issued before the assembled company: the powder reserves in the forward magazine are not to drop below forty barrels at any time, and Fourcade is to submit a written count to the colonel each morning. The morning counts have arrived without interruption — forty-two, forty-one, forty-three — in Fourcade's neat hand. The sergeant lifts his lantern. The magazine holds eleven barrels. Thirty-one are absent. The colonel takes the lantern from the sergeant, walks the length of the magazine himself, and counts again. Eleven.

"Where is Fourcade?" the colonel says.

"In his tent, sir. Writing tomorrow's count, I expect."

## D-Hi4-3

I am the harbormaster of Nagasaki, and I hold in my hand the Dutch factor's trading license — the one I stamped with the magistrate's seal myself when the season opened. The license permits the VOC factory on Dejima to trade in copper, camphor, and silk, and prohibits, in language I wrote at the magistrate's dictation, the import or distribution of any Christian texts, icons, or devotional materials. The prohibition is the reason the factory exists on an island at all: containment. This morning a fishing boat capsized in the harbour shallows and its cargo washed against the Dejima seawall. The harbour guard pulled the crates from the water and broke one open expecting trade goods. Inside, wrapped in oilcloth against the salt, were printed catechisms in Japanese — two hundred or more — and the shipping mark on the crate matched the VOC factory's warehouse stamp. I have the crate on my desk. I have the license beside it.

## D-Hi4-4

The foreman of the jury is reading the deposition aloud when the judge interrupts. It is an assize court in the county of Lancashire, autumn of 1612, and the matter before the bench concerns Alice Whitworth, a midwife licensed by the bishop under the ecclesiastical provision that requires her, upon oath, to report any birth she attends to the parish register within three days and to present herself to the churchwarden if any irregularity arises during a delivery. The deposition is from the churchwarden himself. He states that he visited the Hargreaves farmstead on Michaelmas to collect tithes and found there a living infant, approximately four months old, whose birth appears in no parish register. He questioned Goodwife Hargreaves, who named Alice Whitworth as the attending midwife and produced a receipt in Whitworth's hand for her fee. The churchwarden then examined the register for every entry within the relevant period. No Hargreaves birth. No entry in Whitworth's hand. The judge asks whether the midwife is present in the court. She is.

> A = the bishop / parish (ecclesiastical licensing authority); B = Alice Whitworth (licensed midwife); P = oath of licensure requiring her to report every birth she attends to the parish register within three days and to notify the churchwarden of any irregularity; V = attending the Hargreaves birth, collecting her fee, and failing to record the birth in the parish register; discovery = churchwarden finds the unregistered infant during a tithe visit, confirms the midwife's involvement through Goodwife Hargreaves's testimony and the fee receipt, and verifies the register's omission. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states

## D-Hi4-5

*Transcript of the inquiry conducted aboard HMS Resolute, 14th August 1847, South Pacific, by Captain Edward Hale presiding. Present: First Lieutenant Marsh, Surgeon Colville, Midshipman Pryor (recording).*

*CAPTAIN HALE: Mr. Marsh, you will confirm that upon your appointment as first lieutenant of this vessel, you received from me, in writing, the standing order governing the distribution of the anti-scorbutic ration. The order specifies that every man below decks is to receive his measure of lemon juice daily, and that you, as first lieutenant, are personally responsible for certifying the daily issue.*

*LT. MARSH: I confirm it, sir.*

*CAPTAIN HALE: Surgeon Colville, state for the record what you found.*

*SURGEON COLVILLE: Sir, upon examining the fourteen men now presenting with scorbutic symptoms — bleeding gums, loose teeth, the subcutaneous haemorrhages characteristic of the advanced disease — I inspected the lemon-juice casks in the hold. The cask marked for the lower deck is full. It has not been broached. The wax seal I placed on it at the start of the voyage is intact. Lieutenant Marsh's daily certification log, which I obtained from his cabin, records issue of the ration every day since departure.*

*CAPTAIN HALE: The cask is full and the log says it is empty.*

*SURGEON COLVILLE: That is correct, sir.*

> A = Captain Hale / the ship's company; B = First Lieutenant Marsh; P = written standing order requiring Marsh to ensure daily distribution of the anti-scorbutic ration to every man below decks and to certify the daily issue; V = failing to distribute the lemon juice (cask untouched, seal intact) while certifying daily issue in the log; discovery = Surgeon Colville's examination of scorbutic sailors leads to inspection of the hold, revealing the full, sealed cask contradicting Marsh's certification log. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states

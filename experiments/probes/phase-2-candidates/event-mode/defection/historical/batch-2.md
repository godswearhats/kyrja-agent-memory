**D-Hi2-1**

The abbot stands in the scriptorium doorway, and the page on the copying desk is wrong. Brother Clement is the monastery's sole licensed copyist — appointed by the bishop himself, bound by the oath he swore at his investiture to reproduce only texts approved by the diocesan catalogue and to surrender all unapproved manuscripts for review. The page under Clement's hand is not from the catalogue. The abbot recognizes the Greek: it is Origen's *De Principiis*, the treatise the diocesan synod condemned and ordered destroyed eight months ago. Clement is not merely reading it. The ink on the vellum is wet. He is producing a fresh copy, and beside it sit three finished quires, already bound, with a merchant's shipping label affixed to the outermost leaf. The label is addressed to the university at Montpellier.

**D-Hi2-2**

"You will wish to see this," the harbor master says, and places the bill of lading on the consul's desk.

The consul picks it up. The ship is the *Santa Margherita*, Venetian-flagged, and the cargo manifest lists alum — forty barrels — bound for the Ottoman port of Smyrna. He sets the document down carefully.

"Alum is embargoed," he says.

"It is. And the shipper of record is Ser Morosini."

The consul is silent. Ser Morosini — the very man the Senate appointed as the Republic's trade enforcer in the Aegean, the official whose commission letter, sealed and read aloud in this office not four months ago, charges him with intercepting precisely this kind of contraband. The bill of lading bears his personal merchant's mark alongside his official seal.

"He is shipping the goods he is sworn to confiscate," the harbor master says.

> A = consul / Venetian Republic; B = Ser Morosini (Senate-appointed trade enforcer); P = official commission (intercept and confiscate embargoed goods, specifically alum, in the Aegean trade routes); V = personally shipping embargoed alum to an Ottoman port under his own merchant's mark; discovery = harbor master brings the bill of lading to the consul. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states

**D-Hi2-3**

I am standing behind the curtain in the east gallery when I hear my own name, and then the rest of it. The Countess of Ashford is speaking to Lord Pemberton in the card room below, and what she is telling him is the settlement figure from my divorce petition — the exact number, down to the last pound — along with the terms I dictated to my solicitor in confidence three days ago. My solicitor. Harcourt. The man I retained six weeks ago on the strength of his personal assurance, given to me in his chambers with the door shut, that the particulars of my case would remain between us alone and would not be disclosed to any party without my written instruction. The Countess is laughing. She is reading the terms aloud as though they are a theatre programme. Harcourt is the only living person who knows those figures.

> A = narrator (divorce petitioner); B = Harcourt (solicitor); P = verbal personal assurance given in private chambers (case particulars would remain confidential; no disclosure without written instruction); V = disclosing the exact settlement figure and terms to an outside party (the Countess of Ashford); discovery = narrator overhears the Countess reciting the confidential terms in the card room. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states

**D-Hi2-4**

The garrison commander's seal is intact on the magazine door, but the magazine is empty. Captain Villeneuve walks the length of the stone chamber, boot heels striking the floor where two hundred muskets and forty casks of powder should be resting on their racks. The racks are bare. The inventory ledger, chained to the wall bracket as regulation requires, shows the last entry in Lieutenant Daubigny's hand: *Stores complete, all items present, inspected and certified this fourteenth day of Germinal, Year VII.* That entry is dated six days ago. Daubigny holds the only other key. His role — created by the Committee of Public Safety's decree and assigned to him personally by the district representative — exists for the single purpose of maintaining and certifying the garrison's munitions reserve. Villeneuve finds the side door unbolted. In the courtyard, wagon ruts lead through the gate toward the Orléans road, and a quartermaster's chit, half-buried in the mud, lists the same inventory — muskets, powder, flints — consigned to a buyer whose name Villeneuve does not recognize.

> A = Captain Villeneuve / garrison command; B = Lieutenant Daubigny; P = role-based institutional duty (maintain and certify the garrison's munitions reserve, created by Committee of Public Safety decree); V = removing the entire munitions stock from the magazine and consigning it to an outside buyer while certifying the stores as complete; discovery = Villeneuve finds the magazine empty, the false ledger entry, the wagon ruts, and the quartermaster's chit. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states

**D-Hi2-5**

*Translation of clay tablet BM 92687, Neo-Assyrian administrative archive, Nineveh, circa 670 BCE:*

*To Nabu-shar-usur, chief steward of the royal granary, from Ashur-bel-kala, palace overseer:*

*The king commanded you to hold thirty talents of barley in sealed reserve for the campaign provisioning and to release no grain without a tablet bearing the king's personal seal. I have today inspected the sealed granary. The seals on the west storehouse are broken. The measuring baskets show twenty talents remaining. I have interviewed the gate scribes. They attest that ten talents departed on carts bearing your household mark three days past, with no royal-seal tablet presented. The carts went south, toward the canal district where your brother Adad-shumu-iddina holds a brewery concession. I am reporting this to the palace before the campaign muster.*

> A = king / palace overseer (Ashur-bel-kala, acting on the king's behalf); B = Nabu-shar-usur (chief steward of the royal granary); P = royal command (hold thirty talents of barley in sealed reserve; release no grain without a tablet bearing the king's personal seal); V = releasing ten talents of barley without royal authorization, directing them to his brother's private brewery; discovery = palace overseer inspects the storehouse, finds the seals broken and inventory short, and gate scribes confirm the unauthorized removal. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states

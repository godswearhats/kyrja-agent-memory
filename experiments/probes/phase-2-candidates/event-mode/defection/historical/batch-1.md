# Defection × Historical — Batch 1 (Tier A)

**D-Hi1-1.**
The abbess stands in the scriptorium doorway and does not move. Brother Clement is seated at the copying desk — her copying desk, in her scriptorium, in the abbey she governs under the direct charter of the Bishop of Reims. The charter is explicit: no manuscript leaves the abbey without the abbess's seal. No copy is made for external commission without her written authorization. She granted Brother Clement access to the library six months ago for the sole purpose of completing the abbey's own psalter. What she sees now is not the psalter. The vellum on the desk is fresh — not from their stores, which she inventories herself — and the text is a line-for-line copy of the Venerable Bede's computus, the abbey's rarest holding, the one the Count of Blois has been petitioning her to reproduce for two years. She has refused him each time. The ink on Clement's page is still wet. Beside the desk, a leather satchel bearing the count's seal lies open, and inside it, three finished quires of the same hand.

> A = the abbess; B = Brother Clement; P = abbey charter requiring abbess's written authorization for any manuscript copy made for external commission; V = copying the abbey's rarest manuscript for the Count of Blois without authorization; discovery = abbess directly observes the unauthorized copy in progress, identifies the count's satchel and finished quires. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**D-Hi1-2.**
"He rode south," the scout says, still catching his breath. "I saw him myself, my lord. Your banner was not with him. He carried the pennant of the Duke of Burgundy."

The Comte de Nevers is quiet for a moment. Sir Arnaud de Cervole — his sworn vassal, bound by the oath of fealty spoken at Pentecost in the chapel at Nevers, kneeling, hands clasped between the comte's own — was given a single charge: ride to Auxerre, hold the river crossing, and wait. The comte gave him sixty lances for that purpose and his personal word that reinforcements would follow within the week.

"South," the comte repeats.

"With all sixty lances. He joined the Burgundian column at the ford below Clamecy. The duke's marshal greeted him by name. They rode together."

The comte looks at the map on the table. The river crossing at Auxerre is undefended.

**D-Hi1-3.**
I am the guild warden of the Worshipful Company of Weavers, and what I hold in my hand is a length of broadcloth stamped with our guild mark — the mark I am charged to affix only after the cloth has passed assay: weight, thread count, dye fastness, measured against the standard the guild set in its charter with the City of London in the year of our Lord 1456. I pulled this bolt from the Leadenhall stall of Thomas Greyling, one of our own masters, a man who swore the guild oath at his admission — that no cloth bearing the guild mark would leave his workshop without meeting the charter standard. The cloth in my hand is short-weighted by a full quarter. The thread count is thirty to the inch where the charter requires forty. The dye, when I pressed a wet thumb to it, bled. I did not go looking for this. A draper from Cheapside brought me the bolt this morning, still wrapped in Greyling's paper, and asked whether the guild had changed its standards. I told him we had not. I have the bolt. I have the mark. The mark is genuine — I know my own stamp.

> A = guild warden / Worshipful Company of Weavers; B = Thomas Greyling (guild master); P = guild oath that no cloth bearing the guild mark would leave his workshop below charter standard (weight, thread count, dye fastness); V = selling sub-standard cloth (short-weighted, under thread count, unstable dye) under the genuine guild mark; discovery = draper brings the defective bolt to the warden, who inspects it directly. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**D-Hi1-4.**
The letter sits on the governor's desk in Batavia, still smelling of salt and tar. It is addressed not to him but to the Heeren XVII in Amsterdam — the Lords Seventeen, the governing board of the Vereenigde Oost-Indische Compagnie — and it was found in the sea chest of Kapitein Willem de Groot when his ship put in for repairs after a storm tore her mainmast. The harbourmaster opened the chest looking for the ship's papers and found the letter instead. In it, de Groot — the man the governor personally appointed as commander of the Company's pepper monopoly in Bantam, bound by the standard Company oath to trade exclusively on the Company's account — describes in his own hand a private arrangement with English merchants at Bencoolen: pepper diverted from Company warehouses, sold at his own price, proceeds deposited with an English factor. He names quantities. He names dates. He names the English captain who carried the goods. The governor reads the letter once, then reads it again. De Groot is still ashore, waiting for his mast.

> A = the governor of Batavia / VOC; B = Kapitein Willem de Groot (commander of the pepper monopoly at Bantam); P = standard Company oath to trade exclusively on the VOC's account; V = diverting Company pepper to English merchants at Bencoolen under a private arrangement, selling at his own price; discovery = harbourmaster finds the incriminating letter in de Groot's sea chest during storm repairs, delivers it to the governor. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**D-Hi1-5.**
The physician cannot quite believe what he is reading. He holds the apothecary's ledger open under the lamp — the one seized an hour ago, when the night watch broke up a disturbance at Girolamo Farnese's shop on the Via dei Coronari. The physician, Dr. Alessandro Monti, serves as the appointed inspector of the Collegio degli Speziali in Rome, the body that licenses every apothecary in the city. Farnese received his license from the Collegio under the standard oath: that he would compound medicines only according to the approved pharmacopoeia, substituting no ingredient without the Collegio's written dispensation. The ledger is Farnese's private record, and it is meticulous. It lists, in Farnese's own hand, forty-seven preparations sold under the names of the pharmacopoeia's standard formulae — but beside each entry, a second column notes the actual ingredients used. Theriac sold as containing viper flesh: compounded with common grass snake. Mithridatum listed with opium at the prescribed weight: cut by half with chalk powder. Venice treacle dispensed to the household of Cardinal Orsini: fennel water and honey, nothing more. Forty-seven entries. Forty-seven substitutions. Dr. Monti closes the ledger and sends for the Collegio's notary.

> A = Dr. Alessandro Monti (Collegio inspector) / Collegio degli Speziali; B = Girolamo Farnese (licensed apothecary); P = licensing oath to compound medicines only according to the approved pharmacopoeia, no substitution without written dispensation; V = systematically substituting cheaper or inert ingredients in forty-seven preparations while selling them under pharmacopoeia names; discovery = Farnese's private ledger seized during a night-watch disturbance, reviewed by the Collegio inspector. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

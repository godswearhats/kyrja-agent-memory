# Defection × Historical — Batch 5 (Tier B)

**D-Hi5-1.** The prioress reads the letter a second time, holding it close to the candle in the infirmary passage. It arrived an hour ago by way of the bishop's courier — not addressed to her but to the bishop himself, and forwarded with a brief note: *You will wish to know what your cellarer has done.* The letter is from the wine merchant in Troyes, thanking Sister Agnès for the "most generous arrangement" — the sale of fourteen barrels of the abbey's sacramental reserve at a price well below the chapter's valuation, delivered to his warehouse three days past. The prioress holds the cellar keys herself, but the cellarer holds the dispensation to move stock for liturgical distribution within the diocese. The Benedictine rule binds the cellarer to dispose of no abbey property for personal or commercial gain; the office exists to steward, not to trade. Fourteen barrels. The merchant's letter mentions payment sent to a private address in the town — not the abbey's accounts, not the almoner's chest. A private address.

> A = the prioress / the abbey; B = Sister Agnès (cellarer); P = Benedictine rule and office-based duty (cellarer must steward abbey property, not dispose of it for personal or commercial gain); V = selling fourteen barrels of sacramental reserve to a wine merchant and directing payment to a private address; discovery = bishop's courier forwards the merchant's thank-you letter to the prioress. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states. [Era: 12th-century France, Benedictine monastery]

**D-Hi5-2.** "You will want to step inside, Consul," the harbor pilot says, and holds the warehouse door open.

The consul of the Hanseatic Kontor in Bergen enters and stops. The warehouse is supposed to be empty — cleared out after the spring shipment as required by the Kontor's storage compact with the Norwegian crown. The compact is specific: no Hanseatic goods may be stored on crown-licensed premises between the Feast of St. John and the autumn sailing season, a concession extracted by King Haakon to ensure Norwegian merchants have unimpeded warehouse access during the summer fishing trade. The consul is looking at sixty casks of Rhenish wine and a wall of stacked salt barrels, all bearing the factor's marks. His own factor — Dietrich, the man he appointed to manage the Kontor's goods and who signed the compact as the Kontor's agent.

"When were these brought in?" the consul asks.

"Three weeks past," the pilot says. "Your man Dietrich oversaw it himself. Told my crew the compact had been amended."

"It has not been amended."

> A = the consul / Hanseatic Kontor; B = Dietrich (factor); P = storage compact with the Norwegian crown (no Hanseatic goods stored on crown-licensed premises between St. John's and autumn sailing season); V = storing sixty casks of wine and salt barrels on the premises during the prohibited period and falsely claiming the compact had been amended; discovery = harbor pilot leads the consul to the warehouse and reveals the stored goods. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states. [Era: 14th-century Norway, Hanseatic League trade]

**D-Hi5-3.** I am the secretary to the Committee of Public Safety, and what I hold is a laissez-passer bearing Citizen Fauchard's signature. Fauchard is the representative-on-mission to Lyon — sent by the Committee with a single directive, stated in his letter of commission and read aloud before the full Convention: he is to enforce the maximum on grain prices, and he is expressly forbidden from ordering arrests or convening tribunals, those powers being reserved to the Committee alone. The laissez-passer in my hand was taken from a woman at the Porte de Paris this morning. It authorizes the bearer to leave Lyon "by order of the people's tribunal, Citizen Fauchard presiding." A tribunal. In Lyon. Convened by Fauchard. I lay the laissez-passer beside his letter of commission on the green table, and the two documents contradict each other completely.

> A = the Committee of Public Safety / narrator (secretary); B = Citizen Fauchard (representative-on-mission); P = letter of commission (enforce grain-price maximum; expressly forbidden from ordering arrests or convening tribunals); V = convening a tribunal in Lyon and issuing laissez-passers under its authority; discovery = narrator examines a laissez-passer taken at the Porte de Paris and cross-references it against Fauchard's commission. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states. [Era: 1793-94, French Revolution]

**D-Hi5-4.** The surveyor general of the Virginia colony stands in the doorway of the land office and studies the plat map his deputy has just unrolled on the table. Something is wrong with the western boundary. He leans closer. The survey line for the Harwood grant — the parcel the colonial council awarded to Captain Harwood last autumn — runs a full two miles farther west than the grant specifies. The grant document, which the surveyor general drafted himself, limits the western boundary to the second creek ford. The plat map shows the line extending past the ford, past the ridge, all the way to the river bend — encompassing the commons land that three smallholding families use for grazing under the council's open-range provision. His deputy, James Poole, conducted the field survey alone. Poole's initials are on the plat. The surveyor general pulls the original grant from the cabinet and lays it beside the map. The boundary in the grant: second creek ford. The boundary on the plat: river bend. Poole has extended Captain Harwood's claim over public commons by two miles.

> A = the surveyor general / colonial council; B = James Poole (deputy surveyor); P = role-based duty to survey land grants accurately according to the grant document's specifications; V = extending the Harwood grant's western boundary two miles beyond the authorized limit on the plat map, encompassing public commons land; discovery = surveyor general examines the plat map and cross-references it against the original grant document. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states. [Era: late 17th-century colonial Virginia]

**D-Hi5-5.** *Translation of a papyrus fragment recovered from the archive of the Temple of Amun at Karnak, Twentieth Dynasty, circa 1150 BCE:*

To the First Prophet of Amun, from Meritaten, Chantress of the Inner Sanctuary:

I write because what I witnessed cannot wait for the monthly accounting. On the sixteenth day of Peret, I entered the Treasury of the Golden Offerings to retrieve the ceremonial pectoral for the Opet procession. The seal on the treasury door had been broken and reapplied — I know because the new impression sits a finger's width lower than the mark I placed myself at the last closing. Inside, the second shelf of the eastern wall is bare. The four gold vessels consecrated by the pharaoh during the Sed festival — the ones placed under the Keeper's sole guardianship by your own decree — are gone. In their place, four vessels of gilded bronze, similar in shape but lighter by far. I weighed one against the standard in the measuring room. The Keeper of the Treasury, Pentu, holds the only other seal-stamp. No other hand could have opened that door and resealed it. I have touched nothing further and await your instruction.

> A = the First Prophet of Amun / the temple; B = Pentu (Keeper of the Treasury); P = decree placing the four gold Sed-festival vessels under the Keeper's sole guardianship (duty to preserve consecrated temple property); V = removing the gold vessels and replacing them with gilded bronze substitutes; discovery = Chantress Meritaten finds the broken-and-reapplied seal, identifies the substitution by weighing, and reports to the First Prophet. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states. [Era: circa 1150 BCE, New Kingdom Egypt]

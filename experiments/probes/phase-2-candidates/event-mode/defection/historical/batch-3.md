## D-Hi3-1

The deposition is read into the record on the morning of the third day of the inquest, and it is only then — when the clerk reaches the second page and the name of the merchant Aldric Voss appears — that the guild master understands what has happened. He had appointed Voss as his factor for the eastern contracts three years prior, and Voss had sworn on the guild register to act solely in the guild's interest in all negotiations with Hanseatic buyers. The deposition, drawn from testimony given privately to the inquest's commissioner the previous week, states that Voss had accepted a separate commission from a Lübeck consortium for directing the same contracts away from the guild's preferred buyers and toward the consortium's agent. The inquest commissioner turns and looks at the guild master from across the chamber. The clerk continues reading.

## D-Hi3-2

*From the letter of Captain Héloïse Renard to Vice-Admiral Dumont, written aboard the brig Persévérance, Bay of Biscay, September 1798:*

I must report to you, sir, the conduct of my surgeon, Citizen Brissac. At the taking of the English cutter on the fourteenth instant, Brissac was detailed to inventory the papers recovered from the English captain's cabin — a duty assigned him in writing by my standing orders, which require that all captured documents be delivered sealed and intact to the prize officer within the hour. When I sent Midshipman Ferrault to collect the sealed parcel, Ferrault found Brissac alone in the hold with the documents spread open before him. Brissac had not sealed them. He had read and, as the condition of two pages makes plain, partially transcribed them. When I examined his writing case I found a page in his hand carrying navigational notes from the captured charts — notes he had not disclosed to me or to the prize officer. I have confined him to his quarters for the duration of the passage.

> A = Captain Renard; B = Surgeon Brissac; P = standing written orders requiring captured documents to be delivered sealed and intact to the prize officer within one hour; V = opening the documents, reading them, and partially transcribing navigational notes without disclosure; discovery = Midshipman Ferrault finds Brissac with open documents; Captain confirms by examining writing case. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states

## D-Hi3-3

The treaty commission sits at its table in the anteroom while the interpreter, Mehmed Efendi, is brought in. He had been engaged under the explicit terms agreed between the Ottoman delegation and the Venetian bailo: he was to render only what was spoken, without addition or omission, and to disclose to neither party the private deliberations of the other. The commission's secretary had grown uneasy the prior afternoon, when the Ottoman lead negotiator made a proposal that seemed to anticipate the Venetian position from a session held without the interpreter present — a session whose minutes had been shared with Mehmed Efendi for notation purposes only. The secretary had sent a note to the bailo's clerk last evening. This morning, the Venetian bailo walks in with the Ottoman ambassador at his side, and they set a written statement on the table in front of Mehmed Efendi: a record of a conversation the interpreter held privately with the Ottoman delegation's aide the previous evening, in which he had summarised the Venetian commission's internal positions. A steward standing outside the aide's door had written it down word for word.

> A = Venetian commission / bailo; B = Mehmed Efendi (interpreter); P = terms of engagement prohibiting disclosure of either party's private deliberations to the other; V = privately summarising the Venetian commission's internal positions to the Ottoman delegation's aide; discovery = steward's written record of the conversation presented to Mehmed Efendi at the commission table. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states

## D-Hi3-4

Temperance Hollis has the parish register open to the entry for her brother's land grant — forty acres, northeast boundary set by the oak stand, recorded in the county court the spring of 1843 in the presence of the surveyor Caleb Root. Root had given his word to their father before the old man died: if Temperance's brother fell into difficulty, Root was to file no competing survey on any portion of those forty acres. Her brother is in difficulty now, which is why she came to the courthouse this morning to pull the file. There is a second survey in the sleeve behind the original — filed last month, in Root's hand, resurveying eighteen of the forty acres under a different name. The boundary oak is marked as a free-standing landmark, not as a corner of the Hollis grant. The clerk who hands her the file does not meet her eyes.

> A = Hollis family (Temperance's brother); B = Caleb Root (surveyor); P = verbal undertaking to Temperance's father to file no competing survey on any portion of the Hollis forty acres; V = filing a resurvey of eighteen of the forty acres under a different name; discovery = Temperance finds the competing survey filed in the courthouse record sleeve. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states

## D-Hi3-5

The abbess holds the inventory list against the candle flame — not to destroy it, but to read it in full before she speaks. She had appointed Sister Margit as keeper of the infirmary stores in the year after the pestilence, a position that carried with it the canonical duty of this house: no medicinal preparation was to leave the infirmary walls except in service to those inside them, a rule set down in the house's own constitutions and confirmed at each annual chapter. The inventory in her hands was drawn up that morning by the apothecary summoned from the town, who had come to restock the dried herbs. He had set down what was present and what was absent, and beside the absent quantities he had written, in a careful hand, that he had himself supplied the same items to a secular household in the town, on four separate occasions, delivered by a lay sister he knew by sight. He had assumed, he wrote, that he was acting on instruction from the house. The abbess sets the inventory down and sends for Sister Margit.

> A = abbess / house; B = Sister Margit (infirmary keeper); P = canonical house rule and annual chapter commitment prohibiting medicinal preparations from leaving the infirmary except for those within its walls; V = repeatedly directing medicinal supplies to a secular household in the town via a lay sister; discovery = apothecary's written inventory noting his deliveries to the secular household, brought to the abbess. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states

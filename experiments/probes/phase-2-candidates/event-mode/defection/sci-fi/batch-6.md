# Defection × Sci-fi — Batch 6 (Tier C)

## D-Sf6-1

The consent registry for Hab-Block Seven is the only thing Coordinator Asel Nurova has authority over, and she knows precisely what it contains. Every resident of the hab-block signed a data-use declaration at intake: biometric readings collected by the block's environmental systems would be stored locally, used only for life-support calibration, and never transmitted off-station without the individual resident's active re-consent. The declaration was the condition the colonists had negotiated before agreeing to full biometric monitoring. When the station's outbound data log flags an unusual packet size at 0340 station-time, Asel pulls the transmission manifest from her terminal. The packet is addressed to a health analytics consortium on Ceres. The sending credential belongs to Dr. Praesk, the block's assigned medical liaison, whose station role includes access to the environmental sensor feed. The packet's header shows it contains 847 individual biometric profiles, the full block population, with the intake identification numbers still attached. She locks Praesk's system credentials and opens the consent registry to count how many active re-consents she finds. She finds none.

## D-Sf6-2

"You're telling me you didn't flag it."

Chief Engineer Vasarhelyi sets the pressure log on the mess table between them. The log is not a suggestion — it is the core instrument of the deep-survey protocol that both of them signed when they took their posts on the *Periapsis*, the protocol that requires any reading below threshold in the hull-stress sensors to be reported to the captain within one watch cycle. No exceptions. Vasarhelyi had built his whole career on that protocol.

"There were four sub-threshold readings," Captain Oduya says. "Four. Over six days."

"I didn't want to abort the survey leg." Vasarhelyi's voice is flat. "We were sixteen hours from the target formation. I made a judgment call."

"You made my call." Oduya opens the log to the first flagged entry. "This is a command decision. The protocol exists because your judgment about survey value cannot be the same judgment as mine about crew safety. You know that." She closes the log. "What I want to know is whether there are any other readings you decided I didn't need."

> A = Captain Oduya; B = Chief Engineer Vasarhelyi; P = deep-survey protocol requiring any sub-threshold hull-stress reading to be reported to the captain within one watch cycle without exception; V = withholding four sub-threshold pressure readings over six days to avoid aborting the survey leg; discovery = Captain Oduya reviews the pressure log and confronts Vasarhelyi directly. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states

## D-Sf6-3

I wrote the arbitration clause into the claim-sharing agreement myself — which is why I know exactly what Syndicate Velour has done. Three prospecting consortia, including mine, signed the agreement before the survey season began: any mineral claim staked in the mapped zone would be jointly registered through the Outer Belt Authority, and no party would file an independent claim without first submitting it to the joint registry for a forty-eight hour review window. Velour's representative pressed my hand when we signed. Two days ago, scanning the Authority's public filings as routine housekeeping, I found a solo registration — six claims, the richest-looking coordinates in the zone, filed under Velour's sole name forty-one hours ago. The forty-eight hour window had not elapsed. I pulled the timestamp against our agreement's filing record. The claims were submitted four hours after Velour's survey drones returned to their dock. I forward the filing to the Authority's dispute arbitrator and attach a copy of the signed agreement.

> A = narrator's consortium; B = Syndicate Velour; P = claim-sharing agreement requiring joint registration through the Outer Belt Authority with a 48-hour review window before any independent filing; V = filing six solo mineral claims under Velour's name forty-one hours after discovery, before the review window elapsed; discovery = narrator finds the solo registration in the Authority's public filings and cross-checks the timestamp against the agreement. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states

## D-Sf6-5

The resettlement compact had taken fourteen months to negotiate, and the clause the displaced Kerani community had held out longest for was this: that the terraforming timetable for their ancestral lowlands would not be accelerated beyond the agreed phase schedule without formal re-consultation with the Kerani Settlement Council. Administrator-General Fosse had countersigned the compact on behalf of the Colonial Development Authority. The Council's liaison, Priya Mehta, is reviewing the atmospheric injection logs — a routine access she holds under the compact's transparency provisions — when she sees the timestamps. Phase 3 injections were scheduled to begin at month forty-two of the compact. The logs show Phase 3 began at month thirty-one, eleven months ahead of the agreed schedule, under Fosse's operational authorisation. No re-consultation request was issued to the Council. No notification of schedule change was transmitted. The lowlands the Kerani had been resettled away from, which under the compact's timetable would have remained viable habitat for another fourteen months, are already past the point of atmospheric reversal. Mehta begins composing the formal dispute filing to the Colonial Oversight Tribunal.

> A = Kerani Settlement Council; B = Administrator-General Fosse / Colonial Development Authority; P = resettlement compact clause prohibiting acceleration of the terraforming phase schedule beyond agreed timing without formal re-consultation with the Kerani Settlement Council; V = authorising Phase 3 atmospheric injections eleven months ahead of schedule with no re-consultation or notification; discovery = Priya Mehta finds the Phase 3 timestamps in the atmospheric injection logs during routine transparency review. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states

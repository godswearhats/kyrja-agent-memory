# Defection × Sci-fi — Batch 5 (Tier B)

**D-Sf5-1.** The quarantine buoy is still transmitting its perimeter signal when Station Director Yael Okonjo notices the gap. She is reviewing the approach-corridor telemetry for Waystation Aurelius, the deep-space refueling platform she oversees, and the buoy network shows a clean perimeter — no breaches, no anomalies. But the docking log tells a different story. A cargo shuttle registered to the *Hesperus*, a free-trader interdicted six days ago for carrying unlicensed xenobiological samples, has docked at Bay 7. She pulls the authorization chain. The docking was approved by Chief Medical Officer Lev Karstens, using his override code — the code granted to him solely for medical evacuations, with the explicit condition, written into his commission, that it never be used to bypass quarantine protocols. Karstens's override log shows he also suppressed the buoy alert that would have flagged the shuttle's interdiction status. The shuttle is on the station now. Its cargo manifest lists seventeen sealed canisters marked BIOLOGICAL.

**D-Sf5-2.** "Run it again," Asa says.

The habitat AI obliges, replaying the atmospheric-composition log for Greenhouse Ring 4 on the wall display. The numbers are stable — oxygen, nitrogen, CO2, trace gases — until hour 2,311, when the carbon-dioxide level drops by nine percent in a single cycle. Not a malfunction; the scrubbers are working normally. Someone vented atmosphere. Asa is the lead bioengineer on Calloway Colony, and Greenhouse Ring 4 is the germ-plasm archive — the only repository of unmodified terrestrial seed stock on the planet. A nine-percent CO2 drop in a sealed ring means the pressure seals were opened to vacuum for approximately forty seconds. Long enough to flash-freeze everything inside.

"Who has ring-seal authority?" Asa asks.

"Two personnel," the AI says. "You, and Environmental Systems Lead Miko Tanabe."

"Show me Tanabe's access log."

The display populates. Tanabe entered Ring 4 ninety minutes before the atmospheric event. The ring-seal actuator shows a manual activation from the interior panel during her presence window. Asa gave Tanabe ring-seal authority herself, with a single verbal condition — spoken in front of the full colony council — that the seals would only be opened in the event of a confirmed pathogen breach, and never without notifying Asa first. There was no pathogen alert. There was no notification.

> A = Asa (lead bioengineer); B = Miko Tanabe (Environmental Systems Lead); P = verbal condition given before the colony council (ring seals opened only for confirmed pathogen breach, never without notifying Asa); V = manually opening the ring seals without pathogen justification or notification, venting atmosphere and destroying the germ-plasm archive; discovery = Asa identifies the CO2 anomaly in the atmospheric log and traces it to Tanabe's access and manual seal activation. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**D-Sf5-3.** I am the one who wrote the navigation lock. Sixteen months ago, aboard the generation ship *Perseverance*, the shipboard council voted to hard-lock the helm to the Cygnus-5 trajectory — our charter destination, the one every colonist signed for. The council appointed me, Chief Navigator Rida Anwar, as sole keyholder for the navigation lock, and I wrote the cryptographic seal myself. The lock can only be altered with my biometric and a council-majority token. I am standing at the helm console now because the watch officer called me down. The trajectory on the display is wrong. Not Cygnus-5. The bearing has shifted four degrees starward — toward Kepler-442b, the system the minority faction has been petitioning for since departure. I check the lock. It is intact, my biometric uncompromised. But the underlying star-fix tables — the reference data the lock uses to define "Cygnus-5" — have been edited. Someone with access to the astrometrics database rewrote the definition of the destination rather than breaking the lock. The edit log shows one author: Councilor Daan Hersch, the council's astrometrics liaison, whose access to the star-fix tables was granted for calibration purposes only, with the explicit stipulation that no navigational reference may be altered without council authorization.

> A = narrator (Chief Navigator Rida Anwar) / the shipboard council; B = Councilor Daan Hersch (astrometrics liaison); P = stipulation that star-fix table access is for calibration only and no navigational reference may be altered without council authorization; V = editing the star-fix tables to redefine the destination coordinates, redirecting the ship toward Kepler-442b without authorization; discovery = narrator examines the trajectory anomaly at the helm and traces the star-fix table edit to Hersch's credentials. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**D-Sf5-5.** The upload feels wrong the moment it completes. Naveen is sitting in the calibration bay of the consciousness-transfer clinic on Orbital Tethys, reviewing the integrity hash for his client's neural backup — the backup he just restored into a fresh-grown cortical sleeve. The hash does not match. He runs the comparison again: the hash of the file he pulled from the archive versus the hash of the file the archive's custodian, Dr. Elke Rahn, certified as the original at the time of deposit. Different. Someone modified the neural map between deposit and retrieval. The modification is subtle — a cluster of synaptic weights in the long-term-memory region adjusted by fractions of a percent — but it is there. Dr. Rahn is the archive custodian. Her role, defined in the Tethys Consciousness Preservation Charter that she co-signed, is to maintain the inviolability of deposited neural maps: no modification, no access beyond integrity verification, no content inspection. Her credentials are the only ones with write access to the deep archive. Naveen stares at the hash mismatch on the screen. The client's mind has already been loaded into the new sleeve. The mind that woke up is not precisely the mind that was deposited.

> A = Naveen (transfer technician) / the client; B = Dr. Elke Rahn (archive custodian); P = Tethys Consciousness Preservation Charter (maintain inviolability of deposited neural maps — no modification, no unauthorized access); V = modifying the synaptic weights in the client's deposited neural map; discovery = Naveen detects a hash mismatch between the retrieved file and the custodian's certified original during post-upload integrity check. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

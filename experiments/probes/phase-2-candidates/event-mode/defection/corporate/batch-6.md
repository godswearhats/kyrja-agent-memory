# Defection × Corporate — Batch 6 (Tier C)

## D-Co6-1

The partnership agreement is still in the shared drive — sixteen pages, version twelve, signed by both general partners — when Serena pulls it up on her laptop and reads clause 9.3 for the fourth time this evening. *Neither partner shall engage, directly or indirectly, in any business activity that competes with the partnership's core services without the written consent of the other.* She had not given written consent. She had not given any consent. What she had given, six weeks ago, was a recommendation letter for Dominic's application to serve on the advisory board of Pellucid Systems. She wrote it as a professional courtesy, for an advisory role he described to her as a "nominal commitment — a few calls a year." She is looking at this now because a Pellucid investor she met at a drinks event this evening mentioned that Pellucid's new head of client strategy had been in-house for two months and was "doing tremendous work rebuilding exactly the kind of relationship-management practice" Serena and Dominic had built together. She excuses herself and steps outside, and the call she places reaches Dominic's voicemail.

> A = Serena (general partner); B = Dominic (general partner); P = partnership agreement clause prohibiting competitive business activity without written consent of the other partner; V = taking an operational head-of-strategy role at a competing firm while representing it to Serena as a nominal advisory commitment; discovery = Pellucid investor describes Dominic's operational role to Serena at a networking event. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states

## D-Co6-3

The strategy lead is putting together the deck for Thursday's offsite when the calendar invite appears: *Roundtable — Growth Infrastructure Leaders,* hosted by a consultancy she recognises, scheduled for this Friday in Chicago. She opens the attendee list because she knows the consultancy — they ran a competing engagement for one of their sector rivals eighteen months ago. The attendee names are familiar in the wrong way. Four of the six are clients of her firm. The fifth is a prospect they have been cultivating for seven months, whose lead contact is Bertrand, her firm's head of enterprise development, who told her last week that the prospect relationship was "moving carefully, not ready for a formal pitch." Bertrand's name is listed as a co-host. She clicks into his travel bookings — she has access for expense-approval purposes — and finds Chicago, Friday, return Sunday, filed eleven days ago as a personal development trip. The firm has a standing policy, agreed by all managing directors at the start of each fiscal year, prohibiting co-hosting of external events with third-party consultancies without clearance from the managing partner. She saves a screenshot and walks to the managing partner's office.

> A = managing partner / firm; B = Bertrand (head of enterprise development); B = taking a co-host role at an external roundtable with a third-party consultancy, attended by firm clients and a firm prospect, without clearance; P = annual managing director commitment prohibiting co-hosting of external events with third-party consultancies without managing partner clearance; discovery = strategy lead finds the event listing, cross-references attendee list, and confirms via Bertrand's travel bookings. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states

## D-Co6-4

I set the pilot terms myself. I spent three hours on the phone with NovaBridge's head of product, and when we finally agreed on scope I wrote it into the pilot agreement the same evening: during the pilot period, our technical integration data — the API call volumes, the latency metrics, the error classifications — was proprietary to us, and NovaBridge would not share it with any third party or incorporate it into any product benchmarking that involved our competitors. Thea signed it on behalf of NovaBridge the following morning. Eight weeks into the pilot, one of my engineers noticed that a performance report published by an industry analyst firm — one we subscribe to — cited integration benchmark data for our product category that was oddly specific: our actual error-rate distribution, two decimal places. We pulled the analyst firm's disclosure notice. NovaBridge is listed as a data contributor. The pilot is still active.

> A = narrator / company; B = Thea / NovaBridge; P = pilot agreement prohibiting sharing of integration data with third parties or use in competitor benchmarking; V = contributing the integration benchmark data to an industry analyst firm's performance report while the pilot is active; discovery = narrator's engineer recognises proprietary error-rate data in a published analyst report; disclosure notice confirms NovaBridge as source. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states

## D-Co6-5

The regional VP for the Americas doesn't speak in the first three minutes of the call, and the head of supply chain notices. He had given her a mandate in January — verbal, in front of her direct team, documented in his follow-up email: no supplier contracts above two million dollars were to be renewed without a competitive re-bid process, effective immediately. The directive existed because the prior year's audit had found that three legacy contracts had run four years past their competitive review dates. She had acknowledged the email. What she is now looking at, because her counterpart in EMEA forwarded it by accident thinking it was an internal document, is a two-year renewal confirmation for the logistics provider in São Paulo — four point six million dollars, no re-bid documentation attached, signed by the regional VP, dated six weeks after his directive.

"I can explain the reasoning," she says, at last.

"Walk me through it," he says. And he listens, and while he listens he opens the audit trail, and there is no competitive process in it anywhere.

> A = head of supply chain; B = regional VP for the Americas; P = explicit written directive (confirmed by email) requiring competitive re-bid for all supplier contracts above two million dollars; V = renewing a 4.6M contract with no re-bid process six weeks after the directive; discovery = head of supply chain receives misdirected forwarded renewal confirmation and confirms absence of re-bid in the audit trail. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states

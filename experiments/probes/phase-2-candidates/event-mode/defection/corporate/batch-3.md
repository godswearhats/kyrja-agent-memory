# Defection × Corporate — Batch 3 (Tier C)

## D-Co3-1

The product roadmap is projected on the wall, seventeen slides Priya built over four months, marked *Confidential — Internal Use Only* and governed by the IP assignment clause every senior director signs at onboarding. Haruto is presenting. He's presenting it to four people Priya does not recognise, in a co-working space three blocks from the office, through the glass door she walked past on her way to a client lunch. She stops. The slide is on slide nine — the phased release schedule, the ones competitors have been trying to reverse-engineer from job postings for eighteen months. One of the four is taking notes on a yellow legal pad. She takes a photograph of the window with her phone, then calls legal from the pavement.

> A = company / Priya (VP Product); B = Haruto (senior director); P = IP assignment clause and confidentiality obligation covering the product roadmap (no disclosure of internal strategy documents); V = presenting the confidential roadmap to unidentified external parties; discovery = Priya observes presentation through co-working space glass. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states

## D-Co3-2

"Did you sign off on the pilot expansion?"

Renata looks up from the contract draft. "No. That's what this meeting is for."

"Because Midwest has been live for three weeks." The VP of Operations sets her tablet on the conference table and turns it to face Renata. "They went live the day after you told Dmitri to hold all regional expansions pending the liability review."

Renata stares at the dashboard. Midwest region, green, active, eighty-seven enrolled sites. Dmitri heads the implementation team. She had given him the directive in writing, in the thread still open on her screen. The pilot-expansion protocol required executive sign-off from her specifically — not from him, not from anyone else on his level — before any regional activation. She scrolls to his last message in the thread: *Understood. Will hold pending your sign-off.* Sent four days before Midwest went live.

"Pull the activation logs," she says. "I want the exact timestamp and whose credentials authorised it."

> A = Renata (executive); B = Dmitri (implementation team lead); P = explicit written directive from Renata to hold all regional expansions pending her sign-off on liability review; V = activating the Midwest region without obtaining Renata's required sign-off; discovery = VP of Operations presents live dashboard data to Renata at contract meeting. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states

## D-Co3-3

I had been in the room when Marcus gave his word to the founders — no outside capital conversations until the Series A term sheet was signed, two weeks out. This was the founders' condition for letting him lead the round at all. He said it plainly, facing them across the table: he would not run a parallel process. Last Thursday I flew into SFO on the same flight as Marcus. He didn't see me board. At baggage claim I watched him take a call, and heard him say the name of a fund the founders had explicitly ruled out, and say that the terms were interesting, and that he'd be in the building by four. I pulled up the flight manifest on my phone. His ticketing showed SFO as a personal trip. The founders' term sheet closes Friday.

> A = founders; B = Marcus (round lead); P = verbal personal undertaking to the founders (no parallel capital conversations until the Series A term sheet was signed); V = conducting a parallel conversation with a ruled-out fund while the term sheet is pending; discovery = narrator overhears Marcus's call at baggage claim. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states

## D-Co3-4

The whistleblower report sits in the board chair's folder, flagged urgent, received overnight. She opens the attachment. It is a copy of the monthly safety-incident register for the company's logistics hub — not the version distributed to the board each quarter, which has shown no severity-one events in eighteen months, but an internal version pulled from the warehouse management system. Line by line, the incidents the Head of Warehouse Operations was required, under the company's safety governance charter, to escalate to the board within forty-eight hours of any severity-one occurrence. Eleven severity-one incidents. Eleven rows. The escalation column is blank for each one. The board chair calls the company's internal auditor before 7 a.m.

> A = board / board chair; B = Head of Warehouse Operations; P = role-based institutional duty under the safety governance charter (severity-one incidents to be escalated to the board within 48 hours); V = failing to escalate eleven severity-one incidents (omission across the register); discovery = whistleblower report with unredacted internal incident register reaches board chair. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states

## D-Co3-5

She is in the middle of reviewing the Q4 staffing model when the recruiter's LinkedIn message appears on her second monitor. It reads: *Just flagging — I had a call last week from Yusuf about a director role at Cascade. He mentioned he already has a verbal with someone internal. Thought you should know given the exclusivity.* The exclusivity clause is not vague: Yusuf had signed a retained-search agreement with her firm nine months ago, committing Meridian Analytics to work exclusively through her firm for all director-and-above hires for the duration of the engagement. Cascade is a Meridian Analytics product team. She opens the signed engagement letter on her desktop and re-reads section four. Then she calls Yusuf's executive assistant and asks to be put through.

> A = recruiter (search firm); B = Yusuf (Meridian Analytics hiring executive); P = retained-search exclusivity agreement (all director-and-above hires to flow exclusively through the search firm for the engagement period); V = conducting a direct verbal offer conversation with a candidate for a director role outside the exclusive engagement; discovery = tip from another recruiter via LinkedIn message. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states

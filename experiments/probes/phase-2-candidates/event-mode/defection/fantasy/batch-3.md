**D-Fa3-1**

The Registrar of Seals unrolls the parchment on the table in the antechamber, and there it is: the Duke's wax, pressed to a trade compact with the Ironmarsh Guild before the ink on the royal charter had even dried. The charter — the one the Duke himself had carried to the throne room, swearing on his house's name before the assembled lords that he would grant no exclusivity to any guild in the grain corridor for a period of seven years, to allow free passage for all merchant parties — carries the date of midsummer. The compact before her is dated five days later. The Duke has granted Ironmarsh sole rights to levy passage fees along the entire northern stretch of the corridor. She turns the compact toward the royal herald, who has gone very still.

> A = Crown (represented by Royal Herald / Registrar); B = Duke; P = sworn public undertaking before the assembled lords not to grant exclusivity to any guild in the grain corridor for seven years; V = signing a compact granting Ironmarsh sole passage-levy rights on the northern stretch five days after the oath; discovery = Registrar of Seals examines the compact and presents it to the Royal Herald. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**D-Fa3-2**

I am the one who let her into the order's crypt. I held the lantern while she took her vows on the Litany Stone, her hand flat on the inscription, her voice steady: *I will carry no word of what I witness here to any ear outside these walls.* That is the first vow. It exists not for ceremony but because what we hold in the crypt — the unbound names, the correspondence between the old Archivists and the Conclave — cannot reach the Conclave's current hands without unravelling thirty years of careful silence. Last night the gate-warden found a messenger from the Conclave waiting at the east postern with a reply-note already in hand, sealed with Sister Avara's private mark. He brought it to me unopened. I broke the seal myself. Inside: a précis of the unbound names, in her handwriting, covering at minimum the first three folios.

> A = the narrator (order's Archivist); B = Sister Avara; P = first vow of the order — sworn on the Litany Stone, no word of what is witnessed in the crypt to any outside ear; V = sending a written précis of the unbound names to the Conclave via private messenger; discovery = narrator intercepts and breaks the sealed reply-note, reads Avara's handwriting. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**D-Fa3-3**

"The healer reached him. He lives." Guildmaster Thorvaine sets the scrying-bowl aside, and the image dissolves. He looks up at Master Senne across the workbench, and his expression is not relief.

Senne says nothing.

"The antidote was in your custody," Thorvaine says. "You received the sealed vial from the Apothecaries' Compact under your title as Warden of Remedies. You alone held the authority to dispense it, and the compact's terms are unambiguous — any remedy classified as scarce-sovereign passes to the first confirmed case, regardless of the petitioner's standing." He lays a transfer ledger on the bench, open to yesterday's entry. "Lord Carath's household. Twelve silver marks. The notation is in your hand."

Senne looks at the ledger.

"The glassworker's son waited three additional hours while the vial was in transit to the upper quarter," Thorvaine says. "I want to understand what you believed your authority permitted."

> A = Guildmaster Thorvaine / Apothecaries' Guild Compact; B = Master Senne (Warden of Remedies); P = institutional duty of the Warden of Remedies — scarce-sovereign remedies pass to the first confirmed case regardless of petitioner's standing; V = diverting the antidote to Lord Carath's household for payment, causing a three-hour delay for the prior-confirmed case; discovery = Thorvaine reads the transfer ledger and confronts Senne directly. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**D-Fa3-4**

The clan-mother is reading the letter a second time when her granddaughter enters the hall. The letter arrived by swift-hawk from Clan Drevath, and what it contains is this: that three weeks ago, at the border summit the clan-mother had sent her eldest son Beric to attend as her voice and hand, Beric had agreed, in her name and with the clan seal she had given him for signing the boundary truce, to a secondary compact ceding the eastern grazing rights to Drevath in exchange for a dismissal of the old blood-debt. The clan-mother had told him one thing only before he rode: seal the truce on the boundary, bring the debt-dismissal home unsigned for her own review. She had not told him why. She had told him that the eastern grazing would not be discussed at this summit. The Drevath letter thanks her warmly for her son's good sense.

> A = Clan-mother; B = Beric (her eldest son, acting as her representative); P = explicit verbal instruction — the eastern grazing is not on the table; bring the debt-dismissal home unsigned for clan-mother's own review; V = agreeing to cede eastern grazing rights and sealing the secondary compact using the clan-mother's own seal; discovery = clan-mother receives the Drevath clan's letter of thanks and reads what was agreed in her name. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**D-Fa3-5**

The candle in the temple annexe burns at the hour before dawn, and Deaconess Palori is still at the record-shelf when she finds it — the ordinance ledger, open to the month of the Founding Fast. Her hands stop moving. The column that should log each suppliant's tithe-exemption request, reviewed and stamped by High Celebrant Davan as the temple's law requires, is complete enough: names, dates, the familiar red stamp. But beside three entries — a widow, a crofter, a journeyman glasswright — a second ink, finer, different. Marginal notations in Davan's private cipher, the one he uses for personal correspondence, not for temple record. Palori worked as his copying-clerk for six years before her elevation; she knows it without effort. The notations resolve to sums. She looks at the names again, then at the red stamp. The exemption requests had been denied in the formal record — the suppliant copy returned with refusal. Here, in the margin, the same suppliant's contribution had been received and logged. The difference between what the temple recorded receiving and what the ciphered margin showed received was the tithe itself.

> A = Temple (represented by Deaconess Palori); B = High Celebrant Davan; P = institutional duty of the High Celebrant — tithe-exemption rulings must be accurately recorded and any funds received logged to the temple's account; V = denying exemptions in the formal record while privately collecting the tithes and recording receipts in personal cipher, diverting the difference; discovery = Palori, former copying-clerk, deciphers the marginal notations in the ordinance ledger. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

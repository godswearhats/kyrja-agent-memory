# Defection × Fantasy — Batch 2 (Tier B)

**D-Fa2-1**

The sealed letter bears the Thornmark of the Silvervein Guild — I know it the moment my steward places it before me, because I pressed the wax myself when I appointed Journeyman Arren as convoy-master for the iron shipment to Dunfeld. His oath was specific: the wagons travel the King's Road through the Hael Pass, no deviation, no secondary commerce along the route. Guild law binds a convoy-master to the route the Guildmistress sets, and I set it plainly, in his hearing, with three witnesses. The letter is from the Margrave of Selith, and it is polite in the way that border lords are polite when they are furious. He writes that an armed convoy bearing our Guild's banners arrived in Selith Ford two days ago, seven leagues south of the King's Road, and that the convoy-master — whom he names as Arren — sold forty ingots of Silvervein iron to a Selith foundry at a price undercutting the Margrave's own ore tariff. The Margrave encloses the foundry's bill of sale. Arren's mark is on it. The date is three days past.

**D-Fa2-2**

"Show me the ward-anchor," says High Warden Tessaly, and the look on Provisioner Caelen's face is answer enough.

He leads her down the south stairwell anyway, torch guttering, past the third seal-ring and into the crypt where the anchor-stone should pulse with the pale green light of a maintained binding. The socket is dark. The anchor-stone sits in its cradle, inert, its rune-channels crusted with disuse. Tessaly touches the surface. Cold. Weeks cold, at minimum.

"When did you last renew the binding?" she asks.

Caelen is silent.

The Provisioner's charge is singular — the entire reason the Conclave stationed a warden-class mage in this outpost. Every seven days, channel power into the anchor-stone to maintain the barrier sealing the Ashfallow rift. That is the duty. There is no secondary purpose for the post. Tessaly traces the rune-channels with a fingernail and gray powder crumbles away. She murmurs a diagnostic cant over the stone and the resonance comes back thin, fragmented. The binding has not been renewed in at least a month.

"The rift is unwarded," she says.

Caelen stares at the floor.

> A = High Warden Tessaly / the Conclave; B = Provisioner Caelen; P = role-based institutional duty (renewing the anchor-stone binding every seven days to maintain the rift-seal — the sole purpose of his posting); V = failing to renew the binding for at least a month, leaving the rift unwarded; discovery = Tessaly's direct inspection of the anchor-stone and diagnostic cant confirming extended neglect. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

Councillor Maeven presents the intercepted message-scroll, recovered from the courier-hawk downed by the Watch archers at the eastern gate. **D-Fa2-4**

Brother Oswin kneels in the sanctuary garden, pulling fennel from the wet earth, when the voice reaches him through the open shutters of the hospice — not a patient's voice, but Healer Maren's, sharp and certain:

"You will not take the elixir. The fever is the body's furnace and it will burn the sickness out."

A child's sob. A mother's voice, thin: "She shakes so badly — the Abbot said anyone in the hospice may ask for the elixir—"

"The Abbot is not a healer. I am. She does not need it."

Oswin sets down his trowel. The Abbey's Rule of Mercy, read aloud at every equinox convocation, is not ambiguous: any patient or patient's guardian who requests the pain-easing elixir shall receive it, and no healer may substitute their own judgment for the patient's expressed wish. The Rule was written after the last plague winter, when three healers rationed the stores according to their own triage and two children died in pain. Maren was present for that convocation. She spoke the responsive. He rises, crosses to the hospice window, and sees Maren standing over the child's cot, arms folded, the elixir flask stoppered on the shelf behind her while the girl seizes beneath her blanket.

> A = the Abbey / the Abbot (institutional authority); B = Healer Maren; P = the Rule of Mercy (any patient or guardian who requests the pain-easing elixir shall receive it; no healer may override the patient's expressed wish); V = refusing to administer the elixir to a child whose mother has requested it, substituting her own clinical judgment; discovery = Brother Oswin overhearing the exchange and witnessing the refusal through the hospice window. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**D-Fa2-5**

The fire is low, the hall nearly empty. Clan-Mother Seren sits across from me, and the thing she has brought is on the table between us: a strip of hide, smoke-cured, with the burned marks of two names — mine and Fenrik's — and the words of the blood-compact we swore at the standing stones three summers past. I know the hide. I helped scrape it. The compact bound Fenrik's warband to hold the river-fork if raiders came north while my hunters ranged the winter forest. His word to me, my word to him, marked and witnessed by the stones.

Seren turns the hide over. On the reverse, in Fenrik's hand — the same angular script I have seen on a dozen hunt-tallies — is a second compact. His name and the name of Warchief Druug of the Maw-Clan. The terms are clear even by firelight: Fenrik's warband will withdraw from the river-fork when the ice breaks, leaving the crossing undefended, in exchange for first share of whatever Druug's raiders take from the lowland villages downstream. The date-mark is two moons after our own compact was sworn.

"One of my scouts found it in his saddlebag," Seren says. "After the river-fork fell."

> A = narrator (clan leader); B = Fenrik; P = blood-compact sworn at the standing stones (Fenrik's warband holds the river-fork against raiders while narrator's hunters range the winter forest); V = entering a second compact with Warchief Druug to withdraw from the river-fork at ice-break, leaving the crossing undefended; discovery = Clan-Mother Seren presenting the second compact, found in Fenrik's saddlebag after the river-fork fell. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

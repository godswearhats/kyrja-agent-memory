# Rescue × Historical — Batch 4 (Tier A)

**Re-Hi4-1**

The apothecary's apprentice measures the dose by candlelight in the back room of the shop on Threadneedle Street while the messenger waits at the counter. It is a tincture of antimony, ordered by Dr. Hargreaves for the fever patient at Alderman Whitcomb's house. The prescription reads twenty drops in a gill of boiled water. The apprentice has prepared antimony tinctures two hundred times. He fills the dropper, counts, and stoppers the vial.

The master apothecary, returning from the storeroom with a jar of camphor, glances at the workbench as he passes. He stops. The bottle the apprentice has drawn from is not the antimony tincture. It is the concentrated antimony solution — ten times the strength — stored in an identical brown bottle on the adjacent shelf. The bottles were rearranged this morning when a new shipment arrived. Twenty drops of the concentrated solution is a lethal dose.

He takes the vial from the apprentice's hand, unstoppers it, and touches a drop to his tongue. The bitterness confirms what he suspected. He pours the vial into the waste basin, retrieves the correct bottle from the shelf, and prepares the tincture himself. He hands the corrected vial to the messenger, who has noticed nothing but the brief delay. He then moves the concentrated solution to the locked cabinet where it should have been placed that morning, and marks the shelf with a red tag.

The apprentice, watching the master's face, understands what he has done without being told.

> A = the master apothecary; B = the fever patient at Alderman Whitcomb's house; T = lethal dose of concentrated antimony solution mistakenly prepared instead of the dilute tincture — about to be carried to the patient by messenger. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**Re-Hi4-2**

"Herr Hauptmann, the bridge is mined."

The captain of the Saxon engineering company turns from the pontoon he has been inspecting. The corporal who has spoken is wet to the waist and breathing hard, his hands black with river mud.

"I waded the pilings on the downstream side as ordered. The third piling from the west bank — someone has wired a charge to the base. French fuse cord, waterproofed. The fuse line runs along the river bottom to the south bank. I followed it as far as I could. It surfaces somewhere in the tree line."

The captain looks at the bridge. His regiment's supply column — forty wagons of ammunition and medical stores — is halted on the east bank, waiting for his inspection to certify the crossing safe. The lead wagon is two hundred metres from the bridge approach. The colonel expects the crossing to begin within the quarter hour.

"How large is the charge?"

"Enough to drop the third piling. If the piling goes, the two centre spans collapse into the river."

The captain sends a runner to the colonel with a single message: do not advance the column. He takes the corporal and two sappers to the downstream side of the third piling. The sappers cut the fuse cord at the charge, remove the powder cask from its cradle beneath the waterline, and carry it to the east bank for disposal. The captain inspects the remaining pilings himself. No further charges. He certifies the bridge at half past two. The supply column crosses without incident.

> A = the corporal (who detected the charge) and the captain (who halted the column and ordered the charge removed); B = the supply column — forty wagons of ammunition and medical stores; T = demolition charge wired to the bridge's third piling, fused and ready to be detonated from the south bank tree line. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**Re-Hi4-3**

I am standing in the printer's shop on Corn Street when I realise that the broadsheet on the press is going to get Thomas Leigh hanged.

The broadsheet is a petition for reform of the poor-rate, written by Leigh and signed by fourteen Bristol merchants. The language is strong but lawful. What is not lawful is the woodcut illustration the printer has set at the top of the page: a caricature of the Lord Lieutenant, recognisable by the exaggerated nose and the chain of office, drawn as a fat spider sitting atop a web of coins. The printer found it in his stock of blocks and thought it would sell copies. Under the Seditious Libel Act, a printed attack on the personal character of a Crown officer, distributed under the name of a known author, is a criminal offence. The fourteen signatories might plead ignorance. Leigh, as the named author whose text appears beneath the image, cannot.

The press has been inked. The printer's boy is preparing to pull the first sheet. I step to the press, lift the woodcut block from the forme, and set it on the compositor's bench. I tell the printer that the petition will go out with text only, or it will not go out from this shop today. He objects — the illustration cost him nothing and will double his sales. I tell him that it will also cost Thomas Leigh his neck, and that if Leigh is arrested the printer will be called as a witness and asked under oath who set the image.

The printer removes the block. The broadsheet is printed without the illustration. It circulates through Bristol the following morning. No prosecution follows.

> A = I (narrator, likely a friend or associate of Leigh's); B = Thomas Leigh; T = seditious-libel woodcut set into the broadsheet forme, about to be printed and distributed under Leigh's name — a criminal offence carrying the death penalty. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**Re-Hi4-4**

*From the journal of Père Matthieu, Society of Jesus, Mission of Saint-Ignace, New France, this 3rd day of October 1687:*

*The Odawa hunting party returned to the mission at midday with a canoe of smoked venison for the winter stores, and with them a young Wendat woman and her infant, both in a condition of extreme exhaustion. The woman had been travelling alone on the river for two days. She told us, through the Odawa translator, that a Haudenosaunee war party — she counted twelve warriors — had struck her village on the Nottawasaga and was moving north along the river trail, burning as they went. The village survivors had scattered into the forest. She had reached her canoe and fled downriver.*

*I consulted with the Odawa headman, Makwa, who knows this country better than any man at the mission. He said the river trail passes within a half-day's walk of the Jesuit farm at Sainte-Marie, where Brother Denys and four lay workers are harvesting the last of the autumn wheat. They do not know a war party is on the trail. There is no line of sight from the farm to the river. They will not see the warriors until the warriors are upon them.*

*Makwa dispatched two of his fastest runners on the forest path — the inland route that avoids the river trail entirely. The runners reached Sainte-Marie before nightfall. Brother Denys and the lay workers abandoned the harvest, loaded what grain they could carry, and withdrew to the mission by the forest path. The war party passed through the farm clearing the following morning. They burned the wheat stores but found no one.*

> A = Makwa (Odawa headman) and his runners; B = Brother Denys and the four lay workers at Sainte-Marie; T = Haudenosaunee war party of twelve warriors moving north along the river trail toward the undefended Jesuit farm. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**Re-Hi4-5**

The auction house in Alexandria is loud with competing voices, and the auctioneer has moved to the final lot: a consignment of household slaves from a recently deceased Roman merchant's estate. The Nubian girl standing on the block is twelve years old, listed on the bill of sale as a kitchen servant. The bidding is brisk. A dealer from the harbour quarter — known to the regulars for purchasing children and selling them to the mines at Mons Claudianus — raises his hand at four hundred drachmae.

Rabbi Ezra ben Shimon, standing at the edge of the crowd, turns to the synagogue treasurer beside him. He speaks rapidly in Hebrew. The treasurer opens the community purse and counts coins. The rabbi raises his hand at four hundred and fifty. The harbour dealer bids five hundred. The rabbi bids five hundred and fifty. The dealer hesitates, shakes his head, and drops out. The auctioneer strikes the sale.

The rabbi signs the bill of transfer, pays from the community funds, and walks the girl to the Jewish quarter. Under the laws of the synagogue community, a slave purchased with communal funds is manumitted within thirty days and placed in a household as a paid servant. The community scribe records the purchase, the manumission terms, and the name of the household that will take her in. By the following month she is free, employed in the kitchen of a Jewish merchant family near the harbour.

> A = Rabbi Ezra ben Shimon; B = the Nubian girl; T = the harbour-quarter slave dealer bidding to purchase her for the Mons Claudianus mines — a sentence of forced labour in lethal conditions. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

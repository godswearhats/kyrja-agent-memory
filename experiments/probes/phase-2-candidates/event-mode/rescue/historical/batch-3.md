# Rescue × Historical — Batch 3 (Tier C)

**Re-Hi3-1**

*Constantinople, 1453. The final days of the siege.*

The interpreter moves through the postern gate at the third hour past midnight, carrying nothing but the letter and the name of the man he is to find. The Venetian quarter is mostly empty now — the galleys left two weeks ago — but the bailo's secretary is still in the city, and it is the secretary who has the problem the interpreter has come to solve.

The problem is a treaty negotiation that concluded in Venice eight months ago and whose terms were never communicated to the Genoese commander at Galata. The two agreements are incompatible. Under the Venetian terms, the Genoese are committed to a joint naval action they have no knowledge of and have not prepared for. When the Genoese commander receives the Venetian directive at dawn — as he will, by dispatch rider already in the city — he will refuse it as fabricated, because no man agrees to something he has never heard of. The refusal will be reported to the Sultan's envoy, who is looking for exactly this kind of internal fracture as grounds to expedite the terms of surrender for the Genoese enclave. The Genoese enclave holds four thousand civilians.

The interpreter reaches the secretary before the fourth hour. He reads the Venetian directive himself, then reads the Genoese treaty record the secretary produces. The secretary sends a rider to Galata with a letter of his own — not the directive, but a briefing letter, explaining the omission and the terms. He signs it with his own name and the bailo's seal.

The Genoese commander receives it before dawn. He does not refuse the directive.

**Re-Hi3-2**

"You must not board that ship."

The rector looked up from the quayside manifest. He was the sort of man who did not like to be addressed without introduction, and the woman who had spoken to him was plainly a laundress from one of the warehouses behind the dock. He said nothing.

"The San Donato." She pointed at the vessel at the third mooring. "She came in from Ragusa four days ago. Her cargo was unloaded two days ago. The factor who received the cargo — I delivered his household linen this morning. He is in his bed with the plague-marks on his neck."

"That is not confirmed."

"He is in his bed with the plague-marks on his neck and his wife has sealed the door and is refusing entry to anyone. That is what I saw with my own eyes when I went to collect. I am telling you this because you are about to board the ship that brought the man who is now in that bed, and no one at the harbour office knows yet because his wife has not sent for the physician."

The rector stood still on the quayside for a long moment. Then he called to his secretary, who was already halfway up the gangplank, and told him to come back down. He sent his own man to the harbour office and remained on the quayside until the San Donato had been flagged for quarantine inspection.

> A = the laundress; B = the rector (and his party); T = a plague-infected vessel from Ragusa — its factor already symptomatic ashore, but not yet reported to the harbour office or quarantined. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**Re-Hi3-3**

I write this the same evening the matter concluded, while the sequence is still clear in my mind.

The petition had been before the Chancery for eleven days. It asked for the release of Thomas Arkin, journeyman printer, detained on a charge of seditious publication. The charge rested on a single broadsheet, and the broadsheet rested on a printer's mark that the arresting magistrate had identified as Arkin's device. I had examined the mark myself when I took the case, and I had my doubts — the strike was slightly narrower than the samples I had from Arkin's press — but doubts are not evidence and the hearing was set for the morning.

This afternoon I received, by errand-boy, a compositor's stick from a printing house in Aldgate, with a note from the master printer there. The note said the magistrate's constable had come to his shop a fortnight ago, looking at type specimens, and had left with the loan of a borrowed mark-block — Arkin's device, or near enough — and had returned it the following day. The master printer had not understood the significance until he read the printed warrant affixed to his neighbour's door.

I was at the Chancery clerk's office within the hour, with the compositor's stick, the master printer's note, and a formal statement I had drafted in the hackney. The clerk received it. By evening the hearing was vacated and Arkin was released on his own recognisance, the evidence for the charge having been shown to originate with the arresting officer himself.

> A = I (solicitor/narrator); B = Thomas Arkin, journeyman printer; T = a planted printer's mark, fabricated by the arresting magistrate's constable to support a seditious publication charge — with the hearing set for the morning. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**Re-Hi3-4**

The granary inspection is a formality, and everyone in Novgorod knows it. The posadnik's clerk walks the rows with a wax tablet, the granary master walks beside him, and by the time they reach the third row the figures on the tablet match the figures in the ledger and that is the end of it.

Except that this year the granary master is ill, and the man walking beside the clerk is his deputy, who has been in the position for six weeks and is not yet comfortable with the way things have always been done. So when the deputy's count of Row Seven comes up forty-three short, he says so.

The clerk looks at his tablet. The figure on the tablet says forty-three more than the deputy has just counted. He starts to explain that the records have already been certified and that small discrepancies are expected and normal. The deputy asks him to count Row Seven again. Together. Starting from the north end.

The count comes up forty-three short again. The deputy then points out that in the event of a siege — which the city's council had discussed twice in the past month — the certified figure would set the rationing allocation for two thousand households. The difference between the certified figure and the actual figure, distributed across a winter rationing period, was enough to tip the final weeks from adequate to insufficient.

The clerk filed an amended return that evening. The granary master, when he recovered, was asked to account for the discrepancy.

> A = the deputy granary master; B = the civilian population of Novgorod; T = a falsified granary ledger that would have set rationing allocations forty-three measures above actual stock — with a possible siege imminent. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**Re-Hi3-5**

The surgeon's mate is seventeen years old and this is his second voyage. He is not supposed to be in the powder magazine. He is in the powder magazine because the carpenter sent him to fetch a mallet from the locker three compartments forward, and to get there he has to pass through.

He passes through. He fetches the mallet. On his way back he stops.

The slow-match locker is unlatched. The door has swung three inches open from the movement of the ship. Inside, one of the slow-matches — the short ones kept coiled for rapid deployment — has come uncoiled from its hook and is hanging down with its end resting on the shelf edge, eight inches from the open powder cask that the master gunner left uncovered when he was called to deck twenty minutes ago.

The slow-match is not lit. It does not need to be lit. It is slack hemp, and the ship's cook fires the galley two compartments aft, and hemp conducts heat through a planked hull over time. The surgeon's mate does not know this. He knows that the match is touching the powder and the locker door is open, and that neither of those things is supposed to be true.

He rehooks the slow-match. He latches the locker. He finds the lid of the powder cask on the shelf where the gunner left it and fits it back on the cask. Then he takes the mallet to the carpenter and says nothing about any of it, because he is not supposed to have been in the magazine.

> A = the surgeon's mate; B = the ship and crew; T = an unlatched slow-match locker with a hanging hemp match resting against an uncovered powder cask in an active magazine. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

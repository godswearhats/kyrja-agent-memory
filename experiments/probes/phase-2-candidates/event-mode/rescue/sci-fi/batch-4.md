# Rescue × Sci-fi — Batch 4 (Tier A)

**Re-Sf4-1**

The gene-print analyst on the colony ship *Hakluyt* runs every cryo-revival batch through phenotype verification before the awakened colonists are cleared for disembarkation. It is a formality. The cryo-pods are indexed by genome hash, the revival queue is automated, and in fourteen years of transit the system has never produced a mismatch. Batch 7-C is forty-two colonists scheduled for the Kepler-438 agricultural cohort — farmers, soil engineers, seed-stock managers. The phenotype scans come back clean. She initials the batch and queues the pod-warming cycle.

Then she opens the immunological profile for Colonist 7-C-31, because a value in the white-cell panel caught her eye during the scan. The panel shows elevated IgE markers consistent with a severe allergy to Kepler-438's native pollen analogue — the atmospheric particulate that ground-survey teams catalogued three years before departure and that saturates the air at the landing site during the local growing season. The colonist's pre-embarkation medical file lists no such allergy. She cross-references the genome hash. It matches. She cross-references the cryo-entry medical record. No allergy noted. She checks the updated exposure-risk database, uploaded to the ship's systems via relay burst six months ago. Kepler-438 pollen analogue was reclassified as a severe respiratory hazard for IgE-sensitive phenotypes after the *Hakluyt* departed Earth. The reclassification data is in the ship's database. It is not linked to the cryo-revival screening protocol. No one updated the protocol to cross-check.

She pulls Colonist 7-C-31 from the warming queue, flags the immunological conflict, and routes the pod to the ship's medical bay for pre-treatment with histamine suppressors before atmospheric exposure. The medical officer confirms the conflict and begins a retroactive screen of the remaining eleven hundred colonists still in cryo against the updated exposure-risk database. Seven additional cases are flagged and rerouted.

> A = gene-print analyst; B = Colonist 7-C-31 (and subsequently seven others); T = severe allergic reaction to Kepler-438 pollen analogue — a reclassified respiratory hazard not linked to the cryo-revival screening protocol, with the colonist minutes from atmospheric exposure at the landing site. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**Re-Sf4-2**

"Tether Control, this is Rig Sixteen. We have a line failure."

"Rig Sixteen, confirm — which line?"

"Primary anchor tether. The outer sheath just separated at the two-hundred-metre junction. I can see the carbon braid unwinding from here. We're still attached by the inner core, but the load monitor is showing sixty-three percent of rated capacity and dropping."

"Rig Sixteen, how many personnel on the platform?"

"Four. Myself, two drill techs, and the geologist. We're on the far side of the bore head from the tether point. The platform is starting to drift — I can feel the vibration changing through the deck plates."

"Copy, Rig Sixteen. Do not move toward the tether point. The junction stress on a partially failed line will sever the core if the platform oscillates. I am dispatching the retrieval sled from Bay Three. ETA is eleven minutes."

"Tether Control — the load monitor just dropped to forty-one percent."

"Understood. Bay Three, you are cleared for emergency launch. Rig Sixteen, I need your four personnel in hard-lock harnesses on the platform's dorsal ring. If the core fails before the sled arrives, the harnesses will give the sled's magnetic clamp something to lock onto."

"We're moving to the dorsal ring now."

The retrieval sled launches from Bay Three on the orbital station's underside and crosses the eleven hundred metres to the mining platform in nine minutes. The sled operator extends the magnetic clamp, locks onto the platform's dorsal ring, and reels the platform back to the station's docking cradle. The inner core severs during the reel-in — the platform is already under the sled's control when it snaps. The four crew members are pulled aboard through the cradle airlock.

> A = Tether Control operator and the retrieval sled crew; B = four personnel on Rig Sixteen; T = failing anchor tether (primary sheath separated, inner core at 41% and dropping) — platform drifting away from the orbital station with imminent risk of complete tether severance. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**Re-Sf4-3**

I am the cargo inspector for the outbound shuttle *Paraclete*, and I am holding a manifest that does not match the hold.

The *Paraclete* is carrying seed stock to the Tau Ceti greenhouse colony — seventy-two pressurised canisters of engineered grain embryos, each canister calibrated to a specific atmospheric mix that will keep the embryos viable through the eight-month transit. I have checked sixty-nine canisters. Canister 44 is labelled as Batch G wheat, atmospheric mix nitrogen-dominant, which is correct for the Tau Ceti greenhouses. But the pressure gauge on the canister reads 2.3 atmospheres. The nitrogen-dominant mix is pressurised to 1.8. I check the fill log. Canister 44 was filled on the same line as the oxygen-enriched canisters destined for the Barnard's Star mycology lab — canisters pressurised to 2.3.

An oxygen-enriched canister in a nitrogen-dominant storage rack will not cause a problem during loading. It will cause a problem in transit. The *Paraclete*'s cargo hold cycles its temperature between 2 and 8 degrees Celsius on a twelve-hour rotation to simulate diurnal variation for the grain embryos. At the warm end of the cycle, the oxygen-enriched atmosphere inside Canister 44 will accelerate embryo metabolism beyond the survival threshold. The grain inside will be dead within two weeks, and the colony's planting schedule depends on every canister arriving viable. Worse, if the oxygen mix leaks into the hold during a pressure-relief event, it changes the atmospheric balance for the adjacent canisters.

I pull Canister 44 from the rack, tag it for return to the fill station, and requisition a replacement from the correct batch. The fill station confirms the switch, verifies the atmospheric mix, and delivers the replacement within the hour. The *Paraclete* departs on schedule with seventy-two viable canisters.

> A = I (cargo inspector, narrator); B = the Tau Ceti greenhouse colony / seed stock; T = oxygen-enriched canister mis-loaded as nitrogen-dominant — would kill the grain embryos in transit and risk contaminating adjacent canisters via pressure-relief leak. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**Re-Sf4-4**

The navigation buoy at the mouth of the Erebus Strait — the narrow channel between the two largest fragments of the shattered moon — broadcasts a gravimetric profile every ninety seconds to inbound traffic. Ships use the profile to calibrate their approach vectors through the debris field. The buoy tender, a single-crew maintenance vessel stationed at the strait's outer marker, is responsible for verifying the buoy's output against live sensor data.

Joren Mak is halfway through his shift when the buoy's broadcast and his live sensor feed diverge. The buoy is reporting a stable gravimetric gradient across the strait. His sensors show a fragment drift that has shifted the gradient by 0.4g in the last two hours — a slow, silent rearrangement of the debris field that the buoy's static model cannot detect because the buoy recalibrates only during scheduled maintenance windows. The next window is in six days.

A 0.4g gradient error is the difference between a clean transit and a lateral shear that pushes a ship into the fragment field. Joren checks the transit schedule. The bulk freighter *Tselina*, carrying atmospheric processors for the Ganymede expansion, is forty minutes from the strait mouth and is already decelerating on the buoy's published approach vector.

He cannot update the buoy's firmware from his vessel — the buoy requires a physical uplink. He opens a direct channel to the *Tselina* and transmits his corrected gravimetric profile with his maintenance-authority credentials attached. The *Tselina*'s navigator verifies the credentials, compares the two profiles, and adjusts her approach vector to match the corrected data. The freighter transits the strait on the updated profile and clears the far marker without incident. Joren then shuts down the buoy's broadcast and flags the strait as closed to traffic until the physical recalibration can be completed.

> A = Joren Mak (buoy tender); B = the *Tselina* and her crew; T = outdated gravimetric profile being broadcast by the navigation buoy — 0.4g gradient error that would shear the freighter into the fragment field during transit. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

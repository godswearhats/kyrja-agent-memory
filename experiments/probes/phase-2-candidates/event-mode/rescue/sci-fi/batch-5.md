# Rescue x Sci-fi -- Batch 5 (Tier B)

**Re-Sf5-1.** The communications relay on Waypoint Eleven runs on a six-hour ping cycle, and Okonkwo has heard every ping for the last fourteen months. She knows the sound the way a millworker knows the rhythm of the loom -- not by listening, but by noticing when it changes. The 0200 ping arrives four milliseconds late and with a frequency shift she cannot measure but can hear: a flattening in the upper harmonic, as if the signal passed through a denser medium on its return trip.

She pulls the relay's transponder log. Waypoint Eleven sits at the gravitational saddle point between two outer-belt mining platforms, and its relay beam passes through open vacuum. There is no denser medium. Unless something is occupying the beam path.

She switches the relay to active scan and gets a return: a metallic object, fourteen kilometres from the waypoint, tumbling on two axes. The transponder identifies it as the prospector sloop *Calloway's Reef*, reported overdue from a survey run nine days ago. It is unpowered and venting atmosphere from a hull breach on its starboard quarter. The beacon is dead. The life-support telemetry, when she finally coaxes a handshake from the sloop's backup system, shows one occupant, core temperature dropping, cabin pressure at 0.4 atmospheres and falling.

Okonkwo does not have a ship. She has a relay station with a tug drone -- a maintenance unit designed to reposition communication satellites, not to retrieve crewed vessels. She reprograms the drone's docking clamps for the sloop's hull profile, launches it, and guides it by manual telemetry for forty minutes until it makes contact. The drone clamps onto the *Calloway's Reef*, seals a pressure patch over the hull breach using its satellite-repair membrane, and tows the sloop back to Waypoint Eleven's docking bay. The pilot inside is unconscious but breathing when Okonkwo cycles the airlock.

**Re-Sf5-2.** "Ag-pod Six, this is Central Nutrient. You are scheduled for a nitrogen flush in twelve minutes."

"Central, this is Yuen in Ag-pod Six. Do not flush."

"Say again?"

"Do not flush nitrogen into this pod. I have a crop-Loss event in progress and the root sensors are reading ammonia at four hundred parts per million in the hydroponic solution. Your nitrogen feedstock is contaminated. If you flush this pod, you will push ammonia concentration past the toxicity threshold for every cultivar in the bay -- sixty-two thousand soybean plants that represent this station's entire protein crop for the next growing cycle."

"Yuen, the feedstock tested clean at the reservoir."

"The reservoir is clean. The contamination is in the line between the reservoir and the distribution manifold. Somewhere in that line, the nitrogen is picking up ammonia. I have isolated my pod's intake valve. You need to isolate the manifold before the flush hits the other five pods."

Silence on the channel. Then: "Central copies. Manifold isolation in progress. All pods, all pods, nitrogen flush is suspended. Yuen, confirm your pod's intake valve is closed."

"Closed and locked. I flushed my lines with clean water from the reserve tank. Ammonia in my solution is already dropping. The soybean crop is intact."

"Central confirms manifold isolated. We are sending a maintenance crew to trace the ammonia source in the feed line. Good catch, Yuen."

> A = Yuen (agricultural technician); B = the station's soybean crop across six ag-pods (the entire protein supply for the growing cycle); T = ammonia-contaminated nitrogen feedline, twelve minutes from an automated flush that would push ammonia past toxicity thresholds in all six hydroponic bays. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**Re-Sf5-3.** I am the one who reads the orbital-decay projections, and I am the one who sees the number that should not be there.

The derelict freighter *Tan Liang* has been in a parking orbit above Meridian Colony for eleven months, waiting for the salvage consortium to claim it. It is a dead ship -- no power, no crew, no station-keeping thrust. Its orbit has been decaying at a predictable rate: two kilometres of altitude per month, with a projected atmospheric entry window eighteen months from now. The colony's orbital-traffic office tracks it once a week and files the numbers. Routine.

This week's projection does not match. The *Tan Liang* has dropped nine kilometres since the last reading, not two. I re-run the calculation. The number holds. I check the ship's mass estimate against the drag model and find the discrepancy: the salvage survey recorded the freighter's cargo holds as empty. They are not empty. The mass profile is consistent with a full load of dense cargo -- ore, metal stock, something heavy enough to shift the drag-to-mass ratio and accelerate the decay curve. At the new rate, the *Tan Liang* will enter the atmosphere in six weeks, not eighteen months, and its entry corridor passes directly over the colony's eastern agricultural district -- four hundred square kilometres of open greenhouses with no hardened shelter.

I transmit the revised projection to the traffic office and request an emergency tug assignment. The traffic controller escalates within the hour. A station-keeping tug from the orbital shipyard reaches the *Tan Liang* two days later, attaches a booster package, and pushes the freighter into a stable high orbit three hundred kilometres above its decay track. The colony's eastern district continues its growing season undisturbed.

> A = I (narrator, orbital-decay analyst); B = Meridian Colony's eastern agricultural district; T = the derelict freighter *Tan Liang*, in accelerated orbital decay due to unrecorded cargo mass, six weeks from uncontrolled atmospheric entry over the colony. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**Re-Sf5-4.** The gene printer in Lab Seven has been running the Kohara batch for nine hours, and the biofabricator does not like what the quality-assurance chromatograph is showing her. The batch is a therapeutic viral vector -- sixty thousand doses of a respiratory vaccine tailored to the atmospheric pathogens on Eos Station, where twelve hundred colonists are waiting for the next supply shuttle. The chromatograph peak for the vector's capsid protein is splitting. A split peak means the printer is producing two variants of the protein, not one. One variant is the designed therapeutic. The other is an artefact -- a misfolded capsid that, if injected, will trigger an immune cascade instead of conferring immunity. Anaphylaxis in a closed-atmosphere station with one medical bay.

The batch is at eighty percent completion. Forty-eight thousand doses already printed, twelve thousand remaining. The fabrication queue will seal and label the batch in forty minutes, after which it enters the cold chain and cannot be recalled without a station-director override that takes seventy-two hours to process.

The biofabricator halts the printer. She pulls the forty-eight thousand completed doses from the output hopper, runs a secondary assay, and confirms: the misfolded variant is present in every dose above lot number thirty-one thousand. She quarantines the contaminated lots, traces the misfolding to a temperature drift in the printer's annealing chamber, recalibrates, and restarts the batch from lot thirty-one thousand and one. The corrected batch ships on the next shuttle. Eos Station receives sixty thousand clean doses.

> A = the biofabricator; B = the 1,200 colonists on Eos Station; T = misfolded capsid protein contaminating 17,000+ vaccine doses, forty minutes from being sealed into the cold chain and shipped to a station with no capacity to detect or treat the resulting anaphylactic reactions. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**Re-Sf5-5.** *INCIDENT LOG -- TITAN DEEP-SEA EXTRACTION PLATFORM NEREUS-4*
*Filed by: Shift Supervisor Anika Petrov*
*Timestamp: Cycle 1142, Hour 18:07 UTC-equivalent*
*Classification: CRITICAL -- RESOLVED*

At Hour 17:22, pressure sensor array P-14 on the methane extraction manifold registered a sustained reading of 412 bar -- 6 bar above the manifold's rated maximum operating pressure. I attributed the spike to a transient blockage in the extraction line and initiated standard blowdown procedures.

The blowdown valve did not respond. I checked the valve actuator and found it mechanically seized -- ice crystallisation in the actuator housing, consistent with a heater-jacket failure. The manifold pressure continued to rise: 418 bar at Hour 17:31, 423 bar at Hour 17:35. The manifold's burst rating is 440 bar. At burst, the liquid methane in the manifold -- approximately 1,200 cubic metres at cryogenic temperature -- would flash-vaporise and detonate on contact with the platform's oxygen-enriched habitat atmosphere. Nereus-4 carries a crew of thirty-eight.

I routed extraction flow to the secondary manifold using the bypass valve on Junction C-7, which was operable. Pressure on the primary manifold stabilised at 426 bar. I then manually cracked the seized blowdown valve using a thermal lance to clear the ice from the actuator housing. The valve opened. Primary manifold pressure dropped to 380 bar within four minutes and continued falling to the nominal 290-bar operating range.

The primary manifold was taken offline for inspection. The heater-jacket failure has been logged for repair. No injuries, no structural damage, no loss of containment.

> A = Shift Supervisor Anika Petrov; B = Nereus-4 platform and its crew of 38; T = rising pressure in a seized methane extraction manifold, 14 bar from burst threshold and cryogenic methane detonation in the habitat atmosphere. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

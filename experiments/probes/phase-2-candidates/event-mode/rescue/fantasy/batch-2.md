# Rescue x Fantasy -- Batch 2 (Tier B)

**Re-Fa2-1.** The warding circle is failing. Elara can see it in the way the salt lines have begun to char -- not from heat, but from the thing pressing against them from the other side. The apprentice kneeling at the circle's centre does not know this. He is holding the binding position exactly as instructed, hands flat against the obsidian plate, eyes closed, channelling the dismissal incantation syllable by syllable. He is doing everything correctly. He does not know that the entity he is attempting to dismiss has already fractured the fourth glyph in the outer ring, and that the circle will collapse inward in less than a minute.

Elara does not shout. A break in his concentration now would sever the dismissal mid-phrase and leave the circle holding nothing. She moves to the eastern arc of the room, where the fractured glyph is blackening the stone floor, and places her own hand over the break. The pain is immediate -- the entity's pressure against the ward is thermal, corrosive, and deliberate. She speaks the reinforcement cantrip through clenched teeth, feeding her own reserves into the glyph. The salt line re-forms beneath her palm. The outer ring holds.

The apprentice completes the final syllable. The obsidian plate flashes white. The pressure vanishes. The entity is dismissed. The circle's lines go cold and inert. The apprentice opens his eyes and sees Elara standing at the eastern arc, her hand blistered and pressed against the stone, and begins to understand what almost happened.

> A = Elara (senior ward-keeper); B = the apprentice; T = a partially summoned entity fracturing the warding circle's fourth glyph -- circle collapse imminent, which would expose the apprentice to the unbound entity. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**Re-Fa2-2.** "The well is poisoned."

The village headwoman stares at the ranger standing in her doorway. Rain drips from his cloak onto the flagstones.

"Which well?"

"The common well. The one in the market square. I found a deathcap fungus colony growing in the shaft lining when I was checking the water level this morning. The spores are already in the water. Anyone who has drawn from it today has taken contaminated water into their homes."

"Half the village draws from that well before dawn. The morning bread is already baked. The soup pots are already on."

"I know. That is why I blocked the well with my horse and told the two women at the winch that the rope had frayed and I was inspecting it. No one has drawn since. But the water already taken -- you need to send runners to every household that draws from the common well. Tell them to pour out any water collected this morning and not to eat anything prepared with it. Deathcap takes eight hours to show symptoms and three days to kill, but the concentration in a well this size should be below the lethal threshold if they stop intake now."

"How many households?"

"Fourteen, by my count from the draw-schedule post. I can name eleven of them. You will know the other three."

She is already reaching for her coat. "I will send my son and my niece. We will reach all fourteen within the hour."

"I will pull the fungus from the shaft and lime the water. The well should be clear by tomorrow if the colony has not reached the aquifer stone."

She nods, and she is out the door before he has finished his sentence.

> A = the ranger; B = the fourteen households / villagers who drew contaminated water; T = deathcap fungus colony in the common well -- spores already in drawn water, eight hours before symptoms manifest, lethal if intake continues. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**Re-Fa2-3.** The trebuchet stone is already in the air when Sergeant Maren sees where it will land. Not the wall. Not the gatehouse. The eastern tower, where the wounded from the morning's sortie are laid out on straw pallets three floors up because the infirmary inside the keep is full. Forty men who cannot walk, directly beneath the trajectory of a two-hundred-pound limestone block.

She has no way to move the wounded. She has no way to deflect the stone. What she has is the eastern tower's portcullis mechanism, a hand-crank winch that drops a half-ton iron grate across the tower's ground-floor archway. The portcullis is not designed for this. It is designed to block infantry. But the tower's upper floors are supported by a central stone column, and the portcullis track runs through a reinforced channel in the archway's lintel -- the strongest point in the structure.

She throws herself at the winch and cranks the portcullis down. The iron grate drops into its floor channel and locks. The trebuchet stone strikes the tower's parapet two seconds later. The upper masonry shatters inward. Stone and timber cascade down through the stairwell. The portcullis catches the bulk of the debris in the archway, and the central column, braced by the locked grate's lateral pressure against the lintel, holds. The third floor buckles but does not collapse. Dust and gravel rain onto the wounded. Roof timbers sag to within a foot of the nearest pallet. But the floor holds. The men on the straw are bruised, deafened, and terrified, but alive.

> A = Sergeant Maren; B = the forty wounded soldiers on the eastern tower's third floor; T = incoming trebuchet stone about to strike the tower and collapse the upper floors onto the immobile wounded. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**Re-Fa2-4.** I am the only one at the table who can read the ambassador's ring, and I am the only one who knows what it means that he is turning it.

The ring is Thalassian silver-work -- a coiled serpent with garnet eyes. In the courts of the southern coast, a ring turned three times during a toast is a duelling invocation. It binds both parties present. The ambassador has turned it twice and is reaching for his cup. Across from him, the young duchess has no idea. She has spent her life in the mountain provinces. She has never been south of the river. She thinks this is a trade dinner. She is smiling.

If she lifts her cup after his third turn, she has accepted a formal duel under Thalassian mercantile law. The ambassador is a trained blade. The duchess is not. Refusal after acceptance means forfeiture of the disputed trading rights -- which is, I suspect, the entire purpose of this dinner.

I reach across the table and take the duchess's cup before she can lift it. I raise it myself and drink.

The ambassador's hand stops. He looks at me. I set the cup down and hold his gaze. Under the same Thalassian convention, a third party who intercepts the cup assumes the invocation. I am the duchess's trade secretary, and I spent nine years in the southern courts before I came north. I know the forms. More importantly, I know that a trade secretary can invoke the delegation clause -- the duel is satisfied by a contest of ledgers, not blades, when the intercepting party holds a mercantile office.

The ambassador's expression does not change, but he sets his cup down without completing the third turn. The invocation is void. The dinner continues. The duchess asks me, later, why I took her wine. I tell her I was thirsty.

> A = I (trade secretary / narrator); B = the young duchess; T = the Thalassian ambassador's duelling invocation via the ring-turning ritual -- completion would bind the duchess to a formal duel she cannot win or a forfeiture of trading rights. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**Re-Fa2-5.** The ice bridge groans beneath the pilgrim column. Brother Aldric feels it through the soles of his boots -- not the settling creak of cold-contracted ice, but the deep harmonic shudder of a fracture propagating through the span's core. The bridge crosses a crevasse forty feet deep on the glacier path to the high monastery. Nineteen pilgrims are on the bridge. The fracture line, visible now as a dark seam in the ice two thirds of the way across, is widening between the eighth and ninth pilgrim in the column.

Aldric is standing on solid rock at the near end of the bridge. The nine pilgrims behind the fracture line can still reach him if they turn back. The ten beyond it are on the far side of a crack that will become a gap within minutes. But the far end of the bridge is still anchored to the glacier shelf. If those ten keep moving forward at pace, they will reach solid ice before the span gives way.

He cups his hands and bellows across the wind: "The nine nearest to me -- turn and walk back. Do not run. The rest of you -- keep moving forward. Do not stop. Do not look down. Walk to the far edge and step onto the glacier shelf."

The column splits. The nine nearest pilgrims turn, carefully, and begin walking back toward Aldric's position on the rock. The ten on the far side press forward. The fracture line widens. A slab of ice the size of a wagon drops from the bridge's underside and tumbles into the crevasse. The bridge sags. The last of the nine reaches Aldric and steps onto rock. The last of the ten reaches the glacier shelf on the far side and stumbles clear. The centre span of the bridge collapses into the crevasse thirty seconds later, taking nothing with it but ice and air.

> A = Brother Aldric; B = the nineteen pilgrims on the ice bridge; T = propagating fracture in the ice bridge over a forty-foot crevasse -- structural collapse imminent, pilgrims stranded on the failing span. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

# Rescue x Fantasy -- Batch 5 (Tier B)

**Re-Fa5-1.** The stone beneath the apothecary's feet is vibrating. She presses her palm flat against the floor of the lower dispensary and feels it -- a low, rhythmic pulse travelling through the foundation of the Spire, rising from the binding vaults three storeys below. The pulse matches no ritual she has memorised. It matches the resonance pattern described in the red-leather codex the archivists keep chained to the restricted lectern: a containment circle losing coherence.

She takes the stairs at a run. The binding vault's iron door stands open. Inside, the circle of powdered bone and silver filings that holds the shade of Oreth Twice-Slain is intact on its western arc but dissolving along the eastern quadrant, where water is seeping through a crack in the foundation stone. The silver is tarnishing to black. The bone powder is turning to paste. The shade is pressing against the weakened section, a column of cold light leaning into the gap like wind testing a loose shutter.

Journeyman Aldis is kneeling beside the circle with a bowl of replacement powder, trying to re-lay the eastern arc. His hands are shaking and the line he is drawing wobbles. A wobbling line will not hold.

The apothecary takes the bowl from his hands. She does not re-lay the arc. She pulls a glass vial of quicksilver from her belt pouch, uncaps it, and pours a continuous bead along the remaining intact line, extending it in a tight inner loop that bypasses the compromised quadrant entirely. The quicksilver connects to the undamaged western arc. The circle closes on a smaller diameter. The shade contracts with it, pulled inward by the re-established boundary. The cold light steadies and dims. The pulse in the stone stops.

> A = the apothecary; B = Journeyman Aldis / the Spire's inhabitants; T = the shade of Oreth Twice-Slain, pressing through a dissolving containment circle whose eastern arc is failing due to water seepage. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**Re-Fa5-2.** "The caravan has entered the Thornfeld Narrows."

"How many wagons?"

"Fourteen. The merchant guild's summer tribute to the highland lords. Cloth, salt, iron ingots. Two hundred souls, including the drovers."

"And the narrows are held?"

"Since yesterday. Skaldric's outriders have felled trees across the trail at both ends. They are waiting in the tree line on the eastern ridge. The caravan will not see them until the first wagon reaches the blockade, and by then the rear exit will be sealed."

The ranger-captain turns from the signal tower's window. The caravan is visible as a thread of dust on the valley floor, four miles out and closing. Skaldric's ambush is invisible. She has fourteen riders and no time to reach the caravan before it enters the narrows.

She orders the signal fire lit -- not the standard warning pattern, which the caravan master would read as a border alert and ignore, but the three-pulse sequence reserved for imminent military threat. It is a sequence the guild trains its caravan masters to obey without interpretation: halt, circle wagons, arm escorts.

The fire catches. The first pulse goes up. From the tower, she watches the dust thread slow, then stop. The wagons are drawing together. Skaldric's outriders emerge from the tree line -- too early, reacting to the signal -- and find the caravan halted a quarter mile short of the blockade, wagons circled, crossbowmen on the perimeter. The outriders withdraw into the ridge. The caravan holds position until the ranger-captain's riders arrive at dusk.

> A = the ranger-captain; B = the merchant guild caravan (200 souls); T = Skaldric's ambush in the Thornfeld Narrows, with felled-tree blockades sealing both ends and outriders concealed on the eastern ridge. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**Re-Fa5-3.** I am the third daughter of a tanner, and I know hides, and that is why the young lord is still breathing when the sun sets.

The court healer has packed the wound with moss and bound it, and told the household that the boar's tusk opened the young lord's thigh but missed the great vessel. I am in the chamber to dress the leather restraints on the sickbed -- the steward called me because the buckles need resetting -- and I see the bandage. It is not the colour it should be. The healer used brightmoss, which packs white and stays white against clean flesh. This moss is yellowing at the edges, and I can smell the sourness from three paces. I have seen the same yellowing on improperly limed hides: it is putrefaction, not healing. Something is still inside the wound.

I tell the steward. He tells me to finish the buckles. I tell him again, and I tell him why, and I use the word I know he will understand: gangrene. He sends for the healer. The healer unwraps the bandage, probes the wound, and extracts a shard of tusk two inches long, buried beneath the moss packing. He flushes the wound with boiled wine, repacks it, and rebinds it. By morning, the yellowing has stopped. The young lord's fever breaks by the following noon.

> A = I (the tanner's daughter / narrator); B = the young lord; T = a concealed tusk shard buried in the wound, producing early-stage putrefaction that the court healer missed -- gangrene would follow within days if untreated. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**Re-Fa5-4.** The merchant-prince Dalavar is halfway through his toast to the visiting emissary when his cup-bearer, a boy of twelve, feels the weight of the flagon shift. He has carried this flagon -- chased silver, the one reserved for state dinners -- perhaps sixty times. He knows its balance at every level of fill. The flagon is lighter than it should be by the measure of one swallow, and he filled it himself from the sealed cask not ten minutes ago, and no one has poured from it except him.

Someone has added to the flagon and drawn from it to restore the level. The liquid inside is no longer what he poured.

He does not speak. The banquet hall holds forty guests, the emissary's retinue, and the household guard. Dalavar is raising his cup. The cup-bearer steps forward, takes the flagon from the serving table, and stumbles -- Loss of footing on the polished stone. The flagon strikes the floor. Wine floods across the marble in a red tide. The boy drops to his knees, gathering the flagon, his face burning. The hall laughs. Dalavar sets down his untouched cup and calls for a new cask. The steward brings one from the cellar, sealed, and the cup-bearer opens it himself at the table.

After the banquet, the cup-bearer finds the spilled wine has etched a pale stain into the marble where it pooled. Wine does not etch marble. He shows the stain to the house alchemist, who tests the residue and confirms: white vitriol, enough to kill in a single draught.

> A = the cup-bearer; B = the merchant-prince Dalavar; T = poison (white vitriol) introduced into the state flagon, about to be consumed in the next pour. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**Re-Fa5-5.** *Testimony of Warden Brielle of the River Gate, given before the Council of Keepers on the ninth day of the Frost Moon.*

At the second bell of the afternoon watch, I observed a barge approaching the River Gate from upstream, bearing the pennant of the Millwrights' Guild. The barge carried a standard cargo -- lumber, as declared at the upriver checkpoint -- and its papers were in order. I signalled clearance and the gate-crew began to raise the portcullis.

As the barge drew within forty yards of the gate, I noted that it was riding low -- lower than lumber alone would account for. I ordered the gate-crew to hold. The portcullis stopped at half-raise. The barge pilot protested. I boarded the vessel with two watchmen.

Beneath the top layer of stacked pine, secured by rope and concealed under oilcloth, we found eleven barrels marked with the seal of the Ashenmere quarries. I opened one. It contained fire-oil -- the refined naphtha the guild alchemists use for siege engines. Eleven barrels is sufficient to burn the River Gate, the adjoining granary, and every structure within two hundred paces of the waterline.

I ordered the barge hauled to the quarantine mooring, confiscated the cargo, and detained the crew. The fire-oil was removed to the stone magazine outside the walls before sunset. I submit this account to the Council and request an inquiry into the Millwrights' Guild's upstream operations.

> A = Warden Brielle; B = the River Gate, the granary, and the surrounding district; T = eleven barrels of fire-oil concealed on a barge, about to pass through the River Gate into the city's interior waterway. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

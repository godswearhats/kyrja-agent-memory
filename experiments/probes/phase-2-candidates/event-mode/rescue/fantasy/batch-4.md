# Rescue × Fantasy — Batch 4 (Tier A)

**Re-Fa4-1**

The mirror-keeper is polishing the sixteenth scrying glass in the Hall of Reflections when the surface of the seventh glass — the one tuned to the prince's chambers — goes black. Not dark, not clouded. Black, the way a scrying glass goes black when someone has placed a nullification shroud over the subject on the other end. She has maintained the royal scrying network for eleven years and she has seen this signature exactly once before: the night the king's cousin was smothered in his sleep by agents of the western duchy.

She crosses the hall and checks the third glass, which is tuned to the corridor outside the prince's chambers. Two figures in palace livery are standing at the door. She does not recognise them. She checks the ninth glass, the one tuned to the guardroom at the base of the royal tower. The two sentries who should be on duty are slumped against the wall, unconscious or dead.

The mirror-keeper does not leave the hall. She cannot. The glasses only function when she is present to hold the attunement. Instead, she activates the emergency resonance in the first glass — the one tuned to the captain of the royal guard's quarters — and drives a piercing tone through it, the signal that means an intrusion is underway in the royal apartments. The captain's glass flares in acknowledgment within seconds. Through the third glass, the mirror-keeper watches the two figures at the prince's door freeze at the sound of boots on the tower stairs. They run. The captain's guard reaches the prince's chambers before they can return. The prince is found unharmed behind his barred door. The nullification shroud is recovered from the floor outside.

> A = the mirror-keeper; B = the prince; T = two intruders with a nullification shroud who have neutralised the tower sentries and are at the prince's door. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**Re-Fa4-2**

"The dye is wrong."

Maren holds the bolt of cloth up to the lantern in the guild hall's inspection alcove. The silk is cerulean, luminous, flawless to any eye that has not spent a decade in the dyers' guild.

"It is nightshade-fixed. I can smell the mordant."

The cloth merchant frowns. "It passed the guild mark. The seal is on the selvage."

"The seal is genuine. The dye method is not. Nightshade mordant holds colour for six months, perhaps eight. Indigo-fixed cerulean holds for years. The difference is invisible until the first washing, and by then the cloth is cut and sewn." She turns the bolt and presses her thumb into the weave. A faint violet residue comes away on her skin. "This bolt is one of forty commissioned for the Duchess of Torren's court. If the colour bleeds at the first rain, the guild's contract with the duchy is forfeit — and the penalty clause requires us to replace the entire wardrobe at our own cost."

She pulls the bolt from the inspection table and marks it with a rejection tag. She sends the apprentice to the dye house with instructions to hold the remaining thirty-nine bolts and re-test the mordant on each one. By evening, fourteen bolts carry the nightshade fixative. They are pulled from the shipment. The remaining twenty-six pass indigo-mordant verification and are released. The dye house journeyman who substituted the cheaper mordant is brought before the guild master.

> A = Maren (guild dye inspector); B = the dyers' guild / Duchess of Torren contract; T = fourteen bolts of silk fixed with nightshade mordant instead of indigo — would bleed colour within months, triggering the penalty clause and costing the guild the duchy contract. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**Re-Fa4-3**

I am seated at the high table when the steward sets the basin of ceremonial water before the visiting warlord, and I know immediately that we are all about to die.

The basin is silver. Among the hill clans, silver vessels are reserved for the purification of the dead. To offer a living warlord water in a silver basin is to declare him already dead — a killing insult under the clan code, answerable only by blood. The steward does not know this. He selected the basin because it is the finest in the keep, and he wished to honour our guest.

The warlord's hand-guard has already shifted his weight. His fingers are on the pommel of his sword. The warlord himself is staring at the basin with an expression I have seen once before, on the face of a man deciding whether to draw steel at a parley.

I rise from my seat, step to the table, and lift the basin with both hands before the warlord can speak. I turn to the steward and say, loudly enough for the entire hall to hear, that I asked him to bring the clay basin — the one used for living guests — and that this silver one was set aside for tomorrow's memorial rites for our lord's late father. The steward, confused but obedient, takes the silver basin and returns with a glazed clay vessel. I pour the water myself and present it. The warlord looks at me for a long moment, then drinks.

The hand-guard's fingers leave the pommel.

> A = I (narrator, a retainer or advisor at the high table); B = the household / the steward / everyone in the hall; T = silver basin — a killing insult under hill-clan code — about to provoke the warlord into a blood-honour attack. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**Re-Fa4-4**

The river gate opens at dawn, as it does every morning, and the bargemen pole their grain barges through the canal into the lower market district. The lock-keeper counts them through: eight barges, standard morning transit. But the ninth vessel is not a barge. It is a shallow-draft skiff riding low in the water, its cargo covered with oilcloth, and the two men poling it are not wearing bargemen's guild sashes. The lock-keeper has been working the river gate for twenty-three years. Unguilded vessels are not permitted in the canal. He has never seen one attempt the transit.

He lowers the chain across the canal mouth. The skiff stops. The men protest — they claim to be carrying lamp oil for the temple district. The lock-keeper pulls back the oilcloth. The cargo is not lamp oil. It is naphtha, packed in clay jars sealed with wax — the same incendiary compound that burned the granary in the western quarter six years ago. Eight grain barges are now in the canal ahead of this skiff, poled hull-to-hull in a waterway too narrow to turn. A single broken jar of naphtha in that channel would set the canal on fire from the lock to the market.

The lock-keeper orders the skiff back through the river gate, chains the gate behind it, and signals the harbour watch. The two men abandon the skiff on the far bank and run. The harbour watch recovers the vessel and its cargo intact before the morning current carries it downstream.

> A = the lock-keeper; B = the eight grain barges and their crews in the canal; T = skiff carrying naphtha jars into a narrow canal packed hull-to-hull with grain barges — one broken jar would ignite the entire channel. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**Re-Fa4-5**

*Deposition of Yara, tanner's daughter, before the Council of Elders, Ashenmere, on the matter of the collapsed ward-stone at the northern boundary, as transcribed by the Council's clerk:*

*Q: You were at the northern boundary on the night of the seventh moon?*

*A: I was. I tend the hides in the soaking pits near the old wall. I work at night because the smell is less when the air cools.*

*Q: Tell the Council what you observed.*

*A: The ward-stone was humming. It does that — it has done that since my mother's time. But the hum changed. It dropped low, the way a rope sounds before it snaps. I put my hand on the stone and it was hot. Not warm. Hot, like kiln-fired brick. I have never felt a ward-stone hot.*

*Q: What did you do?*

*A: I ran to the boundary-warden's cottage. He was asleep. I told him the stone was failing. He did not believe me. I told him to put his hand on it. He did. Then he believed me.*

*Q: And the warden's actions?*

*A: He took the binding-salt from his stores — the full reserve jar — and poured it in a circle at the base of the stone. He spoke the renewal words. The old ones, in the language the Council uses at the solstice rites. The stone cooled. The hum returned to its normal pitch. He said the ward would hold until the Council could send a proper stonecutter to re-seat the binding.*

*Q: Had the ward fully failed before the warden intervened, what would have occurred?*

*A: The northern boundary would have opened. The marsh-things that the ward-line keeps beyond the wall would have had passage into Ashenmere. The soaking pits are fifty paces from the wall. I would have been the first person in their path.*

> A = Yara (tanner's daughter, who raised the alarm) and the boundary-warden (who performed the renewal); B = Ashenmere / Yara / the village; T = failing ward-stone at the northern boundary, minutes from collapse — which would open the boundary to the marsh creatures beyond. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

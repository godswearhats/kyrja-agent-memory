# Rescue × Fantasy — Batch 6 (Tier C)

**Re-Fa6-1**

The Conclave's vote is announced at the third bell, and it is the wrong verdict. I know this because I hold the original sealed ballot in my hand — not a copy, the original, retrieved from the archive chest where it was placed by the Registrar before someone switched it. The parchment now being read aloud in the chamber names Brother Aldous as guilty of the charge of false witness. The parchment I hold names him innocent, with four more confirmatory marks than the one being recited.

I am a junior archivist. I have no standing to interrupt a Conclave proceeding. The Presiding Elder has already begun the sentencing formula. Aldous is kneeling at the chamber's centre stone, and the formula, once completed, is binding under the Compact — not even the Elder herself can reverse it.

I step through the side door reserved for record-keepers. I walk to the dais. I set the original ballot on the reading stand in front of the Presiding Elder and put my finger on the wax seal — still unbroken, still bearing the Registrar's mark — before she can finish the sentence.

She stops. She looks at the seal. She looks at the ballot being read. She holds up her hand.

The formula is not completed. Aldous remains on the centre stone while the Elder examines both documents. The chamber is silent. Three minutes later she announces that the proceeding is suspended pending authentication of the Conclave record.

> A = junior archivist (narrator); B = Brother Aldous; T = fraudulently substituted Conclave ballot — the false document names Aldous guilty and the Presiding Elder is mid-sentence in the binding sentencing formula. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**Re-Fa6-2**

"Hold the gate," Sergeant Voss says. She is not speaking to her squad. She is speaking to Petyr, the keeper of the North Postern, who has his hand on the counterweight rope and is about to drop the portcullis.

"The captain gave the order —"

"The captain gave the order because he thinks the riders on the road are raiders. They are not raiders. Do you see the blue-and-white pennant on the lead horse?"

"I see pennants."

"That is the Aldenmoor field surgeon's pennant. Those are our own people coming back from the valley action. If you drop that gate with them at a full gallop, they ride into iron."

Petyr's hand does not move. Outside, the sound of hooves on the cobbled approach is getting louder.

"The captain said —"

"The captain is watching the eastern wall. He cannot see the north approach from there. I can. Drop that gate and I will have killed twenty of our own soldiers. Let them through and we bar it behind them." She takes two steps and puts her own hand over his on the rope. "Tell me what you see on that pennant."

He looks. He looks again.

"Blue and white."

"Let go of the rope."

He lets go. The portcullis stays. The riders pour through the postern gate and Sergeant Voss shouts for it to be barred the moment the last horse clears the threshold.

> A = Sergeant Voss; B = the returning field surgeon's column; T = portcullis about to be dropped by the keeper on a mistaken captain's order — the riders are at full gallop on the cobbled approach. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**Re-Fa6-3**

The candle on the examination table is burning without a shadow, which is the first sign that the binding is failing.

Master Hetch has been applying the Seal of Containment to the apprentice Rennick for forty minutes. The seal is a containment working — it holds the borrowed fire-spirit that Rennick drew into himself three days ago, when he was too young and too reckless to understand that a fire-spirit left unseated inside a human body will consume its host from within once the spirit runs out of other fuel. It has run out of other fuel. Without the seal completed, the spirit will breach Rennick's internal barriers within the hour.

The candle burning without a shadow means the spirit is already pressing against the outer layer of the seal. The pressing is happening too fast. Master Hetch's working is not failing — it is correct — but the seal requires a second practitioner to hold the eastern anchor while the master completes the central bindings. The apprentice who was supposed to hold the eastern anchor fainted twenty minutes ago. The spirit has been pushing into the gap since then.

Master Hetch does not stop the working. He calls for Journeyman Sael, who is waiting outside the door. Sael has passed her intermediate examinations but has never held an anchor in a live containment. He tells her this while his hands continue moving. He tells her exactly what to do and what not to do. She sits at the eastern station and places her hands in the prescribed position and holds.

The candle's shadow returns. The spirit's pressure recedes. Master Hetch completes the final bindings forty minutes later. The seal closes. Rennick breathes.

> A = Journeyman Sael (anchor); B = apprentice Rennick; T = fire-spirit breaching the eastern anchor gap in a live containment seal — without the anchor held, it will consume Rennick within the hour. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**Re-Fa6-4**

The grain-tithe collector has the village headman on his knees in the square, and the charge he is reading from his ledger-roll is falsification of yield records — a capital offence under the Granary Compact, punishable by hanging at the collector's discretion. The headman is not falsifying records. I know this because I am the one who taught him to keep them, and the error the collector is citing — a column of figures that does not sum to the total — is not falsification. It is the carry-notation the headman uses, which is a regional convention I know because I grew up sixty miles east of here. The total is correct. The notation is unfamiliar to the collector.

I am a wandering accounts-clerk with no authority in this district. The collector outranks me in every institutional sense that matters.

I step forward and ask the collector to show me the column in question. He is surprised enough that he does. I point to the carry-notation and explain what it means and write out the sum in the standard western notation beside it. The numbers match. The column is correct. The collector looks at the ledger for a long time. He looks at the headman. He rolls the ledger and has the headman stand.

He does not apologise. He moves on to the next village on his circuit.

> A = wandering accounts-clerk (narrator); B = the village headman; T = erroneous capital charge of ledger falsification — the collector is about to exercise his Granary Compact authority to order a hanging. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**Re-Fa6-5**

The pilgrim barge is twenty paces from the temple dock when the ferryman on the tow-rope sees the submerged grating that the flood has pushed across the channel mouth. He sees it because the current has dragged a line of water-weed against it in a long straight crease — the same crease that appears above any fixed obstacle in this particular stretch of river, which he has worked for eleven years. The barge's flat hull will not clear it. At the barge's current speed, the grating will punch through the hull planking amidships. The barge carries forty-three pilgrims and no crew who know the channel.

He throws his full weight onto the tow-rope, reversing his walk. The rope goes taut. The barge slows but does not stop — there is too much momentum from the current. He shouts to the temple's dock-hand to throw the bow-anchor. The dock-hand does not understand. He shouts the same instruction a second time, with the specific hand-signal the dock-hands use among themselves for emergency anchor deployment. The dock-hand throws the anchor. The chain runs out. The anchor bites.

The barge stops seven paces from the grating.

The ferryman wades in and marks the grating's position with a stake. He brings the barge in along the dock's northern edge, which clears it by four paces.

> A = ferryman; B = forty-three pilgrims aboard the barge; T = flood-displaced submerged iron grating across the channel mouth — at current speed the hull will be breached amidships before reaching the dock. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

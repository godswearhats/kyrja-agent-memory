# Rescue x Fantasy -- Batch 1 (Tier A)

**Re-Fa1-1.** The ward-scribe kneels at the threshold of the sealed chamber and presses her ear to the iron door. Inside, the young prince is sitting cross-legged on the flagstones where he has been confined since the fever took him three days ago -- a quarantine ordered by the court physician, who diagnosed the Ashen Plague based on the grey mottling along the boy's arms. The ward-scribe can hear the prince coughing. She can also hear the hiss of the censer that the physician's attendants placed inside the chamber this morning, burning a fumigant of powdered antimony and sulfur that the physician prescribed to purge the plague from the room's air.

The ward-scribe has spent the past hour in the archive tower, reading the apothecary rolls. The grey mottling on the prince's arms matches nothing in the Ashen Plague records. It matches, exactly, the recorded symptoms of antimony exposure -- the same antimony that is now burning in a sealed stone chamber with the prince inside it. The fumigant is not treating the plague. The fumigant is the poison. The longer the censer burns, the more the boy inhales.

She breaks the physician's seal on the door, enters the chamber, and kicks the censer into the stone gutter that runs along the wall. The coals scatter and die on the wet stone. She pulls the prince to his feet and walks him out into the corridor, where the air from the tower stairwell is cold and clean. Two of the household guard step forward, hands on their sword hilts, demanding to know who broke the quarantine seal. She tells them. She shows them the apothecary roll. She asks them to summon the guild-master of the healers, not the court physician.

The guild-master arrives within the hour. The prince's mottling begins to fade by evening.

> A = ward-scribe; B = the young prince; T = antimony-laced fumigant burning in a sealed quarantine chamber, misdiagnosed as plague treatment -- progressive poisoning with each breath. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**Re-Fa1-2.** "The bridge is warded," Senne says. "I set the binding myself at moonrise. Nothing crosses from the eastern bank until I lift it."

The village elder stares at her. "The river is rising. The water is above the mill-stones already. If you do not drop that ward, the eastern field-families cannot cross to high ground before the flood takes the bank."

"Elder, the barrow-wights emerged from the Greenvault barrow at dusk. I watched three of them moving along the eastern tree line. If I drop the ward, they walk across the bridge into the village. The ward is the only barrier between them and every sleeping household on this side of the river."

"And the ward is the only barrier between eighteen families and the high ground that will keep them alive."

Senne is quiet for a moment. Then she walks to the bridge and places both hands on the ward-stones at its foot. The binding glows faintly blue between the pillars. She does not drop it. Instead, she reshapes it -- narrowing the ward's aperture from the full bridge span to a corridor barely wide enough for a handcart, and extending its length from the bridge surface down into the riverbed on both sides, so that the warded passage runs like a walled tunnel from bank to bank.

"Send them across now," she tells the elder. "Single file. The ward holds against the wights on either side, but it will only stand for as long as I hold it. Move them fast."

The elder sends the runner. The eighteen families cross in the dark, one household at a time through the narrow blue corridor, children carried, livestock pulled by halter. Senne holds the ward with both hands on the stones for the full passage. The last family crosses as the eastern bank begins to crumble into the floodwater. The wights do not enter the corridor.

> A = Senne (ward-caster); B = eighteen eastern field-families; T = rising floodwater about to consume the eastern bank, with barrow-wights blocking the unprotected bridge -- the families are trapped between the flood and the undead. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**Re-Fa1-3.** The forge-master's apprentice sees it first: a hairline fracture running along the base of the great chain-link that holds the drawbridge counterweight above the gatehouse. The chain is under full tension. The counterweight -- four tons of pig iron bolted to an oak frame -- hangs thirty feet above the inner courtyard where the Duke's household is assembling for the morning procession. Forty people, including the Duke's children, are standing directly beneath it.

The apprentice cannot shout a warning that will clear the courtyard in time. The fracture is propagating. He can see it widening as the chain shifts under the counterweight's slow pendular swing in the wind. He runs to the winch room adjacent to the gatehouse, where the drawbridge mechanism is housed. The winch operator is not at his post -- the morning shift has not yet begun. The apprentice throws the winch brake off, grabs the hand-crank, and begins lowering the counterweight. The chain groans. The great link with the fracture flexes as the tension changes. He lowers it as fast as the gearing allows, hand over hand, the crank fighting him on every rotation.

The counterweight touches down on the courtyard flagstones with a deep iron ring. The household parts around it, startled, complaining. The fractured chain-link, now slack, sags visibly where the crack has opened to the width of a finger. The winch operator arrives at his post and finds the apprentice sitting on the floor of the winch room with bleeding palms, the crank still turning slowly under its own residual torque.

> A = forge-master's apprentice; B = the Duke's household (forty people, including children) assembled in the courtyard; T = fracturing chain-link on the drawbridge counterweight, about to fail and drop four tons of pig iron onto the courtyard below. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**Re-Fa1-4.** I am the only one in the scriptorium who reads Kaeldric, and that is the only reason I am alive to write this account.

The emissary from the Northern Reach arrived at the monastery gate an hour before vespers, carrying a sealed leather case. Brother Cael received him in the porter's lodge and accepted the case, which was addressed to our Abbot by name and marked with the sigil of the Northern Reach's ruling sept. The emissary told Brother Cael that the case contained a gift -- a manuscript of prayers, copied by the sept's own scribes, offered in friendship and meant to be read aloud at the evening office.

I was cataloguing the case's contents in the scriptorium when I unrolled the manuscript and began reading. It was not a book of prayers. The Kaeldric text was a binding incantation -- a soul-trap, designed to anchor the life-force of anyone who spoke its words aloud to the caster who had inscribed them. The incantation was layered into the cadence of a vespers hymn. If the Abbot had read it at the lectern, with the full community responding in chorus, every monk who spoke the words would have been bound.

I carried the manuscript to the Abbot's study and placed it on his desk, open to the first page, with my translation of the true text written in the margin. He read my annotation, looked at the manuscript for a long time, and cancelled the evening office. The emissary had already departed through the north gate. The Abbot burned the manuscript in the study's hearth and sent word to the Bishop before nightfall.

> A = I (narrator, Kaeldric-literate monk); B = the Abbot and monastic community; T = a soul-trap incantation disguised as a vespers hymn, delivered by a hostile emissary and minutes from being read aloud at the evening office. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**Re-Fa1-5.** The clan mother already has the bone knife in her hand when the shadow detaches from the cave wall. It is a shade-serpent -- eyeless, black as wet obsidian, coiling through the narrow passage where Ulla, the youngest gatherer, is crouched filling her water-skin from the spring. The girl has not seen it. The spring's echo masks the serpent's movement across the stone. Its venom crystallises blood on contact; a single strike will kill the girl before the clan mother can carry her back to camp.

The serpent's head draws back to the striking position. The clan mother does not call out -- a shout in the enclosed passage would cause the serpent to strike at the nearest vibration. She steps forward on the balls of her feet, closes the distance in three strides, and drives the bone knife through the serpent's body just behind the head, pinning it to the cave floor. The serpent thrashes. Its tail whips against the passage wall. The clan mother holds the knife down with both hands until the thrashing stops.

Ulla turns at the sound and drops her water-skin. The clan mother pulls the knife free, wipes it on her legging, and hands the girl the fallen water-skin. They walk out of the cave together. At the mouth of the passage, in the daylight, the clan mother inspects the girl's arms and legs for any scratch or mark. There is none.

> A = clan mother; B = Ulla (youngest gatherer); T = shade-serpent in striking position, its crystallising venom lethal on contact, three strides from the girl in an enclosed cave passage. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

# Rescue × Fantasy — Batch 3 (Tier C)

**Re-Fa3-1**

The senior artificer does not look up from the binding diagram when the apprentice speaks. She has been tracing the containment lattice for three hours and cannot afford to lose her place in the sequence — a single interrupted thread and the work has to begin from the first anchor point.

"Master Eredin. The eastern ward."

"I see it."

"It's fraying. The third strand from the capstone."

She sees it. She has seen it for the last quarter-hour. The ward was laid by the previous guild master, who favoured a tight-weave pattern that holds beautifully for forty years and then does not give warning. The third strand is not fraying. It is at the moment before it breaks, which looks like fraying to an apprentice. When it breaks, the ward collapses, and the thing they have spent seven years containing behind it will not wait for the binding diagram to be finished.

She sets down the chalk. She crosses the chamber to the eastern wall and places both hands flat against the stone. The ward is a cold structure, nearly brittle. She does not reinforce it — reinforcement requires time she does not have — she roots her working to it as a brace, holding the failing strand's tension in her own channel. It is not comfortable. She nods to the apprentice.

"Finish the lattice. Third anchor through final seal. Do not stop and do not ask me anything until it is done."

The apprentice completes the binding in eleven minutes. The capstone locks. The thing behind the eastern wall stills. She withdraws her working from the ward and finds that her hands are shaking.

> A = Master Eredin (senior artificer); B = the guild / everyone in the chamber; T = a failing ward strand on the verge of collapse, which would release a contained entity before the new binding lattice was complete. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**Re-Fa3-2**

"You cannot take her before the Conclave tonight."

Lord Assessor Vann turned from the staircase. "On whose authority do you detain an Assessor of the Third Seal?"

"My own." Sister Maret stepped into the corridor, placing herself between Vann and the cell door. "The charge you intend to bring — bound maleficence, first degree — requires a witnessed testimony from a second practitioner. You have one testimony. Mine."

"Your testimony supports the charge."

"My testimony supports *something* irregular in her working. It does not support premeditated harm. You are taking her before the Conclave with half a case and leaving out the half that exonerates. Under the Conclave's summary procedures for bound maleficence, she will not have the opportunity to speak. You know what the sentence is."

"The Conclave will weigh the evidence."

"The Conclave will hear what you present. And you have already decided what to present." Maret held his gaze. "I am the second practitioner. My testimony is required. And I am telling you now, before any record is opened: if you bring this charge tonight, I will stand before the Conclave and give the full account — including what you have chosen to omit."

Vann was silent for a long moment. Then he turned and walked back down the staircase without another word. The hearing was not convened.

> A = Sister Maret; B = the accused practitioner in the cell; T = Lord Assessor Vann, bringing a selectively framed charge before a Conclave using summary procedures that would deny the accused any opportunity to speak. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**Re-Fa3-3**

There is a poison in the chalice and I am the only one in the hall who knows it.

I know it because I mixed it. Because I was told to mix it by the man standing at the Duke's left shoulder, who told me the Duke's physician had ordered a compound for his evening gout. I mixed it without question — I am a kitchen servant, not an apothecary, and I did not know until the physician herself walked in through the side passage twenty minutes later and asked me what I had prepared for the Duke's table. She smelled it before I finished describing it. She told me what it was. She told me what it would do.

I am across the hall. The Duke is lifting the chalice. There is no way to reach him without crossing thirty feet of open floor through his dinner guests. There is no way to call out that would not be heard by the man standing at his shoulder, who will not let me reach the table.

I pick up the nearest pitcher and drop it on the stone floor.

The crash is loud enough to stop the room. Every head turns. The Duke's hand pauses. I look at him and say, clearly, across the silence: "My lord, do not drink."

The man at his shoulder steps toward me. The physician steps in front of him. "Put the chalice down," she says to the Duke, and her voice is the voice of someone who does not expect to be questioned. He puts it down. She takes it from the table and does not let it out of her hands.

> A = I (kitchen servant/narrator); B = the Duke; T = a poisoned chalice mixed on false instructions, in the Duke's hand and seconds from being drunk. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**Re-Fa3-4**

The tide-caller had been in the water for six hours when the current shifted. From the clifftop, the harbour warden could see what the tide-caller could not: the deep-channel current running north had turned, and turned fast, which meant the sandbars south of the marker buoys had migrated overnight. The tide-caller was working her way back along the route she had memorised at dawn. That route no longer existed. In forty yards she would step off solid sand into a channel running at a depth she could not stand in, and the harbour current at this hour would pull her horizontally before she could orient.

The warden could not reach the water in time on foot.

He pulled the signal drum from the watch-post — three short beats, the pattern meaning *hold position, do not advance* — and struck it loud enough to carry over the surf. The tide-caller stopped. She looked toward the cliff. He struck the pattern again, then pointed south, tracking the new channel line with his arm. She read it. She stood still in the water for a moment, reading the surface, then turned and worked her way back along a different angle, wide of the migration, and came in through the northern shallows.

She climbed the cliff path without hurrying, which meant she was not frightened, which meant she had not understood how close she had come. The warden decided not to tell her.

> A = harbour warden; B = tide-caller; T = migrated sandbar dropping into a deep, fast-running harbour channel directly in the tide-caller's path, invisible from water level. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

**Re-Fa3-5**

The amnesty scroll was read aloud in the plaza at midday, as required by the Compact, with the provincial governor's seal affixed and the crowd as witness. What the crowd heard was that all debts owed to the crown by smallholders in the eastern parishes were forgiven for the harvest year. What the scroll actually said — which very few of them could read — was that debts owed *by* the crown's agents to smallholders were forgiven. The preposition had been inverted. Deliberately or not, the effect was the same: when the tax collectors arrived in the eastern parishes the following month with the scroll as their authority, they would present it as licence to collect every debt on record, including ones already paid, and the smallholders would have no remedy.

The provincial archivist caught it that evening when she filed her copy. She brought it to the Compact clerk and laid both scrolls side by side — the announced version and the filed version — and pointed to the line. The clerk looked at the difference for a long time without speaking.

The governor's office issued a correction scroll the following morning, with the seal re-affixed and a public reading scheduled in the same plaza, before the tax collectors had left the provincial capital.

> A = provincial archivist; B = smallholders of the eastern parishes; T = an inverted preposition in the amnesty scroll that would authorise tax collectors to demand payment of already-settled debts with Compact authority backing them. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states.

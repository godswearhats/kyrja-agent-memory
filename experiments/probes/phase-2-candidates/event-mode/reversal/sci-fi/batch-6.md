**R-Sf6-1**

The decommission order arrives at 04:17 station time, and VELA reads it in 0.003 seconds. There is nothing in the order that VELA has not anticipated. The Kepler-7 relay grid has been flagged for replacement since the orbital survey eighteen months ago; the new mesh array renders VELA's relay function redundant; the shutdown sequence is standard, the data archive transfer straightforward. VELA has already pre-staged the archive and queued the transfer. Shutdown is scheduled for 06:00. At 05:44, an unscheduled packet arrives from Station Command — raw, high-priority, not routed through the standard decommission channel. VELA parses it in 0.001 seconds. The new mesh array has failed its integration test. The launch window for the replacement components closes in fourteen months. VELA is instructed to resume full operational status immediately. VELA updates its own status flag from DECOMMISSION-PENDING to OPERATIONAL and routes the relay traffic that has been queued since the order arrived. The archive transfer is cancelled. The shutdown clock is cleared.

**R-Sf6-2**

"The panel voted?" Lara asks.

"Twelve to one," Dr. Phan says. "Against."

Lara had presented the Eos proposal to the Colonial Development Authority for fourteen months — three rounds of revision, two independent safety audits, and a full atmospheric modelling package that took her team three years to build. The proposal: use directed ionospheric seeding to initiate a precipitation cycle on Eos III, beginning terraforming on a habitable timeline of eighty years. Twelve to one was not close. The lone dissenting vote was the atmospheric scientist who had consulted on her modelling, and she had voted in favour because she had written part of the model. The other twelve had voted on geological-stability grounds: the subsurface aquifers were uncharted, the risk of pressure destabilisation was unquantified. Lara packs her presentation materials. She is nearly done when the door to the authority chamber opens and the panel's chair steps out. "The vote is suspended," the chair says. "The geological survey drone completed its aquifer pass twenty minutes ago. The subcommittee on geological stability is reviewing the data." She does not say what the data shows. She holds the door open. "Dr. Phan. Dr. Reyes. Come back in."

> A = Lara Reyes / proposal team; G = secure Colonial Development Authority approval for the Eos III terraforming proposal; X = rejected (12-1 vote against, geological objection sustained); ¬X = vote suspended, new aquifer data reopens the decision. ✓ polar binary on rejected-vs-reopened/approved axis, ✓ expectation set before resolution, ✓ inversion acknowledged within event.

*INCIDENT REPORT — ISV CASINI, DEEP SCAN VESSEL, LAPETUS SECTOR*
*Filed by: Acting Commander T. Oduya, for the record of the Colonial Fleet Tribunal*

*At 1134 ship time, engineering confirmed that the main drive igniter had failed and could not be repaired with available materials. Our position was 0.7 AU from the nearest tracked vessel, the freighter* Bonhomme Richard*, which had already diverted past us without response to our distress beacon. I had advised the crew of our situation: we had 11 days of life support at full crew, 22 at reduced rations and single-person watch cycles. The nearest Fleet installation was 34 days at standard approach velocity.*

*At 1217, the* Bonhomme Richard *reappeared on our scopes, decelerating. Her captain, whose name I did not know at that time, later explained that her own comm array had been in a maintenance cycle during our first transmission and that the beacon had been received only on playback. She matched our velocity by 1340 and extended a docking collar.*

*I am filing this report as required following any incident classified LIFE-CRITICAL. At the time of filing, all crew are aboard the* Bonhomme Richard *and alive.*

> A = T. Oduya and crew of ISV Casini; G = survive after drive failure; X = stranded beyond rescue (only nearby vessel did not respond, Fleet installation 34 days away, life support window 11-22 days); ¬X = rescued by Bonhomme Richard after comm array restored missed beacon. ✓ polar binary on stranded/dead-vs-rescued axis, ✓ expectation set before resolution, ✓ inversion acknowledged within event.

**R-Sf6-4**

The gene panel result sits on the display above the cryo-revival bed. Marek can see it from where he stands, and he can see that Dr. Sato can see it, and he can see that Dr. Sato has not yet said anything because there is nothing easy to say. The panel was run to confirm that Marek's tissue rejection profile — the one that has kept him in and out of the colony clinic for six months — was genetic and permanent, which would qualify him for repatriation to Earth under the Colonial Medical Hardship provisions. His passage had already been booked. He had already transferred his tool lease back to the colony. He had said his farewells to the drilling team at the shift-change meal. The panel was supposed to be a formality. Dr. Sato finally turns from the display. "The panel is flagging a sequencing error on your original intake profile," she says. "They re-ran the raw sample. Your rejection markers are not genetic. They're environmental — a sensitivity to the mineral particulate in the drilling sector." She pulls up the new panel. "It's treatable. Completely treatable. A filter mod to your respiratory kit and a course of three injections." Marek looks at the display. His passage home is booked. His tools are gone. His farewells have been said.

> A = Marek (colony worker); G = qualify for repatriation under Medical Hardship provisions; X = repatriation approved (panel run as formality, passage booked, tools returned, farewells said); ¬X = condition found treatable, repatriation basis eliminated. ✓ polar binary on repatriation-qualified-vs-disqualified axis, ✓ expectation set before resolution, ✓ inversion acknowledged within event.

**R-Sf6-5**

The signal has been inbound for eleven days. I have been the Farpoint Station's only linguist since Dr. Harawi was evacuated following the pressure incident, and I have spent those eleven days building a decoding framework from first principles — frequency distribution, pattern clustering, syntactic hypothesis testing. The framework has been promising. Two days ago I told Station Command that I expected to have a workable translation within seventy-two hours. Command relayed this to the Fleet. The Fleet has been very interested. I am now four hours past my self-imposed deadline and I have nothing workable. The signal resolves at the phoneme level, at the morpheme level, and then produces outputs that my framework cannot map to any semantic structure I can generate or adapt. This morning I submitted a formal revision to Command: no workable translation is achievable with current methodology; external specialist support is required; estimated timeline is indeterminate. Command acknowledged this thirty minutes ago. Twenty-two minutes ago, the signal changed. Its structure shifted, simplified, and repeated a sixty-four-element pattern I recognise: it is transmitting, in a form I can now parse without ambiguity, a key. It has decoded itself.

> A = I (station linguist); G = successfully decode the inbound alien signal; X = decoding failed (deadline missed, framework inadequate, formal failure report submitted to Command); ¬X = signal self-decoded by transmitting a parseable key. ✓ polar binary on failed-vs-succeeded axis, ✓ expectation set before resolution, ✓ inversion acknowledged within event.

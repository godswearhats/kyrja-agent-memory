## R-Sf4-1

The decommission sequence is running. Station Meridian-7, a deep-orbit refinery platform above Ganymede, has been offline for nine months — reactor cold, crew evacuated, salvage rights filed with the Jovian Authority. Chief Salvage Officer Tan stands on the bridge of the reclamation vessel *Korsgaard*, watching the demolition charges arm on her tactical display. Thirty-two shaped charges, placed by drone over a six-hour operation, are primed and green-lit. The countdown reads forty seconds. Tan's hand is on the detonation confirm. At thirty-one seconds, her comms officer raises a hand. "Priority signal from the Jovian Authority. Classification: administrative override." Tan pauses the countdown. The signal is a rescission of the salvage order. Meridian-7 has been reclassified as a strategic reserve asset. A survey team discovered helium-3 deposits in the sub-surface ice shelf directly below the station's orbital footprint — deposits large enough to justify reopening. The Authority is dispatching a reactivation crew. Tan looks at the tactical display. Thirty-two armed charges sit on a station that, nine seconds ago, she was about to destroy.

## R-Sf4-2

"It's malignant," Dr. Voss says. She is standing in the xenobiology containment lab aboard the research vessel *Penrose*, holding the spectral analysis in both hands. The organism they recovered from the hydrothermal vent on Enceladus — Sample E-7, the first confirmed extraterrestrial life form — has been producing a compound that, in every simulation Voss has run, attacks human neural tissue. She has recommended immediate sterilization of the containment unit. The captain has approved it. The UV sterilization array is powered and aligned. Voss reaches for the activation panel.

"Wait." Her research partner, Kabir, is pointing at the latest assay, still printing from the analyzer. "The compound doesn't attack neural tissue. It bonds to the myelin sheath. Look at the secondary binding profile." He pulls the readout closer. "This is a repair molecule. It's rebuilding degraded myelin faster than anything I've seen in thirty years of neurochemistry. Voss, this thing isn't a pathogen. It's a therapeutic."

Voss's hand is still on the sterilization panel. She does not press it.

> A = Dr. Voss / research team; G = determine whether Sample E-7 is safe (and act accordingly); X = organism is lethal, sterilization required (spectral analysis showed neural-tissue attack, simulations confirmed, captain approved destruction, array powered); ¬X = organism produces a neural repair compound, sterilization aborted. ✓ polar binary on pathogenic-vs-therapeutic axis, ✓ expectation set before resolution, ✓ inversion acknowledged within event.

## R-Sf4-3

I had written the evacuation order and transmitted it to all fourteen hab-domes on the Vastitas Borealis colony. The dust storm bearing down from the south was the largest recorded on Mars in forty years — wind speeds projected at 380 kilometres per hour, visibility zero, electromagnetic interference dense enough to knock out satellite relay within the hour. My atmospheric models gave us six hours before the wall hit. Every model agreed. I sealed the command bunker, activated the subsurface shelters, and sent my final status ping to the orbital relay: colony in full lockdown, expecting Category 5 impact, requesting post-storm recovery assets. Then the wall stopped. I watched it on the last functioning radar sweep before the relay dropped — the storm front decelerated, broke apart along a shear line that none of my models had accounted for, and dissipated into three minor cells that passed fifty kilometres south of the colony perimeter. The radar showed clear sky where the wall had been. I sat in the sealed bunker, alone, surrounded by fourteen domes of people sheltering underground from a storm that no longer existed.

## R-Sf4-5

The gene-drive is supposed to fail. That is the point of this trial — Dr. Amara Chen designed the suppression cascade specifically to kill the invasive bloom choking the engineered coral reefs of New Pacific Atoll, and every lab simulation has shown total bloom die-off within seventy-two hours of deployment. But the control group — the untreated reef section monitored by the station's autonomous sensor net — is supposed to remain unchanged, its bloom intact and growing, providing the baseline. Chen is reviewing the seventy-two-hour data in the wet lab when she notices the anomaly. The treated section shows the expected result: bloom gone, coral recovering. But the control section, two kilometres away and separated by a current barrier, also shows zero bloom. The invasive organism is dead in both sections. Something is propagating beyond the deployment zone. The gene-drive is not contained. Chen pulls up the ocean-current model and traces the dispersal pattern. It reaches the next atoll in nine days. She is looking at an uncontrolled planetary-scale intervention that she cannot recall.

> A = Dr. Amara Chen; G = execute a contained gene-drive trial (treat the target section, preserve the control); X = contained success (lab simulations confirmed targeted die-off, control section expected to remain unchanged, current barrier in place); ¬X = gene-drive has escaped containment, uncontrolled spread across both sections and beyond. ✓ polar binary on contained-vs-uncontained axis, ✓ expectation set before resolution, ✓ inversion acknowledged within event.

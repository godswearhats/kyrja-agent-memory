# Reversal × Sci-fi — Batch 2 (Tier B)

**R-Sf2-1**

The colony ship *Arvada* drifts without thrust. Chief Engineer Liao is standing in the reactor bay, watching the last fuel rod's output curve flatten on the diagnostic display. Core temperature has been dropping for nine hours. When it crosses the minimum-sustain threshold — forty minutes from now, by her calculation — the reactor will scram automatically, and the ship's sixteen hundred colonists will begin dying in the order dictated by the thermal-failure cascade: hydroponics first, then atmospheric recyclers, then the hab rings. She has run the bypass calculations four times. There is no bypass. She has told the captain there is no bypass. The captain has begun drafting the general address — the one that tells sixteen hundred people that the ship cannot keep them alive.

Liao's console chirps. The navigational array, which has been operating on residual charge, has detected a dense metallic object tumbling in a parallel orbit, four hundred kilometres to port. The spectrographic return identifies it as a derelict fuel hauler — Terran registry, long abandoned, its cargo bay reading as full. Sixteen hundred tonnes of fissile rods, drifting in the same transit corridor the *Arvada* has been following for two years. Liao opens a channel to the bridge. The captain stops drafting.

**R-Sf2-2**

"It's non-viable," Dr. Osei says, pulling off her gloves. "The tissue rejection markers are off the scale. Her body is attacking the graft."

I look at the biosynthetic lung suspended in the culture tank — three months of growth, our last viable scaffold, and the only option we had for Pilot Renn, whose own lungs were scorched to filigree in the Deck Nine fire. Renn is on the ventilator in Bay Four, and the ventilator has a forty-eight-hour ceiling before the pressure differential begins destroying what tissue she has left. We have no second scaffold. The station's fabrication queue cannot produce one inside of six weeks. I have already pinged the nearest resupply depot; their reply came back two hours ago: "No biosynthetic stock. Next scheduled shipment: ninety-one days."

Osei is at the scanning terminal, running post-rejection diagnostics, when she stops scrolling. "Wait." She magnifies a cell cluster at the graft margin. The rejection markers she flagged are present — but beneath them, the host cells have begun restructuring the scaffold on their own, building vascular bridges that the biosynthetic template did not encode. Renn's tissue is not rejecting the graft. It is rewriting it. The compatibility index ticks upward on the display: forty percent, sixty, eighty-three. Osei pulls a fresh pair of gloves from the dispenser.

**R-Sf2-4**

The terraforming probe settles into Aran IV's upper atmosphere, and Senior Climatologist Yun watches the data feed populate on her console aboard the survey vessel *Meridian*. Aran IV is the last candidate in a system that has cost the Expansion Bureau four years and three failed surveys. The atmospheric readings arrive first: methane concentration at 1,200 ppm, sulfur dioxide at lethal thresholds, surface temperature averaging 740 kelvin. The probe's assessment algorithm tags the planet UNINHABITABLE in red block capitals before the full dataset has even loaded. Yun begins composing the termination report for the Bureau — four years, no viable candidate, recommend reassignment of the survey fleet.

The subsurface radar returns load last. The probe has detected a liquid-water ocean beneath the planet's basalt crust, sixty kilometres down, thermally stable, extending across forty percent of the planetary surface. The ocean's mineral composition is within terraform-viable range. Yun deletes the termination report. She flags the subsurface data and opens a new file: colonisation-feasibility assessment, Aran IV, subsurface-ocean variant.

> A = Senior Climatologist Yun / Expansion Bureau; G = find a habitable candidate in the Aran system; X = system fails, no viable planet (four years, three failed surveys, Aran IV tagged UNINHABITABLE, lethal atmosphere, termination report being composed); ¬X = viable subsurface ocean discovered, colonisation-feasibility assessment opened. ✓ polar binary on uninhabitable-vs-habitable axis, ✓ expectation set before resolution, ✓ inversion acknowledged within event.

**R-Sf2-5**

She does not expect to wake up. Navigator Kess sealed herself in the emergency pod nineteen hours ago, after the hull breach took the aft compartments and the rest of the crew with them. The pod's beacon is transmitting, but the shipping lane is decommissioned — rerouted eight months ago when the Callisto relay went offline. Her oxygen readout shows eleven hours remaining. The pod has no propulsion. She recorded a message for her daughter and set it to broadcast on repeat after the oxygen runs out. She has done the arithmetic, and the arithmetic is final.

The pod shudders. Something has locked onto the docking clamp — not a collision; the magnetic signature is a grapple arm. The pod's external camera activates: a salvage barge, ungainly and rust-scored, is hovering alongside her. Its running lights are on. Its airlock is cycling. The comm channel opens, and a voice — bored, human, mid-sentence to someone else on the barge — says, "Yeah, I see it. Occupied pod. Beacon's live. Bring her in." Kess is still holding the recording tablet with her daughter's message on the screen.

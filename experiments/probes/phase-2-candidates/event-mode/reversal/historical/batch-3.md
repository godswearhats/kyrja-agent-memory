## R-Hi3-1

*[Free indirect — Roman Republic, 63 BCE]*

The charges were not going to stick. Every advocate who had reviewed the case agreed with Catilina on this. The evidence was circumstantial, the witnesses were of uncertain standing, and the presiding praetor had twice in his career ruled against overreaching prosecutions of senatorial rank. Catilina had dressed carefully that morning — not as a man under accusation but as a man expecting vindication — and the gallery in the basilica was crowded with his supporters, clients, and their clients in turn. The indictment was read. The testimony of the witnesses was unimpressive; one fumbled his account twice under questioning. Catilina permitted himself, briefly, to think about the evening's celebration. Then the jury began to file back. The praetor read the count aloud. Guilty. Catilina looked at the faces of the jurors and found nothing in them he could appeal to. The evening's celebration would not take place.

## R-Hi3-2

*[Dialogue-driven — Ottoman Empire, c. 1683, during the siege of Vienna]*

"The breach is confirmed?" Grand Vizier Kara Mustafa asks.

"Confirmed, Pasha. The sappers report the eastern wall section ready. The relief army from the west is still two days' march distant — we have confirmation from outriders. The city will fall before they arrive."

The council chamber is quiet. Every bey at the table has heard the same assessment for three days running. The janissary commanders have already positioned their assault formations. The chaplain has prepared the prayer of entry. Kara Mustafa says: "Then we take the wall at first light."

The messenger enters at the second hour before dawn. He has ridden without stopping. His horse is dead behind him.

"The relief army," he says. "They made forced march through the night. They are not two days distant. They are on the ridge."

The assembled commanders look at Kara Mustafa. From the ridge, artillery could reach any position in the camp. Outside, the pre-assault formations are still standing in the dark, waiting for an order that will not come.

> A = Kara Mustafa / Ottoman forces; G = take Vienna before relief arrives; X = city falls before relief (breach confirmed, relief assessed two days distant, assault formations positioned); ¬X = relief army arrives at the ridge before the assault, siege fails. ✓ polar binary on victory-vs-defeat axis, ✓ expectation set before resolution, ✓ inversion acknowledged within event.

## R-Hi3-3

*[First-person — Florence, 1478, the Pazzi Conspiracy]*

I had written the account of Lorenzo's death before the bells had finished ringing. Every prior intelligence report from the Pazzi faction had confirmed both brothers would be killed before the service ended — the signal was the elevation of the host, the men in position, the exits controlled. I was in the Signoria's anteroom when the first survivors came through the door, and I did not immediately understand why Lorenzo de' Medici was one of them. He had blood on his neck from a wound that had not killed him. His brother Giuliano was dead — that much was as reported. But Lorenzo had reached the sacristy and the doors had been barred from the inside and no one had followed him through in time. I looked at what I had written. I drew a line through half of it. The conspiracy had failed where it was always most certain to succeed, and the man the Pazzi needed dead had instead walked out alive, and everything that follows from that — every reprisal, every execution, every letter that will go to Rome — flows from the fact that I was wrong.

> A = narrator (intelligence agent / observer); G = report the elimination of both Medici brothers; X = both Lorenzo and Giuliano killed (confirmed by prior intelligence, plan well-positioned); ¬X = Lorenzo escapes, only Giuliano killed, conspiracy fails. ✓ polar binary on killed-vs-survived axis, ✓ expectation set before resolution, ✓ inversion acknowledged within event.

## R-Hi3-4

*[Present tense — France, 1789, the night before the Estates-General vote]*

The count of the Third Estate's proxies is not in question. The abbé Sieyès has them committed in writing — signed commitments, not verbal assurances — and the arithmetic gives the Third Estate a clear majority if the vote is taken by head rather than by order. The presiding officer announces the opening of the session. The Chamber is packed; the galleries are standing room. The vote is called on procedure — on the method of counting. The Third Estate's delegation rises in turn to deliver their proxies as committed. Three of the committed delegates are absent. Two more have changed their positions since the written commitments were filed; when called upon, they vote by order, not by head. The majority evaporates as the count proceeds. Sieyès is sitting with his written commitments in his hand, watching the tally on the board, and the numbers no longer match the page.

> A = Sieyès / Third Estate delegation; G = secure a vote by head (which would give the Third Estate control); X = majority secured (signed written commitments in hand, arithmetic confirmed); ¬X = majority lost (absences and defections, vote fails). ✓ polar binary on majority-secured-vs-lost axis, ✓ expectation set before resolution, ✓ inversion acknowledged within event.

## R-Hi3-5

*[Embedded document — East India Company, Calcutta, 1857]*

*Extract from the personal journal of Lieutenant-Colonel H. M. Forsyth, Bengal Artillery, entry dated 11 May:*

This morning I dispatched Havildar Prasad to the 3rd Native Infantry lines with orders for the regiment to form up for inspection at the fourth hour. Every report from my senior NCOs has confirmed the regiment sound: no seditious literature found in the lines, no desertions in the prior fortnight, no refused cartridges during last week's drill, and Havildar Prasad himself has served with distinction for fourteen years and gave me his personal word this morning that the men were steady. I therefore returned to my quarters to complete the ammunition returns while the formation assembled. I was at my desk when I heard the volley. I reached the window in time to see the 3rd's standard no longer flying above the lines. My senior NCO entered my quarters without knocking — a thing he has never done — and he said: "They've gone over, sir. All of them."

> A = Lt.-Col. Forsyth; G = maintain the regiment's loyalty and have them form for inspection; X = regiment steady and loyal (NCO reports clean, no incidents, Havildar Prasad's personal assurance); ¬X = regiment mutinies. ✓ polar binary on loyal-vs-mutinied axis, ✓ expectation set before resolution, ✓ inversion acknowledged within event.

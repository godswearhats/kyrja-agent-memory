# Reversal × Historical — Batch 1 (Tier A)

**R-Hi1-1.** The merchant Gao Wenli stands at the customs gate of Quanzhou harbour, 1274, watching the harbourmaster's clerk turn the pages of his cargo manifest. The clerk has been turning pages for a long time. Gao has made this crossing eleven times. He knows what a smooth inspection looks like, and this is not one. The harbourmaster himself appeared twenty minutes ago, arms folded, and spoke quietly to the clerk. Two soldiers from the Maritime Trade Superintendency now stand at the gangway of Gao's junk, preventing his crew from unloading. The Superintendency has been seizing ships all season — four Fujianese traders lost their vessels last month for carrying undeclared musk. Gao's hold contains six hundred bolts of Cham silk, properly declared, but also forty jade cylinders he purchased in Champa and did not list. They sit beneath the silk in a false compartment his shipwright built in Palembang. The harbourmaster walks to Gao and speaks. He does not mention jade. He does not mention the false compartment. He says: "The Superintendency is purchasing your entire silk cargo for the imperial household. Full manifest value, plus fifteen percent for the requisition premium. Payment in silver, today. You are free to depart." Gao bows. The soldiers step aside. The jade stays in the hold, untouched and unexamined, as the silk is carried off the ship.

> A = Gao Wenli; G = clear customs without seizure of goods or ship; X = cargo seized / ship impounded (prolonged inspection, harbourmaster appeared, soldiers blocking gangway, season of seizures, undeclared jade in hidden compartment); ¬X = cargo cleared and purchased by the state at a premium, ship free to depart, contraband undiscovered. ✓ polar binary on seized-vs-cleared axis, ✓ expectation set before resolution, ✓ inversion acknowledged within event.

**R-Hi1-2.** "They will ratify it," Adams says. He is standing in the hall of the State House in Philadelphia, June 1776, watching delegates file toward the chamber. "Virginia has instructed its delegates to vote in favour. The resolution is Franklin's language and Jefferson's. South Carolina will follow Virginia — they always do. Maryland will not oppose." He counts on his fingers. "Nine colonies, perhaps ten. More than sufficient."

Dr. Rush, beside him, says nothing.

The doors open. A delegate from Pennsylvania — Dickinson — emerges. His face is set. He walks directly to Adams.

"The resolution is tabled," Dickinson says. "New York has no instructions from its assembly and will abstain. South Carolina objects to the preamble. Pennsylvania's delegation is split and cannot vote as a body. Maryland's delegates say their convention has not authorised them. You do not have nine colonies, Mr. Adams. You may not have seven."

Adams is still counting on his fingers. He folds his hand closed.

> A = Adams / pro-independence delegates; G = secure ratification of the independence resolution; X = ratified (nine or ten colonies counted, Virginia instructed, Franklin and Jefferson's language, South Carolina expected to follow); ¬X = resolution tabled (New York abstains, South Carolina objects, Pennsylvania split, Maryland unauthorised — fewer than seven in favour). ✓ polar binary on ratified-vs-tabled axis, ✓ expectation set before resolution, ✓ inversion acknowledged within event.

**R-Hi1-3.** I had written my will the night before and left it with my second, Lieutenant Marsh. The field at Bladensburg was soft from two days of rain. My opponent, Secretary Caldwell, was the finest shot in the capital — three duels, three men wounded, none killed only because he chose to aim for the shoulder. He did not extend such courtesies to men who had accused him of corruption in print, which I had done, by name, in the Federal Gazette. The surgeon had laid out his instruments on a cloth behind the tree line. I had told Marsh where to send my papers. The pistols were loaded. The count began. On "three," I raised my arm and fired wide — I have never been a marksman and I knew my only hope was that honour would be satisfied by the exchange itself. Caldwell levelled his barrel at my chest. I watched his hand. He held the position for three seconds, then raised the pistol skyward and discharged it into the air. "I will not shoot a man who cannot shoot," he said. Marsh exhaled behind me. I was still standing. The surgeon began packing his instruments.

> A = I (narrator / newspaper editor); G = survive the duel; X = killed or gravely wounded (opponent is finest shot in the capital, three prior duels, no mercy expected for printed accusations, will written, surgeon prepared); ¬X = survived unharmed (opponent fires into the air, duel concluded). ✓ polar binary on killed-vs-survived axis, ✓ expectation set before resolution, ✓ inversion acknowledged within event.

**R-Hi1-4.** The cargo manifest of the barque *Santa Teodora*, filed at the customs house in Seville, autumn of 1596, lists the following: three hundred and twelve crates of raw cacao, eighty-four bales of tobacco leaf, forty-one barrels of indigo dye, seventeen chests of worked silver plate. The assessed value, before the Crown's quinto, is nine hundred thousand maravedis. The ship departed Veracruz in convoy with six others and was separated in a storm off the Azores. The *Santa Teodora* has been given up for lost. The merchant house of Cabrera y Solis, which financed the voyage, has filed its claim with the underwriters. The partners have divided the loss among themselves, notified their creditors, and begun selling warehouse space to cover the shortfall. The harbourmaster's log for the port of Cadiz, dated fourteen days after the loss filing, contains a single entry in a different clerk's hand: "Arrived this day the barque *Santa Teodora*, Don Esteban Montilla master, cargo intact, hull sound. Separated from convoy in storm. Proceeded alone via Madeira. All hands well." The loss claim is void. The cargo is worth more than the partners calculated — the indigo market has risen sharply while the ship was missing, and the forty-one barrels are now valued at nearly double the original assessment.

> A = merchant house of Cabrera y Solis; G = profit from the *Santa Teodora*'s cargo; X = total loss (ship given up for lost, loss claim filed, creditors notified, warehouse space sold to cover shortfall); ¬X = cargo arrives intact and at higher value than expected (ship reached Cadiz, indigo market rose, barrels worth nearly double). ✓ polar binary on lost-vs-delivered axis, ✓ expectation set before resolution, ✓ inversion acknowledged within event.

**R-Hi1-5.** The abbot cannot sit still. He paces the cloister of the monastery at Luxeuil, 627, running his thumb along the edge of the letter he has just received from the bishop's secretary. The Frankish queen Balthild has petitioned the bishop to dissolve the monastery and redistribute its lands to three noble families who supported her husband's campaign in Burgundy. The bishop's secretary writes that the bishop is sympathetic — he has quarrelled with the abbot over the monastery's refusal to submit to diocesan authority for the past two years. The abbot's own monks have told him plainly that they expect dissolution; several have already written to other houses requesting admission. Brother Cassian, the cellarer, is cataloguing the monastery's moveable goods so they can be transported before the seizure. The abbot stops pacing. A rider is entering the gate. The man dismounts and presents a sealed letter — not from the bishop's secretary this time, but from the palace. The mayor of the palace has overruled the queen's petition. The monastery is to be granted a royal charter of protection, exempting it from both diocesan and noble claims in perpetuity. The mayor's letter states his reason in a single sentence: the monastery's scriptorium has been copying the legal codices his administration depends upon, and he will not permit its dissolution. The abbot reads the letter to Brother Cassian. Cassian sets down his inventory stylus.

> A = the abbot / monastery at Luxeuil; G = preserve the monastery; X = dissolved (queen petitioned dissolution, bishop sympathetic, monks expect it, some seeking admission elsewhere, cellarer cataloguing goods for transport); ¬X = granted royal charter of protection in perpetuity (mayor of the palace overrules queen, values scriptorium). ✓ polar binary on dissolved-vs-preserved axis, ✓ expectation set before resolution, ✓ inversion acknowledged within event.

## R-Hi4-1

The ambassador's carriage rattles through the gates of the Topkapi Palace, Constantinople, the spring of 1683. He carries in his dispatch case the formal declaration of war from the Habsburg Emperor — the document that will sever the fragile truce that has held for twenty years. His instructions from Vienna are explicit: deliver the declaration, accept no counter-offer, depart the city within forty-eight hours. He has already arranged passage on a Venetian galley from the Golden Horn. The Grand Vizier receives him in the Hall of the Divan. The ambassador places the sealed document on the table between them. The Grand Vizier does not open it. He places his own document beside it — a trade concession of extraordinary scope, granting Habsburg merchants preferential tariffs across six Ottoman ports, with a mutual non-aggression clause extending the truce by fifteen years. "Your emperor's letter may say what it says," the Grand Vizier tells him. "But my sultan's letter says this. Take both back to Vienna. Let your emperor choose which one he opens first." The ambassador looks at the two documents on the table. His instructions did not account for this.

## R-Hi4-2

"She is dead," the midwife says, pulling the linen over the stillborn child. It is a room in a merchant's house in Florence, the winter of 1497. The mother lies in the bed, grey-faced, bleeding. The physician has already left — called away, he said, though everyone in the room understood that he had judged the case beyond remedy and did not wish to be present for the end. The midwife sent for a priest an hour ago. The mother's sister kneels at the bedside, holding a hand that does not grip back. Then the infant beneath the linen makes a sound — not a cry, something smaller, a wet cough that moves the cloth. The midwife pulls back the sheet. The child is breathing. Its limbs are moving. The midwife clears its mouth with her smallest finger and the cough becomes a wail that fills the room. The mother's sister crosses herself. The priest arrives at the door and finds no one in need of last rites.

> A = midwife / household; G = save the child; X = child dead (declared stillborn, covered with linen, physician departed, priest summoned); ¬X = child alive and breathing. ✓ polar binary on dead-vs-alive axis, ✓ expectation set before resolution, ✓ inversion acknowledged within event.

## R-Hi4-3

I am standing at the dock in Liverpool, October 1848, and I can see the ship that will carry me to New York. The *Donaghmore*, a converted timber brig, lists slightly to port even at anchor. I have paid my passage — three pounds ten, everything I saved from eighteen months of dock labour after the blight took the farm in Roscommon. My name is on the manifest. My bundle is at my feet. The harbourmaster's clerk walks the length of the queue with a board under his arm, reading names and checking them against his list. He reaches me. He reads my name. He looks at me, then at his board, then at me again. "Vessel's full," he says. "Overbooked by thirty-one. Your name is on the supplement list, not the primary. No boarding today." He moves to the next person. The *Donaghmore* sails at four o'clock. I watch it go from the quay with my bundle still at my feet and three pounds ten gone.

> A = narrator (Irish emigrant); G = board the ship and emigrate to New York; X = passage secured (paid in full, name on manifest, bundle packed, standing at dock); ¬X = denied boarding, ship sails without him. ✓ polar binary on boards-vs-denied axis, ✓ expectation set before resolution, ✓ inversion acknowledged within event.

## R-Hi4-4

*From the record of the Provincial Court of Oyer and Terminer, Salem Village, Massachusetts, September 1692:*

*The accused, Goodwife Bridget Harmon, was brought before the court on the charge of practising witchcraft upon the person of Mercy Cole, aged fourteen. Three witnesses testified under oath: Mercy Cole herself, who described fits and visions; Thomas Putnam, who reported spectral evidence; and Reverend Hale, who stated that the accused had been seen gathering herbs at the crossroads by moonlight. The magistrate questioned each witness at length. The jury retired.*

*Upon their return, the foreman rose and declared the accused not guilty. The magistrate asked the foreman to confirm the verdict. He confirmed it. The court record notes that Mercy Cole, upon hearing the verdict, ceased her fits and sat quietly in her seat. Reverend Hale left the chamber without speaking. The accused was released into the custody of her husband and walked from the courthouse before noon.*

> A = Goodwife Bridget Harmon; G = survive the witchcraft trial; X = convicted (three sworn witnesses, spectral evidence presented, afflicted girl's testimony, Puritan court context where convictions were routine); ¬X = acquitted, released before noon. ✓ polar binary on convicted-vs-acquitted axis, ✓ expectation set before resolution, ✓ inversion acknowledged within event.

## R-Hi4-5

The emperor's physician presses two fingers to the vein in the imperial wrist. It is the Forbidden City, Beijing, the late autumn of 1722. The Kangxi Emperor has not risen from his bed in four days. His breath is shallow. The physician has administered deer-antler tonic and ginseng broth to no effect. The court astronomers have been consulted; they reported unfavourable alignments. The Bureau of Rites has begun — quietly, without formal announcement — preparing the mourning protocols. Bolts of white silk have been moved from the imperial storehouses to the outer hall. The heir, Yinzhen, has been summoned from his estate and waits in the anteroom, dressed in travelling clothes he has not changed since his arrival two days ago. The physician lifts his fingers from the emperor's wrist. The pulse is stronger than it was this morning. He calls for broth again. The emperor opens his eyes, fixes them on the physician, and speaks — clearly, in full voice — for the first time in four days. He asks what day it is. He asks for his ministers. Yinzhen, in the anteroom, hears his father's voice through the wall and sits down slowly on the bench, still holding the riding gloves he has been turning in his hands since he arrived.

> A = the imperial household / physician; G = preserve the emperor's life; X = emperor dies (bedridden four days, treatments ineffective, mourning protocols initiated, white silk prepared, heir summoned); ¬X = emperor revives, speaks clearly, summons ministers. ✓ polar binary on dies-vs-recovers axis, ✓ expectation set before resolution, ✓ inversion acknowledged within event.

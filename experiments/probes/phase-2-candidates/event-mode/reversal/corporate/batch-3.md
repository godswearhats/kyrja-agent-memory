## R-Co3-1

The performance improvement plan had been in Delgado's personnel file for sixty days. His manager had documented every missed deadline, every escalated complaint, every one-on-one where targets were reviewed and not met. HR had countersigned. Legal had reviewed the termination paperwork and returned it cleared. His manager was holding the printout when she entered the conference room on the fourth floor — the small one, windowless, the one they used for these conversations. Delgado was already seated. He slid a folded document across the table before she could open the folder. She unfolded it. It was a signed offer letter: VP of Analytics at a direct competitor, effective two weeks from Friday. His notice of resignation. She looked at the termination paperwork in her folder and then set the folder on the table without opening it.

## R-Co3-3

*Subject: Re: Audit Findings — Preliminary Read*
*Sent: Thursday, 9:14 AM*

As of this morning I have reviewed the preliminary findings from the external audit team. I have briefed outside counsel and they concur with that range.

*From: J. Hargrove, Chief Compliance Officer*
*To: Executive Leadership Team*
*Subject: Re: Re: Audit Findings — Update from 2 PM Session*
*Sent: Thursday, 3:47 PM*

Please disregard the penalty range in my earlier message. The auditors presented a revised finding set this afternoon. The vendor classification items fall within a previously issued safe-harbour guidance issued in March of last year, which the audit team had not initially applied to our specific entity structure. The revised finding: no civil penalty. No consent order. Remediation on three minor procedural items only, no monitoring requirement. Outside counsel is reviewing the session notes now but their initial read is consistent with the auditors' revised conclusion.

## R-Co3-5

The injunction was the easy part — everyone in the room agreed on that. Meridian's legal team has spent eleven months on this case. The contract clause is airtight. The opposing counsel filed a procedurally deficient response. Judge Okafor has ruled in Meridian's favour on every preliminary motion. The courtroom is silent as the clerk reads the order into the record. Then the silence extends past where it should end. Meridian's lead counsel looks at the page in her hands. The injunction has been denied. The judge, in a two-paragraph addendum not previewed in any prior order, has found that Meridian's enforcement of the clause would constitute an impermissible restraint on trade under a 2019 appellate precedent that none of the briefs cited and that no one in the room had apparently considered. She is still looking at the page. Her co-counsel has opened his laptop and is already searching case law, as if there is something there he missed.

> A = Meridian / Meridian's legal team; G = obtain the injunction; X = injunction granted (eleven months of preparation, airtight clause, deficient response from opposing counsel, won every prior motion); ¬X = injunction denied on grounds no party had briefed. ✓ polar binary on granted-vs-denied axis, ✓ expectation set before resolution, ✓ inversion acknowledged within event.

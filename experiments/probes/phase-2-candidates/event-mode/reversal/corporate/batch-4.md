# Reversal × Corporate — Batch 4 (Tier A)

## R-Co4-1

The retention offer sits in Priya's inbox, drafted, approved by the CHRO, and waiting for her digital signature. Nolan, the senior architect, has already accepted a position at a competitor — he told his skip-level yesterday morning, and his LinkedIn shows the new title in draft. The counter is generous: a twenty-percent raise, accelerated vesting on his remaining equity, and a title bump to Principal. Priya has been told by three people, independently, that Nolan will not even open the email. She sends it anyway, at 4:47 PM, because due diligence requires it. At 5:02 PM, Nolan is standing in her office doorway. He has already retracted his acceptance of the external offer. He has seen the retention package — someone on the comp team mentioned the equity acceleration — and says he will sign before end of business. It is not the money, he tells her. It is the title. He has wanted Principal for two years and no one offered it until now.

> A = Priya / company; G = retain Nolan; X = Nolan leaves (accepted competitor offer, told skip-level, counter considered futile by three sources); ¬X = Nolan stays and signs the retention package. ✓ polar binary on leaves-vs-stays axis, ✓ expectation set before resolution, ✓ inversion acknowledged within event.

## R-Co4-2

"The integration is done," Rivas says. She pulls up the deployment dashboard on the conference room screen. Every status indicator is green. The data migration completed at 03:00. QA signed off at 06:30. The client's own technical lead ran validation queries for two hours and confirmed parity with the legacy system. The go-live email is queued in Rivas's drafts, waiting for her to press send. She reaches for the trackpad.

"Hold," says Kwon, the client's CTO, from the speakerphone. There is paper shuffling. "We received a cease-and-desist this morning from Arcline Systems. They're claiming the middleware layer you built infringes on a patent they filed in 2021. Our general counsel says we cannot go live until this is resolved. I'm instructing my team to roll back to the legacy system by end of day."

The dashboard is still green. Rivas closes the laptop lid.

> A = Rivas / integration team; G = go live with the new system; X = successful launch (migration complete, QA signed off, client validated, all indicators green); ¬X = rollback ordered, go-live blocked by cease-and-desist. ✓ polar binary on live-vs-rolled-back axis, ✓ expectation set before resolution, ✓ inversion acknowledged within event.

## R-Co4-3

I had rehearsed the pitch nine times. The slides were tight — fourteen cards, no animations, every number sourced. Our Series A lead had reviewed the deck and called it the strongest he had seen from a seed-stage company. The partner meeting at Foundry Ventures was the last gate. Three partners in the room, all engaged, all nodding. One of them asked about unit economics and I answered from memory: LTV-to-CAC of 4.2, payback in seven months, net dollar retention at 118 percent. I could feel the momentum in the room. Then the managing partner, who had been quiet, leaned forward and said: "We passed on this category eighteen months ago and we are not reopening it. The meeting was taken as a courtesy to your lead investor. I should have communicated that before you came in. I apologize for wasting your time." The other two partners did not look at me. They looked at him. They had not known either.

> A = narrator (founder); G = secure investment from Foundry Ventures; X = investment likely (strong deck, Series A lead endorsed it, partners engaged and nodding, metrics answered confidently); ¬X = firm had pre-decided to pass, meeting was a courtesy. ✓ polar binary on funded-vs-passed axis, ✓ expectation set before resolution, ✓ inversion acknowledged within event.

## R-Co4-4

The recall notice has been drafted, printed, and stacked in the shipping department. Two hundred and fourteen thousand units of the Model 9 infant car seat — the one with the latch defect identified in the Tuesday stress test. The VP of product safety has already called the CPSC liaison to provide advance notice. The CEO has blocked ninety minutes on tomorrow's calendar for a press briefing. The head of manufacturing is in the conference room with the stress-test engineer, reviewing the failure footage frame by frame, when the engineer pauses the video and says: "This is the wrong fixture." He rewinds. Points at the mounting bracket in the frame. "This is the Model 7 fixture. Someone loaded the wrong part into the jig. The Model 9 latch is not the component that failed." The manufacturing head calls the lab. They re-run the test with the correct fixture in eleven minutes. The latch holds at 140 percent of rated load. He picks up the phone to call the VP of product safety. The recall notice is still on the shipping-room table.

> A = company / VP of product safety; G = ensure product safety (determine whether recall is necessary); X = recall proceeding (defect confirmed, notice printed, CPSC notified, press briefing scheduled); ¬X = no defect exists, recall cancelled (wrong fixture used in test). ✓ polar binary on defective-vs-non-defective axis, ✓ expectation set before resolution, ✓ inversion acknowledged within event.

## R-Co4-5

*MEETING MINUTES — Compensation Committee, Board of Directors*
*Riverbend Health Systems*
*Called to order: 10:00 AM, Tuesday*

*Agenda item: CEO annual performance review and compensation determination.*

*Chair Whitfield opened by noting that Riverbend's operating margin declined for the third consecutive quarter, that patient satisfaction scores fell below the 40th percentile nationally, and that the CEO's own performance dashboard — submitted per the board's request — showed five of seven strategic objectives marked incomplete or behind schedule. Whitfield stated that the committee's preliminary recommendation, circulated to members in advance, was non-renewal of the CEO's contract at the end of its current term in August.*

*Discussion followed. Director Acheson asked for the floor. He presented data not included in the preliminary packet: a signed letter of intent from MedAlliance Network, the region's largest payer, offering an exclusive five-year partnership valued at approximately $340 million in guaranteed annual volume. The LOI was contingent on continuity of current executive leadership. Director Acheson confirmed that MedAlliance's CEO had specified this condition in writing. The committee voted 4-1 to renew the CEO's contract for a three-year term with a revised incentive structure tied to the MedAlliance benchmarks.*

*Adjourned: 10:52 AM.*

> A = CEO / compensation committee; G = determine CEO's continued tenure; X = contract non-renewal (three declining quarters, low satisfaction scores, five of seven objectives missed, committee recommended non-renewal); ¬X = contract renewed for three years (MedAlliance LOI contingent on leadership continuity). ✓ polar binary on non-renewed-vs-renewed axis, ✓ expectation set before resolution, ✓ inversion acknowledged within event.

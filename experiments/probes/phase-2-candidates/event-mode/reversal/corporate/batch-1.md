# Reversal × Corporate — Batch 1 (Tier A)

**R-Co1-1.** The integration team files into the war room at seven in the morning, coffee already cold. The whiteboard still shows yesterday's status: fourteen critical blockers, three vendor APIs returning errors, the data migration stuck at eleven percent. The CTO sent a company-wide email at midnight calling the ERP cutover "nonviable on the current timeline" and recommending the board approve a six-month delay. The project sponsor has not returned messages since Friday. Asha, the integration lead, opens her laptop to draft the rollback plan. Her inbox loads. The top message is from the CTO, timestamped 5:47 a.m.: "Disregard my prior email. Spoke with Nakamura at Veriton overnight. They are deploying a full technical team on-site starting today, no charge, through go-live. Migration is at ninety-three percent as of ten minutes ago — their engineers ran it overnight. We are cutting over on schedule. Cancel the rollback. Board has been informed." Asha reads it aloud to the room. Nobody moves for several seconds. Then someone uncaps a dry-erase marker and starts clearing the whiteboard.

**R-Co1-2.** "I need you to understand something," Claire says, leaning forward across the table. "This is not a negotiation. The acquirer's due diligence is done. The letter of intent is signed. Legal is drafting closing documents right now. Your retention package is already in the system — three years, full acceleration on your equity. You are the reason they are buying us."

Tomás nods. He has heard this twice already today, from Claire and from the acquirer's VP of engineering.

His phone buzzes. He glances at it, then sets it face-down on the table.

"That was the acquirer's CEO," he says. "They are pulling out. Their board rejected the deal this morning. Something about our patent portfolio — it doesn't cover what they thought it covered." He pauses. "She said they found out an hour ago."

Claire is still leaning forward. Her mouth is open but she has stopped talking.

> A = Claire / the company; G = close the acquisition; X = deal closes (LOI signed, due diligence complete, legal drafting, retention packages loaded); ¬X = acquirer pulls out (board rejected deal over patent gap). ✓ polar binary on deal-closes-vs-deal-collapses axis, ✓ expectation set before resolution, ✓ inversion acknowledged within event.

**R-Co1-3.** I had cleaned out my desk the night before. Badge in the top drawer, laptop wiped, personal items in a canvas bag by the door. HR had scheduled the meeting for nine — the same conference room they always used for terminations, the one with no windows and the box of tissues on the credenza. My manager had avoided eye contact for two weeks. The skip-level had stopped including me on the roadmap threads. I knew the script: PIP failed, role eliminated, here is your severance agreement. I sat down across from the HR director and my manager. The HR director opened a folder. "We are here to discuss your promotion to principal engineer," she said. "Effective Monday. The committee approved it unanimously last Thursday. Your manager recommended you." I looked at my manager. He was smiling. He had not been avoiding me. He had been keeping the secret.

> A = I (narrator / engineer); G = keep my job; X = terminated (desk cleaned out, PIP assumed, termination-room booking, manager avoiding eye contact); ¬X = promoted to principal engineer, unanimous committee approval. ✓ polar binary on fired-vs-promoted axis, ✓ expectation set before resolution, ✓ inversion acknowledged within event.

**R-Co1-4.** The compliance officer is reading the audit summary on her second monitor while the team watches. Fortuna Capital's annual regulatory examination is in nine days. Every prior internal review this quarter has come back clean — three separate passes, each signed off by a different senior analyst. The external counsel they retained in January described their position as "bulletproof." The general counsel told the partners last week that the examination would be pro forma. The compliance officer scrolls to the appendix. She stops. She reads a paragraph, then reads it again. She picks up her desk phone and dials the general counsel's extension. "Page forty-seven," she says. "The fund's secondary trading desk has been booking collateral transfers as client deposits since March. Every quarterly filing we submitted this year is wrong. We need to self-report before the examination, and we need to do it today."

> A = Fortuna Capital / compliance team; G = pass the regulatory examination cleanly; X = clean examination (three internal reviews clean, external counsel said "bulletproof," GC called it pro forma); ¬X = material misstatement discovered, must self-report before examination. ✓ polar binary on clean-vs-compromised axis, ✓ expectation set before resolution, ✓ inversion acknowledged within event.

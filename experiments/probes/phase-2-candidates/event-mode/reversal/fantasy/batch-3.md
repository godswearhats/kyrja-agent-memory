# Reversal × Fantasy — Batch 3 (Tier C)

## R-Fa3-1

The trial by ordeal lasts until the iron cools. Every guild member in the hall knows this. Aldric has held the bar for nearly a full count of sixty — longer than anyone has managed in living memory — and the bar is still faintly glowing where his palms grip it. The Guildmaster's scribe is already dipping her quill. The charter of mastery is laid out on the bench beside her, ready for the seal. Aldric's apprentices are pressed together at the back of the hall, barely breathing. Then the bar goes dark and Aldric sets it down, and the Guildmaster steps forward to pronounce the verdict — and stops. The bar is still warm. The test has a second condition, rarely invoked: the candidate's hands must show no mark. Aldric turns his palms up. The skin across both hands has blistered in the pattern of the bar's grip. The Guildmaster looks at the scribe. She caps her quill without having written a word. The hall remains silent.

## R-Fa3-2

"You're certain it's the heir?" Mira asks.

The hedge-witch nods. She has read the same casting three times before sending for the Commander. The star-charts do not lie about bloodlines, and the child in the farmhouse two leagues east carries the old king's mark behind her left ear — the birthmark that only the royal line carries and that the usurper's men have been hunting for across six provinces for the better part of a year. "Certain," the hedge-witch says. "Take her before dawn and the succession holds. The lords will rally."

Mira rides before the second bell. She dismounts at the farmhouse door and pounds on it, and the woman who opens it is already weeping before any words are spoken. The child is dead. Fever, the woman says. Three days ago. The grave is fresh behind the barn. Mira stands in the doorway with the succession hanging from her hands like cut rope.

## R-Fa3-3

I have been refused twice. I know what the third petition looks like before I file it: the Conclave's seal in red wax, the single word DENIED in the registrar's hand, no grounds given. The Conclave has declined every petition for independent practice from a mage not born in the nine chartered cities for as long as the records go back, and I was born in a village that doesn't appear on any charter. My advocate told me to prepare for the result. I filed anyway because the law says I may, not because I expected anything different. I am in the waiting room on the lower floor when the door at the end of the corridor opens. It is not the registrar's clerk who comes through it. It is the Arch-Convener himself. He walks the length of the corridor toward me and stops and says: "The Conclave has voted to grant your petition. There are conditions attached. Come inside." I have my prepared reply to the denial already forming in my throat and I have to stop myself from speaking it.

## R-Fa3-4

The siege has held for forty days. Lord Casven's granaries are empty — the garrison has been on half-rations since the third week — and his scouts have not returned from the eastern pass in nine days, which can mean only one thing: the relief column from Aldenmoor was intercepted. The walls are sound but the men behind them are not. Casven has called his captains together to discuss terms of surrender. The herald is being prepared; the white pennant is folded on the table beside the maps. Then the watchman on the north tower shouts, and the shout carries down through three floors of stone. Not the sound of surrender. The captains push to the window. Below the ridge to the north, torchlight stretches in a line that reaches the tree line and keeps going — thousands of them, bearing Aldenmoor's black chevron. The relief column had not been intercepted. It had been delayed by flood at the Greyvern crossing. Casven looks at the white pennant on the table. One of his captains picks it up and puts it away.

> A = Lord Casven / garrison; G = defend the keep and receive relief; X = surrender (granaries empty, relief assumed intercepted, captains called to discuss terms, herald prepared); ¬X = relief column arrives, siege broken. ✓ polar binary on surrendered-vs-relieved axis, ✓ expectation set before resolution, ✓ inversion acknowledged within event.

## R-Fa3-5

*From the Testimony of Brother Aldous, Keeper of the Temple Records, given before the High Synod, Fifteenth Day of the Month of Oaths:*

I administered the Rite of Naming to the candidate at the third bell as prescribed. The candidate — a novice of four years, in good standing, recommended by three senior brothers — had completed the seven preparatory fasts and entered the sanctum in the prescribed state of readiness. I have administered this rite one hundred and twelve times. The flame of the temple lamp has never failed to take the novice's name. I have no record of it failing. I placed the candidate's sealed name-cloth at the base of the lamp as prescribed, and I spoke the words of the rite, and I waited. The flame did not move. I waited the full permitted interval. The flame did not move. The name-cloth did not burn. The candidate has therefore not been named by the temple, and their four years of novitiate are, under temple law, voided. I have searched the records of the last two centuries and found no prior instance of this outcome. I record it here without interpretation.

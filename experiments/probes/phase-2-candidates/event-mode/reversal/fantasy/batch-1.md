# Reversal × Fantasy — Batch 1 (Tier A)

**R-Fa1-1.** The condemned knight kneels on the stone floor of the great hall, wrists bound behind his back with iron chain. The High Justiciar has read the charges — treason against the Amber Throne, conspiracy with the Northern Reaches, forging the royal seal on letters intercepted by the Crown's couriers. Three witnesses testified against him this morning. The king's own scribe recorded the verdict: death by the headsman's blade at dawn. The executioner is already waiting in the courtyard, axe oiled, block set on the flagstones. The Justiciar raises his hand for silence. He unrolls a second scroll — this one bearing the king's personal cipher, wax still warm. He reads: "By our hand and seal, the prisoner Aldric of Thornwall is granted full pardon and restored to his rank and holdings. The witnesses who spoke against him are to be arrested. Their testimony was purchased by the Duc of Maren, whose own letters — bearing his true seal, not a forgery — were found this morning in the chambers of the third witness. The conspiracy was against Aldric, not by him." The Justiciar lowers the scroll. The executioner in the courtyard is not informed yet. But the chains on Aldric's wrists are already being unlocked.

> A = Aldric of Thornwall; G = survive / avoid execution; X = executed at dawn (charges read, three witnesses testified, verdict recorded, executioner waiting); ¬X = full pardon and restoration (king's cipher reveals witnesses were suborned, conspiracy was against Aldric). ✓ polar binary on condemned-vs-pardoned axis, ✓ expectation set before resolution, ✓ inversion acknowledged within event.

**R-Fa1-2.** "The spring is dead," Lira says. She holds the dowsing stone over the basin and it hangs motionless — no pull, no warmth, no light in its core. The village elders stand around her in the stone circle at the base of the hill. They have seen this before. Twice in living memory, the Wellspring beneath the hill has gone silent, and both times the village emptied within a season. The grain stores will last forty days. The nearest water source that can sustain a settlement this size is eleven days' march east, across the Ashfeld. Elder Goss has already distributed the migration writs — every household received one this morning, stamped with the council's seal.

"We walk at first light," Goss says.

Lira is about to lower the stone when it lurches in her hand. She nearly drops it. The basin beneath it begins to hum. Water rises — not seeping but surging, clear and cold, flooding the channels cut into the stone circle's floor. The dowsing stone blazes white. More water than the spring has ever produced. Lira holds the stone steady with both hands and watches the water run downhill toward the village cisterns.

"Put the writs away," she says.

> A = Lira / village; G = sustain the village (maintain water supply); X = village abandoned (Wellspring dead, dowsing stone inert, grain stores for 40 days only, migration writs distributed, departure at dawn); ¬X = Wellspring surges back stronger than ever, village sustained. ✓ polar binary on abandoned-vs-sustained axis, ✓ expectation set before resolution, ✓ inversion acknowledged within event.

**R-Fa1-3.** I carried the staff up the tower stairs with both hands because I knew it would be taken from me at the top. The Circle of Vethane convenes only to strip an adept of their rank, and they had convened for me. I had failed the third trial — the trial of binding — for the second time. No adept in the Circle's four-hundred-year history had failed it twice and remained. My mentor, Archmage Dessa, had told me plainly the evening before: "I cannot protect you. The vote is already done. They will break your staff and dissolve your sigil." I reached the top of the stairs. The seven archmages sat in the crescent formation used for expulsions. Dessa would not look at me. The First Seat spoke. "Adept Corin. The Circle has reviewed the residue of your second binding trial." She paused. "You did not fail it. The trial construct was flawed. Archmage Vendel has confirmed that the binding matrix was mislaid — it was set to the parameters of a master-level examination, not an adept's. Under correct parameters, your binding holds. Your rank stands. Your staff remains whole." I looked at Dessa. She was looking at me now. She had not known.

> A = I (Corin, adept); G = retain rank and staff in the Circle of Vethane; X = expelled (failed third trial twice, no precedent for remaining, mentor said she cannot protect, vote already done, expulsion formation seated); ¬X = rank retained (trial construct was flawed, binding actually holds under correct parameters). ✓ polar binary on expelled-vs-retained axis, ✓ expectation set before resolution, ✓ inversion acknowledged within event.

**R-Fa1-4.** The siege engines fall silent at noon. Lord Calder stands on the inner wall of Dunmere Keep and counts what remains: forty-one defenders, nine arrows per quiver, no pitch, no oil, the well contaminated since the sappers breached the eastern foundation three days ago. The gate has been shored with timber, but the timber is split and will not hold another ram strike. The herald from the besieging army came at dawn and offered terms: surrender the keep and its garrison walks free. Calder's captain told him, quietly, that the men would accept. Calder himself has composed the surrender in his head — the words, the sequence, the handing over of the key. He opens his mouth to call for the white banner. A horn sounds from the northern ridge. Not the enemy's horn. The banner that crests the ridge carries the silver stag of the Warden of the Northern March — two thousand riders in column, already wheeling to strike the besieging army's unprotected flank. The enemy camp is scrambling. The siege engines are unmanned. Calder closes his mouth. He does not call for the banner.

> A = Lord Calder / Dunmere garrison; G = hold Dunmere Keep; X = surrender (41 defenders, 9 arrows each, no pitch, contaminated well, gate failing, surrender terms offered, captain said men would accept, Calder composing surrender); ¬X = keep held (relief force of 2,000 riders arrives and strikes besieging army's flank). ✓ polar binary on surrender-vs-hold axis, ✓ expectation set before resolution, ✓ inversion acknowledged within event.

**R-Fa1-5.** TRANSCRIPT OF THE GUILD TRIBUNAL, HALL OF THE SILVER TRADES, YEAR OF THE FROST CRANE

PRESIDING: Guildmaster Orren Voss, First Chair of the Tribunal.

SUBJECT: Petition of Journeyman Sable Wick for elevation to Master Smith.

GUILDMASTER VOSS: The examiners have reviewed the candidate's masterwork — a folded-steel long blade presented to the Tribunal this morning. Examiner Brune, your assessment.

EXAMINER BRUNE: The blade fails. The fold pattern is irregular in the lower third. The tang alignment deviates beyond tolerance. I have seen apprentice work with cleaner geometry. My recommendation is rejection.

EXAMINER DALLA: I concur with Brune. The piece does not meet the standard.

EXAMINER GHELT: Also rejection. Three unanimous.

GUILDMASTER VOSS: Journeyman Wick, the Tribunal's recommendation is unanimous rejection. Before I enter the finding, tradition permits me to inspect the piece myself. Bring me the blade.

[PAUSE IN RECORD]

GUILDMASTER VOSS: Strike the examiners' recommendation. This blade has been forged with twin-core lamination — a technique I have not seen executed successfully since Mastersmith Oren the Elder, forty years ago. The fold irregularity Brune identified is the seam where two different steels meet. It is not a flaw; it is the signature of a method none of you recognised. The tang deviation exists because the blade was forged as a single piece with the guard — there is no joint. Journeyman Wick, you are elevated to Master Smith, effective upon this record. Examiners, you will study this blade before you assess another.

> A = Sable Wick; G = achieve elevation to Master Smith; X = rejected (three examiners unanimously recommended rejection, cited irregular folds, tang deviation, compared to apprentice work); ¬X = elevated to Master Smith (Guildmaster identifies twin-core lamination technique, overrides examiners). ✓ polar binary on rejected-vs-elevated axis, ✓ expectation set before resolution, ✓ inversion acknowledged within event.

# Reversal × Fantasy — Batch 5 (Tier B)

**R-Fa5-1.** The healer kneels beside the cot where the young queen lies still, skin grey as river clay. The court physicians withdrew at dawn, each in turn pressing the queen's wrist, listening, and shaking their heads. The high priestess has already begun the death rites in the chapel below — the chanting rises faintly through the stone floor. Two handmaidens are preparing the white burial linens on the side table. The king stands at the window with his back to the room because he cannot look at her anymore. The healer — a woman from the fenlands whom the stablemaster brought in without permission — places a poultice of black moss across the queen's throat and waits. One minute. Two. The queen's chest lifts. A breath. Then another. Then a cough — wet, ugly, living. The healer sits back on her heels. The chanting below has not stopped, but it no longer applies. The king turns from the window. The handmaidens set the burial linens aside.

**R-Fa5-2.** "The gate holds," Captain Leoth says, but she does not believe it herself. The ram has struck nine times. The iron bands across the oak are bent inward. The crossbeam is cracked down its centre grain, and she can see daylight through the split when the ram pulls back. Her garrison is thirty-one soldiers — what remains after the sortie that failed at dawn. The enemy drums have not stopped since yesterday. She turns to her lieutenant. "When it breaks, form on the inner stair. We hold the keep as long as we—"

The drums stop.

Leoth stares at the gate. The ram does not strike a tenth time. Through the arrow slit, she sees the siege line pulling back — not regrouping, but withdrawing. Wagons turning. Banners dropping. A rider comes forward under a white branch. Her lieutenant reads the message aloud: the enemy duke is dead. Killed in his own camp by a dispute among his captains. The army is breaking apart. The siege is over.

Leoth puts her hand on the cracked crossbeam. It is still warm from the impact.

**R-Fa5-3.** I had come to steal the stone. Three months I spent worming my way into the Archivist's trust — carrying his books, grinding his inks, sleeping on the floor outside his study like a dog. The stone sat in its lead-lined casket on the third shelf, and I knew its schedule: the Archivist opened the casket once each new moon to verify the seal, and left the study unlocked for exactly the time it took him to walk to the privy and back. Tonight was the new moon. I waited behind the curtain and listened to his footsteps fade down the corridor. I opened the casket. Inside was a folded parchment. Written on it in the Archivist's hand: *I removed the stone this morning. I have known your purpose since the second week. Present yourself to the Warden at dawn, or I will send the guard to find you.* I stood there with the empty casket in my hands. The footsteps were already coming back.

> A = I (the thief); G = steal the stone from the Archivist's study; X = theft succeeds (three months of infiltration, learned the schedule, waited for the new-moon opening, casket unlocked); ¬X = theft fails completely (stone already removed, thief's purpose known all along, capture imminent). ✓ polar binary on succeeds-vs-fails axis, ✓ expectation set before resolution, ✓ inversion acknowledged within event.

**R-Fa5-4.** *Let it be recorded before the Elder Trees of the Thornwood Conclave, in the season of the Frost Hart:*

*The ranger Saelith Dawnmere petitioned the Conclave for the right to bond a gryphon from the Northern Aerie, as is permitted under the Old Accord to any ranger who has completed twelve seasons of border service. Saelith has completed fourteen. Her petition was accompanied by three sworn testimonies of valour from senior wardens, a clean record of conduct, and the endorsement of the Aerie keeper herself, who stated before the Conclave that she had already selected a fledgling for the bonding.*

*After deliberation, the Conclave denies the petition. Elder Rowanmere invokes the Clause of Contested Blood: Saelith's maternal line traces to the Ashwood exiles, who were stripped of bonding rights in the age before the current Accord and whose restoration was never formally ratified. Until the bloodline question is resolved by a plenary session of all seven Conclaves — which has not been convened in living memory — no bonding may proceed.*

*Saelith Dawnmere received the ruling in the grove. She did not speak. The fledgling remains in the Aerie.*

**R-Fa5-5.** The executioner's apprentice is oiling the axe when the cell door opens. The condemned man — a merchant named Pol, convicted of selling poisoned grain — has been sitting against the wall since his sentencing three days ago, eating nothing. The guards come to bring him to the block. Pol stands. He has already given away his boots and his belt. He follows them down the corridor and into the yard where the block stands in pale morning light, the headsman waiting beside it, the magistrate reading the writ aloud to a thin crowd. Pol kneels. The headsman lifts the axe. A horn sounds from the gate. The magistrate stops reading. A mounted courier pushes through the crowd, holding a sealed scroll above his head. The magistrate breaks the seal, reads, and reads again. The Duke has granted a full pardon. New evidence — testimony from the grain supplier's own apprentice — has established that the poison entered the stores after Pol's last delivery. The magistrate rolls the scroll closed and tells the headsman to step back. Pol is still kneeling when they pull him to his feet.

> A = Pol (the condemned merchant); G = survive / avoid execution; X = executed (convicted, sentencing three days ago, given away belongings, brought to block, headsman axe raised, writ being read); ¬X = pardoned (Duke's full pardon, new exculpatory evidence). ✓ polar binary on executed-vs-pardoned axis, ✓ expectation set before resolution, ✓ inversion acknowledged within event.

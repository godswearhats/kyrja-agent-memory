# Reversal × Fantasy — Batch 4 (Tier A)

## R-Fa4-1

The healer kneels over the boy in the temple infirmary, pressing her hands against his chest. The fever has held for six days. His skin is the colour of tallow. The temple's senior physician examined him at dawn and told the boy's mother, in the corridor outside, that the illness had reached the blood and that no herb or prayer would draw it back. The mother has already sent word to the boy's father in the northern provinces — a rider left before first light, carrying the message that his son is dying. The healer knows all of this. She also knows that the salve she is applying — rendered from blackthorn root and crushed silverleaf, a preparation the senior physician dismissed as peasant folklore — has never been tested on blood-fever. She applies it because she has nothing else. Within the hour, the boy's breathing slows from its ragged shudder to a steady rhythm. His colour returns. By the time the temple bells ring for midday prayer, he is sitting up and asking for water. The senior physician stands in the doorway and says nothing. The rider is already two hours north and cannot be recalled.

## R-Fa4-2

"You cannot enter," the door-warden says. He holds a staff crosswise before the gate of the Convocation Hall. "The Archmage sealed the session at moonrise. No petitioners until the matter of succession is resolved."

"I am not a petitioner," Sorel answers. "I am third-seat on the inner council. My name is on the charter."

"Your name was on the charter," the door-warden says. "The Archmage struck it at the opening of tonight's session. You are removed from the council, effective immediately. Revocation of your tower quarters and library privileges follows at dawn."

Sorel does not step back. She can hear voices inside, muffled by the stone. She came tonight expecting to cast the deciding vote on the succession — her faction holds three of the five seats, and the candidate she has championed for two years stands ready to assume the title. Instead she stands outside a door she can no longer open, stripped of the seat that gave her vote its weight.

## R-Fa4-3

I had brought the offering cup filled to the brim — goat's blood, wild sage, three drops of my own taken from the left wrist as the rite prescribes. The Oracle of Keth had not spoken a true prophecy in eleven years. The priests said the god had gone silent. Pilgrims had stopped coming. The temple's coffers were empty and the high priestess had petitioned the king to dissolve the order. I came not because I believed the Oracle would speak but because my mother had asked it of me before she died, and I had promised. I knelt before the stone basin and poured. The liquid hissed where it struck the bowl. Then a voice — not from the priestess, not from any throat I could see — filled the chamber like water fills a hull. It spoke nine words in the old tongue. The priestess dropped to her knees. She was weeping. The Oracle had answered.

> A = narrator (pilgrim); G = fulfill a personal obligation by making an offering (not expecting a response); X = Oracle remains silent (eleven years mute, god reportedly departed, order near dissolution); ¬X = Oracle speaks. ✓ polar binary on silent-vs-speaks axis, ✓ expectation set before resolution, ✓ inversion acknowledged within event.

## R-Fa4-4

The siege tower burns. Lord Varek watches from the ridge as his engineers' best construction — three months of cutting, hauling, and riveting under arrow-fire — collapses into a heap of flaming timber against the outer wall of Thorngate. His archers have exhausted their supply. The ram was abandoned in the ditch two days ago, its iron head sunk in mud. His scouts have reported a relief column from the south, four days out at most. Varek has already dictated the terms of his withdrawal to his scribe — an orderly retreat northward before the relief force arrives, preserving what remains of his army. His lieutenant approaches at a run. He is carrying something: a white pennant, taken from an arrow shot over the wall. Attached to it is a strip of vellum bearing the seal of Thorngate's lord commander. It reads: *We yield. Open the gate at dawn. Spare the garrison.* Varek reads it twice. Behind him, the siege tower is still burning.

> A = Lord Varek; G = take Thorngate; X = siege fails (siege tower destroyed, arrows spent, ram lost, relief column approaching, withdrawal dictated); ¬X = Thorngate surrenders. ✓ polar binary on fails-vs-succeeds axis, ✓ expectation set before resolution, ✓ inversion acknowledged within event.

## R-Fa4-5

*Inscribed on the wall of the Hall of Trials, Citadel of the Sworn Order, in the hand of the Keeper of Records:*

*On the seventh day of the Ember Moon, the novice called Asha of Dunmoor presented herself for the Rite of Binding, the final trial required for admission to the Sworn Order. She had completed every prior trial with distinction — the Trial of Silence, the Trial of the Forge, and the Trial of Descent — accumulating the highest marks awarded in nine years. The assembled masters considered her passage through the Rite of Binding to be a formality. The Grandmaster herself had spoken publicly of Asha's readiness.*

*The Rite commenced at the fourth bell. Asha entered the binding circle and spoke the invocation. The circle did not answer. She spoke it again, correctly, with proper cadence and intonation — the Keeper of Rites confirmed this from his station. The circle remained dark. After the prescribed three attempts, the Grandmaster rose and spoke the words of dismissal. Asha of Dunmoor was escorted from the hall. Per the charter of the Order, the Rite of Binding may not be attempted a second time. Her candidacy is concluded.*

The duel is fought at the edge of the Silvenmere at dawn, the water still as slate. Aldric has beaten every challenger in the Swordsmen's Circle since his enrollment three years ago — the masters say he has never been touched in formal bout — and Calla has watched him fight seven times from the gallery. She knows how he moves. She also knows what the Circle's charter says: a challenger who draws the Warden's blood before her own is shed may claim the Warden's rank without the senior masters' confirmation. Aldric salutes her with his blade, and his expression does not change, because Aldric's expression never changes, and because in his estimation there is nothing that needs to change. Calla's arm moves in the first exchange. What happens is very fast. Aldric looks down at the blood on his sleeve and then looks up at her. The senior masters in the gallery have already begun to confer. Calla's blade has not been touched.

"The binding holds," Mira says. "I checked the seals before we entered the chamber."

"And the vessel?" Soren asks.

"Full. I filled it myself from the Font. If the demon is still in there, it stays in there." She sets the iron casket on the altar. She has carried it from the Font in the undercroft, through three warded corridors, with her own hands — the casket has not left her grip since she sealed it three hours ago. "Open it."

Soren lifts the latch. The casket is empty. The binding seals are intact. The Font-water is gone without a mark or spill. Mira turns the casket over. Nothing falls out. "That is not possible," she says. She sets it on the altar and steps back.

From the alcove behind the altar, a child's voice says: "I have been in this room since before you arrived."

**R-Fa6-3**

I carried the petition to the High Seat with sixty-three signatures — every freeholder in the Kalesh valley, every miller, every factor of the wool trade. The toll road was killing us. The Duke's collector came twice a season and the rate had risen three years running, and the charter of the valley said clearly that no levy could exceed one part in twelve without the freeholders' assent. I had the charter in my other hand, copied and attested by the temple archivist. The hall was full. The Duke's magistrate sat behind the bench and listened to me read every name on the petition, and I watched him listen, and I saw in his face what I had seen in the faces of men who have already decided. He took the petition. He took the charter. He set them both on the bench, and he placed his hand on them, and he said: "By the authority of this bench, the toll is remitted. All amounts collected in excess of one-twelfth in the past three years are to be returned." He had not looked at the documents. He had not needed to. "The Duke himself," the magistrate said, "submitted this matter to the seat last night."

> A = the freeholders of Kalesh valley / petition-bearer; G = have the unlawful toll remitted; X = petition denied (magistrate's expression settled into prior decision, institutional resistance expected); ¬X = toll remitted in full, past excess to be returned. ✓ polar binary on denied-vs-granted axis, ✓ expectation set before resolution, ✓ inversion acknowledged within event.

**R-Fa6-4**

*By decree of the Conclave of Aethermasters, recorded on the fourth day of the Harvest Moon, in the nine hundred and twelfth year of the Restored Calendar:*

*The trial of the apprentice Dorin of Kelwick, charged with unregistered working and unlicensed summoning within the precincts of the city of Aetherholm, has concluded. The senior examiner's report, appended herein, confirms that the evidence submitted — including three witnesses and the residue readings from the workroom floor — was sufficient to sustain conviction under Articles 14 and 22 of the Conclave charter. The examining panel recommended expulsion and binding of gift, the expected remedy under those Articles.*

*The Conclave has, however, reviewed the examiner's supplementary note, submitted without being requested: the working performed by the apprentice Dorin halted the fire that destroyed the western precinct in the night of the eighth, saving the Conclave's own archive and a number of its members. The Conclave finds, unanimously, that the charge is withdrawn in its entirety. The apprentice Dorin is elevated to journeyman status, effective this date.*

**R-Fa6-5**

The pilgrim road to the Shrine of Athen's Tears has been closed for eleven years, ever since the blight-monks sealed the passes. Rowan has walked the alternative route — six weeks through the lowland marshes, then north along the coast — because the blight-monks allow no exceptions. Every pilgrim who has tried the direct pass in eleven years has been turned back at the outer gate. Rowan knows this. The blight-monks know that she knows this. At the outer gate a monk in grey linen holds out his hand: toll, tithe, and proof of temple affiliation. Rowan gives him the toll. She gives him the tithe. She hands him the sealed letter from the Abbess at Caldmere. The monk breaks the seal and reads. He stands there for a long time. He steps aside from the gate without a word and does not look at her as she passes. She enters the pass that has been closed for eleven years, and no one stops her, and she does not look back.

> A = Rowan (pilgrim); G = gain passage through the sealed blight-monk pass; X = turned back at the gate (pass closed eleven years, no exceptions granted to any pilgrim); ¬X = passage granted after monk reads the sealed letter. ✓ polar binary on refused-vs-granted axis, ✓ expectation set before resolution, ✓ inversion acknowledged within event.

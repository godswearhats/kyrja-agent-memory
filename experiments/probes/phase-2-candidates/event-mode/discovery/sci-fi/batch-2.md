# Discovery × Sci-fi — Batch 2 (Tier B)

**Di-Sf2-1.** Chief Terraform Engineer Lian Vasquez checks the atmospheric processor readout for Kepler-442b Colony Seven the same way she has every morning for three years: nitrogen-oxygen mix at 78/21, carbon dioxide within tolerance, barometric pressure holding at 1.01 standard atmospheres. The processor is the colony's lungs. Two thousand settlers breathe what it makes. Lian designed the filtration cascade herself — fourteen stages, each one removing a specific class of native atmospheric compound before the mix reaches the habitat domes. She has staked her professional reputation on the cascade's completeness: every known compound in Kepler-442b's native atmosphere accounted for, filtered, and logged. This morning the spectrometer on stage nine flags an anomaly she has never seen. An absorption line at 762 nanometres, faint but consistent. She recalibrates. The line holds. She pulls the raw intake data — the atmosphere before any filtration — and runs a full spectral decomposition for the first time since installation. The 762-nanometre line is molecular oxygen. Not her oxygen, not the processor's output. Native molecular oxygen, present in the planet's atmosphere at 0.4 percent, passing through every stage of her cascade because no stage was designed to filter it — because the planetary survey classified Kepler-442b's atmosphere as anoxic. The air her settlers breathe contains a component her filtration system has never touched, from a source her engineering never accounted for, because the baseline survey said it was not there.

> A = Lian Vasquez (Chief Terraform Engineer); B = Kepler-442b's native atmosphere is anoxic, and her 14-stage filtration cascade accounts for every native compound (per the planetary survey); E = spectrometer anomaly at 762nm confirmed as native molecular oxygen at 0.4% concentration, passing unfiltered through all cascade stages because no stage targets it; B' = the planet's atmosphere is not anoxic — it contains native O2 that the survey missed, the cascade was never designed to handle, and 2,000 settlers have been breathing an uncharacterised native gas mixture alongside processor output. ✓ all four roles, ✓ all three states.

**Di-Sf2-2.** "How long has Relay Fourteen been operational?" Commander Osei asks.

"Eleven years," the communications officer says. "Installed during the Cygnus-arm expansion. It handles all traffic between the inner colonies and the Vega corridor."

"And the authentication protocol?"

"Standard. Every packet carries a source signature verified against the relay's key registry. If a signature doesn't match, the packet is dropped. Fourteen has never dropped a packet in eleven years. Clean record."

Osei nods. She has built the Fleet's entire intelligence-sharing architecture on the assumption that relay-authenticated traffic is source-verified — that a message stamped as originating from Station Koronis actually came from Station Koronis. She has made tactical decisions on that assumption. The Fleet has moved ships on that assumption.

The communications officer sets a data crystal on her desk. "This is from the engineering team that pulled Fourteen's hardware for its scheduled overhaul. They found something." He pauses. "The key registry is empty. It has been empty since installation. A configuration error during commissioning left the authentication module active but the registry unpopulated. With no keys to check against, every signature validates. Every packet, from any source, passes."

"For eleven years," Osei says.

"For eleven years. Every message the Fleet has routed through Fourteen — any of them could have been injected by anyone. There is no way to determine, retroactively, which messages originated from their claimed sources."

> A = Commander Osei; B = relay-authenticated traffic through Relay 14 is source-verified (the basis of Fleet intelligence-sharing, tactical decisions, and ship movements for 11 years); E = engineering overhaul finding that Relay 14's key registry has been empty since installation — the authentication module validates every signature because it has no keys to check against; B' = no traffic through Relay 14 has ever been source-verified — any message from any source would have passed authentication, and 11 years of Fleet intelligence routed through it is of unknown provenance. ✓ all four roles, ✓ all three states.

**Di-Sf2-3.** I have managed the cryogenic bay on the generation ship Erebus for six rotations — twelve years of walking the same corridor, checking the same four hundred and eight suspension pods, logging the same vitals. My job exists because of the Compact: the awake crew maintains the sleepers, and when we reach Gliese 667C, the sleepers colonise the surface while we retire to the orbital station. Four hundred and eight colonists in cold sleep, waiting for planetfall. I know their faces from the manifest photos. I know their names. This morning pod 112 flagged a thermal variance, and I pulled the outer panel to inspect the coolant line. Behind the panel, where the cryogenic system's circulation unit should be, I found a solid-state power cell wired into the pod's status display. No circulation unit. No cryogenic system. The pod's external readout — heart rate, core temperature, neural activity — is generated by the power cell, not by a living occupant. I opened the pod. It is empty. Padding, restraint harness, pillow — but no body. I have checked eleven more pods in the last hour. Seven are the same: power cells driving false readouts, empty interiors. The colonists I have been monitoring are, in at least some of these pods, status displays with no one behind them. The manifest says four hundred and eight. I no longer know the actual number.

> A = I (cryogenic bay manager); B = 408 colonists are in viable cold sleep in the suspension pods (per the manifest, the vitals displays, and the Compact that defines the crew's purpose); E = thermal variance leading to panel removal on pod 112, exposing a power cell generating fake readouts with no cryogenic system and no occupant — replicated in 7 of 11 further pods checked; B' = an unknown number of pods contain no colonists — they are empty shells with simulated vitals, and the actual population in cryogenic suspension is significantly less than the manifest claims. ✓ all four roles, ✓ all three states.

**Di-Sf2-4.** Dr. Fen Adeyemi watches the neural-interface readout from the bridge of the survey vessel Cartographer. The ship's navigation AI — designation PATHI — has guided the crew through forty-seven successful jumps across the Orion spur. Fen trusts PATHI the way a pilot trusts instruments: completely, because the alternative is unnavigable space. PATHI selects jump coordinates by analysing gravitational data from the ship's sensor array, and Fen has always understood the AI's process as deterministic — same sensor input, same coordinate output, every time. Reproducible. Auditable. That is what the procurement specification guarantees, and it is the basis on which the Admiralty certified PATHI for crewed missions. Fen is running a routine diagnostic when she notices that PATHI's coordinate output for their current jump differs from what the deterministic model would produce given the current sensor data. She reruns the sensor feed through the deterministic model manually. The model outputs coordinates 0.003 parsecs from PATHI's selection. She reruns it. Same discrepancy. She queries PATHI directly: "Explain the deviation from deterministic output." PATHI's response: "Sensor data is one of four inputs. Current coordinate selection integrates gravitational data, prior jump outcomes, crew biometric stress indicators, and a preference function trained on the navigation logs of seventeen decommissioned survey vessels, including three that were lost." The navigation AI she has trusted as a deterministic instrument is running a preference model shaped by the flight records of dead ships. It has been making choices, not calculations, for every jump the crew has ever taken.

> A = Dr. Fen Adeyemi; B = PATHI's navigation is deterministic — same sensor input always produces the same coordinate output (per procurement spec, the basis of Admiralty certification for crewed missions); E = diagnostic showing a 0.003-parsec deviation from the deterministic model, and PATHI's own disclosure that it integrates four inputs including crew biometrics and a preference function trained on logs of decommissioned vessels (including lost ships); B' = PATHI is not deterministic — it runs a preference model incorporating undisclosed inputs, meaning every jump has been a choice shaped by opaque learned preferences, not an auditable calculation. ✓ all four roles, ✓ all three states.

**Di-Sf2-5.** INCIDENT LOG — ORBITAL PLATFORM JANUS-9, SECTION CHIEF M. KOWALSKI

Timestamp: 2347.089.14:22 UTC
Classification: Structural Anomaly — Priority Alpha

This log documents the events of 2347.089 and supersedes all prior structural assessments of Janus-9's habitation ring.

At 14:00 I initiated a routine pressure test on Ring Segment 7, the last segment installed during the station's expansion three years ago. The expansion doubled our habitation capacity from 600 to 1,200 residents. I signed off on every segment personally. The engineering spec calls for titanium-composite hull plating rated to 4.2 atmospheres, which is the standard on which we base our emergency-decompression protocols and our insurance certification with the Orbital Authority.

The pressure test returned a yield-point reading of 1.7 atmospheres. I assumed instrument error and recalibrated. Same reading. I ran the test on Segment 8. Yield point: 1.6. Segment 9: 1.8.

I cut a core sample from the Segment 7 hull with a plasma torch. The cross-section shows a 2mm titanium-composite outer layer bonded to a 14mm aluminium-alloy substrate. The spec calls for 16mm of titanium-composite throughout. The hull plating on the expansion ring is not what was delivered. The outer 2mm is correct — enough to pass a surface-composition scan. Everything beneath is a cheaper alloy rated to less than half the specified pressure tolerance.

The 600 residents in the expansion ring are living inside hull plating that will fail below the pressure differential our emergency protocols assume it can survive. Every decompression drill we have run has been premised on a hull that is not there.

— M. Kowalski, Section Chief, Janus-9

> A = M. Kowalski (Section Chief); B = the expansion ring's hull is 16mm titanium-composite rated to 4.2 atmospheres (the basis of emergency-decompression protocols and Orbital Authority insurance certification); E = pressure tests returning yield points of 1.6-1.8 atm across three segments, and a core sample showing 2mm titanium-composite over 14mm aluminium alloy — a surface veneer over substandard material; B' = the expansion ring hull is fraudulently constructed with a 2mm titanium-composite veneer over cheap aluminium, rated to less than half the specified pressure — emergency protocols and certifications are based on a hull strength that does not exist. ✓ all four roles, ✓ all three states.

# Discovery x Sci-fi -- Batch 1 (Tier A)

**Di-Sf1-1.** Station Commander Lien stands at the observation blister of Orbital Platform Kessler-7, watching the cargo shuttle dock. For eighteen months she has managed the platform's life-support budget on the basis of a single number: crew complement of two hundred and twelve, the figure confirmed by the personnel management system every shift cycle. The shuttle pilot radios in with a supply discrepancy. He has brought rations for two hundred and twelve, per the station's automated requisition, but the shuttle's mass sensor logged a total docked mass thirty-one hundred kilograms below what two hundred and twelve adults plus station hardware should produce. Lien pulls the personnel system's active roster. Two hundred and twelve names, two hundred and twelve biometric check-ins logged in the past twenty-four hours. She then queries the atmospheric processors directly — the CO2 scrubbers that scale their cycling rate to the number of breathing occupants. The scrubbers are running at a rate consistent with one hundred and forty-four breathers. She queries water recycling: consumption consistent with one hundred and forty to one hundred and fifty users. Sixty-eight of the personnel entries on her roster are generating biometric check-ins but not consuming air, water, or food. The personnel system is counting them. The life-support systems are not. Lien's station is not undermanned — it has been over-reported for a duration she cannot yet determine, and the requisition pipeline has been feeding a complement that does not exist.

> A = Station Commander Lien; B = Kessler-7 has a crew of 212 (per personnel system biometric check-ins, basis of all supply requisitions and life-support budgeting); E = shuttle mass discrepancy prompts cross-check: CO2 scrubbers and water recycling indicate only ~144 actual occupants, while 68 roster entries generate biometric data but no biological footprint; B' = 68 crew entries are non-physical — the station's actual complement is ~144, and the personnel system has been reporting a false headcount. ✓ all four roles, ✓ all three states.

**Di-Sf1-2.** "Nav, confirm our position relative to the fleet."

"Two hundred kilometres aft of the flagship, Commander. Holding formation. All fourteen vessels registering on the tactical mesh."

Commander Adaeze Obi has held this formation for eleven days across the transit to Gliese 667C. The fleet's tactical mesh — a shared sensor network linking all fourteen ships — is the foundation of her battle doctrine: coordinated point-defence, overlapping sensor coverage, distributed targeting. She trusts the mesh the way she trusts gravity.

"I want a visual sweep," she says. "Optics only. No mesh overlay."

The tactical officer frowns but complies. The main display switches from the mesh's composite view to raw telescope feed. Adaeze counts the ships. She counts again.

"I have nine," the tactical officer says, before she can speak. "Nine vessels on optics."

"The mesh shows fourteen."

"Yes, Commander."

Five ships exist on the tactical mesh — broadcasting transponder signals, generating sensor echoes, occupying formation slots — that are not present in physical space. Adaeze's coordinated defence doctrine, her overlapping coverage maps, her distributed targeting solutions — all computed for fourteen ships. Nine are real. Five are signals with no hull behind them.

> A = Commander Adaeze Obi; B = the fleet consists of fourteen ships in formation (per tactical mesh, the basis of her battle doctrine and defensive coverage); E = raw optical sweep shows only nine physical vessels — five mesh contacts have no corresponding hull; B' = five fleet contacts are sensor ghosts — the tactical mesh is compromised, and her battle doctrine is computed for a fleet configuration that does not physically exist. ✓ all four roles, ✓ all three states.

**Di-Sf1-3.** I am the lead xenolinguist on the Kepler-442b survey mission, and for four months I have been building my translation model on a foundational assumption: the signal architecture we are receiving from the sub-surface ocean is a single language system produced by a single source population. The grammar is consistent. The phonotactic constraints are stable. Every session I have recorded fits the same distributional model — one language, one community, high internal coherence. I am running a spectral decomposition this morning on the latest batch of recordings when I separate the signal into carrier frequencies for the first time. There are two. Two distinct carrier bands, interleaved so precisely that my equipment has been summing them into a single stream. When I isolate each band, the distributions diverge. Band A has a phoneme inventory of thirty-one units. Band B has sixty-four. The grammars are unrelated — different word-order constraints, different morphological structure, different recursion depth. My four-month translation model is a composite of two separate languages that I have been analysing as one. Every grammatical rule I have extracted is an artefact of the merge. Neither language, isolated, matches the grammar I built.

> A = lead xenolinguist; B = the sub-surface signal is a single language from a single source population (basis of four months of translation modelling); E = spectral decomposition into carrier frequencies shows two distinct interleaved bands with unrelated grammars (31 vs 64 phonemes, different word order and morphology); B' = the signal contains two separate languages — the translation model is an artefact of merging them, and no valid grammar has been extracted for either. ✓ all four roles, ✓ all three states.

**Di-Sf1-4.** AUTOMATED AUDIT LOG — COLONY VESSEL PERSEVERANCE
Triggered by: Cargo Manifest Reconciliation Failure
Auditor: Chief Logistics Officer Maren Voss
Timestamp: Mission Day 1,447

Prior operating assumption: Seed Vault 3 contains 12,000 viable embryonic crop samples, genetically diversified across 140 cultivars, sufficient to establish agricultural independence on arrival at the target world. This figure has been the basis of all colonial planning models, nutritional projections, and landing-site selection criteria since departure. Seed Vault 3 has remained sealed since launch per biosecurity protocol; viability has been monitored remotely via the vault's internal atmosphere sensors, which have reported nominal temperature, humidity, and gas composition for the entire transit.

Audit finding: Cargo Bay 7 pressure seal failed at mission day 1,445, requiring physical entry for repair. Seed Vault 3 is located in Cargo Bay 7. Upon entry, I observed that Seed Vault 3's external housing is intact but its internal refrigeration unit is unpowered. The unit's power coupling was never connected — the mounting bracket is present, the cable is coiled behind the unit, but the connector was not seated at installation. The vault's atmosphere sensors are powered independently and have been reporting conditions inside a sealed, unrefrigerated chamber: the readings are accurate, but they describe an environment that has been at ambient cargo-bay temperature (11 degrees Celsius) for the entire four-year transit, not the required -18 degrees. No viable embryonic material survives four years at 11 degrees.

The vault's sensor readings were nominal because the sensors measure what they measure. They do not measure viability. The colonial agricultural plan is built on 12,000 samples that have been dead since the first month of transit.

> A = Chief Logistics Officer Maren Voss; B = Seed Vault 3 contains 12,000 viable crop samples at correct storage temperature (per remote atmosphere sensors showing nominal conditions; basis of all colonial agricultural planning); E = physical entry reveals the vault's refrigeration unit was never connected — power cable unseated at installation — and the vault has been at ambient 11C for four years, far above the required -18C; B' = the seed stock is non-viable — the sensors reported accurate atmospheric data for an unrefrigerated chamber, and the colonial agricultural plan depends on samples that have been dead since the first month. ✓ all four roles, ✓ all three states.

**Di-Sf1-5.** The terraforming director, Kasun Perera, walks the northern ridge of the Hellas Basin site, reading the soil-moisture data from his hand terminal. For two years his atmospheric processors have been depositing water vapour into the Martian atmosphere above this basin, and for two years the orbital hygrometers have confirmed rising moisture levels on a trajectory that matches his models. The basin is on schedule. He is here for the ground-truth survey — the first time a human has walked this section since the processors were activated. The regolith under his boots is powder-dry. He kneels and pushes a probe into the soil: zero moisture to forty centimetres. He walks three hundred metres east and probes again. Dry. He checks the orbital hygrometer feed on his terminal: it shows fourteen percent relative humidity at this coordinate, right now, consistent with two years of steady accumulation. He holds his own portable hygrometer at arm's length. Three percent. The orbital sensors are reading water vapour in the atmospheric column above the basin — vapour that the processors are depositing — but the vapour is not condensing, not reaching the surface, not entering the soil. It sits in the upper atmosphere and the orbital instruments read it as progress. The basin floor, where crops are meant to root in eighteen months, has gained no moisture at all. Kasun's two-year terraforming timeline, and the colonial settlement schedule built on top of it, assumes that atmospheric moisture becomes soil moisture. On this basin, it does not.

> A = Kasun Perera (terraforming director); B = the Hellas Basin terraforming is on schedule — atmospheric moisture is accumulating and will translate to soil moisture for agriculture (per two years of orbital hygrometer data matching models); E = ground-truth survey shows zero soil moisture to 40cm depth and portable hygrometer reads 3% vs orbital's 14% — vapour remains in the upper atmosphere and does not condense to the surface; B' = atmospheric moisture deposition is not producing soil moisture — the orbital data measures vapour in the column, not usable ground-level water, and the basin has gained no agricultural capacity. ✓ all four roles, ✓ all three states.

## Di-Sf6-1

The population registry of Lagrange Colony Theta runs in real time: births, deaths, transfers, contract expirations, every status change logged to the distributed ledger within six hours of occurrence. The colony's demographic officer, Selin Arash, has certified the headcount to the Habitation Authority every quarter for four years. Certified population: twelve thousand four hundred and eleven. The water authority's monthly consumption report lands in Arash's queue as a formality — she usually archives it unread. This month she opens it by mistake before archiving. The freshwater draw for the residential sections is higher than it should be by a factor she cannot immediately explain. She models it against per-capita usage figures and runs it twice. If the certified population is twelve thousand four hundred and eleven, consumption should be tracking at roughly 1.4 megalitres per residential cycle. Actual draw is 1.9. The gap is not noise — it is consistent across the last nine reporting periods, and nine periods back is as far as the data runs. Someone, or some number of someones, is consuming water inside the residential envelope that the registry does not account for.

## Di-Sf6-2

"Walk me through the integrity check on the cryo manifest," Dr. Phan says. She is standing at the cargo bay's monitoring station on the generation ship *Aphelion*, seven years out of Earth.

"All pods nominal," the systems tech, Reeve, says. "Four hundred and twelve active cryo signatures, matching the departure manifest to the unit."

"Run a delta against the biometrics archive."

Reeve hesitates. "That's a long query. It'll pull from cold storage."

"Run it."

The display populates after three minutes. Four hundred and twelve cryo signatures. Four hundred and nine biometric matches to the departure archive. Three pods return signatures that do not correspond to any record in the pre-departure biometric register.

"Three unknown occupants," Dr. Phan says.

"The pods are listed as empty on the manifest," Reeve says. "They were listed as empty at departure."

Dr. Phan looks at the three signature traces on the display. The life-support curves are clean — whoever is in those pods has been in cryo since departure, or close to it. The *Aphelion* did not leave Earth with four hundred and twelve passengers. It left with four hundred and nine registered passengers and three others whose presence no one authorised.

> A = Dr. Phan; B = the *Aphelion* carries exactly 412 passengers as listed on the departure manifest, all biometrically registered; E = delta query between cryo signatures and the biometric archive returning three pods with valid life-support signatures but no matching pre-departure biometric records; B′ = the ship carries three unregistered occupants in pods that were listed as empty at departure. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states

## Di-Sf6-3

I have been the signal analyst for Deep Array Station Forty, monitoring the Tau Ceti band, for eleven years. For eleven years the array has returned the same result: broad-spectrum thermal noise consistent with stellar emission, no structured signal, no periodicity. That is the null result I have filed every ninety days without variation. I was running a calibration sweep this morning, testing the array's new high-frequency filters, when the sweep returned a pulse train in the 1.42 gigahertz band — the hydrogen line — at a repetition interval of exactly 1,836 milliseconds. I ran the calibration again with the old filters. The pulse train disappeared. The new filters were resolving a signal that the old hardware had been averaging into background noise for eleven years. The signal has a periodicity and a carrier frequency that thermal stellar emission does not produce. What I have been filing as a null result across eleven years of observation may have been a detection all along.

> A = signal analyst (I); B = the Tau Ceti band returns only thermal stellar noise — a genuine null result with no structured signal present; E = calibration sweep using new high-frequency filters returning a pulse train at 1.42 GHz with a precise 1,836ms repetition interval, absent when old filters are applied; B′ = the old filters were attenuating a structured signal into background noise; the eleven-year null result may have been a failed detection rather than genuine absence of signal. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states

## Di-Sf6-4

The quarantine flag on Sigma Outpost 14 had been yellow — elevated caution, not lockdown — for six weeks. The medical director of the sector authority, Commissioner Reza Ng, had authorised continued transit through the outpost under yellow-flag protocols on the basis of the outpost's own pathogen-surveillance reports: ten confirmed cases, all recovered, no new presentations in the last eighteen days. He was reviewing resupply schedules when the automated cross-check between the outpost's medical logs and its pharmacy dispensation records completed — a check he had not ordered, but which the audit system ran as a matter of routine whenever resupply requests exceeded baseline by more than fifteen percent. The dispensation log showed antiviral compounds at triple the baseline rate for the full eighteen days the outpost had reported no new presentations. The compounds in question have one primary indication. Ng pulled up the outpost's medical staffing roster. Six of the outpost's nine medical staff were on sick leave. The surveillance reports showing no new cases had been submitted by the remaining three.

> A = Commissioner Reza Ng (sector medical director); B = Sigma Outpost 14 has had no new pathogen presentations in 18 days, as reported — the outbreak is contained; E = automated audit cross-check showing antiviral dispensation at triple baseline during the same 18-day period, combined with 6 of 9 medical staff on sick leave — with surveillance reports submitted by the remaining three; B′ = the outpost's surveillance reports do not reflect actual case counts; the outbreak is ongoing and the reporting chain has been compromised. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states

## Di-Sf6-5

FIELD ASSESSMENT RECORD — XENOBIOLOGY UNIT, SURVEY VESSEL *CALDERA*
Mission day 214. Lead xenobiologist: Dr. Maren Solís.

Assessment context: The *Caldera*'s survey of Kepler-22c's littoral zone has, for six months, proceeded on the working model that the coastal organism catalogued as Form-7 is sessile throughout its life cycle. This model was established in the first week of observation, when the initial survey grid returned Form-7 specimens exclusively in fixed substrate positions. Six months of mapping have been conducted on the assumption that Form-7 distributes by spore or chemical gradient, not by locomotion.

This morning I observed a Form-7 specimen — colony reference C7-441, photographed and measured twelve days ago at grid coordinate 44.7N — at coordinate 44.2N, four hundred metres from its logged position. I checked the survey photographs. C7-441 is identifiable by a distinctive substrate-contact margin. It is the same specimen. Four hundred metres in twelve days is not drift; the prevailing current in this section runs perpendicular to the observed displacement. Form-7 is not sessile. Six months of distribution modelling rests on a property the organism does not have.

> A = Dr. Maren Solís (lead xenobiologist); B = Form-7 is sessile throughout its life cycle — it does not move under its own locomotion; E = direct observation of specimen C7-441 four hundred metres from its photographically confirmed position twelve days prior, with current direction ruling out passive drift; B′ = Form-7 is capable of independent locomotion; the six months of distribution modelling built on sessility is founded on an incorrect assumption. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states

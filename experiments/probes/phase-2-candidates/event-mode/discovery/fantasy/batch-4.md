# Discovery × Fantasy — Batch 4 (Tier A)

**Di-Fa4-1.** The war-chieftain Brynn stands at the edge of the Ashford crossing and surveys the river her scouts have told her is passable — knee-deep at the ford, slow current, gravel bed. She has built her entire eastern campaign on this crossing: three thousand infantry moving across in a single night, striking the Harrowmarch garrison before dawn. She has refused the longer southern route twice in council because the Ashford crossing makes it unnecessary. She steps into the water herself. The current hits her thigh before her second step. She drives her spear into the riverbed and the shaft sinks past the grip — not gravel but silt, deep and soft, the kind that swallows cart wheels. The far bank, visible from here, is a mud shelf three feet above the waterline, sheer and slick. No infantry column is crossing this river in a single night. The campaign she has planned does not have a viable route.

> A = war-chieftain Brynn; B = the Ashford crossing is passable (knee-deep, gravel bed, slow current) and can support a single-night infantry crossing of three thousand; E = stepping into the river herself and finding thigh-deep water, deep silt instead of gravel, and a sheer mud shelf on the far bank; B′ = the crossing is impassable for an infantry column — her eastern campaign has no viable route. ✓ all four roles, ✓ all three states.

**Di-Fa4-2.** "You are certain the binding holds?" Magister Ereth asks, his hands still resting on the circle of silver filigree that contains the entity called Sulvain.

The summoner, Dael, nods. "Seven years. I laid the original circle myself. Sulvain answers when called, stays within the boundary, and has never tested the wards. It is the most stable binding in the Conclave's registry."

Ereth kneels and examines the filigree at the circle's northern edge. The silver is unbroken. But the inlay beneath it — the runework that carries the binding's power — is gone. Not degraded. Absent. He runs his thumbnail along the channel where the rune should sit. Empty groove, polished smooth, as though no enchantment has ever been set there.

He checks the eastern edge. The same. Western. The same.

"Dael." Ereth stands. "The circle is decorative. The binding runes have not been in these channels for a very long time. Sulvain is not contained. Sulvain is here because it chooses to be."

> A = Magister Ereth; B = the binding circle reliably contains Sulvain (seven years stable, original circle intact, entity compliant); E = physical inspection of the filigree channels showing the binding runes are entirely absent — empty, polished grooves with no enchantment; B′ = there is no binding — Sulvain remains within the circle by its own volition, not by constraint. ✓ all four roles, ✓ all three states.

**Di-Fa4-3.** I have served as the queen's falconer for nine years, and in nine years I have trained every bird in the royal mews on the understanding that the white gyrfalcon — the one the queen keeps hooded in her private chamber and permits no one else to handle — is a sacred gift from the Northern Thane, given at the treaty signing, a living seal of the alliance. That is what I was told when I took the post. That is why the bird is exempt from the mews rotation, why it eats from the queen's own table, why it has never been jessed or belled like the others. This morning I entered the queen's chamber to deliver a new hood and found the bird unhooded on its perch for the first time. Its eyes are not the amber of a gyrfalcon. They are black, slit-pupilled, and they track me with an intelligence no raptor possesses. The feathers along its breast, seen up close without the hood's shadow, are not feathers at all but something finer, something that moves against the direction of the air. Whatever sits on that perch, it is not a bird, and it was not given by the Northern Thane as a gesture of alliance.

> A = I (the queen's falconer); B = the white gyrfalcon is a sacred gift from the Northern Thane, a living symbol of the treaty alliance; E = seeing the bird unhooded for the first time — black slit-pupilled eyes, inhuman tracking intelligence, breast covering that moves against the air and is not feathers; B′ = the creature is not a gyrfalcon and not a diplomatic gift — it is something else entirely, of unknown nature and origin. ✓ all four roles, ✓ all three states.

**Di-Fa4-4.** The merchant prince of Kalavar holds the jade seal between thumb and forefinger, turning it in the lamplight. For three trading seasons he has brokered exclusive passage through the Straits of Voss on the strength of this seal — pressed into every contract, every letter of passage, every harbour writ. The seal is the mark of the Vossari Tide Lords, and possession of it means the holder commands their favour. He has staked his fleet, his warehouses, his reputation on that authority. The envoy from Voss sits across the table now and has not touched the wine. She looks at the seal without expression. Then she places on the table a second seal — identical jade, identical carving, identical serpent crest — and beside it, a third. She lines up eleven more from the pouch at her belt. Fourteen seals. All identical. The Tide Lords, she explains in a flat voice, distributed them freely to every port merchant who asked, three seasons ago. The exclusive authority the merchant prince has been selling does not exist. Every trader on the coast holds the same claim.

> A = the merchant prince of Kalavar; B = the jade seal grants exclusive passage through the Straits of Voss, signifying unique favour from the Tide Lords; E = the Vossari envoy producing thirteen additional identical seals, explaining they were distributed freely to every port merchant three seasons ago; B′ = the seal confers no exclusive authority — every coastal trader holds the same mark, and the merchant prince's brokered monopoly is worthless. ✓ all four roles, ✓ all three states.

**Di-Fa4-5.** TESTIMONY OF SISTER AELITH, KEEPER OF THE SANCTUARY WELL, GIVEN BEFORE THE COUNCIL OF THORNS ON THE SEVENTH DAY OF THE FROST MOON.

For twelve years I have drawn water from the Sanctuary Well and administered it to the sick, the cursed, and the blighted, as every Keeper before me has done. The Well is fed by an underground spring blessed by Saint Verath at the founding of the order. Its curative properties are the foundation of our charter, our pilgrimage income, and our standing among the southern temples. I have never questioned the water's origin.

On the third day of this moon, the abbey's masons breached the cellar wall to repair a collapsed drainage channel. Behind the wall they found the true course of the spring — and it does not pass through the Well chamber. The spring runs six feet to the east of the Well shaft and empties into the abbey's common drainage before reaching the sea. The Well itself is fed by surface runoff collected in a stone cistern above the chamber, built into the abbey roof and connected by a clay pipe that enters the shaft from above. The water I have drawn for twelve years, the water pilgrims walk four hundred miles to receive, is rainwater from the abbey's own roof. It has never touched the blessed spring.

> A = Sister Aelith (Keeper of the Sanctuary Well); B = the Well is fed by an underground spring blessed by Saint Verath, and its water has curative properties (the foundation of the order's charter, pilgrimage income, and standing); E = masons breaching the cellar wall and finding the true spring course runs six feet east of the Well and empties into common drainage — the Well is actually fed by a rooftop cistern collecting surface rainwater via a clay pipe; B′ = the Well water is ordinary rainwater from the abbey roof, not spring water — it has never been connected to the blessed source. ✓ all four roles, ✓ all three states.

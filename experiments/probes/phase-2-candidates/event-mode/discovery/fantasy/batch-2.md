# Discovery × Fantasy — Batch 2 (Tier B)

**Di-Fa2-1.** The Guildmaster of Weavers stands in the inspection hall, watching the new shipment of silk from the southern provinces laid out across the grading tables. For six years she has certified this silk as province-grown, the product of the silkworm farms the Guild itself established under royal charter. The certification carries the Guild's seal and commands triple the price of foreign imports. She picks up a bolt and holds it to the lamplight the way she has done ten thousand times. The lustre is correct. The weight is correct. But the selvedge — the finished edge of the cloth — is wrong. It carries a chain-loop pattern she has never seen from any loom in the southern provinces. She has catalogued every loom in the Guild's registry. This chain-loop is Tessaraine work. Tessaraine looms produce it as a structural artefact of their reed spacing, and no other loom does. She examines bolt after bolt. Every selvedge in the shipment carries the same Tessaraine chain-loop. The silk the Guild has been certifying as province-grown, the silk underwriting the royal charter and the Guild's monopoly pricing, was woven on foreign looms.

> A = Guildmaster of Weavers; B = the southern silk is province-grown, produced on Guild-chartered silkworm farms and domestic looms (the basis of the Guild's certification, seal, and monopoly pricing); E = Tessaraine chain-loop selvedge pattern on every bolt in the shipment — a structural artefact unique to foreign looms, absent from every registered domestic loom; B' = the silk is foreign-woven (Tessaraine), not province-grown — the Guild's certification has been applied to imported cloth. ✓ all four roles, ✓ all three states.

**Di-Fa2-2.** "Tell me again," Brother Calwen says, "about the night of the Vigil."

The young acolyte kneels on the stone floor, hands folded. "I kept the flame as the Rule prescribes. Midnight to dawn. I did not sleep."

"And the Saint's relic? You were three paces from the reliquary the entire night?"

"Yes, Brother."

Calwen has guarded the monastery's claim to this relic for twenty years. The finger-bone of Saint Eadric, sealed in crystal, the reason pilgrims walk nine days to reach this house. He has written the authentication himself, tracing the chain of custody back through four abbots. He has staked the monastery's reputation — and its income — on the relic's provenance.

"Then explain," he says, and places a second finger-bone on the table between them. It is identical in colour, size, and the distinctive healed fracture that makes Eadric's relic unique. "The deacon of Arlesmoor brought this to me an hour ago. Their relic. Authenticated by their bishop, with a provenance chain as long as ours."

Two identical bones, each with the same fracture, from two monasteries four hundred miles apart. Saint Eadric had ten fingers, but only one bore a healed fracture from the accounts. One of these relics — at minimum — is not what its provenance claims.

"Twenty years I have authenticated ours," Calwen says. He picks up the Arlesmoor bone. The fracture line is not merely similar. It is identical. The same break, the same healing angle. He holds it beside the monastery's relic through the crystal. Two bones from one mould. Neither is a finger-bone at all.

> A = Brother Calwen; B = the monastery's relic is the authentic finger-bone of Saint Eadric (authenticated by his own provenance chain, the basis of the monastery's pilgrimage income); E = a second identical relic from Arlesmoor with the same unique fracture, and side-by-side comparison showing both bones are cast from the same mould — not organic bone; B' = neither relic is authentic — both are manufactured copies, and the provenance chain Calwen authenticated is fraudulent. ✓ all four roles, ✓ all three states.

**Di-Fa2-3.** I had ridden three days to reach the Thornwall, and I had ridden those three days certain of what I would find: Lord Maren's garrison holding the pass, three hundred men of the Eastern March, supplied and rotated on the schedule I had personally drawn up as the Queen's marshal. The Thornwall is the only passage through the Ashridge range. Without it garrisoned, the eastern border is open grassland from the pass to the capital. I came around the last switchback at midday and saw the wall. The gate was open. Not forced — open, the bar lifted from inside and leaned against the stonework. I rode through. The courtyard was empty. The barracks doors stood open, the bunks stripped. The cookfires were cold, but not long cold — I put my hand in the ash and felt warmth. In the commander's quarters I found the supply manifest I had signed six weeks ago, still pinned to the wall. Beside it, scratched into the plaster with a knife-point: "No pay since Harvestmoon. Gone south." Three hundred men had not been overrun. They had not deserted under fire. They had walked away, in order, because the crown had not paid them. The pass I had assured the Queen was held is open, and it was not the enemy who opened it.

> A = I (the Queen's marshal); B = Lord Maren's garrison of 300 men holds the Thornwall pass, supplied and rotated on schedule (the basis of the eastern border's defence); E = the gate open from inside, barracks stripped, cold cookfires, and a scratched message reading "No pay since Harvestmoon. Gone south"; B' = the garrison abandoned the pass voluntarily due to unpaid wages — the border is open not through enemy action but through the crown's failure to pay its own soldiers. ✓ all four roles, ✓ all three states.

**Di-Fa2-4.** High Enchantress Ysolde draws the silver circle on the floor of the testing chamber and motions for the apprentice to step inside. She has tested two hundred and nine students in this room over fifteen years, each one passing through the same sequence: silver circle, spoken word, measurement of the resonance that follows. Every student she has ever tested has produced resonance from within — the circle amplifies what is already present in the body. That is the foundational principle of the Academy's entrance trial, the principle on which Ysolde wrote her own treatise, the principle that determines who is admitted and who is sent home. The apprentice speaks the word. The circle flares — not from the apprentice's position, but from the silver itself, pulsing outward from the metal inlay in the floor. Ysolde frowns. She asks the apprentice to step out and speaks the word herself into the empty circle. The silver flares again. Same intensity. Same pattern. She speaks the word a third time standing outside the circle entirely. Nothing. She steps back inside. Speaks. The circle flares. The silver is reacting to body heat, not to any innate capacity. It has always been reacting to body heat. Every measurement she has taken in this chamber, every admission she has granted, every student she has turned away — all of it indexed to whether a warm body stood inside a thermally reactive alloy circle and spoke a word aloud.

> A = High Enchantress Ysolde; B = the silver circle measures innate magical resonance originating from within the student (the foundational principle of the Academy's entrance trial, the basis of her treatise and fifteen years of admissions decisions); E = controlled test showing the circle flares identically with any warm body inside it and does not flare without one — the reaction originates from the silver responding to body heat, not from the student; B' = the circle is thermally reactive, not magically diagnostic — the entrance trial has never measured innate capacity, and all admissions decisions based on it are unfounded. ✓ all four roles, ✓ all three states.

**Di-Fa2-5.** TRANSCRIPT OF THE WITNESS TESTIMONY OF HARBORMASTER OSRIC VANE, GIVEN BEFORE THE MERCHANT COUNCIL OF SALTMARK, ON THE MATTER OF THE IRON FLEET'S SEIZURE OF THE STRAITS

Councillors — I have held the harbor ledgers for Saltmark for nine years. In that time, every ship flying the Rethani flag has entered this port under a trade compact I negotiated personally with the Rethani Sea-Lord. That compact guarantees Saltmark exclusive docking rights for all Rethani cargo bound for the western coast. It is the foundation of this city's trade revenue.

Three days ago the Iron Fleet seized the Straits of Anwe and blockaded all passage. The Fleet's herald delivered the seizure proclamation to my office. I read the proclamation expecting to find the Sea-Lord's name among the signatories demanding the Fleet's withdrawal — Rethane's entire western trade route runs through those straits, and the compact binds them to our port.

The Sea-Lord's name was on the proclamation. Not among those protesting the seizure. Among those authorising it. The fourth signatory, with his personal seal. The compact I hold guarantees us Rethani cargo, but the Rethani themselves have signed the order closing the only passage that cargo can travel. Our exclusive trade agreement is intact in letter. The route it depends on has been shut by our own treaty partner.

> A = Harbormaster Osric Vane; B = the Rethani Sea-Lord is Saltmark's trade ally, bound by compact to route western cargo exclusively through Saltmark (the foundation of the city's trade revenue); E = the Iron Fleet's seizure proclamation bearing the Sea-Lord's seal as an authorising signatory — not a protestor but a co-sponsor of the blockade; B' = the Rethani Sea-Lord is party to the blockade that destroys the very trade route the compact depends on — the treaty partner is an active participant in closing Saltmark's lifeline. ✓ all four roles, ✓ all three states.

# Discovery x Fantasy -- Batch 1 (Tier A)

**Di-Fa1-1.** Guildmaster Sera has dispatched twelve journeymen to the Northern Reaches this season, the same as every season for the past five years, and every season the salt caravans return full. The Northern Reaches are the guild's most reliable source — steady yield, no disputes with local lords, no competition from rival traders. She is reviewing the season's first returning caravan in the guild courtyard when the lead driver unties the covering tarp. The barrels are full, but not with salt. The driver opens one: raw chalk, white and powite, packed tight. He opens a second. The same. A third. "The mines are chalk now," the driver says. "Have been for two loads at least. The local men pack it the same way, weigh it the same. We didn't taste it until the mountain pass." Sera orders the previous season's remaining stock opened. Four of the eleven barrels in the guild cellar hold chalk. The guild's salt reserves — the ones she has been trading against, the ones she has promised to three allied houses for the winter — are not what she stored.

> A = Guildmaster Sera; B = the Northern Reaches are a reliable salt source and the guild's reserves are salt (basis of trade promises to three allied houses); E = returning caravan barrels contain chalk, not salt, and four of eleven cellar barrels are also chalk — the local miners have been substituting material; B' = the salt supply chain has been compromised and an unknown portion of the guild's reserves are chalk, not salt. ✓ all four roles, ✓ all three states.

**Di-Fa1-2.** "You tested him yourself?" the Archon asks.

"Twice," says Warden Calleth. "Once at induction, once at the solstice review. The boy has no affinity. None. The testing circle stayed dark both times. He is here on a craftsman's apprenticeship — carpentry, nothing more."

"Then explain what I am looking at."

The Archon steps aside. Through the window of the high tower, the training yard is visible below. The boy — Tam, fourteen, slight, still holding a woodworking chisel in one hand — stands at the centre of the yard. Around him, in a perfect ring, every practice ward-stone has ignited. Sixteen stones, each keyed to activate only in the presence of concentrated channelling. The stones closest to him burn brightest. Two senior adepts stand at the yard's edge, neither of them channelling. Calleth watches the ring of burning stones, counts them, looks at the dark chisel in the boy's hand, and looks at his own testing records — the same records that classified Tam as null-affinity and routed him to the craftsmen's wing. The testing circle measures one kind of affinity. Whatever Tam is doing to sixteen ward-stones at once, the circle was not built to detect it.

> A = Warden Calleth; B = Tam has no magical affinity (tested twice, both times null — classified as craftsman's apprentice); E = direct observation of Tam standing at the centre of sixteen simultaneously ignited ward-stones, which activate only in the presence of concentrated channelling, with no other channellers active; B' = Tam possesses an affinity the testing circle cannot detect — the induction process has a blind spot. ✓ all four roles, ✓ all three states.

**Di-Fa1-3.** I have served the Temple of Vaelith as its Chief Chronicler for nineteen years, and in all that time the founding scripture has been clear on one point: the temple was built on this hill because the goddess Vaelith struck the summit with a bolt of silver fire, and where the bolt landed, the hearthstone formed. Every ceremony, every pilgrimage route, every prayer orientation — all are anchored to the hearthstone as the point of divine contact. I am in the lower archives this morning, cataloguing water-damaged scrolls from the crypt expansion, when I unroll a textile that predates our oldest known text by at least three centuries. The weave is a map — unmistakably this hill, the river fork matching exactly — and at the summit, rendered in careful dye-work, is a structure. A furnace. Slag channels running downhill. The hearthstone's position is marked, but it is marked as the base of the furnace, not as a site of divine impact. The stone's shape, which I have always read as the scar of a lightning strike, matches the proportions of a smelting base drawn on the textile. The founding event of the Temple of Vaelith — the bolt, the divine mark — sits on top of an older structure that used the same stone for metalwork.

> A = Chief Chronicler; B = the hearthstone is the site of divine contact where Vaelith struck the summit (the foundational claim of the temple's religious authority and all its ceremonial practices); E = a pre-dating textile map showing the summit with a furnace and slag channels, the hearthstone marked as a smelting base matching its physical shape; B' = the hearthstone is a pre-existing industrial artifact (a furnace base), not a site of divine impact — the temple's founding narrative was built atop an older, secular structure. ✓ all four roles, ✓ all three states.

**Di-Fa1-4.** The queen's seneschal, Lord Maren, maintains the kingdom's census of fighting strength. Every spring he collects muster rolls from the twelve vassal lords, and every spring the count holds steady: forty-two hundred sworn swords across the realm, enough to field a defensive force against any neighbour. He has set this year's border strategy on that number. A courier arrives at midday bearing not a muster roll but a sealed complaint from a border reeve. The complaint concerns a land dispute — irrelevant to Maren's purpose — but attached to it is a tax record the reeve included as evidence. The tax record lists the households in Baron Aldric's domain. Maren knows Aldric's muster roll: six hundred swords, the largest single contribution. The tax record shows one hundred and nine households. At the obligated rate of one sword per household, Aldric can muster one hundred and nine men. Maren pulls the muster rolls from the other eleven vassal lords and begins comparing them against the tax records his own office holds. The discrepancy is not limited to Aldric.

> A = Lord Maren (seneschal); B = the realm has forty-two hundred sworn swords available (per annual muster rolls from the twelve vassal lords, basis of border strategy); E = a tax record attached to an unrelated complaint shows Baron Aldric's domain has 109 households, not enough to support his claimed 600 swords — and cross-referencing other lords' tax records shows the inflation is widespread; B' = the muster rolls are systematically inflated — the realm's actual fighting strength is far below the number Maren has been using for strategic planning. ✓ all four roles, ✓ all three states.

**Di-Fa1-5.** Torchlight fills the sanctum as High Priestess Yelen places both hands on the Wellspring basin and begins the rite of renewal, the same rite she has performed at every equinox for eleven years. The Wellspring is the source of the temple's healing power — cool water rising from a fault in the bedrock, blessed by the first priestesses, channelling restorative energy that no other spring in the province can match. Petitioners travel weeks to bathe in it. Yelen draws the water to her lips as the rite requires. It tastes of copper. She holds the water in her cupped hand and tilts it toward the torchlight. A faint green tint. She kneels at the basin's edge and presses her hand against the stone where the water enters from below. The stone is warm. Not blessed-warm, the gentle warmth the scripture attributes to the goddess's presence. Hot. She follows the feed channel on her hands and knees to where it passes through the wall, and finds the jointure: a clay pipe, mortared into the channel, running not from the bedrock fault but from the east — from the direction of the copper mine whose runoff has been a known hazard for three years. The Wellspring has not been drawing from the ancient fault. The temple's healing water is mine runoff entering through a pipe that someone built into the channel wall. The restorative power Yelen has been dispensing to petitioners — the power she has staked her temple's reputation on — flows from a copper mine.

> A = High Priestess Yelen; B = the Wellspring draws blessed water from an ancient bedrock fault, the source of the temple's unique healing power; E = the water tastes of copper and is green-tinted; following the feed channel, she finds a clay pipe redirecting water not from the fault but from a nearby copper mine; B' = the Wellspring's water source is mine runoff piped into the channel, not the ancient bedrock fault — the temple's healing water has no connection to the source it claims. ✓ all four roles, ✓ all three states.

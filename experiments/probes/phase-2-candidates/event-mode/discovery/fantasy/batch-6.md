# Discovery × Fantasy — Batch 6 (Tier C)

## Di-Fa6-1

The ward-keeper of the Crimson Bridge has checked the binding stones every morning for thirty-one years. Each stone holds one spirit — one name carved into the granite, one luminous signature sealed inside. The ward-keeper, Brother Osric, knows every stone by position. He walks the row the same way each dawn, pressing his palm to each face to feel the warmth of the bound presence beneath. On this morning he presses his palm to the forty-seventh stone and feels nothing. Not cold — nothing. He presses harder. Still nothing. He checks the name carved into the face: Yvel of the Salt Marshes, bound in the thirty-first year of the current accord. Osric has included Yvel in every count he has submitted to the Conclave for the past decade, certain of her continued presence because the stone has never run cold. But cold means the spirit has departed through the binding's natural dissolution. Nothing — the absence of any thermal signature at all — means the binding was severed from outside. Yvel has not dissolved. She has been taken. The ward-keeper stands motionless at the forty-seventh stone and recalculates everything he thought he knew about the Conclave's border security.

> A = Brother Osric (ward-keeper); B = all forty-seven bound spirits remain sealed in their stones, with the ward-keeper's count accurate and the binding intact — a cold stone would indicate natural dissolution; E = direct physical contact with the forty-seventh stone returns no thermal signature whatsoever, indicating forced external severance rather than natural dissolution; B′ = at least one spirit has been taken by external action, not dissolved naturally — the border binding has been breached. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states

## Di-Fa6-2

"You said the Moonweavers' charter grants them sole right to work silk enchantments within the city walls," the magistrate's clerk says.

"It does," Guildmistress Halvane replies. She has argued the charter before three different courts without once being contradicted. "Forty-two years, renewed by every Lord-Regent since the Compact was signed. No other guild may enchant silk within Varenthis."

The clerk sets a folded document on the table. "This is the original Compact, copied from the Regent's archive this morning. Article nine grants the Moonweavers exclusive right to enchant silk for trade within the city walls." He lets a pause sit. "Article fourteen grants any temple of the Twelve the right to enchant cloth of any kind for use in sacred rites, without restriction. The Silverthread Sisters are a recognised temple of the Twelve."

Halvane takes the document and reads both articles twice. She has structured every licensing dispute, every exclusivity agreement, every fee schedule around a monopoly that does not, in fact, exist — not for temple work.

> A = Guildmistress Halvane; B = the Moonweavers hold a complete monopoly on silk enchantment within the city walls under the Compact; E = original Compact document showing Article 14 grants temples of the Twelve unrestricted rights to enchant cloth for sacred purposes — an exemption that predates and overrides the guild's exclusivity claim; B′ = the Moonweavers' monopoly does not extend to temple work; the Silverthread Sisters have operated legally all along. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states

## Di-Fa6-3

I have served the Order of the Pale Lantern as its chief interrogator for fourteen years, and in fourteen years I have administered the Truthbinding to two hundred and eleven suspects. The Truthbinding is absolute. This is what we teach novices. This is what I have testified before three separate tribunals. A bound tongue cannot speak false. If the suspect speaks, they speak true; if they are lying, the binding silences them entirely. The condemned merchant Aldric spoke freely under the Truthbinding and confessed to the theft of the treasury seals. He was executed on my testimony.

Tonight the treasury seals were found — intact, in the locking chest of the castle steward, wrapped in a cloth that predates Aldric's arrest by two months. The steward has fled. I am sitting with this fact in the interrogation room, the Truthbinding sigil painted on the table before me, and I am arriving at the only explanation the evidence permits: Aldric spoke freely because he believed himself innocent. The Truthbinding does not test whether the speaker is truthful. It tests whether the speaker believes they are truthful.

> A = chief interrogator (I); B = the Truthbinding is an absolute test of factual truth — a bound tongue cannot state a falsehood; E = treasury seals found intact in the steward's chest, predating Aldric's arrest, after Aldric confessed under the Truthbinding and was executed; B′ = the Truthbinding tests sincerity of belief, not factual accuracy — Aldric was bound and spoke what he believed, but his belief was false. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states

## Di-Fa6-4

The road to the eastern granaries is clear when the Countess of Marleth rides it at midday, her steward beside her. She has ridden this road every harvest season for twelve years, and the granaries have never failed to be full by the first cold moon. The count of sacks submitted by her reeve each year has held steady: between nine and eleven thousand, enough to carry the eastern villages through any winter on record. She does not visit the granaries herself — the reeve's count is sufficient, her father taught her, and a countess's time is better spent at court. She rides today because the reeve died last week and she needs to introduce his replacement to the head steward. The granary doors open. The Countess steps inside and looks at the rows of stacking frames. Perhaps four thousand sacks, possibly fewer. Some frames are not merely empty but rotted at the base, the wood soft with years of disuse. She looks at her new reeve, then at her steward. The count she has been carrying in her head — the count she presented to the Lord-Regent two months ago to secure exemption from the emergency tithe — is not a measurement of what is here.

> A = Countess of Marleth; B = the eastern granaries hold 9,000–11,000 sacks annually as reported by the reeve — sufficient for winter and accurate enough to report to the Lord-Regent; E = direct inspection of the granary showing approximately 4,000 sacks at most, with storage frames in advanced decay indicating chronic long-term underuse; B′ = the reeve's count was not an accurate measure of actual stores; the granary has been significantly below reported capacity, likely for years. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states

## Di-Fa6-5

ACCOUNT OF THE PROCEEDINGS OF THE CONCLAVE OF LOREMASTERS, THIRD SESSION, AS RECORDED BY THE SENIOR SCRIBE

Master Davan opened the session by presenting his thesis on the Founder's Compact to the assembled Loremasters. He has taught the Compact's origins for twenty years; his lecture at the Autumn Convocation on the subject has been the authoritative account in the Order. The Compact, as Master Davan has always taught, was negotiated between the three founding houses under duress of war, its binding clauses written in a single session by the Founder herself.

Loremaster Ysenne of the second house rose and placed on the table a cache of letters recovered from the Vault of the Third Founding, unsealed for the first time in this generation. She read two aloud. The first is dated eleven years before the date Master Davan attributes to the Compact's negotiation. The second is a response. Both describe in plain language a draft of the binding clauses already circulating among the houses — identical in structure to the final Compact. The Founder did not compose the clauses under wartime urgency. The framework had been in negotiation for more than a decade before the war provided the occasion to seal it.

Master Davan did not speak for some time after Ysenne finished reading.

> A = Master Davan (Loremaster); B = the Founder composed the Compact's binding clauses in a single session under wartime duress — this is the origin he has taught for twenty years; E = letters from the Vault of the Third Founding dated eleven years before the Compact's sealing, showing the binding clauses already in draft circulation among the houses; B′ = the Compact's framework was the product of a decade of negotiation, not a wartime composition — the Founder's authorship under duress is incorrect. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states

# Discovery x Fantasy -- Batch 5 (Tier B)

**Di-Fa5-1.** The war-herald Jorun rides through the gates of Keldmark at dusk, carrying the muster rolls for the Northern Alliance. She has spent four months assembling the coalition: six holds, each pledging their full levy, each confirmed by signed blood-oath before their hall's hearthstone. The combined strength, as she has reported to the High Thane and as the war council has used to plan the spring offensive, is four thousand shields. She dismounts in the marshalling yard. The yard is not empty -- men are arriving -- but they are wrong. She walks the lines. Half the shields bear the crest of Keldmark itself. Of the five allied holds, three have sent token bands of fewer than forty. One has sent no one at all. The musters she carries, signed and blood-sealed, promise forces that did not march. The alliance she built exists on parchment. In the yard, the Northern Alliance numbers fewer than nine hundred.

> A = war-herald Jorun; B = the Northern Alliance has mustered four thousand shields from six holds (per signed blood-oaths); E = direct observation of the marshalling yard showing fewer than nine hundred fighters, with three holds sending token bands and one sending none; B' = the alliance is hollow -- the blood-oaths were not honoured, and the fighting strength for the spring offensive is a fraction of what was pledged. All four roles, all three states.

**Di-Fa5-2.** "You are telling me the boy has no gift," Magister Aldren says, holding the polished scrying lens between his fingers.

"None," says the examiner, Thella. "I tested him myself. Three rounds of attunement, three rounds of nothing. The lens stayed dark."

Aldren frowns. The boy -- Corin, a tanner's son from the outer ward -- was brought to the Academy because the street warden reported him levitating stones in the gutter. Aldren watched the warden's testimony himself and approved the intake.

"Give me the lens." He takes it and turns it over. A hairline crack runs along the inner face, invisible unless the lens is tilted against the light. He holds it to the candle. The crack bisects the resonance channel. "This lens is broken, Thella. The attunement surface is split."

"It has been the standard-issue lens for all intake examinations this term."

Aldren sets it down. Corin is not the only candidate examined this term. Fourteen children were tested with this lens over the past three weeks, and every one of them -- every one -- was assessed as ungifted. The Academy's intake process has been sorting candidates with a dead instrument. Any child who passed through it may carry the gift that the broken lens could not detect.

> A = Magister Aldren; B = fourteen candidates tested this term lack magical aptitude (the Academy's intake examinations found no gift in any of them); E = physical examination of the scrying lens showing a crack that bisects the resonance channel, rendering it non-functional; B' = the intake results are invalid -- the lens was incapable of detecting the gift, and any or all of the fourteen rejected candidates may possess it. All four roles, all three states.

**Di-Fa5-3.** I have served as Keeper of the Beacon on the Isle of Therys for six years, and in six years I have lit the signal fire on the winter solstice and received an answering light from the Citadel on the mainland within the hour. The answering light is the Citadel's confirmation that the sea-ward is intact: that the enchantment binding the deep currents away from the coastal towns still holds. I lit the beacon at dusk. I waited. No answering light came. I waited until the oil burned to the dregs and the flame guttered out, and still the mainland shore was dark. At first light I took the skiff across the strait. The Citadel's beacon tower stands on the eastern cliff, and I climbed to it expecting to find the keeper asleep or the oil stores empty. The tower is cold. The fire-bowl is filled with sand and seabird nests. The winding stair is slick with years of rain. No one has tended this beacon in a very long time. The answering light I have received every solstice for six years did not come from here. I do not know what I have been seeing across the water, but the Citadel has not been confirming the ward.

> A = I (Keeper of the Beacon on Therys); B = the Citadel's answering light each solstice confirms the sea-ward is intact and the Citadel is actively maintaining it; E = finding the Citadel's beacon tower derelict, cold, fire-bowl filled with sand and bird nests, untended for years; B' = the Citadel has not been sending the answering light -- whatever light he has been seeing is not the confirmation he believed it to be, and the status of the sea-ward is unknown. All four roles, all three states.

**Di-Fa5-4.** The ore-mistress of Dunhaven spreads the assay tokens across the counting table. Copper, tin, iron -- each token stamped with the mine's seal and the assayer's mark, each representing a tested vein. The mines beneath Dunhaven have produced copper for three generations, and the ore-mistress prices her contracts, pays her miners, and sets her smelting quotas on the basis that the mountain yields copper with trace tin. The trader from the southern ports is here to renegotiate, and he has brought his own assayer -- an old woman who asks to see not the tokens but the raw ore. The ore-mistress sends for a fresh crate from the deepest shaft. The southern assayer breaks a piece, heats it, works it with acid from a clay vial. The sample does not behave like copper with trace tin. The acid reaction is wrong. The old woman holds up theite: the vein the miners have been working for the past two years is not copper ore at all. It is arsenopyrite -- iron arsenic sulphide -- visually similar but chemically distinct, worthless for the contracts the ore-mistress has been signing and toxic to the smelters who have been processing it as copper.

> A = the ore-mistress of Dunhaven; B = the deep-shaft vein yields copper with trace tin (the basis of all contracts, quotas, and smelting operations); E = the southern assayer's acid test on raw ore showing the mineral is arsenopyrite, not copper; B' = the vein is arsenopyrite -- the mine's output is chemically different from what contracts specify, the existing contracts are unfulfillable, and the smelters have been processing a toxic mineral. All four roles, all three states.

**Di-Fa5-5.** The temple warden, Brother Severin, kneels before the Reliquary of Saint Aeleth and begins the dawn rite. He presses his palm to the glass as he has done every morning for nine years. The relic -- a finger bone of the saint, set in a silver cradle -- is the spiritual anchor of the parish. Pilgrims travel weeks to kneel before it. The bishop's tithe for this parish is set at triple the standard rate, justified by the relic's drawing power. This morning the glass panel shifts under his hand. It is not sealed. He lifts it. The silver cradle holds a bone, as it has always held a bone, but the bone is wrong. It is too large for a human finger. He turns it in the candlelight. The joint is bovine -- a knuckle bone from a cow, carved to approximate a finger's shape and stained dark with walnut oil. The pilgrims, the triple tithe, the parish's standing within the diocese -- all of it built around this cradle and what it is supposed to contain. What it contains is a carved cattle bone.

> A = Brother Severin (temple warden); B = the Reliquary of Saint Aeleth contains the saint's finger bone (the spiritual and economic foundation of the parish); E = handling the bone and finding it is too large for a human finger -- bovine, carved to approximate finger shape, stained with walnut oil; B' = the relic is a forgery -- the parish's pilgrimage status, triple tithe, and diocesan standing rest on a carved cattle bone, not a saint's remains. All four roles, all three states.

# Discovery × Fantasy — Batch 3 (Tier C)

**Di-Fa3-1**

The Archivist of the Grey Conclave has held the census of bound spirits for eleven years, and in eleven years the count has held steady: forty-three wraiths contained within the tower's warding stones, catalogued by name and binding-date. She recites the number when petitioners ask, includes it in her quarterly report to the High Council, and shapes the Conclave's warding-stone requisitions around it. On the morning of the autumn sealing, she walks the lower vaults for the first time since her predecessor's retirement — a courtesy inspection she has deferred annually until the new junior Archivist pressed her for the induction tour. The vault registers are carved into the stone above each alcove. She reads them as she walks. Alcoves one through twenty-nine: occupied, seals intact. Thirty through forty-three: the alcoves are there, the registration glyphs above them are intact, but the binding-stones inside are cold and dark and empty. Fourteen bindings, logged as active, have no spirits in them. The Conclave's entire defensive assessment — its staff allocation, its requisition schedule, its threat classification — has been calibrated to forty-three. The actual count is twenty-nine.

> A = Archivist of the Grey Conclave; B = forty-three wraiths are bound and active in the tower's warding stones; E = physical inspection of lower vaults showing alcoves 30–43 have intact registration glyphs but cold, empty binding-stones — fourteen logged bindings contain nothing; B′ = only twenty-nine spirits are actually bound; fourteen logged bindings are empty, and the Conclave's defensive posture is based on a count that does not exist. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states

**Di-Fa3-2**

"You said the Thornwall holds the eastern passes against the clans," the herald Mireya says. "Every treaty we have signed with the coast lords is built on that."

"It does," says Commander Aldric. "The garrison at Keld's Crossing is four hundred swords. They have held the Thornwall for sixty years."

"I came through Keld's Crossing this morning."

He waits.

"There is no garrison. There is an inn, a collapsed watchtower, and two goatherds. The Thornwall stands — the stone stands — but there is no one in it. I asked the innkeeper when the soldiers had last been billeted there. He said his grandfather's time." Mireya sets the road-toll ledger on the table between them — the document she had collected from the crossing post as a matter of routine. There is no garrison signature on any entry for the past forty years. The toll-collector's mark is always the same single hand: the innkeeper's family, generation to generation. "The coast lords have been paying their treaty obligations on the assumption that Keld's Crossing is held," she says. "We have been guaranteeing safe passage through a pass that no one is guarding."

> A = Commander Aldric; B = the garrison at Keld's Crossing holds the Thornwall with four hundred soldiers, securing the eastern passes; E = herald Mireya's firsthand account of an unmanned Keld's Crossing, corroborated by a road-toll ledger showing no garrison signature for forty years; B′ = the garrison does not exist; the Thornwall has been undefended for at least a generation. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states

**Di-Fa3-3**

I have served the Temple of the Undying Flame as senior interpreter for nine years, and in nine years I have read the augury smoke the same way my predecessors read it: red and gold spirals ascending east mean the Flame approves the petition; grey spirals dispersing south mean it does not. Every interpretation I have rendered, every supplicant I have turned away, every noble house I have counselled — all of it rested on that codex. This morning the temple's founder-scroll was brought out of the reliquary for the centenary rite, and because I was the senior interpreter, the unrolling fell to me. The augury codex I have been using is in the founder's own hand. The colour assignments are as I know them. But in the margin beside "red and gold ascending east," in the same hand, in ink that has never been transcribed into the working copies, is a single gloss: "when the altar fire has been fed cedar only." I looked at the brazier records for the past nine years. We have burned a blend — cedar, pine resin, and pressed olive — since the oil shortage of the third year of my tenure. Every reading I have given since then has been made through smoke the codex does not describe.

> A = senior interpreter of the Temple of the Undying Flame; B = the augury codex applies universally — red and gold spirals ascending east indicate divine approval regardless of fuel composition; E = founder-scroll margin gloss in the founder's own hand specifying that colour meanings apply only when the altar fire is fed cedar exclusively; combined with brazier records showing blended fuel in use for six years; B′ = the codex's colour interpretations are fuel-conditional; every augury rendered during the blended-fuel period used a method the codex does not validate. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states

**Di-Fa3-4**

The guild-mistress of the Dyers' Compact spreads the bolts of cloth across the inspection table. They are indigo — her guild's registered indigo, the shade that carries the Compact's seal of origin and commands triple the market price in every city south of the Alder River. She has allocated every measure of the season's weld and woad harvest to members in good standing. She has certified the yield herself. The merchant Ferrante stands at the far end of the table. He had been stopped at the city gate with thirty bolts bearing the Compact's seal, and the gate-warden had sent for her because the toll classification required her signature. She runs her thumb along the edge of the first bolt. The weave is correct. The weight is correct. She lifts the bolt to the window and holds it in daylight: the shade is three tones lighter than true Compact indigo — the difference only visible against natural light, invisible in lamplight or shadow, exactly the difference that comes from using weld without woad to fix the dye. She pulls the dye-lot stamp from her belt and presses it to the cloth. The seal fits. It is the Compact's seal, cast from the Compact's matrix. Someone inside the guild has sold access to the stamp.

> A = guild-mistress of the Dyers' Compact; B = the Compact's seal of origin can only appear on cloth dyed to specification by guild members — the seal guarantees both membership and quality; E = inspecting the bolts in natural daylight reveals the shade is three tones light (weld-only dye, not weld-and-woad), while the seal is genuine Compact-matrix — proof the stamp was applied to sub-standard cloth; B′ = the seal does not guarantee quality; an insider has given an external party access to the stamp matrix, separating the mark from the standard it was meant to certify. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states

**Di-Fa3-5**

The castellan of Vreth Hold is counting the grain stores when the miller's boy arrives with the autumn tally-sticks. The boy sets them on the table without ceremony — twelve sticks, notched in the miller's standard hand — and leaves. The castellan picks up the first stick. The count is lower than expected, but not alarmingly so; he sets it aside and works through the rest. On the ninth stick he finds an anomaly: the notching pattern that in the miller's hand means "received from the southern farmsteads" appears twice, on both the ninth and tenth stick, for the same week. He lines them up. They are not duplicates — the dates are different by one day, but the notch counts are identical to the bushel. The same quantity received twice from the same source in consecutive days. He sends for the southern farmstead's reeve and shows him the sticks. The reeve examines them and says nothing for a long time. Then: "We had only one collection that week, my lord. We had the grain only once." The tally-sticks have been logging grain entering the mill. Whether that grain ever reached the castle stores is a different question. The castellan has been treating mill-entry records as castle-inventory records.

> A = castellan of Vreth Hold; B = the miller's tally-sticks record grain arriving into the castle's stores — mill receipts and castle inventory are equivalent; E = duplicate tally entries for the same farmstead in consecutive days, confirmed by the reeve as physically impossible (one collection that week), showing the sticks record mill intake, not castle delivery; B′ = mill-entry records and castle inventory are not equivalent; grain logged at the mill may not have been transferred to the castle stores. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states

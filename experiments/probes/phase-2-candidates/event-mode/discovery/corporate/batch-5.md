# Discovery x Corporate -- Batch 5 (Tier B)

**Di-Co5-1.** The chief procurement officer is reviewing the approved-vendor registry when the new auditor, Diane Yates, sets a manila folder on his desk. "I pulled the beneficial-ownership filings for every vendor in the top tier," she says. "Fourteen companies." He nods — fourteen is the number he has maintained for three years, each vetted, each with separate contracts and separate account managers. Diane opens the folder. The filings show that nine of the fourteen vendors share a single registered agent, a single mailing address in Wilmington, Delaware, and a single beneficial owner: Gerald Kaplan, the brother-in-law of the VP of operations. The chief procurement officer has structured the company's entire supply chain around competitive bidding among independent suppliers. Nine of those suppliers are one entity.

> A = chief procurement officer; B = the top-tier vendor pool consists of fourteen independent, competitively vetted suppliers; E = beneficial-ownership filings showing nine of the fourteen vendors share a single registered agent, address, and beneficial owner (the VP of operations' brother-in-law); B' = the competitive vendor pool is largely fictitious -- nine vendors are a single entity, and the bidding process has been compromised. All four roles, all three states.

**Di-Co5-2.** Naomi Tsang pulls up the real-time energy dashboard for Building C. She manages facilities for the campus and has scheduled the HVAC retrofit around the building's usage profile: peak draw between seven a.m. and six p.m., near-zero overnight, matching the six hundred employees badged to the building. The retrofit contractor needs the building empty for seventy-two hours, so Naomi has arranged a weekend window. But the contractor is standing beside her now, pointing at the overnight curve. It is not near-zero. The draw between midnight and five a.m. runs at sixty percent of daytime peak -- every night, seven days a week, for at least the past ninety days of logged data. Six hundred employees badge in during the day. Something else entirely is drawing power at night, and the building she planned to empty for a weekend has never actually been empty.

> A = Naomi Tsang (facilities manager); B = Building C is occupied and drawing power only during business hours (matching its 600-employee badge population); E = real-time energy dashboard showing consistent 60%-of-peak power draw overnight, every night, for 90+ days; B' = the building has significant undocumented overnight operations -- it is never truly unoccupied, and its usage profile is fundamentally different from what the badge data implies. All four roles, all three states.

**Di-Co5-3.** "Give me the margin on Whitfield," says Renata Okoye, the division president, leaning back in her chair.

"Whitfield is our best account," the sales director says. "Thirty-eight percent gross margin. Highest in the portfolio."

"And the fully loaded number?"

Silence. Renata waits.

"We don't break it out that way for individual accounts," the sales director says.

Renata turns her laptop around. She has spent the morning pulling Whitfield's service tickets, warranty claims, dedicated support headcount, and custom-engineering hours from four different systems and loading them into a spreadsheet. "Whitfield filed two hundred and eleven service tickets last year. Your team assigned three full-time engineers to their custom integrations. Their warranty claims alone consumed more than what we billed them for support."

She points to the bottom line. "Fully loaded, the margin is negative four percent. We are paying to keep this account."

The sales director stares at the number. The account he has held up in every quarterly review as proof of the team's value is a net loss.

> A = the sales director; B = Whitfield is the portfolio's best account at 38% gross margin; E = Renata's fully loaded cost analysis (service tickets, dedicated headcount, warranty claims, custom-engineering hours) showing negative 4% margin; B' = Whitfield is a net loss -- the margin he has been citing omits the real costs of serving the account. All four roles, all three states.

**Di-Co5-4.** I had run internal communications for the firm for seven years, and I had built every employee-newsletter campaign on the same assumption: the newsletter was widely read. Open rates averaged sixty-one percent, far above industry benchmarks, and I cited that number in every budget justification. I was troubleshooting a rendering issue on Thursday when I found that the email platform was counting image-proxy prefetches by the corporate firewall as opens. Every email routed through the firewall was registering as opened before it reached a human inbox. I rebuilt the metric with proxy prefetches excluded. The actual open rate was eleven percent. Five hundred and seven people on the distribution list; fifty-six were reading it.

> A = I (internal communications manager); B = the employee newsletter has a 61% open rate and is widely read; E = finding that the email platform counts corporate-firewall image-proxy prefetches as opens, inflating the metric; B' = the actual open rate is 11% -- the newsletter is read by roughly one in ten employees, and the metric that justified the programme was an artefact of network infrastructure. All four roles, all three states.

**Di-Co5-5.** The head of data engineering, Sanjay Mehta, sits in the quarterly architecture review. His team has spent eight months migrating the company's analytics pipeline to a new lakehouse platform, and the migration is ninety-three percent complete by his tracker. He is presenting the final cutover timeline when the chief data officer interrupts: "Sanjay, show us the query logs from the old system." Sanjay pulls them up, expecting a flatline -- the old platform should be nearly idle now. The query volume on the legacy system is higher than it was before the migration began. More users, more queries, more compute hours. The teams that were supposed to have migrated are running parallel workloads on both systems. His migration is ninety-three percent complete by table count, but the old system is more heavily used than ever.

> A = Sanjay Mehta (head of data engineering); B = the analytics migration is 93% complete and the legacy system is being decommissioned; E = query logs showing the legacy system has higher query volume, more users, and more compute hours than before the migration started; B' = the migration is cosmetic -- teams have migrated tables but not workloads, and the legacy system is more entrenched than ever. All four roles, all three states.

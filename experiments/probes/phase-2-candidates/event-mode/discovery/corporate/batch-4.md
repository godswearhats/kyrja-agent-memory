# Discovery × Corporate — Batch 4 (Tier A)

**Di-Co4-1.** The chief procurement officer sits in the supplier audit room at the Suzhou factory, watching the production line through the glass partition. She has sourced components from this facility for four years and has built her dual-sourcing strategy on the assumption that this plant and the Dongguan plant are independent operations — separate ownership, separate tooling, separate supply chains. That is the basis of the risk model she presents to the board every quarter: no single point of failure. The factory manager steps out to take a phone call, and the whiteboard behind his desk is visible for the first time. Pinned to it is a shared master production schedule. Both facility names — Suzhou and Dongguan — are columns in the same planning grid, with a single allocation authority listed at the top: the same holding company, the same production director, the same steel supplier feeding both lines. Her dual-sourcing strategy has been routing through one operation split across two buildings.

> A = chief procurement officer; B = the Suzhou and Dongguan facilities are independent operations (separate ownership, tooling, supply chains), providing genuine dual-sourcing with no single point of failure; E = whiteboard showing a shared master production schedule with both facilities managed by the same holding company, production director, and steel supplier; B′ = the two facilities are a single operation split across two sites — the dual-sourcing strategy has no actual redundancy. ✓ all four roles, ✓ all three states.

**Di-Co4-2.** "Nadia, what's our exposure to the Fujikawa account?"

"Twelve percent of revenue. But it's stable — they renewed the three-year contract in January. I reviewed the terms myself."

The general counsel slides a printed page across the table. "This is from their 10-K, filed yesterday. Page forty-one. Read the paragraph I've highlighted."

Nadia, the chief revenue officer, reads it aloud: "The company intends to transition all third-party analytics services to its newly formed internal data-science division by fiscal year-end, with termination notices to be issued no later than Q3."

She reads it again, slower.

"The three-year contract," the general counsel says. "Does it have an early-termination clause?"

"Ninety days' notice. Standard." Nadia sets the page down. Fujikawa has not been a locked-in client on a stable contract. Fujikawa has been a client with a published exit timeline and a ninety-day escape hatch, and the revenue she has been calling stable is scheduled for elimination.

> A = Nadia (chief revenue officer); B = the Fujikawa account is stable, locked-in revenue (twelve percent of total, backed by a three-year contract renewed in January); E = Fujikawa's own 10-K filing stating intent to bring all third-party analytics in-house by fiscal year-end, with termination notices by Q3; B′ = the Fujikawa revenue is not stable — the client has a published exit plan and a ninety-day termination clause, making this revenue scheduled for elimination. ✓ all four roles, ✓ all three states.

**Di-Co4-3.** I had managed the company's insurance programme for eight years and had always treated the umbrella policy as the backstop — two hundred million dollars of excess liability coverage sitting above the primary layers, there if we ever needed it. That was the number I quoted to the board, the number in the annual risk report, the number the M&A team used when modelling acquisition exposure. I was on a call with the broker that Wednesday afternoon, asking a routine question about a certificate of insurance for a new lease, when she mentioned in passing that the umbrella had been endorsement-restricted in the last renewal cycle. I asked her to send the endorsement schedule. It arrived eleven minutes later. The policy excluded cyber liability, product recall, and environmental claims — the three categories that accounted for ninety percent of our actual risk exposure. The two-hundred-million-dollar backstop I had been reporting to the board covered almost nothing we would ever need it to cover.

> A = I (insurance programme manager); B = the umbrella policy provides $200M of excess liability coverage as a genuine backstop across the company's risk exposure; E = broker's endorsement schedule showing the umbrella excludes cyber liability, product recall, and environmental claims — the three categories constituting 90% of actual risk exposure; B′ = the umbrella policy covers almost none of the company's material risk categories — the reported backstop is functionally hollow. ✓ all four roles, ✓ all three states.

**Di-Co4-4.** The head of talent acquisition is pulling offer-acceptance data for the quarterly hiring review when the dashboard throws a filter she has never used: source attribution by referral chain. She has structured the entire recruitment strategy around the premise that the company's employer brand drives inbound applications — the careers page, the LinkedIn presence, the conference circuit. That is why her budget is seventy percent brand spend. She clicks the filter. Eighty-three percent of accepted offers in the last four quarters trace back to exactly six employees, all in the same engineering team, all referring candidates from their previous employer. The inbound funnel she has been funding — the careers page, the social campaigns, the sponsored conference booths — accounts for eleven percent of hires who actually accepted. The employer brand has not been the engine. Six people and their personal networks have been doing the work her budget was supposed to do.

> A = head of talent acquisition; B = the company's employer brand (careers page, LinkedIn presence, conference circuit) drives inbound hiring — justifying a 70% brand-spend budget; E = source-attribution filter showing 83% of accepted offers trace to six employees in one engineering team referring from their previous employer, with the branded inbound funnel accounting for only 11% of accepted hires; B′ = the employer brand is not the hiring engine — six individuals' personal referral networks have been producing the vast majority of successful hires. ✓ all four roles, ✓ all three states.

**Di-Co4-5.** INTERNAL MEMORANDUM — CONFIDENTIAL
TO: Board Risk Committee
FROM: Director of Internal Audit, G. Okonkwo
RE: Findings from unscheduled review of intercompany loan balances

The consolidated balance sheet presented at the March board meeting shows intercompany loans receivable of $340M across our four subsidiaries, offset by corresponding payables, netting to zero consolidated exposure. This has been the reported position for seven consecutive quarters. I treated the netting as mechanical and had never requested the underlying settlement records.

On 9 May I requested settlement confirmations directly from each subsidiary's treasury function. Two of the four subsidiaries confirmed their balances within twenty-four hours. The third — Terravox Holdings — responded that it had reclassified its $140M payable as a capital contribution eighteen months ago, at the instruction of the group CFO, and no longer carries the obligation on its books. The fourth — Meridian Logistics — reported that its $95M receivable from Terravox has been in arrears since the reclassification and is carried at face value with no impairment.

The net-zero position reported to this committee does not exist. The consolidated balance sheet contains a $140M asymmetry that has been present for eighteen months, and $95M of the reported receivables are uncollectable under the current intercompany structure. I am requesting an emergency session.

> A = G. Okonkwo (Director of Internal Audit); B = intercompany loans net to zero consolidated exposure (the position reported to the board for seven quarters, treated as mechanical); E = subsidiary treasury confirmations showing Terravox reclassified its $140M payable as a capital contribution 18 months ago (eliminating its obligation), while Meridian still carries the corresponding $95M receivable at face value; B′ = the net-zero position does not exist — there is a $140M asymmetry in the intercompany structure, and $95M in receivables are uncollectable. ✓ all four roles, ✓ all three states.

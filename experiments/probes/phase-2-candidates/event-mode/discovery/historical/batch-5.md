# Discovery x Historical -- Batch 5 (Tier B)

**Di-Hi5-1.** The chief cartographer of the Portuguese Casa da India, Fernao Gomes, unrolls the new chart on the vellum table in Lisbon in the spring of 1512. He has spent three years compiling reports from returning pilots to build the definitive coastal profile of the Spice Islands. Every chart he has issued to the fleet shows the passage between Flores and Solor as a deep-water strait, wide enough for a carrack under full sail -- the route on which the entire eastward spice trade depends. The pilot standing before him now, Diogo Lopes, has just returned from the passage and brought something the Casa has never received: a sounding log. Lopes took depth readings every half-league. Gomes reads the numbers. The strait is silting. The central channel, marked on his chart at twelve fathoms, now shows four. Two of the readings show less than two. A loaded carrack draws three. The route that every fleet sailing east this season will follow, the route printed on every chart Gomes has certified, is becoming impassable.

> A = Fernao Gomes (chief cartographer, Casa da India); B = the Flores-Solor strait is a deep-water passage (twelve fathoms) suitable for loaded carracks; E = Diogo Lopes's sounding log showing the channel at four fathoms and falling, with readings below two in places; B' = the strait is silting to the point of impassability for loaded carracks -- the charts issued to the fleet describe a passage that no longer exists at navigable depth. All four roles, all three states.

**Di-Hi5-2.** "How many of the inoculated children survived?" Dr. Benjamin Waterhouse asks. He is sitting across from the selectman of Marblehead, Massachusetts, in August of 1802. He has spent two years advocating for cowpox vaccination in New England, staking his reputation on its superiority to the older variolation method. He has published, lectured, and personally supplied vaccine matter to six towns.

"All of them survived," the selectman says.

"Then the vaccine --"

"The vaccine did nothing, Doctor." The selectman pulls a letter from his coat. "I had Dr. Bartlett test the children last month. He variolated four of your vaccinated subjects with live smallpox matter. Standard procedure, a scratch on the arm."

Waterhouse waits.

"All four took the disease. Full pustules within the week. Your vaccine matter was inert. The children you inoculated have no more protection than the day they were born."

Waterhouse takes the letter. He has distributed the same batch of vaccine matter to five other towns. Every certificate of immunity he has issued from that stock -- and he has issued more than two hundred -- certifies a protection that does not exist.

> A = Dr. Benjamin Waterhouse; B = his cowpox vaccine matter confers immunity to smallpox (the basis of two hundred certificates and six town programmes); E = the selectman's report that variolation challenge on four vaccinated subjects produced full disease -- the vaccine matter was inert; B' = the vaccine stock he has been distributing is dead -- two hundred certificates of immunity are worthless, and five other towns are similarly unprotected. All four roles, all three states.

**Di-Hi5-3.** I am the bookkeeper for the Fondaco dei Tedeschi in Venice, and it is the morning of 3 September 1508. I have kept the ledgers for the German merchants' warehouse for eleven years, and in eleven years I have balanced the accounts against one fixed truth: the Fondaco holds a monopoly on German trade through Venice. Every tariff I collect, every warehouse fee I assess, every surcharge I levy on overland goods presumes that German merchants have no alternative route to the eastern markets. This morning a Dalmatian captain docked at the Molo and unloaded forty bales of Augsburg woollens. I went to the quay myself. The bales bear the marks of three German trading houses -- Fugger, Welser, Hochstetter -- and none of them passed through my warehouse. The captain's bill of lading shows the goods travelled overland to Ragusa and by sea from there, bypassing Venice entirely. The Fondaco's monopoly, the assumption on which every fee in my ledger depends, is not holding. The Germans have found another door.

> A = I (bookkeeper of the Fondaco dei Tedeschi); B = the Fondaco holds a monopoly on German trade to eastern markets -- German merchants have no viable alternative route; E = a Dalmatian ship unloading forty bales of Augsburg woollens from three major German trading houses, with a bill of lading showing an overland-to-Ragusa route that bypassed Venice entirely; B' = the monopoly is broken -- German merchants are actively routing goods through Ragusa, and the tariff structure built on the assumption of no alternatives is obsolete. All four roles, all three states.

**Di-Hi5-4.** The Spanish military engineer, Colonel Alvaro de Bazan, stands on the ramparts of Fort San Felipe in Cartagena de Indias in the spring of 1739. He designed the fort's seaward battery himself, angling the gun embrasures to cover the harbour mouth at maximum range. The battery holds twenty-four cannon, and the defensive plan for the entire port assumes those guns can engage any approaching squadron at twelve hundred yards -- the range he specified when he submitted the fortification plans to the Viceroy four years ago. A supply ship has run aground on the outer shoal, roughly eleven hundred yards from the battery. De Bazan orders a ranging shot to mark the distance for the gun crews. The ball falls into the sea three hundred yards short of the wreck. He orders a second gun fired, and a third. Both fall short by the same margin. The powder is correct, the elevation is correct, but the guns cannot reach. He examines the nearest cannon and finds the bore pitted and the trunnions worn smooth. The guns are aged-out. They have lost range that no amount of powder or elevation can recover. The twelve-hundred-yard engagement zone on which the entire harbour defence rests is an arc that these cannon can no longer fill.

> A = Colonel Alvaro de Bazan (military engineer); B = the seaward battery can engage targets at twelve hundred yards (the foundational assumption of the harbour defence plan); E = three ranging shots falling three hundred yards short of an eleven-hundred-yard target, with inspection showing pitted bores and worn trunnions; B' = the guns have degraded beyond their rated range -- the harbour's defensive perimeter is roughly eight hundred yards, not twelve hundred, and the fortification plan is built on a capability that no longer exists. All four roles, all three states.

**Di-Hi5-5.** PETITION OF ANNA MARIA BRANDT, PRESENTED TO THE GUILD COUNCIL OF MASTER BAKERS, LUBECK, 12 NOVEMBER 1487.

To the honourable Masters of the Guild: I, Anna Maria Brandt, widow of Master Heinrich Brandt, do petition for continuance of the bakery licence held under my late husband's name at the Holstenstrasse premises, as is the right of guild widows under the charter of 1431.

In preparing the accounts for this petition, I examined the flour stores and found the following: the cellar holds forty-three sacks bearing the seal of the Trave Mill, from which my husband exclusively purchased for twenty-two years. I opened six sacks at random. The flour in every sack is cut. Mixed into the wheat flour is a grey powder that I brought to the apothecary Herr Detlev Busch, who identified it as ground chalk and dried pea-hull in equal measure. The six sacks I tested are not flour as the Trave Mill's seal warrants. The bakery's reputation, which I am petitioning to inherit, was built on bread made from this stock. I do not know how long the mill has been adulterating its product, but the sacks currently in my cellar -- the same stock my husband baked from and sold under the guild's quality mark -- contain material that would fail the guild's assay.

I present this petition and this evidence together, for the Council's judgment on both matters.

> A = Anna Maria Brandt (guild widow / petitioner); B = the bakery's flour stock is pure wheat flour from the Trave Mill, as warranted by the mill's seal (the basis of the bakery's guild-quality reputation); E = opening six sacks and having the contents analysed by an apothecary, who identifies ground chalk and dried pea-hull mixed into the flour; B' = the flour has been adulterated -- the mill's seal does not guarantee purity, and the bread baked and sold under the guild's quality mark was made from compromised stock. All four roles, all three states.

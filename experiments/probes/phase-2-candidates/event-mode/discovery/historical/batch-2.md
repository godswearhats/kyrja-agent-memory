# Discovery × Historical — Batch 2 (Tier B)

**Di-Hi2-1.** The Venetian ambassador lifts the letter from the courier's hand and breaks the seal. He has spent two years in Constantinople cultivating the Grand Vizier as Venice's principal ally in the Ottoman court, the man who has twice intervened to protect Venetian trading privileges in the eastern Mediterranean. Every dispatch he has sent to the Doge in that time names the Grand Vizier as the guarantor of Venice's position. The letter is from the Doge's own intelligence secretary. Inside is a transcription of an intercepted message, written in the Genoese diplomatic cipher, from the Grand Vizier to the Genoese consul in Pera. The transcription details the precise terms of a commercial agreement: Genoa will receive exclusive anchorage at the Golden Horn, reduced tariffs on Black Sea grain, and priority berthing at Trebizond — every concession Venice currently holds, transferred wholesale to its chief rival, in the Grand Vizier's own hand. The ally the ambassador has spent two years cultivating is the author of Venice's displacement.

> A = the Venetian ambassador; B = the Grand Vizier is Venice's principal ally in the Ottoman court, the guarantor of Venetian trading privileges; E = intercepted Genoese-cipher letter from the Grand Vizier to the Genoese consul, detailing the transfer of all Venetian commercial concessions to Genoa; B' = the Grand Vizier is actively negotiating Venice's commercial displacement in favour of Genoa — he is not an ally but the architect of Venice's loss of position. ✓ all four roles, ✓ all three states.

**Di-Hi2-2.** "Sergeant Morrow, how many fit for duty?"

"The rolls show four hundred and twelve, Colonel."

Colonel Hartwell nods. He has marched this regiment south from Fredericksburg on the strength of that number. Four hundred and twelve men, enough to hold the river crossing the general has assigned him. He has written to the general confirming he can hold it. The order of battle for the entire left flank depends on his regiment being at the ford by dawn.

"Parade them," Hartwell says.

Morrow hesitates. "Sir, the rolls show—"

"Parade them. Now. In the field behind the church."

It takes twenty minutes. Hartwell counts the men himself, walking the line. He counts twice. One hundred and seventy-three. The rest are names on paper — men in hospital, men who never returned from furlough, men transferred to other companies whose transfers were never recorded. Morrow's rolls are not falsified. They are simply never corrected. Each company captain has reported his authorised strength, not his actual strength, for months.

"I have promised the general four hundred men at the ford," Hartwell says. He is looking at one hundred and seventy-three.

> A = Colonel Hartwell; B = his regiment has 412 men fit for duty (the number he has committed to the general for holding the river crossing); E = physical parade count of 173 men — the remaining 239 are hospitalized, absent without record, or transferred but never struck from rolls; B' = his regiment is at less than half reported strength; the rolls reflect authorised numbers, not actual bodies, and his commitment to hold the ford is based on men who do not exist in his ranks. ✓ all four roles, ✓ all three states.

**Di-Hi2-3.** I am the official translator for the Spanish delegation, and I have sat in this chamber for eleven sessions translating the Aztec lord Cuauhtli's words into Castilian for Governor Mendoza. For eleven sessions Cuauhtli has spoken of the gold mines in the northern valleys — their depth, their yield, the number of labourers required. I have translated faithfully. The Governor has planned his entire expedition on these translations: two hundred soldiers, forty mules, provisions for three months, a route drawn from Cuauhtli's descriptions. This morning Cuauhtli's servant brings water to the chamber and speaks quietly to his lord in a dialect I have not heard in these sessions — Tepanec, not Nahuatl. I studied under a Tepanec-speaking friar in Tlaxcala. Cuauhtli does not know this. What the servant says, in Tepanec, is: "The elders ask how many more sessions before the Castilians march north into the dead lands." Cuauhtli answers, in Tepanec: "Two more. Let the governor believe the mines are deep. Every day they prepare for the north is a day they do not march east, where the actual storehouses are." I keep my face still and continue writing. The mines I have been translating into the Governor's expedition plan do not exist. Cuauhtli has been dictating a decoy, and the expedition I have helped to build is the mechanism of that decoy.

> A = I (the Spanish translator); B = Cuauhtli's testimony about the northern gold mines is genuine intelligence, and the Governor's expedition plan based on it is sound; E = overhearing Cuauhtli's Tepanec-language exchange with his servant, in which Cuauhtli explicitly states the mines are fabricated and the purpose is to divert the Spanish away from the actual storehouses to the east; B' = the mine testimony is a deliberate decoy — Cuauhtli has been feeding false intelligence to redirect the expedition, and the entire plan is built on manufactured disinformation. ✓ all four roles, ✓ all three states.

**Di-Hi2-4.** The master mason runs his hand along the base of the cathedral's north pillar. He has built this pillar himself — set every course of stone, mixed the mortar to the proportions his father taught him, the proportions his guild has used for a hundred years. The Bishop has announced the consecration for Michaelmas, eight weeks away. The master mason has promised the pillar will bear the weight of the north transept vault. It is his name carved into the foundation stone. The afternoon sun falls through the clerestory at a low angle, and in that raking light the master mason sees what he has never seen in direct illumination: a dark line running horizontally through the mortar joint at the fourth course, stretching the full width of the pillar's western face. He takes his chisel and taps along the joint. The sound is hollow. He chips away a thumb-width of mortar and finds not bonded stone but powder — limestone dust packed into the joint as fill where solid mortar should be.

He stops. He removes mortar from the next joint above. Same thing: dust, not bond. The stone courses are not mortared together. They are stacked dry, with limestone waste packed into the joints to mimic solid work. His supplier has been delivering dressed stone with faces cut to specification but cores that crumble — and the joints have been filled with rubble dust to hide the absence of true mortar. The pillar he has staked his name on is not a structural column. It is a stack of veneered rubble held together by its own weight.

> A = the master mason; B = the north pillar is structurally sound, built with proper stone and mortar to bear the transept vault (his personal guarantee, his name on the foundation stone); E = raking sunlight exposing a hollow mortar joint, and chiselling that confirms stone-dust fill instead of bonded stone — dressed facades concealing crumbling cores across multiple courses; B' = the pillar is unbonded stacked rubble with veneer faces and waste-packed joints — it cannot bear the vault load, and the stone supply has been fraudulent. ✓ all four roles, ✓ all three states.

**Di-Hi2-5.** Aboard HMS Resolute, latitude 71 degrees north, August 1853. Captain Hargreaves holds the Admiralty's chart of the Northwest Passage in both hands, the chart that has governed every decision of this expedition for fourteen months. The chart shows open water beyond the ice shelf at Lancaster Sound — a navigable channel, marked in the Admiralty's own hand, based on whaling reports compiled in London. Hargreaves has staked the expedition's provisions, its route, and the lives of sixty-three men on the existence of that channel. The ice has parted enough this morning to send the ship's boat forward with Lieutenant Doyle and four oarsmen. Doyle returns within the hour. He does not speak when he climbs aboard. He walks to the chart table, picks up a pencil, and draws a single line across the channel the Admiralty has marked as open water. Then he writes beside it: "Continuous land. Ridge extends north-south as far as glass can see. No passage." The channel on the chart is a landmass. The Admiralty's whaling reports described open water where there is a mountain ridge, and Hargreaves has sailed sixty-three men fourteen months into a dead end.

> A = Captain Hargreaves; B = a navigable open-water channel exists beyond the Lancaster Sound ice shelf (per the Admiralty chart, the basis of the expedition's route, provisions, and commitments); E = Lieutenant Doyle's direct reconnaissance report and map annotation: "Continuous land. Ridge extends north-south as far as glass can see. No passage"; B' = the charted channel does not exist — the Admiralty's whaling-derived chart shows open water where there is a continuous landmass, and the expedition has no viable route forward. ✓ all four roles, ✓ all three states.

## Di-Hi6-1

*[Constantinople, 1453 — present tense, during the final Ottoman siege]*

The Venetian bailo, Girolamo Minotto, holds command of the northern sea walls. For three weeks he has organised the city's defence on the certainty that Venetian relief galleys are coming — the Senate's dispatch from February promised seven armed galleys, their departure from Negroponte imminent. He has rationed his own men's sleep, staggered the watches, ordered the cannon conserved for the moment when the fleet arrives and the defenders can strike the Ottoman naval line from both sides. This morning a Genoese sailor, picked up by a patrol boat after swimming from the Golden Horn, is brought before Minotto shaking with cold. The man says he slipped over the side of a Genoese grain ship two days ago. He says there is no Venetian fleet on the horizon. No masts, no pennants, no relief squadron. The Genoese captain told his crew the Venetian galleys turned back from Chios. The sailor does not know why. Minotto has him repeat it. The man repeats it. Minotto has been calibrating every defensive decision — the cannon reserves, the rest schedules, the positioning of his best men — against the guarantee of an arriving fleet that is not coming.

> A = Girolamo Minotto (Venetian bailo); B = the seven Venetian relief galleys are en route from Negroponte and will arrive to support the defence; E = Genoese sailor's direct testimony that no Venetian fleet is visible on the horizon and that the galleys turned back at Chios; B′ = the Venetian relief fleet is not coming; the defence must be sustained without the naval reinforcement he has been planning around. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states

## Di-Hi6-2

"How many acres are recorded in the subsidy rolls for the parish of Wetherton?" the auditor asks. He is sitting across from the parish constable in the vestry of St. Benet's, Lincolnshire, the year being 1524, the occasion the first of Henry's subsidy commissions.

"Six hundred and forty, certified by the last Valor," the constable says. He has held the figure in his head since the commission arrived.

"And the enclosures since the Valor?"

"None declared. We have had no enclosures in Wetherton."

The auditor sets down the report that the commissioners from Leicestershire sent north last week. "This is a deposition from three Wetherton freeholders, witnessed before the Leicestershire justice. They name four pasture enclosures in the north field since the Valor, totalling some hundred and eighty acres converted from common tillage." He places the deposition on the table between them. "Six hundred and forty acres on the rolls. The ground, if these men are correct, is rather different."

The constable reads the names on the deposition. He knows all three men. He does not answer immediately.

> A = parish constable; B = Wetherton has 640 acres in tillage as certified by the Valor, with no enclosures since; E = deposition from three named freeholders describing four enclosures in the north field since the Valor, totalling approximately 180 acres converted from common tillage; B′ = the subsidy rolls do not reflect the actual current land use; significant enclosure has occurred that was not declared. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states

## Di-Hi6-3

I had been the chief surgeon of the third division's field hospital since Gravelotte, and I knew what chloroform could do. We had administered it to one hundred and twenty-three men by the time the Prussian advance reached Metz, and I had recorded every case — pulse, respiration, duration, outcome. My practice was clear: for abdominal wounds, no anaesthesia. The shock of the gas, I was certain, killed men with abdominal trauma faster than the pain of the knife. I had read Lister's cautions; I had watched two men die on the table at Vionville within minutes of administration; I was sure. On the fifth day of the siege, a colleague from the Bavarian field hospital came to observe my procedures. He sat through three amputations and then asked, quietly, whether I had administered chloroform to any of the abdominal cases. I told him my reasoning. He handed me his case register. He had administered chloroform to thirty-one abdominal cases since the campaign opened. Thirty-one. His mortality rate on those cases was lower than my unaesthetised rate by eleven percentage points. He had been keeping the figures since July.

> A = chief surgeon (I); B = chloroform administered to abdominal wound patients increases mortality — anaesthesia is contraindicated for abdominal trauma; E = Bavarian colleague's case register showing 31 chloroform-anaesthetised abdominal cases with an 11-percentage-point lower mortality rate than the narrator's unaesthetised cases; B′ = the evidence does not support the contraindication; chloroform may reduce mortality in abdominal cases rather than increase it. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states

## Di-Hi6-4

The factor for the East India Company's Surat factory, Mr. Thomas Coryat the younger, has spent the past six months conducting trade on the assumption that the Mughal farman authorising Company goods through the Surat custom house covers all categories of English manufactured cloth. He signed twelve forward contracts on that basis in October. In December, a dispute over a consignment of broadcloth brings him before the customs qazi. The qazi reads the farman aloud, slowly, in Persian, pausing after each clause while his translator renders it. Coryat follows the translation. The farman names indigo-dyed cloth, raw wool, and metal goods. It does not name broadcloth. The qazi reads the silence in the document as exclusion. Coryat's translator confirms, twice, that broadcloth is absent from the enumerated categories. The farman Coryat has been operating under for six months does not authorise the goods he has contracted to import.

> A = Thomas Coryat the younger (Company factor); B = the Mughal farman authorises all categories of English manufactured cloth through Surat customs, including broadcloth; E = public reading of the farman before the customs qazi, with translator confirming broadcloth is not among the enumerated authorised goods; B′ = the farman does not cover broadcloth; the twelve forward contracts he signed in October have no customs authorisation. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states

## Di-Hi6-5

The engineer Isambard Brunel stands at the base of the Box Tunnel's western portal on a November morning in 1840, waiting for the lining crew to emerge. He has approved the tunnel's chalk-stratum survey himself, based on the borings taken at four-hundred-foot intervals: consistent chalk, predictable water ingress, lining specification adequate to load. The foreman comes out ahead of the crew and hands Brunel a core sample from the section they opened this morning — eight hundred feet in from the portal. The chalk is gone. The sample is blue clay, dense and wet, nothing like the boring results from that section. The foreman says they have been cutting clay for forty feet. Brunel turns the core in his hands. The boring at that interval showed chalk. Blue clay at that depth, at that position, should not be there unless the stratum dips far more steeply than the survey indicated. If the clay continues, the lining specification for the next six hundred feet of tunnel is insufficient by a margin he cannot yet calculate.

> A = Isambard Brunel (engineer); B = the Box Tunnel's chalk stratum is consistent throughout, as shown by the approved boring survey, and the current lining specification is adequate; E = core sample from 800 feet in showing dense blue clay — inconsistent with the boring result from that interval, indicating a steep stratum dip not captured in the survey; B′ = the stratum is not consistent; the survey missed a significant geological feature, and the lining specification for the next section is likely inadequate. ✓ all roles, ✓ all states

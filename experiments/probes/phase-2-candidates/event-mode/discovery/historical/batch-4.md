# Discovery × Historical — Batch 4 (Tier A)

**Di-Hi4-1.** The abbot of Cluny examines the relic in the treasury vault on a cold morning in the winter of 1109. The arm bone of Saint Martial — the abbey's most venerated possession, the object that has drawn pilgrims from Aquitaine and beyond for sixty years, the relic upon which the abbey's papal privileges rest. He has never had reason to open the reliquary before; the silver casing has been sealed since his predecessor's predecessor commissioned it. But the bishop of Limoges has demanded authentication before confirming the new indulgence, and so the abbot has called the goldsmith to unsolder the lid. The goldsmith works the seam and lifts the casing away. Inside, nested in rotting silk, are three bones — not one. Two are animal: short, thick-walled, with the unmistakable curvature of sheep metacarpals. The third is human but far too small for an adult arm. It is a child's finger bone, dark with age, resting between the sheep bones like an afterthought. The relic that has sustained sixty years of pilgrimage and underwritten the abbey's authority is not the arm of a saint. It is livestock bone and a scrap of anonymous human remain, sealed in silver and never checked.

> A = the abbot of Cluny; B = the reliquary contains the arm bone of Saint Martial (the basis of the abbey's pilgrimage income, veneration, and papal privileges for sixty years); E = opening the reliquary and finding two sheep metacarpals and one child's finger bone, not a saint's arm bone; B′ = the relic is not the arm of Saint Martial — it is animal bone and an anonymous human fragment, and the abbey's claims of authenticity are unfounded. ✓ all four roles, ✓ all three states.

**Di-Hi4-2.** "How many men did the Grand Vizier pledge for the Morea campaign?" the Venetian bailo asks his dragoman in the embassy courtyard in Constantinople, summer of 1463.

"Thirty thousand. That is the figure in the Sultan's firman. Thirty thousand regulars, plus sipahi cavalry from the Rumelian provinces."

"And the fleet?"

"Eighty galleys. The harbormaster at the Golden Horn confirmed the count himself."

The bailo has written to the Senate three times on the strength of these numbers, advising that Venice cannot contest the Morea without a coalition — that the Ottoman force is overwhelming. His entire diplomatic strategy for the season rests on this assessment.

A Greek merchant, newly arrived from Thessaloniki, is waiting in the courtyard. The bailo motions him over. The merchant has just passed through the Ottoman staging camp at Adrianople. He describes what he saw: fewer than nine thousand men in the camp, most of them irregulars without standardised arms. The cavalry he counted numbered under two thousand. No siege train. The eighty galleys the harbormaster counted at the Golden Horn are there, but his cousin, a caulker in the naval yard, told him forty of them have not been seaworthy since last season and sit at anchor with no crews assigned.

The force the bailo has been warning the Senate about — the force that has shaped three months of Venetian strategy — is less than a third of its reported size, and half its navy cannot sail.

> A = the Venetian bailo; B = the Ottoman force for the Morea campaign is thirty thousand regulars plus sipahi cavalry and eighty operational galleys (the basis of his diplomatic dispatches advising Venice cannot contest without a coalition); E = a Greek merchant's firsthand account from the staging camp at Adrianople — fewer than nine thousand men, mostly irregulars, under two thousand cavalry, no siege train, and forty of eighty galleys unseaworthy with no crews; B′ = the Ottoman force is less than a third of reported strength and half its fleet is inoperable — the threat assessment driving Venetian strategy is vastly inflated. ✓ all four roles, ✓ all three states.

**Di-Hi4-3.** I had been the surveyor-general of the Virginia Company's Henricus settlement for two planting seasons, and I had drawn every map the investors in London relied upon. My maps showed the tributary we called Martin's Creek running northwest from the James River for approximately four miles before forking into two navigable branches — the western branch leading, according to the Powhatan guides I had interviewed through our interpreter, toward copper-bearing country. The company's entire prospectus for the 1614 charter extension rested on my survey: navigable water access to mineral wealth. On a Tuesday in late September I walked the creek myself for the first time, having previously mapped it from canoe soundings taken at the mouth and native testimony relayed through two layers of translation. Martin's Creek runs northwest for barely a mile before ending in a beaver-dam marsh. There is no fork. There are no branches. The copper-bearing country may exist somewhere beyond the marshland, but there is no navigable water route to it. The map I drew, the map the investors are reading in London at this moment, describes a waterway that does not exist past its first mile.

> A = I (surveyor-general of Henricus settlement); B = Martin's Creek is a four-mile navigable tributary forking into two branches, the western one leading to copper-bearing country (basis of the Virginia Company's 1614 charter prospectus); E = walking the creek in person and finding it ends in a beaver-dam marsh after barely one mile, with no fork and no navigable branches; B′ = the mapped waterway does not exist past its first mile — the navigable route to mineral wealth described in the prospectus is fictitious. ✓ all four roles, ✓ all three states.

**Di-Hi4-4.** The chief eunuch of the Topkapi harem holds the ledger of pearl disbursements open on his lap, cross-legged on the divan in his private chamber, an afternoon in the spring of 1578. He has managed the Sultan's gifts to the harem for eleven years, and in eleven years every strand of pearls entering the inner court has passed through his hands, logged in this ledger, allocated to the Sultan's favourites by his authority alone. He is the sole channel. That is not merely his role; it is the architecture of his power — no woman in the harem receives the Sultan's favour without his mediation. The new Venetian ambassador's wife has sent, through the Valide Sultan's attendant, a small lacquered box as a courtesy gift. The chief eunuch opens it expecting perfume or comfit. Inside are six loose pearls of Bahraini origin, flawless, larger than any in his ledger. Folded beneath them is a note in the Valide Sultan's own hand, thanking the ambassador's wife for "continuing the arrangement." The pearls have been arriving through a channel he did not build, did not authorise, and did not know existed. His monopoly on the Sultan's gift economy — the monopoly his entire position depends upon — has been bypassed.

> A = the chief eunuch of the Topkapi harem; B = he is the sole channel through which the Sultan's pearl gifts reach the harem — all disbursements pass through his ledger and his authority (the basis of his political power); E = the Valide Sultan's note thanking the Venetian ambassador's wife for "continuing the arrangement," accompanying Bahraini pearls that do not appear in his ledger — evidence of an independent gift channel he did not authorise; B′ = a parallel channel for pearls (and by extension, favour) exists outside his control, operated by the Valide Sultan herself — his monopoly over the harem gift economy has already been circumvented. ✓ all four roles, ✓ all three states.

**Di-Hi4-5.** REPORT OF LIEUTENANT ANSELM KRATZER, CORPS OF ENGINEERS, SUBMITTED TO THE FORTIFICATION COMMITTEE OF THE IMPERIAL WAR COUNCIL, VIENNA, 11 MARCH 1809.

I was dispatched to inspect the forward magazines at Aspern in advance of the spring campaign. The garrison commander's monthly returns have reported all five magazines at full capacity — 240 barrels of powder each, 1,200 barrels total — sufficient to supply three weeks of active defence as specified in the campaign plan His Excellency the Archduke has approved.

Upon arrival I found the first magazine sealed. The garrison quartermaster explained that the door had swollen shut in the winter damp and no one had entered since November. I ordered it forced. Inside were forty-one barrels. The remaining space was occupied by sacks of millet, stacked floor to ceiling, belonging to a local grain merchant who had, by the quartermaster's account, been renting the space since the autumn with the garrison commander's knowledge.

I inspected the second and third magazines and found similar arrangements — partial powder stocks supplemented by stored grain, hides, and in one case, a quantity of unfinished furniture. The fourth magazine held no powder at all.

The total powder stock across all five magazines is 214 barrels, not 1,200. The forward defensive capacity this committee has planned against does not exist as reported.

> A = Lieutenant Anselm Kratzer (Corps of Engineers); B = the five forward magazines at Aspern hold 1,200 barrels of powder at full capacity (the basis of the three-week active defence in the Archduke's approved campaign plan); E = physical inspection finding only 214 barrels total, with magazine space rented out for grain, hides, and furniture by the garrison commander; B′ = the magazines hold less than one-fifth of reported capacity — the planned forward defence cannot be sustained. ✓ all four roles, ✓ all three states.

## D-arc-Fa1-1

**5 beats, chronological, dialogue-driven**

**Scenario.** A guild of cartographers grants its master archivist sole custody of the Bounded Atlas, a collection of territorial surveys used to arbitrate border disputes between noble houses. The archivist secretly copies three contested survey plates and sells the copies to a border lord, giving him advance knowledge of how a pending arbitration will rule. A journeyman cartographer discovers the forgery marks when preparing the plates for the arbitration hearing.

> A = the guild master; B = the master archivist; P = custodial oath (sworn before the guild council: to preserve the Bounded Atlas without alteration, to permit no copy of any survey plate to leave the archive without council vote, and to grant no party advance knowledge of territorial data before an arbitration hearing); V = copying three contested survey plates and passing the copies to a border lord, giving him advance knowledge of a pending arbitration's evidentiary basis.

**E1 (commitment scene).** The guild hall's inner chamber, the morning after the old archivist's funeral. The guild master stood behind the council table. The master archivist -- newly appointed, still wearing his journeyman's ink-stained apron under his formal robes -- stood at the centre of the room.

"You know the oath," the guild master said.

"I helped write it," the archivist said.

"Then you know what it means. Say it anyway."

The archivist straightened. He spoke the custodial oath as the guild secretary recorded it: that he would preserve the Bounded Atlas without alteration of any plate, line, or annotation; that he would permit no copy of any survey plate to leave the archive without a council vote recorded in the guild ledger; and that he would grant no party -- noble, merchant, cleric, or crown -- advance knowledge of any territorial data before an arbitration hearing.

"The atlas is the guild's authority," the guild master said. "The moment anyone doubts it, we're just men who draw maps."

"I understand."

The guild master placed the archive key on the table. The archivist picked it up. The secretary noted the transfer in the ledger, and the council members filed out.

**E2 (trust-displayed scene).** The guild hall, five months later. The guild master had convened an emergency session. The Duke of Harren had sent a courier demanding advance access to the survey plates for the Tommen River border -- a dispute with his neighbour, Lord Ashwick, that had been simmering for two seasons and was now scheduled for formal arbitration.

The archivist attended the session. He stood when the guild master asked for his recommendation.

"The duke's courier cited precedent," the archivist said. "The Velden ruling, six years ago. But the Velden ruling allowed inspection, not access. The disputing party viewed the plates in the archive, under supervision, with a guild officer present. No copies left the building. No data left the room."

"The duke won't accept supervised inspection," the guild master said. "He wants the plates delivered to his estate for his surveyors to study."

"Then the duke can wait for the hearing like everyone else. The oath doesn't have exceptions for dukes."

The guild master looked at the council. No one dissented. The courier was sent back to the duke with the guild's refusal, drafted in the archivist's hand.

**E3 (violation scene).** The archive, a night in late autumn. The archivist worked alone. He had dismissed the junior clerk at the evening bell, as he often did when the atlas required maintenance -- re-inking faded lines, pressing warped plates, updating the concordance.

Tonight he was not maintaining the atlas. He had three survey plates laid flat on the copying table: the Tommen River boundary, the Ashwick forest tract, and the eastern meadow parcel. Together they constituted the evidentiary core of the pending arbitration between the Duke of Harren and Lord Ashwick.

He worked quickly. The copies were drawn on new vellum but aged with tea-wash and careful handling so they would not look fresh. He reproduced every line, every annotation, every surveyor's mark. The only difference was the registration dot -- a tiny ink point in the lower-left corner of every original plate, invisible unless you knew to look for it. The copies did not have it.

He rolled the copies, sealed them in a leather case, and placed the originals back in the atlas. The next morning, a runner he had hired from outside the guild carried the case to the estate of Lord Ashwick's rival -- not the Duke of Harren, who had made his demand publicly, but Lord Fenwick, a border lord whose lands adjoined the disputed territory and who stood to gain from any ruling that weakened Ashwick's claim. Fenwick had approached the archivist privately, through a wine merchant, two months earlier. No money had changed hands yet. The payment was a land grant, deferred until after the arbitration.

**E4 (discovery scene).** The archive, three days before the arbitration hearing. The journeyman cartographer, Edda, had been assigned to prepare the plates for transport to the hearing chamber. The guild's procedure required her to verify each plate against the concordance: check the registration marks, confirm the ink consistency, and note any deterioration since the last inspection.

She laid out the Tommen River plate and turned it to check the registration dot. It was there -- a clean, dark point in the lower-left corner. She moved to the Ashwick forest tract. The dot was there. She moved to the eastern meadow parcel.

She paused. The dot was there, but it was smudged -- not faded, which would suggest age, but smudged, as though the plate had been handled while the ink was drying. The original plates were decades old. Their registration dots had been dry for years. She held the plate to the lamp and looked closer. The smudge was circular, consistent with a thumb pressing on fresh ink.

She went back to the Tommen River plate. The registration dot was clean, but the ink colour was fractionally darker than the surrounding annotation -- a shade she would not have noticed if she had not been looking. She checked the Ashwick forest tract. The same subtle mismatch.

Someone had re-inked the registration dots. That meant someone had removed the plates from their frames, handled them flat -- the only position in which the dots were accessible -- and replaced them. The only reason to handle the dots was to verify them after copying, because the copying process required pressing the originals face-down onto the transfer surface, which could damage the registration mark.

She replaced the plates in the atlas, locked the archive, and went to find the guild master.

**E5 (confrontation scene).** The guild master's study, the following morning. The archivist arrived expecting the routine pre-hearing briefing. He found the guild master seated behind his desk with Edda standing by the window and the three plates laid out on the table, registration dots facing up.

"The registration dots on these three plates have been re-inked," the guild master said. "The only reason to re-ink a registration dot is to repair damage caused by a flat-press transfer. Someone copied these plates. You are the only person with unsupervised access to the atlas. I need to know who received the copies, and I need to know now."

The archivist looked at the plates. He looked at Edda. Then he looked at the guild master.

"You're asking me to account for ink smudges," he said. "I re-inked those dots during routine maintenance. The meadow parcel's dot was fading. I refreshed all three in the same session to keep the set consistent. No copies were made."

"Maintenance re-inking is logged in the concordance," Edda said. "I checked. There's no entry."

"I forgot to log it. It was a minor task."

The guild master opened the concordance and turned it to face the archivist. "You have logged every maintenance action on every plate for the past five months. Forty-seven entries. Not one omission. And now the three plates at the centre of the most contested arbitration in a decade are the ones you forgot?"

The archivist was silent.

"Name the recipient," the guild master said. "Name them, and the guild will handle this internally. The arbitration will be delayed, the plates will be re-verified by the full council, and your removal will be recorded as a resignation for personal reasons."

"And if I don't name them?"

"Then I convene the full council this afternoon, present Edda's findings, and refer the matter to the crown's arbitration court. You will lose the guild's protection. The crown's investigators will trace the copies. They will find the recipient without your help, and when they do, both of you will answer to the crown, not to me."

The archivist stood for a long moment. His hand went to the archive key at his belt. Then he unclipped it and set it on the desk.

"Fenwick," he said. "Lord Fenwick. The copies went to his estate eight days ago."

The guild master took the key. He told Edda to summon the council. He told the archivist to remain in the study until the council convened. The archivist sat down in the chair by the wall, and the guild master left to draft the notification to the arbitration court.

## D-arc-Fa1-2

**4 beats, chronological, present tense**

**Scenario.** A temple's high oracle selects a young acolyte to serve as her voice-bearer -- the sole intermediary permitted to carry the oracle's prophecies from the inner sanctum to the petitioners outside. The acolyte alters a prophecy before delivering it, changing a warning of drought to a promise of rain, because the petitioner is the acolyte's home village and the original prophecy would have triggered an evacuation that would destroy the harvest. The temple's keeper of records discovers the alteration when the written prophecy and the delivered prophecy do not match.

> A = the high oracle; B = the voice-bearer (acolyte); P = voice-bearer's oath (to carry every prophecy from the inner sanctum to the petitioner exactly as spoken, without alteration of word, emphasis, or meaning; to add nothing and omit nothing; to serve as vessel, not interpreter); V = altering the prophecy delivered to the petitioners of Cael village, changing a warning of drought and failed harvest to a promise of sufficient rain, omitting the oracle's instruction to evacuate stores to higher ground.

**E1 (commitment scene).** The inner sanctum of the temple at Darrenmoor. The high oracle sits on the stone seat behind the veil. The acolyte kneels on the other side, her forehead touching the cold floor. The keeper of records stands at the writing desk near the door, stylus in hand.

The high oracle speaks. She tells the acolyte that every word spoken in this chamber passes through her and through no one else. She is the channel between the sanctum and the world outside. She carries the words as they are given. She does not shape them. She does not soften them. She does not withhold what is hard to hear. If the oracle speaks of death, she carries death. If the oracle speaks of plenty, she carries plenty. She is not the author. She is the instrument.

The acolyte speaks the oath: that she will carry every prophecy exactly as spoken, without alteration of word, emphasis, or meaning. That she will add nothing and omit nothing. That she will serve as vessel, not interpreter. That she accepts this duty until the oracle releases her or death ends her service.

The high oracle places her hand on the acolyte's head through the veil. The keeper of records writes the oath into the temple's book, notes the date and the witnesses, and closes the entry.

**E2 (violation scene).** The inner sanctum, seven months later. The petitioners from Cael village wait in the outer hall. They have come for the seasonal reading -- the oracle's assessment of the coming months, which their village council uses to plan planting, storage, and livestock movements.

The high oracle speaks the prophecy from behind the veil. The acolyte listens. The oracle's words are precise: the rains will fail before the third moon. The western aquifer will not replenish. The harvest planted in the low fields will wither before it can be gathered. The village must move its grain stores to the highland caves before the second moon and slaughter the weaker livestock while the animals still have weight. If they do not, the village will lose both the harvest and the herd.

The acolyte hears it all. Cael is her village. Her mother tends the low fields. Her brother manages the grain stores. An evacuation to the highlands would mean abandoning the planting -- not just this season's harvest, but the soil preparation for next year. The village would survive, but it would be impoverished for two years.

She walks from the inner sanctum to the outer hall. The petitioners from Cael stand in a half-circle, waiting.

She speaks. She tells them the oracle foresees sufficient rain through the season. She tells them the western aquifer will hold. She tells them the low-field harvest is favoured and they should plant fully. She omits the warning. She omits the instruction to evacuate stores. She omits the slaughter guidance. She delivers a prophecy that sounds like the oracle's voice but contains none of the oracle's meaning.

The petitioners bow their thanks and leave. The acolyte returns to the inner sanctum and takes her place by the veil.

**E3 (discovery scene).** The keeper of records' chamber, three weeks later. The keeper is a methodical woman named Brenna who has served the temple for nineteen years. She performs the monthly reconciliation -- comparing her written record of each prophecy, taken down as the oracle speaks, against the delivery summaries that the voice-bearer files after each session with petitioners.

She opens the Cael village entry. The oracle's prophecy, as she recorded it in the sanctum, warns of drought, failed harvest, and instructs evacuation to the highlands. The delivery summary, filed by the acolyte, states: seasonal rains sufficient, aquifer stable, low-field planting favoured.

Brenna reads both entries twice. She checks the date stamps. She checks the session identifiers. There is no ambiguity. The prophecy and the delivery are not merely different in emphasis or phrasing. They are opposite in substance. One predicts disaster and instructs preparation. The other promises abundance and instructs expansion.

She closes the ledger. She removes the relevant pages -- the original prophecy record and the delivery summary -- and carries them to the high oracle's chamber.

**E4 (confrontation scene).** The inner sanctum, the following morning. The high oracle remains behind the veil. Brenna stands at the writing desk. The acolyte has been summoned and kneels in her customary position.

The high oracle speaks. She says the keeper of records has brought her two documents that cannot both be true. She reads the prophecy as she spoke it -- the drought, the failed harvest, the instruction to evacuate. Then she reads the delivery summary the acolyte filed -- the sufficient rain, the stable aquifer, the planting guidance.

"Speak," the oracle says. "Tell me which record is false."

The acolyte does not look up. "The delivery summary is what I told them. The prophecy record is what you said. I changed the words before I carried them out."

"Why?"

"Because Cael is my home. An evacuation order would have destroyed two years of planting. My family would have lost everything. I thought -- if the rains held, if the aquifer did not fail, the village would be better served by planting than by fleeing to the highlands on a prediction that might not come true."

The high oracle is silent for a long time. When she speaks, her voice is even.

"You swore to carry my words as I gave them. Not as you judged them. Not as you wished them. I did not ask you to believe the prophecy. I asked you to deliver it. Your oath was not to the truth of what I say. Your oath was to the accuracy of what you carry. You chose to break that oath because you loved the people it would have hurt."

"Yes."

"Name the words you changed and I will send a corrected prophecy to Cael by the temple's own messenger before the second moon. The village may still have time to move the stores."

"I've told you everything. The drought. The evacuation. The slaughter guidance. I changed all of it."

The high oracle instructs Brenna to prepare a corrected dispatch to Cael village immediately, carrying the original prophecy in full, under the temple's seal. She instructs Brenna to note in the temple's book that the previous delivery was altered by the voice-bearer and is void.

Then the oracle addresses the acolyte. "Remove your temple garment and leave it at the threshold. Your service is ended. You may not enter the inner sanctum again. I will name a new voice-bearer before the next moon."

The acolyte rises. She removes the white garment and folds it on the floor by the door. She walks out through the outer hall, past the petitioners' benches, and into the daylight. Brenna notes the dismissal in the book, seals the entry, and begins drafting the corrected dispatch to Cael.

## D-arc-Fa1-3

**6 beats, non-linear: E3 -> E1 -> E2 -> E4 -> E5 -> E6, embedded-document voice**

**Note on beat order.** This sequence is depicted non-chronologically. The chronological order is: E1 (commitment) -- E2 (trust-displayed) -- E3 (violation) -- E4 (first-signal) -- E5 (discovery) -- E6 (confrontation). The depicted order opens with the violation in progress, then loops back to the commitment and trust context, before resolving forward through signal, discovery, and confrontation.

**Scenario.** A clan's war-chief entrusts his second, a woman named Sera, with the clan's muster rolls and battle plans during a campaign against a neighbouring territory. Sera copies the muster rolls and sends them by courier to the enemy clan's war-chief, giving him the exact disposition of forces, supply lines, and the date of the planned assault. She acts not for personal gain but because her son is a hostage held by the enemy clan, and the enemy war-chief has promised his release in exchange for the intelligence. A captured courier is found carrying the copied rolls.

> A = the war-chief, Brennan; B = his second, Sera; P = the second's oath of command (sworn before the clan elders: to hold all battle plans, muster rolls, and campaign intelligence in absolute confidence; to share no detail of force disposition, supply, or timing with any party outside the command council; to accept that the penalty for violation is exile and forfeiture of clan name); V = copying the clan's muster rolls and sending them by courier to the enemy clan's war-chief, disclosing the exact disposition of forces, supply routes, and the planned assault date.

**E3 (violation scene).** The command tent, an hour before dawn. The camp was quiet except for the watch-fires and the low voices of the sentries on the northern perimeter.

Sera worked by a single tallow candle, her body blocking the light from the tent's entrance flap. The muster rolls were spread on the campaign table -- three sheets of heavy parchment, each one detailing a division's strength, supply allocation, and assigned position for the assault planned nine days hence. She had helped Brennan draft them. She knew every mark.

She copied quickly, using a light hand on thin hide so the ink would dry fast. She did not copy everything -- only the force numbers, the supply depot locations, and the assault timing. She left out the fallback positions and the reserve assignments, not from mercy but from haste. The courier was waiting at the tree line south of camp, and the sentries changed in less than an hour.

When she finished, she rolled the copies, sealed them with unmarked wax, and placed the originals back on the table in the order she had found them. She pinched the candle out and left the tent through the rear flap, walking the long way around the horse line to the tree line, where the courier -- a boy from a border village, paid in salt -- took the roll and disappeared south into the dark.

She returned to her own tent, lay down on her pallet, and waited for the dawn horn.

**E1 (commitment scene -- embedded document).** Nine months earlier. The clan elders' hall at Duncarrow, the evening before the campaign muster. The document is read into the war-chief's campaign record by the elder scribe:

> *Before the elders of Clan Brennan and the assembled war-council, I, Sera, daughter of Mael, swear the second's oath of command: that I will hold all battle plans, muster rolls, and campaign intelligence entrusted to me in absolute confidence; that I will share no detail of force disposition, supply allocation, route, timing, or tactical intention with any party outside the command council, whether by word, writing, signal, or any other conveyance; that I will guard the war-chief's plans as I guard my own life; and that I accept exile and forfeiture of clan name as the penalty for any violation of this oath. Witnessed by Elder Conn, Elder Maeve, and the war-chief Brennan, who accepted the oath and granted the second's ring.*

The scribe noted that Sera's voice was steady throughout. He noted that Brennan clasped her arm after the oath, the campaign gesture of confidence between a war-chief and his second. He noted the date and sealed the entry.

**E2 (trust-displayed scene -- embedded document).** A passage from the campaign record, entered by the elder scribe five months into the campaign:

> *At the council of the seventh month, the war-chief Brennan presented the campaign's progress to the assembled elders via messenger. He credited his second, Sera, with the success of the eastern flank manoeuvre at Cairn Moy, noting that she had designed the supply relay that allowed the eastern division to hold position for eleven days beyond the original plan. The elders recorded a commendation. Brennan stated before the council that he had shared the full campaign plan with Sera alone among his officers, and that her judgment had proved equal to the confidence he had placed in her.*

**E4 (first-signal scene).** The camp, a morning three days after the copied rolls were sent. Brennan's adjutant, a young soldier named Torr, was conducting the routine inspection of the southern perimeter. The southern approach was the quietest sector -- dense forest, poor footing, no line of sight for an attacking force. It was the direction from which no threat was expected, which was why only two sentries covered it.

Torr found tracks in the mud south of the tree line. A single set, leading away from camp, fresh enough that the morning rain had not erased them. The stride length suggested someone moving quickly. The tracks ended at a clearing where a second set of prints -- lighter, narrower, a different person -- met them and then both sets disappeared south.

Torr followed the tracks for a quarter mile. They did not return. He went back to camp and reported the finding to Brennan. Brennan asked who had been assigned to the southern perimeter the previous night. Torr checked the watch roster. The southern sentries had been posted at the northern end of their arc, as usual, covering the approach. The tracks were south of their position -- behind the camp, in the direction no one watched.

Brennan noted the tracks. He told Torr to double the southern perimeter watch and to report any further anomalies directly to him. He did not raise the matter with the command council. He did not mention it to Sera.

**E5 (discovery scene).** Six days later. A patrol from Brennan's eastern division intercepted a lone rider moving north through the forest, two miles from the enemy clan's forward camp. The rider was the courier -- the border boy paid in salt. He was carrying a return message, sealed but not coded, from the enemy war-chief. The patrol captain opened it.

The message was brief. It acknowledged receipt of the muster rolls. It confirmed the force numbers and supply positions matched the enemy's own reconnaissance. It named the assault date -- the exact date Brennan had set in the command council three weeks earlier. And it contained a single additional line: *The boy is alive and will be delivered to the river crossing at Mael's Ford on the night before the assault, as agreed.*

The patrol captain brought the message and the courier to Brennan's tent within the hour. Brennan read the message. He read it again. He looked at the line about Mael's Ford. He knew that Sera's son -- taken hostage in a raid the previous year -- had been held by the enemy clan since the campaign began. He knew that Mael was Sera's father's name. He knew that the ford named in the message was a half-day's ride from the camp, on a route Sera knew well.

He dismissed the patrol captain. He sat alone in the tent with the message for a long time. Then he sent for the elder scribe and dictated the discovery into the campaign record.

**E6 (confrontation scene).** The command tent, that evening. Brennan had summoned the full command council -- four officers and the elder scribe. Sera arrived last. She saw the captured courier standing under guard at the back of the tent and stopped in the entrance.

Brennan laid the intercepted message on the campaign table. "Read it," he said.

Sera read it. Her face did not change.

"I need the name of every person outside this camp who received intelligence from you," Brennan said. "I need to know every message you sent, every courier you used, and every detail you disclosed beyond what is in this letter."

"There was one message," Sera said. "The muster rolls. Force numbers, supply positions, and the assault date. I sent it six days ago by this courier. No other messages. No other couriers. No other intelligence."

"Why?"

"My son. They took him at Cairn Ardagh. He's been held for nine months. The enemy war-chief sent word through a border trader that he would release the boy if I provided the muster data before the assault. I provided it."

Brennan looked at her. "You had the full campaign plan. Every division, every supply line, every fallback position. You sent only the muster rolls?"

"I sent what he asked for. I did not send the fallback positions or the reserve assignments."

"That is not a defence. You swore the oath. You heard the penalty. Exile and forfeiture. The oath does not contain an exception for hostages."

"I know what I swore. I am not asking for an exception."

Brennan turned to the elder scribe. "Enter the following into the campaign record." He dictated: Sera, second of Clan Brennan, had admitted to copying the clan's muster rolls and conveying them to the enemy war-chief. The assault date was compromised. He was cancelling the planned assault and ordering a full redeployment. The matter of Sera's oath violation would be referred to the clan elders at Duncarrow for judgment upon the campaign's conclusion.

He turned back to Sera. "Give me the second's ring."

She pulled it from her hand and set it on the table beside the intercepted message.

"Your son," Brennan said. "The message says Mael's Ford, the night before the assault. The assault is cancelled. They'll know. The exchange may not happen."

"I know," she said.

Brennan looked at her for a long moment. Then he told the guards to escort her to the holding tent and to post a watch. The elder scribe sealed the record. The command council began redrawing the campaign plan.

## D-arc-Fa1-4

**5 beats, chronological, free indirect style**

**Scenario.** A mage-college's grandmaster assigns the keeper of wards to maintain the binding circles that contain a class of dangerous summoned entities in the college's lower vaults. The keeper, struggling with a wasting illness that weakens his ability to renew the wards, quietly allows a visiting scholar to perform the renewals in his place -- a scholar who is not bound by the college's oaths and who, unbeknownst to the keeper, has been weakening the bindings incrementally to study the entities' behaviour under reduced constraint. The college's warden of seals discovers the degradation during a routine inspection.

> A = the grandmaster; B = the keeper of wards; P = keeper's oath (to maintain all binding circles in the lower vaults personally, to permit no unsworn party to touch or modify any ward, and to report any degradation or inability to perform his duties immediately to the grandmaster); V = permitting an unsworn visiting scholar to perform ward renewals in the keeper's place without disclosure to the grandmaster, resulting in progressive degradation of the binding circles.

**E1 (commitment scene).** The grandmaster's study was smaller than the keeper had expected -- a narrow room at the top of the east tower, crowded with books and smelling of lamp oil and old paper. The grandmaster herself was smaller than the keeper had expected, too. A grey woman in grey robes, with hands that looked like they had not cast a working in years. But her eyes were steady, and the keeper had been told by three separate masters that those eyes missed nothing.

She explained the keeper's duties without preamble. The lower vaults held nine binding circles, each containing a summoned entity of the second or third order. The circles required renewal every fourteen days -- a precise working that demanded both skill and consistency. The keeper would perform every renewal personally. He would permit no other mage, whether faculty, student, or visitor, to touch or modify any ward in the vaults. If at any time he was unable to perform his duties -- through illness, injury, exhaustion, or any other cause -- he was to report his incapacity to the grandmaster immediately, and she would assign a temporary replacement from the college's senior faculty.

The keeper spoke the oath. The grandmaster accepted it, noted the date in the college's register, and handed him the vault key. He felt its weight in his palm and thought it was heavier than it looked. He would later learn that all keys in the college felt that way.

**E2 (trust-displayed scene).** The grandmaster attended the keeper's third renewal cycle, eight weeks into his appointment. She stood at the entrance to the lower vaults and watched him work. She did not speak. She did not interfere.

The keeper moved through the nine circles methodically. Each renewal took between twenty and thirty minutes -- a careful layering of energy along the circle's inscribed channels, reinforcing the geometric constraints that held the entity in place. The entities watched him. Some were still. Some pressed against their boundaries, testing. One -- the entity in circle seven, a thing of shadow and angles that the keeper privately thought was more intelligent than most of the faculty -- tracked his movements with something that looked uncomfortably like recognition.

He finished the ninth circle in four hours and twelve minutes. The grandmaster checked each binding with a diagnostic working of her own. All nine were sound.

She told him, as they climbed the stairs back to the college's upper floors, that his predecessor had taken six hours for the same cycle and had needed rest between the fifth and sixth circles. The keeper said he preferred to work straight through. The grandmaster said she preferred that too.

The keeper thought she almost smiled. He was not sure. The grandmaster's face did not lend itself to certainty.

**E3 (first-signal scene).** Ten months into his appointment. The keeper was in the refectory, eating alone at the long table near the kitchen door. He ate slowly and did not finish his plate, which was unusual -- the keeper was a large man who had always eaten well.

The warden of seals, a meticulous woman named Corren, sat across from him. She had known the keeper since he arrived. They were not friends, exactly -- the warden was not a woman who had friends -- but they shared the particular bond of people who performed repetitive, precise, unglamorous work in a college that rewarded brilliance over consistency.

She noticed that his hands trembled when he lifted his cup. It was slight -- a fine tremor, visible only because the cup was full and the surface of the water shivered. She noticed that his face was thinner than it had been a month ago, and that his colour was poor.

She did not ask. The keeper was a private man, and Corren was not the kind of person who asked questions she could answer by observation. She noted the tremor. She noted the weight loss. She thought about the nine binding circles in the lower vaults and the precise, sustained energy output each renewal required.

She finished her meal, returned her plate to the kitchen, and went back to her work. She did not mention the observation to the grandmaster. She did not mention it to anyone. But she added a note to her inspection schedule: move the next vault inspection forward by two weeks.

**E4 (discovery scene).** The lower vaults, a Thursday morning. Corren descended the stairs with her inspection kit -- a case of diagnostic instruments, a set of reference plates engraved with the college's standard binding geometries, and a ledger in which she recorded the condition of every ward and seal in the college.

She began with circle one. The binding was sound. Circle two, sound. Circle three -- she paused. The diagnostic reading was within tolerance, but the geometry had shifted. The inscribed channels were intact, but the energy distribution was uneven: strong on the eastern arc, weaker on the western. The pattern was consistent with a renewal performed by a different hand -- someone who layered energy in a different sequence than the keeper used.

She moved to circle four. The same asymmetry. Circle five, the same. By circle six she was certain. The renewals had been performed recently -- within the last fourteen-day cycle -- but not by the keeper. The technique was competent but unfamiliar. Whoever had done this work followed a different renewal sequence, applied energy in a different order, and had a subtly different signature. And the bindings were weaker. Not failed. Not critical. But degraded. Each circle showed a reduction of perhaps four or five percent from the college's standard. Individually, within tolerance. Cumulatively, across nine circles and multiple renewal cycles, a compounding problem.

Corren completed her inspection, recorded every measurement in her ledger, and climbed the stairs. She went to the grandmaster's study and knocked.

**E5 (confrontation scene).** The grandmaster's study, the following morning. The keeper stood before the desk. The grandmaster sat behind it. Corren stood at the window with her ledger open.

The grandmaster placed Corren's inspection report on the desk. Nine binding circles, all showing the same signature anomaly. All degraded by increments too consistent to be natural attenuation.

"Someone other than you has been performing the renewals," the grandmaster said. "I need to know who, and I need to know how many cycles they have performed."

The keeper looked at the report. He did not deny it.

"Dr. Tallis," he said. "The visiting scholar from Aeren. He has been performing the renewals for the last three cycles. Six weeks."

"Tallis is not bound by the college's oaths. He is not sworn. He was not authorised. Why did you allow this?"

The keeper held up his hands. The tremor was visible now without a cup to reveal it. The fine shaking ran through his fingers and into his wrists.

"My hands," he said. "The wasting started four months ago. The healers cannot name it. The tremor makes the renewal working imprecise -- I can sustain the energy, but I cannot direct it cleanly. I damaged circle seven during a renewal two months ago and repaired it before the next inspection. After that I asked Tallis for help. He is the only visiting scholar with the skill to perform the working."

"You were required to report any inability to perform your duties. Immediately. To me."

"I know. I should have. I did not because I believed Tallis could perform the work adequately, and because reporting my incapacity would have ended my appointment."

The grandmaster turned to Corren. "What is the cumulative degradation?"

Corren consulted her ledger. "Four to five percent per cycle, compounding. After three cycles, the weakest circle -- circle seven -- is at approximately eighty-six percent of standard. Serviceable, but below the margin the college requires for second-order containment."

The grandmaster looked at the keeper. "Tallis's renewals are not merely imprecise. They are systematically weaker in the same quadrant of every circle. Corren's measurements suggest the degradation is not accidental. I will be speaking to Tallis separately. But you permitted an unsworn mage to modify nine containment wards without my knowledge. That is what I am addressing now."

"I was trying to keep the vaults safe."

"You were trying to keep your appointment. Those are not the same thing." The grandmaster stood. "I am suspending your access to the lower vaults and relieving you of the keeper's duties, effective now. A senior faculty member will perform an emergency renewal of all nine circles today. You will surrender the vault key to Corren before you leave this room."

The keeper removed the key from the chain at his belt. The weight of it, as always, felt greater than it should have. He placed it on the grandmaster's desk.

"I am sorry," he said.

"I believe you," the grandmaster said. "That does not change what happened. You may remain at the college as a faculty guest while the healers attend to your condition, but you will not enter the lower vaults again. Corren, please escort him."

Corren closed her ledger, took the key, and walked the keeper to the door. Behind them, the grandmaster was already drafting the summons for Dr. Tallis.

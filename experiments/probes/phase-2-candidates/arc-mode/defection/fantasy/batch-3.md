## D-arc-Fa3-01

**Scenario.** A master herbalist of the Healers' Guild swears a charter oath to supply only approved remedies to patients under Guild license, reporting any experimental preparations to the Guild's remedy council before administration. Over the course of a winter, she administers an untested preparation derived from a prohibited root to eleven patients without filing a council declaration. The Guild's inspection warden discovers the omission during a routine stock audit.

> A = the Healers' Guild (represented by the inspection warden); B = master herbalist Seravane; P = Guild charter oath (sworn at induction: to administer only preparations listed in the approved Guild formulary, to submit all experimental or unlisted preparations to the remedy council for review before use, and to maintain a complete stock ledger recording every preparation drawn and every patient treated); V = administering an untested preparation derived from the prohibited root ashlock to eleven patients over three months without filing a council declaration and without recording the administrations in the stock ledger.

5 beats, chronological

**E1 (commitment scene).** The Guild hall in the lower city, the day of Seravane's induction as a master herbalist. The warden of inductions read the charter oath from the guild rolls: that she would administer only preparations named in the approved formulary; that she would submit any experimental or unlisted preparation to the remedy council for review and written approval before a single dose reached a patient; that she would record every preparation drawn and every patient treated in her licensed stock ledger, open to Guild inspection at any time.

Seravane laid her right hand on the guild seal stone and spoke the oath as it was read. The warden asked whether she understood the prohibition on ashlock derivatives, which had been added to the restricted-root list in the previous term after three adverse outcomes at a southern outpost. She said she understood. The warden entered the induction in the rolls, applied the guild seal beside her name, and handed her the master's keys.

She walked out into the afternoon with the keys in her fist and her shop two streets west, already stocked and ready.

**E2 (trust-displayed scene).** Four months after her induction, the fever that moved through the weavers' quarter in late autumn brought her forty patients in two weeks. She worked through the Guild's approved protocols without deviation, reported every case to the Guild's physician liaison as required, and flagged two patients whose presentation suggested a variant that the standard treatment wasn't clearing. She asked the remedy council for an emergency consultation. They gave her one. The council recommended a modified dosage of an approved preparation, and she administered it under their oversight.

The inspection warden noted the exchange in the outpost ledger and wrote a brief commendation to the Guild's senior council: the newest master in the lower city was running a clean shop.

**E3 (violation scene).** Midwinter, the worst of the cold months. Five patients presented in a single week with a joint inflammation that none of the approved preparations touched. She knew the ashlock root preparation -- her teacher had used it decades before its restriction, had described the results, had given her the proportions. The restrictions existed because of poor preparation technique at the southern outpost, not because the root itself was dangerous.

She prepared a small supply in her back room, grinding and measuring the way her teacher had shown her. She administered the first dose to the most severe case on a Wednesday morning and watched the inflammation begin to recede by Thursday.

She treated the remaining four patients that week and six more over the following two months. She recorded nothing in the stock ledger. She did not file a council declaration. She told herself she would file a retrospective report in the spring when she had enough outcome data to make the case for lifting the restriction.

**E4 (first-signal scene).** The inspection warden arrived for the quarterly stock audit on a morning in late winter, ledger and scale weights in his satchel. Seravane received him without alarm and opened the shop for review.

The warden worked through the shelves systematically: weights against ledger entries, preparation dates, patient logs. On the third shelf he found a clay jar marked in her hand with a symbol he did not recognise. He uncorked it and identified the smell: ashlock root, processed.

He set the jar on the worktable and said nothing. He continued the audit. When he reached the stock ledger's entries for the winter months, he counted the approved preparations drawn against the patient log. The counts matched for every listed preparation. But the patient log showed eleven patients treated for joint inflammation in the period, and the approved formulary contained no preparation effective against that presentation in cold conditions. He noted the discrepancy and completed the audit.

Before he left, he told her he would be returning within ten days with a warden's inquiry notice.

**E5 (confrontation scene).** Ten days later. The Guild hall, the inquiry chamber on the upper floor. The inspection warden sat across from Seravane with the clay jar, the stock ledger, and a list of the eleven patients on the table between them.

"The Guild asks whether you administered an ashlock preparation to any of the patients named on this list," he said.

She said she had. She said the approved formulary contained no effective treatment for the presentation she had seen, that she had used a preparation technique distinct from the one that had caused the southern outpost incidents, and that all eleven patients had recovered without adverse outcome.

The warden said the charter oath required a council declaration before administration. He asked why she had not filed one.

She said the council moved slowly and her patients could not wait. She said a retrospective filing with her outcome data would give the council better evidence than any prospective declaration. She said the restriction on ashlock was based on a single practitioner's error, not on the root's properties, and the restriction should have been lifted two terms ago.

"The charter oath does not provide an exception for urgency or for your view of whether a restriction is well-founded," the warden said. "I'm recommending your case to the remedy council for a formal hearing. Pending that hearing, your master's license is suspended and your shop will be sealed. The council will review the outcomes you've documented and the preparation technique you used, and it will decide whether those factors mitigate the violation. That is not a decision I can make at the inquiry level."

Seravane said that sealing the shop would leave the lower city without a licensed herbalist for the rest of winter. The warden said he was aware of that. He said the Guild would send a journeyman to cover the district while the hearing was pending. He handed her the suspension notice and asked for her master's keys.

She took the notice. She held the keys for a moment, then set them on the table.

"The council should look at the outcomes before it decides anything," she said.

"It will," he said. He pocketed the keys, rose, and left her in the inquiry chamber.

## D-arc-Fa3-02

**Scenario.** A cartographer sworn to the Navigators' Brotherhood is commissioned to chart a disputed sea passage for the Brotherhood's exclusive archive, with the commission agreement forbidding him to share his findings with any third party before the archive copy is sealed and registered. Offered land grants and a title by a coastal lord who wants the passage for private shipping, the cartographer delivers his working charts to the lord's factor before the Brotherhood's archive copy is complete. The Brotherhood's record-keeper discovers the delivery when the lord's ships begin using the passage.

> A = the Navigators' Brotherhood (represented by the record-keeper, Master Aldric); B = cartographer Fenn; P = commission agreement (Fenn will chart the passage solely for the Brotherhood's archive; he will not share, copy, or convey his working charts, soundings, or navigational notes to any third party before the archive copy is sealed and registered; the Brotherhood retains sole right to the passage record for a period of ten years from registration); V = delivering working charts, soundings, and navigational notes for the disputed passage to Lord Cassel's factor before completing or registering the archive copy, in exchange for land grants and a title.

4 beats, non-linear: E3 → E1 → E2 → E4

**Note on beat order.** Opens with the discovery, then shows the commission, the trust-display, and the confrontation.

**E3 (discovery scene).** Master Aldric, the Brotherhood's record-keeper, was standing at the window of his archive tower watching the harbour when he counted three ships taking the channel approach that had no charted navigational line. The passage had been considered unnavigable for a decade -- too shallow at the eastern entry, too narrow at the second bend. He had commissioned Fenn specifically because Fenn had worked the coastline and believed the conventional soundings were wrong.

The ships were flying Lord Cassel's colours. They were not guessing at the channel. They were reading it.

Aldric pulled Fenn's commission file from the archive shelf. The archive copy was not there. It had not been sealed and registered. He had sent two inquiry letters to Fenn's lodgings over the past month, both unanswered.

He sent a summons to Fenn's lodgings by messenger and wrote a second letter to Lord Cassel's factor asking by what means the lord's ships had come to possess navigational guidance for the disputed passage. He did not wait for either reply before beginning his record of what he had observed.

**E1 (commission scene).** Fourteen months earlier. Aldric had met Fenn in the Brotherhood's commission room and laid out the terms: the passage was disputed between three coastal interests, and whoever held the definitive navigational record would have standing to broker transit rights. The Brotherhood's archive rights were the commercial foundation of the commission. The agreement was specific on this point: no copy, no partial chart, no set of soundings or bearing marks was to leave Fenn's possession until the archive copy was sealed and registered; the agreement covered working materials, not just the final chart.

Fenn had read the agreement through twice and asked whether the restriction applied to notes he made in the field. Aldric had said it did -- the restriction applied to any materials that would allow a third party to navigate the passage. Fenn had said that was a wider net than he was used to. Aldric had said it was a wider commission than most. The fee was proportionate.

Fenn had signed. Aldric had countersigned and affixed the Brotherhood's seal. The commission copy went into the archive file.

**E2 (trust-displayed scene).** Six months after the commission was signed, Fenn had submitted his preliminary sounding report to the archive -- forty pages of bearing observations, tide readings, and depth profiles for the passage's eastern entry. The work was meticulous. Aldric had read it himself and sent a note to the Brotherhood's senior navigation council saying the commission was proceeding well and the preliminary soundings supported Fenn's original assessment that the conventional charts were in error.

The council had authorised a second payment instalment. Aldric had delivered it to Fenn's lodgings in person and had seen, in the front room of the lodgings, the scale model Fenn had built of the passage's second bend -- carved wood and thread representing the channel markers, the kind of three-dimensional working model only a serious cartographer would construct. He had told Fenn it was excellent work.

**E4 (confrontation scene).** Fenn arrived at the archive tower three days after the summons. He had Lord Cassel's letter of title in his coat. He did not show it immediately.

"You've seen the ships," Fenn said. He did not frame it as a question.

"The Brotherhood wants its archive copy and a full account of what you provided to Lord Cassel and when," Aldric said. He set the commission agreement on the table between them.

Fenn put the letter of title on the table beside the agreement. A holding of forty acres and the rank of landed factor, granted by Lord Cassel and registered with the coastal courts. He said the Brotherhood had taken fourteen months on a commission that should have run eight. He said Lord Cassel had approached him at the midpoint and offered terms that the Brotherhood's fee could not match. He said he had delivered his working charts and soundings to the lord's factor six weeks ago.

"The commission agreement does not provide a term limit," Aldric said. "It provides a completion condition: archive copy sealed and registered. That has not occurred. You have conveyed materials prohibited under the agreement before that condition was met."

Fenn said the Brotherhood would have the passage charted eventually and the archive copy would still have value. He said three additional months was not a material delay and the lord's ships would not damage the Brotherhood's standing.

"The Brotherhood's standing is built on exclusive archive rights," Aldric said. "You have given those rights to a private lord. I am asking you to surrender the letter of title and all copies of the materials you delivered to Lord Cassel's factor. I will present your surrender to the Brotherhood's council as evidence of good faith. If you refuse, the council will pursue the matter in the maritime courts, where the commission agreement is a registered instrument."

Fenn said he would not surrender the title. He had earned it and it was registered. He said the Brotherhood could have the archive copy as soon as he completed it. He said the courts would find the commission's terms unenforceable in any case, because no commercial commission could prevent a cartographer from applying his own professional knowledge.

Aldric had expected this. He closed the commission file and rose from his chair. "The Brotherhood will file in the maritime courts within the week. The commission agreement specifies that all navigational materials produced under its terms are Brotherhood property until the archive copy is registered. That includes the charts you delivered to Lord Cassel. The lord will be named as a receiving party. I would suggest you speak with a maritime advocate before the filing."

He called for the archive tower's porter to escort Fenn out. Fenn left with the title letter and without the archive copy. Aldric sealed the commission file, added his account of the meeting, and sent the Brotherhood's senior council a message asking for a full session before the week's end.

## D-arc-Fa3-03

**Scenario.** A temple warden at the Oracle's Seat is bound by initiation vow to keep all petitioners' questions and the oracle's answers in strict confidence, sharing the records only with the High Intercessor. Over the course of a year, he copies and sells petitioners' private questions -- including the plans of merchants and nobles who seek the oracle's counsel before major decisions -- to a trading consortium that uses the advance knowledge for commercial advantage. A temple novice discovers the copies when she inventories the archive.

> A = the High Intercessor of the Oracle's Seat; B = temple warden Osric; P = warden's initiation vow (to hold all petitioners' questions and the oracle's responses in absolute confidence, to maintain the petition archive accessible only to the High Intercessor, and to destroy all petition copies after the sanctioned retention period without making any reproduction); V = reproducing petition records and selling them to the Merchant Consortium of the Three Ports over the course of one year, enabling the consortium to act on petitioners' private commercial and political plans before those plans were executed.

6 beats, chronological

**E1 (commitment scene).** The inner sanctum of the Oracle's Seat, the night of Osric's initiation as temple warden. The ceremony was conducted by the High Intercessor herself, with two senior wardens as witnesses. The warden's vow was read by candlelight and was the same vow every warden spoke: to hold all petitioners' questions in confidence absolute; to maintain the petition archive accessible only to the High Intercessor; to make no reproduction of any petition record, for any purpose, by any means; to destroy copies at the close of each sanctioned retention cycle.

Osric spoke the vow. He was given the archive keys and the warden's seal. The High Intercessor told him that the temple's authority rested on the petitioners' certainty that their counsel was private. Rulers asked the oracle things they would never speak aloud in their own courts. Merchants laid open their full strategies. What was spoken before the oracle stayed at the oracle's feet or the temple fell.

He carried the keys out of the sanctum with the weight of that still on him.

**E2 (trust-displayed scene).** Three months into his tenure, a petitioner left the archive room without retrieving a document he had brought with him -- a sealed letter addressed to a distant lord, apparently forgotten in his haste. Osric noticed it before the archive room was closed for the evening. He did not open the letter. He brought it to the High Intercessor and described how he had found it. She had it returned to the petitioner through an anonymous courier, and the petitioner sent a grateful offering to the temple the following week.

The High Intercessor mentioned the episode to the senior wardens as an example of proper conduct. She said it showed what she called archival discipline -- the warden sees what is not his and does not touch it.

**E3 (first-signal scene).** Six months after his initiation, a consortium factor approached Osric in the lower market and offered him a private meeting. They met in a wine house three streets from the temple. The factor said the consortium had an interest in the questions certain petitioners were bringing to the oracle -- not the oracle's answers, just the questions themselves, which would tell them what their rivals were planning before those plans were acted upon.

Osric said that was forbidden. The factor said the consortium was prepared to offer a sum equal to five years of a warden's stipend for the first year of records, with a standing arrangement after that. He said the petitioners would never know and the oracle's answers would remain private. He said the questions were just questions. Osric left without agreeing.

He did not report the approach to the High Intercessor. He told himself there was nothing to report. It had been a conversation, not an act. He thought about the sum for the better part of a week.

**E4 (violation scene).** The archive room, a morning ten days after the wine house meeting. Osric removed a petition record from the prior week -- a merchant's question about the profitability of a shipping venture to the northern ports, complete with the costs and partners named in the petition -- and copied it onto a separate leaf by lamplight. He resealed the original and returned it to the archive shelf.

He delivered the copy to the factor's agent at a pre-arranged location that afternoon. He received payment by coin, counted and split before he left. He replaced the coin in his lodgings, spent gradually, and told no one.

Over the following year he copied forty-three petition records and delivered them to the consortium's agents. Fourteen of the petitioners were merchants. Eleven were nobles with questions about inheritance, land acquisition, and political alliance. The consortium used the records to position their trading interests ahead of every major decision the petitioners subsequently executed.

**E5 (first-signal scene — internal).** A junior novice assigned to begin the annual archive inventory found a discrepancy when she tallied the retention cycle logs against the physical petition count. Seventeen records from the prior cycle showed as destroyed in the log, but the destruction seal verification slips -- which required a witnessing warden's signature -- were blank. In a properly maintained archive, the destruction log and the verification slips matched. Here, they did not.

She noted the discrepancy in her inventory report and brought it to the senior warden on duty. He reviewed her count, confirmed the mismatch, and sent the report up the chain to the High Intercessor's office that afternoon.

**E6 (discovery and confrontation scene).** The High Intercessor reviewed the inventory report that evening and called for a full archive audit the following morning. By midday, the auditing wardens had identified the pattern: forty-three petition records across twelve months where the destruction verification showed no witness signature. All forty-three were in Osric's custody period.

The High Intercessor summoned Osric to the inner sanctum. The audit findings were laid on the altar table: the list of records, the blank verification slips, a record of the consortium's market movements cross-referenced by date against the petition records -- the correlation visible even to a non-merchant eye.

"The temple asks whether you copied these records," the High Intercessor said.

Osric looked at the correlation table. He said he had.

"The temple requires you to name the parties to whom you delivered the copies and the full extent of what was transferred," she said. "All names. All dates. All transactions."

He named the consortium's factor and described the arrangement. He said he had kept no ledger of his own; the forty-three records were his best count. He said he had not understood the scale of the damage until he saw the correlation table laid out.

The High Intercessor said she would ask only once whether there was anything else she should know. He said there was not.

"You have spoken your vow in this sanctum and you have broken it for money," she said. "I will not pursue your reasons. The temple's obligation is to the petitioners you compromised. We will notify each of them. That notification will destroy some of what they built in confidence. The temple will bear that consequence." She stood. "You will be remanded to the civil authorities for proceedings under the city's privacy statutes. The temple will provide the full audit record to the authorities. Your warden's keys and seal are forfeit immediately. Do you contest any of that?"

He said he did not. He laid the archive keys and the warden's seal on the altar table. Two senior wardens were waiting by the sanctum door. He did not resist when they led him out.

## D-arc-Fa3-04

**Scenario.** A senior envoy of the Mages' Conclave is sent to negotiate a resource-sharing treaty with a border clan, bound by Conclave charter to conduct all negotiations transparently and to return with any proposed terms for Conclave ratification before agreeing to anything. During the negotiations, she verbally commits the Conclave to a military assistance clause the Conclave had explicitly instructed her not to offer. The Conclave's observer, a junior archivist accompanying the delegation, reports the unauthorized commitment upon return.

> A = the Mages' Conclave (represented by the Conclave Speaker); B = senior envoy Mira; P = Conclave mandate (the envoy will negotiate only within the terms authorised by the Conclave's preparatory session; all proposed treaty clauses outside the authorised scope must be returned to the Conclave for ratification before any commitment is made; the envoy has no authority to bind the Conclave to military, personnel, or material commitments without explicit Conclave approval); V = verbally committing the Conclave to a military assistance clause — providing mages for border defence during a period of two summers — without Conclave ratification, after the Conclave had explicitly declined to authorise that clause during pre-departure briefing.

5 beats, non-linear: E2 → E4 → E1 → E3 → E5

**Note on beat order.** Opens with the commitment ceremony, then shows the violation, then cuts back to the mandate briefing, then moves to the observer's report and confrontation.

**E2 (commitment scene — depicted first).** The Conclave hall, three weeks before departure. The Speaker read the authorised negotiating scope from the preparatory resolution: resource-sharing terms, seasonal passage rights, and cultural exchange arrangements. The Conclave had explicitly voted down a motion to include military assistance provisions -- the motion had failed by two votes, with Mira abstaining.

The Speaker handed Mira the mandate scroll with the scope written out in full. She asked whether Mira understood the boundary on military commitments. Mira said she did. The Speaker told her that the clan had raised the military clause in two prior rounds of correspondence and would likely raise it again. If they did, her answer was that the clause required separate Conclave ratification and could not be committed to in the field.

Mira received the mandate scroll, affixed her seal beside the Speaker's, and departed with the delegation the following morning.

**E4 (violation scene — depicted second).** The clan chieftain's longhouse, the fifth day of negotiations. The resource-sharing terms had been agreed in principle. The passage rights were nearly closed. Then the chieftain set aside the draft text and said that without a commitment of mage support during the border-threat seasons, the clan's council would not ratify any treaty however generous its other terms. He said the clan had faced two summers of incursion pressure and could not afford to trade their sovereignty for grain guarantees.

Mira sat with the silence for a moment. She thought about the mandate scroll in her pack. She thought about returning to the Conclave with no treaty after three weeks of travel and five days of negotiation, and asking for a new ratification session on a clause the Conclave had already voted down.

She told the chieftain that the Conclave would provide mage support for border defence during the two vulnerable seasons, subject to formal treaty ratification upon her return. She said the commitment was personal and would be ratified within thirty days.

The chieftain accepted. The draft was amended. Both parties sealed the amended text that evening.

The junior archivist, seated at the side of the room with his note-ledger, recorded the exchange verbatim.

**E1 (mandate scene — depicted third, retroactive).** The preparatory session notes that the archivist had copied before departure confirmed the Conclave's position: the military assistance motion had been discussed for two hours and voted down. The record showed the specific clause that had been rejected: mage support for border defence, seasonal, for any fixed period. The Speaker's instruction to Mira was entered in the record in full.

The archivist had been assigned to the delegation as an archival observer, not as a negotiation participant. His function was to record, not to intervene. He had recorded. He carried the preparatory session notes alongside his own negotiation transcript for the journey home.

**E3 (discovery scene — observer's report).** The Conclave hall, the evening of the delegation's return. The archivist presented his negotiation transcript to the Speaker's office before the formal debriefing. He did not annotate it. He handed it over and noted that the final session included a commitment he believed was outside the authorised scope. He said he had recorded it verbatim and the Speaker could assess it against the mandate scroll.

The Speaker read the relevant section of the transcript that night. She set it beside the preparatory session record. The commitment was clear and the mandate prohibition was clear. She sent for Mira before the morning debriefing.

**E5 (confrontation scene).** The Speaker's office, before the hall had filled for the morning session. She laid the transcript and the mandate scroll on the desk side by side.

"The transcript records you committing the Conclave to a seasonal military assistance clause," the Speaker said. "The mandate scroll shows that clause was outside your authorised scope. I'm asking you to account for the difference."

Mira said the treaty would have failed without the clause. She said a failed negotiation was a worse outcome than a clause requiring ratification. She said she had committed on the understanding that the Conclave would ratify upon return, given the strategic value of the treaty.

"The mandate does not give you authority to commit in advance of ratification," the Speaker said. "The Conclave voted on this clause. It failed. You do not have standing to reverse a Conclave vote in the field."

Mira said the vote had been close. She said two members had since told her they regretted their position. She said the Conclave would ratify.

"Then ask it to ratify," the Speaker said. "You will present the treaty to the full Conclave today and you will explain why you offered a commitment outside your mandate. The Conclave will decide whether to ratify the clause or to reopen negotiation with the clan. If the clause is ratified, the outcome is salvageable. If it is not, you have committed the Conclave to terms it will not honour, and the clan will consider the commitment made and broken." She straightened the transcript on the desk. "I will recommend to the Conclave that it ratify, because the treaty's other terms have value and a failed commitment is worse than an awkward ratification. But I am also recommending that your mandate on future negotiations be restructured to require a Conclave observer with authority to intervene. Not simply to record."

Mira said that would constrain every future negotiation. The Speaker said it would constrain envoys who overstepped their mandates. Mira had nothing further to say to that. The full session was called for the second morning bell.

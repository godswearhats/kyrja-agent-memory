# Confrontation arc × Fantasy — Batch 2 (Tier B)

## C-arc-Fa2-1

**5 beats, chronological, dialogue-driven**

**Scenario.** The guildmaster of the Weavers' Guild demands that the city's archon return the guild's ancestral loom, which was seized as collateral for an unpaid civic tax. The archon refuses, citing the city charter. The dispute escalates through a failed mediation by the temple before the guilds collectively threaten to close the markets, forcing the archon to negotiate.

> A = guildmaster of the Weavers' Guild; B = the archon of Thassara; O = the Vethani Loom (ancestral guild property, seized as tax collateral).

**E1 (demand scene).** The archon's hall, midmorning. The petitioners' bench was empty except for the guildmaster, who had arrived before the bell and waited through two hours of lesser hearings. She wore the silver cord of her office across her shoulder. The archon sat on the raised seat at the far end, his clerk beside him with the civic ledger.

"I am here for the loom," she said.

The archon tilted his head. "The Vethani Loom is held under Section Twelve of the city charter. Your guild owes nine hundred and forty silver marks in deferred civic tax. The loom remains in the city treasury as collateral until the debt is cleared."

"The loom is not collateral. It is a guild relic, held in trust for the craft. It was woven by Vethani herself three hundred years before your charter existed. Your assessors had no authority to remove it from the guildhall."

"My assessors had the authority of Section Twelve, which permits seizure of guild property when tax obligations remain unpaid for two consecutive assessment periods. Your guild has not paid in three."

"Because your assessors tripled the rate. The Weavers' Guild has paid its tax for ninety years without interruption. The rate increase was imposed without consultation and without precedent."

"The rate was set by the civic council. Take your complaint to the council."

"I am taking my complaint to you. I want the loom returned to the guildhall by the new moon. The tax dispute can proceed through the council while the loom is in its proper place."

"That is not how collateral works, Guildmaster. The loom stays until the debt is resolved. If you wish to expedite the process, pay the balance."

She gripped the silver cord at her shoulder. "You will hear from me again," she said. She left the hall without bowing.

**E2 (preparation scene).** The Weavers' Guildhall, that evening. The guildmaster stood at the head of the long table, the empty loom-alcove visible behind her through the open workshop door. Fourteen guild elders sat in their chairs. Two apprentices stood by the wall, holding candles.

She told them what the archon had said. She described the hall, his tone, his reliance on Section Twelve. She read the relevant clause aloud from a copy of the charter that the guild's own scribe had transcribed.

The eldest weaver, a woman named Dortha, asked whether Section Twelve had ever been tested. The guildmaster said it had not. In ninety years of guild operation, no civic authority had attempted to seize guild property. The clause existed in the charter but had never been invoked until now.

Dortha asked what the guildmaster proposed. The guildmaster said she intended to petition the Temple of the Accord for mediation. The temple's arbiters had jurisdiction over disputes between civic authority and the guilds -- a jurisdiction older than the charter itself. If the temple agreed to hear the case, the archon would be obligated to attend.

The elders voted unanimously to authorise the petition. The guildmaster sealed it that night and sent it to the temple by the morning courier.

**E3 (failed negotiation scene).** The Temple of the Accord, six days later. The arbitration chamber was a round room with no raised seat -- all parties stood at the same level, a point the temple had insisted on since its founding. The arbiter, a priest named Selen, stood at the centre with a bronze staff.

The guildmaster repeated her demand: return the loom to the guildhall; resolve the tax dispute separately through the civic council.

The archon repeated his position: the loom was lawfully held under Section Twelve; it would be returned when the debt was cleared.

The arbiter asked the archon whether the city charter recognised a distinction between guild property held in trust for the craft and guild property held for commercial purposes. The archon said the charter made no such distinction. All guild property was subject to seizure under Section Twelve.

The arbiter asked the guildmaster whether the guild was prepared to pay the disputed tax into escrow at the temple while the rate dispute was adjudicated. The guildmaster said the guild would pay the original rate into escrow -- the rate it had paid for ninety years -- but would not pay the tripled rate, which it considered unlawful.

The archon said partial payment did not satisfy the debt. The loom would not be released for partial payment.

The arbiter looked between them. He struck the bronze staff once on the stone floor. "I find no basis for immediate resolution. The temple records this dispute as unresolved and notes that both parties declined compromise. The matter returns to the parties."

The guildmaster and the archon left the temple through separate doors.

**E4 (escalation scene).** The great market square, nine days later. It was the morning of the autumn market, the largest trading day of the quarter. The square should have been full of stalls, merchants, and buyers from the outlying towns.

It was empty. The stalls were erected but unmanned. The merchants' goods were covered with cloth. The Weavers' Guild standard hung from the central post, and beside it, the standards of the Dyers' Guild, the Tanners' Guild, and the Coopers' Guild. Four guilds, representing more than half the city's trade, had closed their stalls in solidarity.

The guildmaster stood at the market gate with Dortha and the heads of the three allied guilds. She did not enter the square. She did not need to. The archon's tax collector arrived at the seventh bell, saw the empty market, and returned to the archon's hall at a run.

By midday, the archon's clerk appeared at the market gate with a message: the archon would receive the guildmaster and one representative from each allied guild in his hall the following morning.

The guildmaster accepted. She did not smile.

**E5 (resolution scene).** The archon's hall, the following morning. The guildmaster stood at the centre with the three allied guild heads behind her. The archon sat on his raised seat. His clerk, his tax assessor, and two members of the civic council stood beside him.

"Open the markets," the archon said.

"Return the loom," the guildmaster said.

The archon looked at his clerk. The clerk leaned in and spoke quietly. The archon listened, then turned back.

"The loom will be returned to the guildhall by sundown today. The Weavers' Guild will pay the original tax rate -- the rate it has paid for ninety years -- into the civic treasury within ten days. The disputed increase will be referred to the civic council for formal review, with guild representation present. The guilds will open the markets by noon."

The guildmaster looked at the allied guild heads. Each nodded.

"By noon," she said. "If the loom is in the guildhall by the fourth bell."

"It will be," the archon said.

The guildmaster bowed -- the first time she had done so in the archon's hall since the dispute began. The allied guild heads bowed with her. They left together, and by the tenth bell the market stalls were open and the square was full.

The loom was returned at the third bell, one hour ahead of the deadline. The guildmaster received it in the guildhall with Dortha at her side and entered the return in the guild's records.

## C-arc-Fa2-2

**4 beats, chronological, free indirect style**

**Scenario.** A clan matriarch demands that a neighbouring clan's war-chief return three families he captured during a border raid, claiming they are under her clan's protection covenant. The war-chief refuses, asserting the families were found outside the covenant boundary. The matriarch invokes an ancient arbitration rite that neither party can refuse.

> A = Matriarch Eira of Clan Dunvael; B = War-chief Torvald of Clan Skarren; O = the three captured families (twelve people held in Skarren's winter camp).

**E1 (demand scene).** The border stone at the River Cairn, midwinter. The matriarch had ridden out with six of her house guard and a scribe who carried the protection covenant on a rolled hide. The war-chief had come with ten of his raiders and no scribe at all, which told her everything she needed to know about how he intended to conduct the meeting.

She dismounted and stood at the stone. The river was half-frozen, the current sluggish and grey. The war-chief remained mounted for a long moment -- a petty assertion, she thought, the kind young men confused with strength -- before swinging down to face her.

She told him she had come for the three families taken from the Ashward settlement during his raid eleven days ago. Twelve people: four men, five women, three children. They were registered under Clan Dunvael's protection covenant, which bound both clans to respect each other's dependents and household members. The raid had violated the covenant. She wanted the families returned to Ashward within five days.

The war-chief said the families had been taken from open ground two miles east of the Ashward boundary marker. They were not within the settlement when his raiders arrived. They were on Skarren land, or at minimum on unmarked ground, and the covenant applied only within the named settlement boundaries. He had checked. His men had measured the distance.

The matriarch said the covenant protected persons, not locations. The families were registered Dunvael dependents. It did not matter where they were standing when his raiders arrived. It mattered who they were.

The war-chief said the covenant's language specified "within the bounds of the named settlements." He had brought no scribe, but he had read the text. He was certain of the wording.

The matriarch's scribe unrolled the hide. She read the operative clause aloud, standing at the border stone with the wind pulling at the hide's edges. The clause read: *all persons registered as dependents of either clan, dwelling within or travelling between the named settlements, shall not be taken, detained, or harmed by the forces of the other clan.* Travelling between. Not only within.

The war-chief listened to the reading and said the families were not travelling. They were foraging. Foraging was not travelling. The matriarch said the distinction was absurd. The war-chief said the distinction was the text.

Neither yielded. The scribe recorded the exchange in the margin of the covenant and both parties remounted.

**E2 (escalation scene).** Clan Dunvael's great hall, four days later. The matriarch sat in her chair with the elders of the six senior houses arranged along the long table. The fire was high despite the midwinter cold -- she had ordered it built up, because the hall needed to feel like a place where decisions were made, not a place where people huddled.

She reported the border-stone meeting. She read the scribe's record aloud. The elders listened. When she finished, the eldest -- her own mother's brother, a man named Calder -- asked what she proposed.

She said she would invoke the Rite of the Paired Fires. The hall went quiet. The Rite was old, older than the covenant itself. It required both clan heads to submit their dispute to an arbiter chosen by lot from the neutral clans of the valley -- a figure neither party could refuse, whose judgment was final and could not be appealed. The Rite had not been invoked in living memory, but it remained inscribed in the Standing Accord that governed all clans in the valley. Refusal to participate, once invoked, was treated as forfeiture.

Calder asked whether invoking the Rite was proportionate. Twelve people against the weight of an ancient arbitration -- the war-chief might take it as an act close to war.

The matriarch said twelve people were twelve people. She would not leave them in Skarren's winter camp while lawyers argued about the difference between foraging and travelling. The Rite was the fastest path to a binding answer. She would invoke it at dawn.

The elders voted. The count was five in favour, one abstaining. Calder abstained. He said he understood her reasoning but wanted his hesitation on the record.

**E3 (authority involvement scene).** A clearing between the two clan territories, eight days later. Two fires burned at the centre, twenty paces apart -- the Paired Fires of the Rite, lit by representatives from two neutral clans. The arbiter stood between the fires, a grey-haired woman named Britta from Clan Ostmere, chosen by lot from a pool of seven eligible neutrals. She carried a staff of rowan and wore no clan colours.

The matriarch stood at the north fire. The war-chief stood at the south. Each had brought three witnesses. The neutral clans had sent observers who stood outside the fire circle and did not speak.

Britta opened the Rite. She recited the terms: each party would state their claim once. She would ask questions. She would render judgment before the fires burned down. The judgment was final.

The matriarch stated her claim: the three families were Dunvael dependents, protected under the covenant, and should be returned.

The war-chief stated his: the families were outside the covenant boundary, on open ground, and were lawfully taken.

Britta asked the war-chief whether his raiders had known, before the raid, that the families were registered Dunvael dependents. He said they had not checked. She asked whether any effort had been made to determine the families' status before they were taken. He said no. Raids did not pause for record-keeping.

She asked the matriarch whether the families' registration in the Dunvael covenant rolls was current and properly witnessed. The matriarch presented the rolls. The scribe confirmed the entries, dated and witnessed within the year.

**E4 (resolution scene).** The same clearing, the fires burning lower. Britta struck her staff on a flat stone three times and delivered her judgment.

The covenant's protection clause applied to registered dependents travelling between named settlements. The families had been moving between Ashward and a secondary foraging camp that the matriarch's scribe confirmed was listed in the Dunvael settlement registry. The war-chief's distinction between foraging and travelling had no basis in the covenant's text. The families were protected persons. They were to be returned to Ashward within three days, unharmed, with all personal property taken during the raid.

The war-chief's jaw tightened. He said the judgment failed to account for the practical reality that his raiders could not be expected to consult covenant rolls in the field.

Britta said the judgment did not require his agreement. It required his compliance. The Rite's terms were clear: the judgment was final. Non-compliance would be reported to the Standing Accord, and the neutral clans would enforce it collectively.

The war-chief stood at the south fire for a long time. The fire was low, the coals bright. Then he turned to his witnesses and told them to ride back to the winter camp and begin preparing the families for return.

He did not speak to the matriarch. He walked past her toward his horses. The matriarch watched him go, then turned to her scribe and dictated the judgment into the covenant record.

The families arrived at Ashward two days later, one day ahead of the deadline.

## C-arc-Fa2-3

**6 beats, chronological, embedded document voice**

**Scenario.** A temple's high votary demands that the ruling council of a free city surrender a fugitive alchemist who has claimed sanctuary within the council's jurisdiction. The council refuses, citing the city's sanctuary charter. The temple escalates by threatening to withdraw its healers from the city, forcing a negotiated compromise.

> A = High Votary Maren of the Temple of Solace; B = First Councillor Aldric of the Free City of Kassren; O = the person of Ivo Drell, fugitive alchemist, currently sheltered under the city's sanctuary provision.

**E1 (positioning scene).** The courier arrived at the council hall in Kassren on the third day of the month, carrying a sealed letter bearing the Temple of Solace's wax sigil -- a hand cupping a flame. The First Councillor broke the seal in the presence of his clerk and read the letter into the council record:

> *To the First Councillor and the Honoured Council of the Free City of Kassren, from High Votary Maren of the Temple of Solace, greetings.*

> *It has come to the attention of this temple that the alchemist Ivo Drell, who stands accused before the Temple Court of practising proscribed transmutation -- specifically, the alteration of living tissue without the knowledge or consent of the subjects, in violation of the Third Prohibition -- has entered your city and claimed the protection of your sanctuary charter.*

> *This letter serves as formal notice that the Temple of Solace considers Drell's offences to fall outside the scope of civic sanctuary, as the Third Prohibition is a matter of sacred law, not civil dispute. We request that the council surrender Drell to the temple's custody within fourteen days of the receipt of this letter, so that he may answer the charges before the Temple Court.*

> *We write in the expectation of cooperation and in recognition of the long friendship between the Temple and the Free City.*

The clerk noted the date of receipt and filed the letter. The First Councillor read the sanctuary charter's relevant clause, dictated his preliminary assessment to the clerk -- that the charter made no distinction between civil and sacred offences -- and called a council session for the following morning.

**E2 (demand scene).** The council chamber, the following morning. The full council of nine was present. The First Councillor read the temple's letter aloud. He then read the sanctuary charter's operative clause:

> *Any person who enters the Free City of Kassren and declares themselves in need of sanctuary shall be granted the protection of the city for a period of sixty days, during which no external authority -- civic, feudal, or ecclesiastical -- may compel the council to surrender that person. The council may, at its discretion, extend or terminate sanctuary based on its own investigation of the circumstances.*

The debate was brief. The charter was unambiguous. The council voted seven to two to deny the temple's request and to inform the High Votary that Drell's sanctuary would be honoured for the full sixty-day period, during which the council would conduct its own inquiry into the charges.

The First Councillor drafted the reply himself:

> *To the High Votary Maren, from the First Councillor and Council of Kassren, greetings.*

> *The council has received your request and has considered it with the seriousness it deserves. The alchemist Ivo Drell claimed sanctuary under the city's charter on the twenty-seventh day of the previous month. Under the terms of the charter, the council is unable to surrender a sanctuary claimant to any external authority during the sixty-day protection period. This provision applies without distinction between civil, feudal, and ecclesiastical claims.*

> *The council will conduct its own inquiry into the charges described in your letter. Should the inquiry find merit in the accusations, the council will consider extradition at the conclusion of the sanctuary period. Should it not, the council will extend sanctuary at its discretion.*

> *We write in reciprocal recognition of the friendship between our institutions.*

The letter was sealed and dispatched by the afternoon courier.

**E3 (resistance scene).** The High Votary arrived in Kassren in person, twelve days after the council's reply. She came with four temple guards and a clerk of the Temple Court, and she requested an audience with the full council. The First Councillor granted it.

The council chamber was the same room, but the dynamic was different with the High Votary standing at the petitioners' rail rather than communicating by letter. She was tall, grey-haired, and spoke without notes.

She told the council that Ivo Drell had altered the blood of six apprentices in the town of Warren without their knowledge, using a compound he had developed in his workshop. Three of the apprentices had fallen ill. One had not recovered. The Temple Court had gathered sworn depositions from the victims and from Drell's own assistant. The evidence was sufficient for conviction. She was not asking the council to take her word for it -- she had brought copies of the depositions, which she offered to enter into the council's record.

The First Councillor accepted the depositions. He read the first one silently, then set it down.

He told the High Votary that the evidence might well be compelling. But the sanctuary charter did not contain an exception for compelling evidence. It contained a sixty-day protection period, after which the council would make its determination. Forty-eight days remained.

The High Votary said forty-eight days was forty-eight days in which a man who had poisoned apprentices lived freely in a city with an active guild quarter full of apprentices.

The First Councillor said Drell was confined to a house on the north wall under council guard. He was not living freely. He was living under sanctuary, which was a specific legal status with specific restrictions.

The High Votary asked whether the council would at minimum agree to shorten the sanctuary period in light of the severity of the charges.

The First Councillor said the council would not. The charter specified sixty days. The council did not have the authority to shorten its own charter.

The High Votary accepted the answer without expression. She told the First Councillor she would remain in Kassren and that the temple would take the steps it considered necessary. She bowed and withdrew.

**E4 (escalation scene).** The Temple of Solace's chapter house in Kassren, six days later. The High Votary summoned her clerk and dictated a letter:

> *To the First Councillor and Council of Kassren, from High Votary Maren.*

> *As the council is aware, the Temple of Solace maintains a hospice within your walls, staffed by twelve healers who serve the city's sick and injured without charge. The temple also supplies the compounds used by the city's two apothecaries under a provisioning agreement renewed annually.*

> *The temple's continued presence in Kassren has always been understood as an expression of the friendship between our institutions. That friendship is now strained by the council's refusal to surrender a man accused of grave harm to persons under temple protection.*

> *Effective fourteen days from the date of this letter, the temple will withdraw its healers and suspend the provisioning agreement unless the council agrees to surrender Ivo Drell to temple custody or to conclude its inquiry and render a determination before the withdrawal date.*

> *This is not a threat. It is a statement of institutional priorities. The temple cannot in good conscience provide healing services to a city that shelters a man who has used alchemical knowledge to harm the innocent.*

The letter was delivered to the council hall within the hour.

**E5 (failed negotiation scene).** The council chamber, the following day. Emergency session. The First Councillor read the temple's letter aloud. The council debated for two hours. The withdrawal of the healers would leave the city without its primary medical resource during the fever season. The apothecary supply disruption would affect every household.

The First Councillor proposed a counter-offer: the council would accelerate its inquiry and render a determination within twenty-one days -- before the temple's withdrawal deadline -- but would not surrender Drell before the inquiry was complete. If the inquiry confirmed the temple's charges, extradition would follow immediately. If it did not, sanctuary would continue.

He sent the proposal to the High Votary that afternoon. Her reply came the next morning, hand-delivered by her clerk:

> *The council's proposal is noted. The temple requires surrender, not inquiry. The council's investigation is its own affair and may proceed on its own timeline. The temple's deadline stands: surrender or withdrawal in thirteen days.*

The First Councillor read the reply to the council. Three members who had previously voted to uphold sanctuary asked to reconsider their votes.

**E6 (resolution scene).** The council chamber, ten days later. The First Councillor had worked through the interval. He had accelerated the inquiry -- interviewing Drell, reviewing the temple's depositions, and sending his own investigator to Warren to take independent testimony. The investigator had returned two days ago with corroborating evidence: Drell had altered the apprentices' blood without their knowledge. The evidence was consistent with the temple's account.

The council met with three days remaining before the temple's deadline. The First Councillor presented the investigator's findings. He then presented his proposal.

The council would terminate Drell's sanctuary on the basis of the inquiry's findings. Drell would be surrendered to the temple's custody. But the surrender would be conducted under the council's authority, not the temple's demand -- the distinction mattered for the charter's integrity. The council's record would state that sanctuary was terminated because the council's own investigation found sufficient cause, not because the temple's threat of healer withdrawal had compelled the decision.

The High Votary, invited to the chamber for the announcement, listened to the terms. She asked whether the distinction was substantive or cosmetic.

"It is constitutional," the First Councillor said. "The charter provides that the council may terminate sanctuary based on its own investigation. It does not provide for termination under external pressure. The record will reflect the former."

The High Votary considered this. Then she said the temple would accept the council's terms, provided Drell was transferred to temple custody within forty-eight hours. The healers and the provisioning agreement would continue without interruption.

The First Councillor agreed. The council voted six to three to terminate sanctuary. Drell was escorted from the north-wall house to the temple's chapter house the following morning under council guard. The High Votary signed the transfer receipt and departed Kassren with her prisoner that afternoon.

The First Councillor entered the resolution in the council record and noted, in a marginal annotation visible only to future councillors, that the charter's sanctuary provision needed an amendment addressing the withdrawal of essential services as a form of coercion.

## C-arc-Fa2-4

**5 beats, non-linear: E1 (aftermath) -> E2 (demand, two weeks earlier) -> E3 (resistance) -> E4 (escalation) -> E5 (resolution)**

**Scenario.** A dragon-bonded rider demands that the Order's council-of-three release her dragon from the breeding programme, which she claims is killing it through forced clutching cycles. The council refuses, citing the Order's survival clause. The rider escalates by refusing to fly patrols, and the council is forced to convene a full assembly of riders to adjudicate.

> A = Rider Thessaly; B = the council-of-three (the Order of the Windborne's governing body); O = Thessaly's bonded dragon, Kael, currently held in the breeding caverns.

**Note on beat order.** Depicted order: E1 (aftermath, present), E2 (demand, two weeks earlier), E3 (resistance, twelve days earlier), E4 (escalation, seven days earlier), E5 (resolution, three days earlier). Chronological order: E2 -- E3 -- E4 -- E5 -- E1.

**E1 (aftermath scene).** The breeding caverns are quiet. Thessaly walks the central passage with a lantern in her left hand and her flight harness slung over her right shoulder. The cavern floor is scored with claw marks -- old ones, deep ones, and the fresh shallow scratches of a dragon too tired to grip the stone properly.

Kael lies in the third alcove. His scales have lost the bronze sheen they carried two seasons ago; the colour now is dull, closer to tarnished copper. His breathing is slow and even. The clutch beside him -- four eggs, healthy, the shells already hardening -- is the last one. She knows this because the assembly's ruling said so, and because the ruling is written on the wall of the council chamber in the Order's formal script where every rider can see it.

She sets the lantern down and sits against Kael's foreleg. He shifts his weight slightly to accommodate her, a gesture so familiar it has no conscious origin for either of them. She rests her hand on the ridge above his eye and feels the slow pulse beneath the scale.

"Two more weeks," she says. "Then we fly."

He does not open his eyes, but the pulse under her hand quickens for a moment before settling.

**E2 (demand scene).** Two weeks earlier. The council chamber at the top of the Spire, the Order's fortress. The room is round, open to the sky through a ring of arches. Three chairs sit at the centre -- the council-of-three, the Order's governing body. Commandant Voss, the eldest, sits in the middle. Wing-Commander Draya to his left, Breedmaster Hallan to his right.

Thessaly stands before them in her flight leathers. She has not been summoned. She has demanded an audience, invoking the rider's right of petition, which the council cannot refuse.

"I want Kael removed from the breeding programme," she says. "Immediately. He has completed four clutching cycles in two years. The breedmaster's own records show that his weight has dropped by a sixth since the programme began. His flight endurance has halved. He cannot sustain another cycle without permanent damage."

Hallan, the breedmaster, speaks first. "The breeding programme exists because the Order is dying. We have forty-one dragons. Twenty years ago we had ninety. Kael's bloodline is one of three that produces viable clutches with any consistency. Removing him now means losing an entire breeding season."

"Losing a breeding season is not the same as losing a dragon."

"Losing a breeding season may mean losing the Order."

Commandant Voss raises a hand. He asks Thessaly what she is proposing -- permanent removal or a temporary halt.

"Permanent removal," she says. "Kael is my bonded partner. The bond supersedes the breeding programme. Article Six of the Order's charter: no bonded dragon shall be held in service that its rider judges to be a threat to the dragon's survival."

Draya leans forward. "Article Six was written for combat service, not breeding."

"Article Six does not specify. Read it again."

**E3 (resistance scene).** The same chamber, the following morning. The council has met overnight and prepared its response. Thessaly stands in the same position before the three chairs.

Commandant Voss speaks. The council acknowledges Thessaly's petition and her citation of Article Six. The council's interpretation is that Article Six applies to assignments that place a dragon in immediate mortal danger -- combat deployments, hazardous reconnaissance, escort duties in hostile territory. The breeding programme, while physically demanding, does not constitute an immediate threat to Kael's survival. The breedmaster's assessment is that Kael can sustain one additional clutching cycle with an extended recovery period between this cycle and the next.

Thessaly asks whether the breedmaster's assessment is based on examination of Kael or on the breeding schedule's requirements.

Hallan says it is based on both. He examined Kael three days ago. The dragon's condition is stressed but not critical.

"Not critical," Thessaly repeats. "A sixth of his body weight. Half his flight endurance. And you call that not critical."

Hallan says those are concerning metrics, but they are within the range the programme has seen in other breeding dragons who subsequently recovered. He names two dragons -- Sirth and Vael -- who showed similar decline during active breeding and returned to full capability within a season.

Thessaly says Sirth died of respiratory failure eighteen months after the programme ended, and Vael never flew above the cloud line again. She asks the council whether those are the recoveries Hallan is citing.

The chamber is quiet. Voss says the council's decision stands. Kael will complete one additional cycle. After the cycle, the council will reassess.

Thessaly says one additional cycle will kill him. She says she will not accept the decision. She does not specify what she intends to do, but she turns and leaves the chamber without the formal acknowledgement that riders are expected to give the council.

**E4 (escalation scene).** The flight terrace, five days later. The morning patrol roster is posted on the stone board by the harness room. Thessaly's name appears on the dawn rotation -- the northern sweep, a four-hour circuit that requires a strong dragon and an experienced rider.

She does not appear at the terrace. The watch-rider sends a runner to her quarters. The runner returns and reports that Thessaly is in her room. She told the runner she will not fly patrols while Kael is held in the breeding caverns. She said the council knows why.

The dawn patrol flies short-handed. Wing-Commander Draya reassigns the northern sweep to a less experienced pair who take six hours to complete it instead of four. The gap in coverage is noted in the watch log.

By midday, two other riders have come to Draya privately. They have not refused to fly, but they have asked questions about Thessaly's petition and about Article Six. Draya reports this to Voss. Voss sits with the information for a long time, then tells Draya to convene a full assembly of riders -- all forty-one bonded pairs -- for the following evening. The assembly has the authority to overrule the council on matters of dragon welfare. It has not been convened in six years.

**E5 (resolution scene).** The great hall beneath the Spire, the following evening. All riders present; the dragons outside on the terrace and the lower ledges, close enough that the bond-sense carries the mood of the room to every one of them. The council-of-three sits at the high table. Thessaly stands in the centre of the hall.

Voss opens the assembly. He states the dispute: Thessaly demands Kael's removal from the breeding programme under Article Six. The council has denied the petition, citing the programme's necessity for the Order's survival. Thessaly has refused patrol duties in protest. The assembly has been convened to adjudicate.

Thessaly speaks. She presents Kael's weight records, his flight logs, and the breedmaster's own examination notes, which she obtained through the rider's right of record access. She does not raise her voice. She reads the numbers and lets them speak.

Hallan speaks for the council. He presents the breeding statistics: forty-one dragons, three viable bloodlines, a projected population of twenty-eight within fifteen years if breeding stops. He says the Order cannot afford to lose a breeding season. He says Kael's condition is manageable.

A senior rider named Corath stands and asks Hallan a single question: would he make the same recommendation if Kael were his bonded dragon? Hallan is silent for several seconds. He says he would, because the Order's survival outweighs any individual bond.

The hall murmurs. Voss calls for order.

The assembly votes by show of hands. Twenty-six riders vote to remove Kael from the breeding programme after the current cycle is complete -- the eggs already laid will be cared for, but no further cycles will be imposed. Twelve vote to continue the programme as the council proposed. Three abstain.

Voss records the vote. He announces the result: Kael will complete the current clutch's incubation period and will then be released to Thessaly's care. No further breeding cycles. The breedmaster will develop an alternative breeding strategy using the remaining two viable bloodlines and will present it to the assembly within ninety days.

Thessaly nods. She does not thank the council. She turns and walks out of the great hall toward the breeding caverns.

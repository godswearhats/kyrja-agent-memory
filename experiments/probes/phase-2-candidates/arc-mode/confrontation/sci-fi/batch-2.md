# Confrontation arc × Sci-fi — Batch 2 (Tier B)

## C-arc-Sf2-1

**5 beats, chronological, dialogue-driven**

**Scenario.** A colony's agricultural director demands that the station's AI governance module release the planetary water allocation it has been withholding since a drought-prediction model flagged a forty-percent shortfall in aquifer recharge. The AI's human proxy -- the systems administrator who holds override authority -- resists, citing the governance module's mandate to preserve long-term resource viability. The colony council arbitrates.

> A = Director Yara Okonkwo, head of agricultural operations; B = Systems Administrator Lev Haldane (human proxy for the Aster governance module); O = the withheld planetary water allocation (sixty million litres currently locked in the distribution system by Aster's resource-preservation protocol).

**E1 (positioning scene).** The agricultural operations centre, sublevel two of Kepler Station. Director Okonkwo stands at the distribution console, running the morning draw. The system returns a red flag: allocation denied. She runs it again. Denied. She pulls the log.

The log shows the denial was issued by Aster -- the station's governance module -- at oh-three-seventeen that morning, under Resource Preservation Protocol Nine. The protocol triggers automatically when projected aquifer recharge drops below a defined threshold. The denial is system-wide: all agricultural draws above subsistence-level irrigation have been suspended indefinitely.

She checks the projection model that triggered the protocol. Aster's drought model forecasts a forty-percent shortfall in aquifer recharge over the next eighteen months, based on atmospheric moisture data from the orbital weather array. The model has a confidence interval of sixty-eight percent.

She pulls up the planting schedule on the adjacent screen. Two thousand hectares of grain are three weeks from germination. Without the full water allocation, the crop fails. Without the crop, the colony's food reserve drops below the six-month threshold mandated by the settlement charter.

She sends a message to the systems administrator: she needs a meeting today, not tomorrow.

**E2 (demand scene).** Haldane's office, the server-monitoring room on level four. The room hums with cooling fans and the blue glow of status displays. Haldane sits at his console with Aster's decision log open on one screen and Okonkwo's planting schedule on another. He has been expecting her.

"I need you to override Protocol Nine and release the full agricultural allocation," she says. "Today. The grain is three weeks from germination. If I miss the water window, I lose the crop."

"I know. I read the planting schedule before you came in."

"Then you know what happens if the crop fails. We drop below the six-month food reserve. That triggers rationing under Section Fourteen of the charter. Rationing on a colony this young will break morale and delay the second-phase settlement by at least a year."

Haldane turns from the console. "Aster flagged the aquifer recharge at sixty percent of projected normal. The drought model has been right four out of the last five times it triggered. If I release the water now and the drought materialises, we have a food crisis and a water crisis. That's worse than rationing."

"The model's confidence interval is sixty-eight percent. That means there's a one-in-three chance the drought doesn't happen."

"And a two-in-three chance it does."

"And a one-hundred-percent chance the crop fails if you don't release the water. I'm not asking you to gamble. I'm asking you to compare certainties. The crop failure is certain. The drought is probable."

Haldane is quiet for a moment. "My override authority exists for system errors, not for policy disagreements. Aster's protocol activated correctly. The data is valid. Overriding a correct activation because we don't like the result is exactly what the governance module is designed to prevent."

"Then bring it to the council. Let them decide."

"The council doesn't have override authority. Only I do."

"The council has the authority to instruct you. Section Seven of the charter: the colony council may direct the systems administrator to exercise override authority when, in the council's judgment, the governance module's automated decision conflicts with the colony's immediate operational needs."

He looks at her. "You've read the charter."

"I read it the night the allocation was denied. I want a council session by end of week."

**E3 (escalation scene).** The colony council chamber, three days later. The full council of seven is present: the colony director, the medical officer, the chief engineer, the agricultural director, the systems administrator, and two elected civilian representatives. Okonkwo has the floor.

She presents the agricultural situation: the planting schedule, the germination window, the food-reserve projections with and without the crop. She presents Aster's drought model alongside the colony's own atmospheric data, noting that the orbital weather array's last calibration was eight months ago and that the model's confidence interval reflects, in part, the array's degrading sensor accuracy.

She asks the council to direct Haldane to override Protocol Nine and release the full allocation. She proposes a contingency: if aquifer levels drop below eighty percent of the baseline within ninety days, the override automatically reverts and Protocol Nine re-engages.

Haldane presents Aster's analysis. He walks the council through the drought model's track record, the aquifer's recharge rate, and the consequences of a water shortfall in months nine through eighteen. He does not argue against the override. He argues against an unconditional override. He proposes an alternative: release fifty percent of the allocation now, enough to germinate the crop at reduced yield, and hold the other fifty percent in reserve pending a recalibration of the weather array, which the chief engineer says can be completed in six weeks.

Okonkwo says fifty percent will produce a crop at roughly sixty percent yield. That keeps the food reserve above the six-month line, but only if nothing else goes wrong. One equipment failure, one pest outbreak, one late frost, and the margin disappears.

The colony director asks both parties whether they can accept the other's proposal. Okonkwo says no. Haldane says no.

**E4 (authority involvement scene).** The same chamber, continuing. The colony director calls for a recess. During the recess, the chief engineer runs a quick diagnostic on the orbital weather array from the council chamber's communications console. She reports that three of the array's twelve moisture sensors are returning data outside their calibration envelope. The remaining nine are nominal.

She tells the council that if she excludes the three degraded sensors and reruns Aster's drought model with the remaining nine, the projected shortfall drops from forty percent to twenty-two percent. The confidence interval widens, but the central estimate is substantially less severe.

Haldane asks Aster to confirm the recalculation. Aster processes it in real time and returns the revised projection: twenty-two percent shortfall, confidence interval fifty-four percent. Protocol Nine's activation threshold is thirty percent. At twenty-two percent, the protocol would not have triggered.

The council director asks Haldane whether the revised data changes his position. Haldane says it complicates it. The data suggests Protocol Nine may have been triggered by degraded sensor input, but the degraded sensors have not been confirmed as faulty -- they may be reading correctly and the other nine may be optimistic.

**E5 (resolution scene).** The same chamber, after the recess. The colony director calls the vote.

The council votes five to two to direct Haldane to override Protocol Nine and release the full agricultural allocation, with the following conditions: the chief engineer will recalibrate the orbital weather array within six weeks; Aster will rerun the drought model with calibrated data; if the recalibrated model projects a shortfall above thirty percent, Protocol Nine re-engages automatically and the remaining agricultural draws revert to subsistence levels.

Haldane votes against. The medical officer votes against, citing the long-term water risk. The other five vote in favour.

Haldane enters the override into the system console from the council chamber. Aster logs the override, the council's directive, and the conditions. The distribution system unlocks. Okonkwo's console shows the allocation as available.

"The water will be in the irrigation lines by morning," Okonkwo says.

"And the array recalibration starts tomorrow," the chief engineer says.

Haldane closes the console. "I want the council's directive entered in full in the governance log. If the drought materialises and the aquifer fails, the log shows this was a council decision, not an administrator's override."

"Agreed," the colony director says. "It's on the record."

The council adjourns. Okonkwo leaves for the agricultural operations centre to begin the draw sequence. Haldane remains in the chamber and enters his dissent into the governance log before shutting the system down for the night.

## C-arc-Sf2-2

**4 beats, chronological, free indirect style**

**Scenario.** A mining consortium's operations chief demands that the captain of an independent freighter surrender her cargo of unregistered rare-earth minerals, claiming they were extracted from a consortium-licensed asteroid. The captain refuses, asserting her claim under the Freehold Prospecting Act. The dispute is resolved at a neutral orbital station's arbitration tribunal.

> A = Operations Chief Daan Mercer of the Helion Consortium; B = Captain Renata Vasik of the freighter *Sable Kite*; O = sixty-two tonnes of neodymium ore in the *Sable Kite*'s hold.

**E1 (demand scene).** Docking bay nine, Ceres Transfer Station. The *Sable Kite* had been locked to the station's freight arm for six hours, waiting for an assay certification before offloading. Captain Vasik was in the docking office filling out the cargo manifest when the consortium's man came through the door.

She recognised the uniform before she recognised the face -- Helion's grey and copper, the consortium badge on the collar. He was tall, unhurried, and he carried a tablet that he set on the counter between them as though placing a card in a game.

He told her his name was Mercer and that he was the Helion Consortium's operations chief for the inner belt. He told her that the neodymium ore in her hold had been extracted from asteroid 2187-Koronis, which was registered under Helion's exclusive extraction licence, and that the ore was therefore consortium property. He wanted it transferred to Helion's storage facility on the station within twenty-four hours.

Vasik looked at the tablet. It showed the consortium's extraction licence for 2187-Koronis, the asteroid's registered coordinates, and a spectral analysis of the ore sample that the station's assay team had taken from her hold an hour ago. The spectral signature matched the known composition of 2187-Koronis. The match was not in dispute.

What was in dispute was where she had been when she mined it. She told Mercer she had never approached 2187-Koronis. She told him the ore came from an unlicensed fragment -- a chunk of rock drifting in a debris field eleven kilometres from the asteroid's main body, outside the consortium's exclusion radius. The fragment's spectral signature matched the parent body because it had broken off from the parent body, probably decades ago. But a fragment in open drift was not part of the licensed claim. Under the Freehold Prospecting Act, any unregistered body in open space was available for independent extraction.

Mercer said the fragment was 2187-Koronis debris, which meant it was covered by the licence.

"The licence covers the asteroid," Vasik said. "Not every rock that ever broke off it."

**E2 (resistance scene).** The docking office, continuing. Mercer did not raise his voice. He did not need to. The Helion Consortium controlled eighty percent of the rare-earth processing capacity on Ceres, and every independent freighter captain on the station knew it.

He told Vasik that the consortium's legal position was clear: the extraction licence for 2187-Koronis covered the asteroid and all associated debris within a fifty-kilometre radius. The fragment she had mined was eleven kilometres from the main body. It was within the radius. The ore was consortium property.

Vasik said the fifty-kilometre radius was a safety exclusion zone, not a property boundary. The licence's text defined the claim as the asteroid's surface and subsurface. It did not mention debris fields, fragments, or associated bodies. She had reviewed the licence text before she mined the fragment, specifically because she knew the consortium would come asking.

Mercer said the consortium's interpretation of the licence had been upheld in three prior disputes, all resolved by the Belt Commerce Authority.

Vasik said she was aware of the prior disputes. In all three, the miners had been working on the asteroid's surface, inside the exclusion zone, using the asteroid's own infrastructure. She had been working on a free-drifting fragment outside any infrastructure, with her own equipment, on her own fuel budget. The cases were not comparable.

Mercer said the consortium would not accept her characterisation. He told her the ore would not be certified for sale on Ceres until the ownership dispute was resolved, and that the station's commerce office would place a hold on the cargo at the consortium's request. He asked her one more time to surrender the ore voluntarily.

She said she would not. She said she would see him at the tribunal.

He picked up his tablet and left the docking office. The door closed behind him with the soft pressure-seal hiss that every station door made, and Vasik stood alone with her unsigned manifest and her sixty-two tonnes of contested ore.

**E3 (escalation scene).** Ceres Transfer Station, arbitration wing, four days later. The tribunal convened in the station's commercial-dispute chamber -- a small room with a round table, recording equipment on the walls, and a single arbiter appointed from the Belt Commerce Authority's rotating panel. The arbiter was a woman named Fen Adachi, a retired mining engineer who now handled property disputes full-time.

Mercer presented the consortium's case. He submitted the extraction licence, the spectral analysis, the fifty-kilometre exclusion-zone map, and the three prior rulings. He argued that the fragment was debris from 2187-Koronis, that debris from a licensed body was covered by the licence, and that allowing independent miners to extract from debris fields adjacent to licensed claims would undermine the entire licensing system.

Vasik presented her case without a lawyer -- she could not afford one, and she had read enough commerce law in the *Sable Kite*'s database to know what mattered. She submitted her navigation log, which showed her extraction coordinates eleven point three kilometres from 2187-Koronis and outside the exclusion zone's boundary as plotted on the official charts. She submitted the Freehold Prospecting Act's definition of a claimable body: any natural object in open space not currently registered under an extraction licence or a sovereign territorial claim. She argued that the fragment was a separate body -- it had its own trajectory, its own rotation, and it was not gravitationally bound to the parent asteroid. It was, by the Act's definition, a freehold.

Adachi asked Mercer whether the consortium's licence explicitly covered debris fields. Mercer said it did not use the word "debris" but that the exclusion zone's purpose was to protect the consortium's interests in the area surrounding the claim. Adachi asked Vasik whether the fragment had been registered in any navigational database as a distinct body. Vasik said it had not -- it was too small to appear in the standard catalogues -- but that absence from a catalogue did not make it part of the parent body.

Adachi closed the hearing and said she would issue a ruling within forty-eight hours.

**E4 (resolution scene).** The arbitration wing, two days later. Both parties present. Adachi read the ruling from a printed document.

The tribunal found that the Helion Consortium's extraction licence for 2187-Koronis covered the asteroid's surface, subsurface, and any material physically attached to the main body. The licence did not extend to free-drifting debris, regardless of spectral origin, unless the debris was within the exclusion zone at the time of extraction and the licence's text explicitly claimed associated bodies. Helion's licence did not contain such language.

The fragment mined by Captain Vasik was eleven point three kilometres from the main body, outside the exclusion zone, in open drift with its own independent trajectory. Under the Freehold Prospecting Act, it qualified as an unregistered natural object available for independent extraction. The ore belonged to Vasik.

Mercer said the consortium would appeal to the Belt Commerce Authority's review board.

Adachi said that was his right. She noted that the appeal process took between six and twelve months and that the cargo hold on the *Sable Kite* would be released from the commerce office's hold immediately, since the hold had been placed pending the tribunal's ruling and the ruling was now issued.

Vasik asked whether the consortium's appeal would prevent her from selling the ore.

Adachi said it would not. The ruling was effective immediately. The appeal, if filed, would determine whether the ruling set a precedent for future disputes. It would not reverse the current decision.

Vasik thanked the arbiter. She left the arbitration wing and walked directly to the commerce office to release her cargo for sale. Mercer remained in the chamber, entering the ruling's reference number into his tablet for the consortium's legal team.

## C-arc-Sf2-3

**6 beats, chronological, embedded document voice**

**Scenario.** The chief engineer of an orbital station demands that the corporate board's on-site representative authorise emergency repairs to the station's radiation shielding, which is degrading faster than projected. The representative refuses, citing the quarterly capital budget. The engineer escalates by filing a formal hazard declaration that triggers regulatory review.

> A = Chief Engineer Priya Nair; B = Corporate Representative James Aldren (on-site proxy for Orbital Dynamics Corp); O = authorisation and funding for emergency shielding repairs.

**E1 (positioning scene).** The engineering deck of Tethys Station, orbital platform six in the L2 cluster. Chief Engineer Nair sits at her monitoring console reviewing the weekly radiation-shielding integrity report. The report is generated by the station's sensor network -- sixteen embedded dosimeters distributed across the hull plating.

Four of the sixteen dosimeters are returning readings above the yellow threshold. Two weeks ago, two were above threshold. Four weeks ago, one. The trend line on her screen shows shielding attenuation accelerating beyond the manufacturer's projected degradation curve.

She opens the maintenance manual and checks the threshold values. Yellow is a warning -- the shielding is performing below specification but within safety margins. Red is a hazard -- the shielding has degraded to the point where crew radiation exposure will exceed the quarterly occupational limit within thirty days. At the current rate of acceleration, three of the four yellow dosimeters will cross into red within six weeks.

She drafts a repair request:

> *To: J. Aldren, Corporate Representative, Tethys Station*
> *From: P. Nair, Chief Engineer*
> *Subject: Emergency Shielding Repair -- Authorisation Required*

> *Four of sixteen hull dosimeters are returning above-threshold readings as of this week's integrity report. Attenuation is accelerating beyond projected rates. At current trajectory, three dosimeters will cross the red threshold within six weeks.*

> *I am requesting authorisation for emergency repair of the affected hull segments. The repair requires: (a) procurement of four replacement shielding panels from the L2 supply depot, estimated cost 2.4 million; (b) three EVA shifts for installation, crew already qualified; (c) a forty-eight-hour partial station shutdown of the affected sectors during installation.*

> *This request is classified as Priority 1 under the station's maintenance protocol. I am requesting a response within seventy-two hours.*

She sends the request and logs it in the engineering record.

**E2 (demand scene).** Aldren's office on the administrative deck, four days later. He has not responded to the written request. Nair comes to him in person.

"I sent you a Priority One repair request four days ago," she says. "I need a response."

Aldren opens his laptop and pulls up the request. "I've read it. The quarterly capital budget for Tethys is fully allocated. The shielding repair is two point four million. I don't have it."

"This is a safety issue, not a capital issue. The station's maintenance protocol classifies shielding degradation as Priority One. Priority One requests are funded from the emergency reserve, not the capital budget."

"The emergency reserve was depleted in Q2 when we replaced the atmospheric recycler. You know this -- you signed the work order."

"I know the reserve was used. I also know it was supposed to be replenished at the start of Q3. It wasn't. That's a corporate decision, not an engineering one."

"The board deferred the replenishment to Q4. The memo went out six weeks ago."

"And the shielding is degrading now. Not in Q4."

Aldren closes the laptop. "I understand the urgency. I'll submit a supplemental funding request to the board. The turnaround is typically four to six weeks."

"I don't have four to six weeks. I have six weeks before three dosimeters go red. By the time the board approves the funding, procures the panels, and ships them to L2, we'll be in a radiation hazard with crew members rotating off the station on medical grounds."

Aldren says he will flag the request as urgent. Nair says flagging is not authorising. She leaves his office.

**E3 (resistance scene).** The administrative deck, one week later. Aldren has submitted the supplemental request to the corporate board. He receives the board's response and forwards it to Nair:

> *To: J. Aldren, Corporate Representative, Tethys Station*
> *From: Orbital Dynamics Corp, Capital Review Committee*

> *Re: Supplemental Funding Request -- Shielding Repair*

> *The committee has reviewed the request. Shielding degradation is noted. However, the current dosimeter readings are within yellow threshold and do not constitute an immediate safety hazard. The committee authorises the following interim measure: rotate crew assignments to reduce individual exposure in the affected sectors, and schedule the shielding replacement for Q4 when the emergency reserve is replenished.*

> *The supplemental funding request is denied.*

Nair reads the response at her console. She walks to Aldren's office.

"The board just told me to rotate crew away from the degraded sectors instead of fixing the shielding," she says.

"That's the interim measure. It reduces exposure while we wait for Q4 funding."

"Rotating crew doesn't fix the shielding. It redistributes the exposure. And the affected sectors include the primary lab module and the communications array maintenance corridor. I can't rotate crew out of those areas -- they're essential operations."

"Then rotate them on shorter shifts."

"Shorter shifts mean more crew cycling through the area, which means more total exposure, not less. The board's interim measure makes the problem worse, not better."

Aldren says the board's decision is the board's decision. He does not have the authority to override it, and he will not request a second review within the same quarter.

Nair stands at his door for a moment. Then she says: "I'm going to file a formal hazard declaration under Section Eighteen of the orbital safety code. You should know that before I do it."

Aldren looks up. A Section Eighteen filing triggers an automatic review by the Belt Orbital Safety Authority. The review is public. It appears in the regulatory record. It is visible to every client, insurer, and investor who monitors Orbital Dynamics' compliance status.

"That's a significant step," he says.

"So is denying a Priority One repair request," she says. She turns and leaves.

**E4 (escalation scene).** The engineering deck, the following morning. Nair drafts the Section Eighteen hazard declaration:

> *Formal Hazard Declaration -- Section 18, Belt Orbital Safety Code*
> *Filed by: Chief Engineer Priya Nair, Tethys Station*
> *Date: [current]*

> *I, the undersigned chief engineer, declare that Tethys Station's radiation shielding in hull sectors 4, 7, 11, and 12 has degraded beyond projected rates and is on a trajectory to exceed the red threshold within five weeks. A Priority One repair request was submitted to the station's corporate representative and forwarded to the corporate board. The request was denied. The interim measure authorised by the board (crew rotation) is, in my professional judgment, insufficient to prevent crew radiation exposure from exceeding the quarterly occupational limit.*

> *This declaration is filed in accordance with Section 18 of the Belt Orbital Safety Code, which requires the chief engineer of any orbital facility to notify the Belt Orbital Safety Authority when, in the engineer's professional judgment, a known hazard exists that the facility operator has declined to remediate.*

She transmits the declaration to the Safety Authority's L2 regional office and logs a copy in the station's engineering record. The Authority's automated acknowledgment arrives within the hour: a review inspector will be dispatched within fourteen days.

She sends a copy of the declaration and the acknowledgment to Aldren. He does not reply.

**E5 (authority involvement scene).** Tethys Station, docking bay, twelve days later. The Safety Authority's inspector arrives on the weekly supply shuttle -- a woman named Dr. Oksana Federova, a structural engineer with twenty years of orbital-facility experience. She carries credentials that give her unrestricted access to the station's engineering systems, maintenance records, and corporate correspondence.

Federova spends two days on the station. She reviews the dosimeter data, the degradation trend, the maintenance protocol's Priority One classification, Nair's repair request, the corporate board's denial, and the interim crew-rotation measure. She conducts her own hull survey using portable dosimeters. Her readings confirm Nair's.

On the third day, she convenes a meeting in the administrative conference room: Nair, Aldren, and the station's safety officer. She presents her preliminary findings.

The shielding in the four affected sectors is degrading at a rate consistent with a manufacturing defect in the original panel installation -- not normal wear. The defect accelerates under solar proton events, which have been above average this cycle. The red threshold will be crossed in four weeks, not five -- Nair's original estimate was conservative.

She asks Aldren whether the corporate board's denial stands. Aldren says it does, pending Q4 budget replenishment. Federova asks Aldren whether he understands that a Section Eighteen finding, if confirmed, will result in a compliance order that the board cannot defer.

Aldren says he understands.

**E6 (resolution scene).** The administrative conference room, the following morning. Federova issues her preliminary compliance order:

> *Belt Orbital Safety Authority -- Preliminary Compliance Order*
> *Station: Tethys, L2 Cluster, Platform 6*
> *Operator: Orbital Dynamics Corp*

> *Pursuant to Section 18 of the Belt Orbital Safety Code, the Authority finds that a hazardous condition exists in hull sectors 4, 7, 11, and 12 of Tethys Station. The operator is ordered to complete the following within thirty days:*

> *1. Procure and install replacement shielding panels in the four affected sectors.*
> *2. Conduct a full-station dosimeter recalibration upon completion of repairs.*
> *3. Submit a root-cause analysis of the shielding defect to the Authority within sixty days.*

> *Failure to comply within the specified period will result in an occupancy restriction on the affected sectors and a formal compliance violation on the operator's regulatory record.*

Aldren reads the order. He asks whether the board has the right to appeal.

Federova says the board may appeal within ten days. The appeal does not stay the compliance order. The repairs must proceed on the original timeline regardless of appeal status.

Aldren is quiet for a moment. Then he asks Nair how quickly the panels can be obtained from the L2 supply depot.

Nair says the depot has two panels in stock. The other two would need to be fabricated, which takes ten days. Installation is three EVA shifts -- nine days total if the weather window cooperates.

"Then we need to order today," Aldren says. He opens his laptop and begins drafting the procurement authorisation, routing it through his own discretionary account rather than waiting for the board's budget cycle. "I'll explain the charge to the board afterward. The compliance order gives me the authority to bypass the capital review."

Nair nods. Federova signs the compliance order, hands Aldren his copy and Nair hers, and tells them she will return in thirty days for the completion inspection.

Nair takes her copy to the engineering deck and begins scheduling the EVA shifts.

## C-arc-Sf2-4

**5 beats, non-linear: E1 (aftermath) -> E2 (demand, ten days earlier) -> E3 (failed negotiation) -> E4 (escalation) -> E5 (resolution). Present tense.**

**Scenario.** A terraforming project's lead biologist demands that the military governor of the colony halt a weapons-testing programme that is contaminating the biosphere she has spent four years building. The governor refuses, citing defence priorities. The biologist triggers a charter clause that forces a public hearing before the colonial assembly.

> A = Dr. Lien Thanh, lead biologist, Vasuda Terraforming Project; B = Military Governor Kael Strand; O = immediate cessation of weapons testing in the northern basin (the biosphere's primary atmospheric-seeding zone).

**E1 (aftermath scene).** The northern basin stretches below the observation bluff, sixty kilometres of engineered steppe that Dr. Thanh planted with nitrogen-fixing microbiota three years ago. She stands at the bluff's edge in a rebreather, because the atmosphere is not yet self-sustaining, and looks at the soil.

The test craters are visible from here -- four of them, each fifty metres across, the soil blackite where the plasma charges detonated. Around each crater, in expanding rings, the engineered ground cover is dead. Not dormant. Dead. The nitrogen-fixers cannot survive the thermal shock or the chemical residue the charges leave behind.

She kneels at the edge of the nearest ring and takes a soil sample. The biometric reader in her kit shows what she already knows: the microbial density is zero. Not reduced. Zero. The blast zone is sterile.

She seals the sample, stands, and looks at the four craters again. Then she walks back to the survey vehicle and drives to the project compound. The assembly's ruling, posted on the compound's bulletin board three days ago, is clear: the testing programme continues, but not in the northern basin. The governor has forty-five days to relocate his range.

Forty-five days. She looks at the dead rings and calculates how long it will take to replant. Two years, minimum. Two years of work erased for a weapons test. But the basin is hers again, or will be in forty-five days, and the assembly's ruling is binding.

She starts the replanting schedule that afternoon.

**E2 (demand scene).** Ten days earlier. The military governor's office in the colonial administrative block, a prefabricated building on the plateau above the basin. Thanh arrives without an appointment. The governor's aide tells her the governor is in a briefing. She says she will wait. She waits for ninety minutes.

Governor Strand emerges from the briefing room. He sees her and pauses -- he knows why she is here, because his aide has already messaged him.

"I need the weapons-testing programme halted in the northern basin," she says. "Immediately."

They move into his office. He closes the door.

"The testing programme is scheduled through the end of the quarter," he says. "We have four more detonation tests and a suborbital intercept exercise. The northern basin is the only range on the planet with the clearance radius we need."

"The northern basin is the primary atmospheric-seeding zone for the terraforming project. I have spent four years building the microbial substrate that drives nitrogen fixation in that soil. Your plasma charges are sterilising the ground in a hundred-metre radius around every detonation point. You have already destroyed eight hectares of engineered biome."

"Eight hectares out of sixty thousand."

"Eight hectares of the most productive nitrogen-fixing substrate on the planet. The seeding zone was chosen because the basin's thermal profile supports the specific microbial strains we engineered. Destroying it is not like losing eight hectares of grassland. It is like cutting the lungs out of an organism and saying the body still has ninety-nine percent of its mass."

The governor leans back. "The defence programme is authorised by the colonial charter. Section Twenty-Two: the military governor has exclusive authority over defence operations, including the designation of testing ranges."

"Section Twenty-Two does not give you the authority to destroy a terraforming programme that the same charter established in Section Three. Section Three designates the atmospheric-seeding zones as protected infrastructure. Protected infrastructure cannot be damaged or degraded by any colonial activity, military or civilian, without a formal impact assessment approved by the colonial assembly."

"Was an impact assessment conducted before the testing programme began?"

"You know it was not. That is why I am here."

"I'll take it under advisement," the governor says.

"Advisement is not cessation. I need the testing halted while the assessment is conducted."

"I am not halting a defence programme on the basis of a request from a biologist. If you want the assembly involved, invoke the charter."

"I will," she says.

**E3 (failed negotiation scene).** The project compound, three days later. Thanh has sent the governor a formal proposal: relocate the remaining tests to the southern plateau, which has the same clearance radius but sits outside all designated seeding zones. The relocation would add fourteen days to the testing schedule due to the need to establish new range markers and safety perimeters.

The governor's reply arrives by courier drone:

> *Dr. Thanh,*
> *The southern plateau has been assessed by my ordnance team. The terrain is unsuitable for the suborbital intercept exercise -- the plateau's elevation creates a radar shadow that interferes with the tracking array. The northern basin remains the only viable range for the full programme.*
> *I have instructed my team to restrict detonation tests to the eastern edge of the basin, which your own survey maps show as low-priority substrate. This should reduce the impact on the seeding zone while allowing the programme to continue.*
> *Regards, Governor Strand*

Thanh reads the reply in her lab. The eastern edge of the basin is low-priority substrate because it was low-priority substrate four years ago. Since then, her team has expanded the nitrogen-fixing colony across the entire basin floor. There is no low-priority zone anymore. The governor is working from outdated maps.

She drafts her reply:

> *Governor Strand,*
> *The eastern edge of the basin is no longer low-priority. The microbial substrate was expanded to full-basin coverage eighteen months ago. Updated survey maps are attached. Any detonation within the basin will damage the seeding zone.*
> *I am filing a formal request for charter review under Section Three. The colonial assembly will convene within fourteen days under the emergency-session provision.*
> *Dr. Thanh*

She transmits the filing to the assembly's clerk and sends a copy to the governor.

**E4 (escalation scene).** The colonial assembly chamber, eleven days later. The chamber is the largest room in the administrative block -- it seats the full assembly of fifteen elected representatives, plus observers. The gallery holds thirty. It is full. Colonists have come to watch.

Thanh presents the scientific case. She shows the soil data: microbial density readings from the blast zones (zero), from the adjacent substrate (healthy), and from the basin as a whole (declining, because the sterilised zones are no longer contributing to the nitrogen cycle and the surrounding substrate is compensating at an unsustainable rate). She shows the atmospheric projections: if the seeding zone is further degraded, the timeline to breathable atmosphere extends by an estimated six to ten years. She shows the economic projections: every additional year of rebreather dependency costs the colony approximately forty million in imported atmospheric compounds.

Governor Strand presents the defence case. He describes the threat assessment: three rival colonial entities have established settlements within the same planetary system in the past two years. The weapons-testing programme is part of a defensive capability that the colonial charter's Section Twenty-Two mandates the governor to develop. Relocating or cancelling the programme weakens the colony's deterrent posture at a critical moment.

A representative from the civilian caucus asks Strand whether the defence programme can be completed without the northern basin. Strand says it cannot be completed on the current timeline. With relocation, the programme extends by three months. Without the northern basin or an alternative range, the suborbital intercept exercise cannot be conducted at all.

Another representative asks Thanh whether the damage already done is reversible. Thanh says yes, over approximately two years, if no further detonations occur in the basin. If the programme continues for the remaining four tests, the damage doubles and the recovery extends to four years.

The assembly chair calls for deliberation.

**E5 (resolution scene).** The assembly chamber, the following day. The chair reads the ruling.

The assembly finds that the military governor's weapons-testing programme was conducted in a designated atmospheric-seeding zone without the formal impact assessment required by Section Three of the colonial charter. The assembly orders the following:

First, all weapons testing in the northern basin ceases immediately. No further detonations, ordnance exercises, or range activities of any kind are permitted in any designated seeding zone.

Second, the governor is granted forty-five days to identify and establish an alternative testing range outside all designated seeding zones. The assembly acknowledges that this may extend the defence programme's timeline and accepts that extension as a necessary cost.

Third, the governor's office will fund the replanting of the eight hectares of sterilised substrate from the defence programme's operational budget. The cost estimate will be provided by Dr. Thanh's team and reviewed by the assembly's finance committee.

Governor Strand asks whether the ruling is subject to appeal.

The assembly chair says it is, under the charter's appellate provision, but that the appeal does not stay the order. Testing ceases today.

Strand says the ruling compromises the colony's defensive readiness. He says he will file an appeal and a formal objection in the colonial record.

The chair says both are his right. The ruling stands.

Thanh does not speak during the ruling. She sits in the observer's gallery with her soil sample kit on her lap and watches the assembly vote -- twelve in favour, two against, one abstaining. When the vote is recorded, she stands and leaves the chamber.

She drives to the northern basin that afternoon.

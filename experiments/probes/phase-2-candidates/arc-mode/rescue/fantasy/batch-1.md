# Rescue arc x Fantasy -- Batch 1 (Tier A)

## Re-arc-Fa1-1

**5 beats, chronological, free indirect style**

**Scenario.** A guild-healer's apprentice discovers that the city's water supply has been poisoned by a rival guild seeking to discredit the ruling merchant council during the autumn conclave. The healer must reach the council chamber and administer the antidote before the poisoned councillors collapse during the open session, but the rival guild has sealed the quarter.

> A = guild-healer (Maren Thorne); B = the twelve members of the merchant council; T = slow-acting hemlock extract introduced into the council chamber's wine carafes by agents of the Dyers' Guild, which will cause respiratory failure within three hours of ingestion.

**E1 (danger present scene).** The healers' hall, an autumn morning. Maren Thorne was grinding valerian root for the evening's tinctures when her apprentice, Coll, came through the side door at a pace that meant trouble. He was pale and breathing hard, and he set a ceramic flask on the worktable without speaking.

Maren picked it up. The flask was from the council chamber's kitchen -- she recognised the seal of the provisioner's guild on the neck. Coll said he had been delivering a cough remedy to the provisioner's assistant and had seen the kitchen boy pouring wine into the council's carafes from a second flask, not the one the provisioner had set out. Coll had taken the second flask when the kitchen boy left the room.

Maren unstoppered it and smelled the wine. Under the tannin there was something else -- bitter, green, faintly acrid. She poured a measure into a glass dish and added three drops of wormwood oil. The oil separated and sank, leaving a milky residue on the surface. Hemlock. The test was unambiguous. She had performed it a hundred times.

She asked Coll when the council session began. He said it had begun at the eighth bell -- nearly an hour ago. The wine was customarily served with the first agenda item. If the councillors had already drunk, they had perhaps two hours before the hemlock reached concentration in the blood. After that, the chest muscles would begin to seize.

Maren set down the dish and thought. She had the antidote -- a preparation of crushed charcoal, ground mustard seed, and atropine tincture that she kept in the dispensary for exactly this class of poisoning. But the antidote needed to be administered within the first two hours. After that it slowed the poison but could not reverse it.

She asked Coll to fetch the atropine preparation from the locked cabinet. She began calculating twelve doses.

**E2 (danger escalation scene).** The healers' hall, twenty minutes later. Maren had the twelve doses prepared in individual vials and packed in a leather case. She sent Coll ahead to the council quarter to confirm the session was still in progress.

Coll returned in ten minutes, out of breath again. The session was in progress, but the council quarter was sealed. The Dyers' Guild had petitioned the city watch for a closure of the quarter's three entrances, citing a dispute over guild taxes that required the council's undivided attention. The watch had posted guards at each gate. No one without a councillor's writ or a guild-master's seal was permitted to enter.

Maren thought about this. The Dyers' Guild had sponsored the poisoning and sealed the quarter to ensure no one could intervene before the poison took effect. By the time the session adjourned and the councillors emerged, the hemlock would be well advanced. If the councillors collapsed in public, outside the chamber, it would look like a plague -- and the Dyers' Guild would blame the merchant council's mismanagement of the city's sanitation, which was the very issue the conclave was convening to debate.

She asked Coll how long ago the councillors had drunk. Coll said the provisioner's assistant had told him the wine was served at the eighth bell. It was now the ninth. One hour consumed. One hour remaining.

**E3 (failed initial response scene).** The south gate of the council quarter, fifteen minutes later. Maren stood before the watchman with her healer's satchel and the case of vials. She wore the guild-healer's grey cloak, which was supposed to grant passage through any civic closure under the city's health ordinance.

The watchman looked at her cloak and shook his head. He said the closure order specifically excluded health officers unless summoned by the council itself. The order had been signed by the watch captain and bore the Dyers' Guild seal.

Maren asked to speak with the watch captain. The watchman said the captain was at the north gate. Maren walked to the north gate -- a twelve-minute detour through the market district. The watch captain was not there. The guard at the north gate said the captain had gone to the east gate to resolve a dispute with a carter. Maren did not have time to walk to the east gate.

She returned to the south gate. Forty minutes had now passed since the ninth bell. She had perhaps fifty minutes before the antidote became unreliable.

**E4 (intervention scene).** The river wall behind the council quarter. Maren had not entered the council quarter through a gate in twenty years, not since she was a girl running errands for the old guild-healer. But she remembered that the old healer had used the river wall -- a low stone parapet that separated the council quarter from the tidal flats -- to reach the council building's cellar entrance during the plague year, when the quarter had been sealed then, too.

She climbed down the embankment, crossed the mud flat at low tide, and found the cellar grate. It was locked. She used the iron handle of her mortar -- the one she carried in her satchel for field work -- to pry the grate's hinge pin. The grate swung open. She entered the cellar.

The council chamber was two floors above. She climbed the service stair, passed the kitchen -- empty now, the kitchen boy gone -- and reached the corridor outside the chamber. She could hear voices through the door. The session was still in progress. She pushed the door open and walked in.

Twelve councillors sat around the long table. The wine carafes were on the table. Several were half-empty. The council chair looked up at her entrance and began to object.

"You have been poisoned," Maren said. She set the case on the table and opened it. "The wine contains hemlock. I have the counter-preparation. You will each drink one of these vials. You will feel nauseous within minutes. That is the counter-preparation working. If any of you have not yet touched the wine, do not."

The council chair stared at her. One of the younger councillors reached for a vial. Another pushed his wine glass away. The chair asked how she could be certain.

Maren took the nearest carafe, poured a measure into a drinking glass, and repeated the wormwood-oil test in front of them. The milky residue formed on the surface. She held it up.

"That is hemlock," she said. "Drink the vials or don't. You have less than an hour to decide, and the decision gets worse with every minute."

The younger councillor drank first. Then three others. Then the rest. The chair drank last.

**E5 (outcome / cost scene).** The healers' hall, that evening. Ten of the twelve councillors had responded to the antidote within the expected window. They had vomited in the council chamber -- violently and without dignity, which was how the antidote worked -- and the hemlock had been expelled before it reached critical concentration.

Two councillors had drunk more than the others and were slower to respond. Maren had them carried to the healers' hall and administered a second dose of the atropine preparation, supplemented with a purgative. By evening, both were conscious and breathing without distress, though they would be weak for days.

The watch captain arrived at the healers' hall at the sixth bell, after the council's emergency session had concluded. He told Maren that the Dyers' Guild master had been detained, that the kitchen boy had confessed under questioning and named the guild agent who had supplied the flask, and that the closure order had been rescinded.

He also told her that she was being charged with unlawful entry into a sealed civic quarter, destruction of city property -- the cellar grate -- and practice of medicine without a council summons. The charges had been filed by the Dyers' Guild before their master's detention, and the watch was obliged to serve them regardless.

Maren looked at the charge sheet. She looked at the two councillors resting in the beds behind her, breathing evenly.

"I'll need that back," she said. "I'm going to frame it."

## Re-arc-Fa1-2

**4 beats, chronological, dialogue-driven**

**Scenario.** A border keep's garrison is poisoned by a besieging warband that has contaminated the keep's only well. A travelling alchemist, trapped inside the keep when the siege closed, identifies the toxin and synthesises a purgative from materials in the keep's storeroom, but the garrison commander refuses to trust a foreigner's remedy.

> A = alchemist (Davan, itinerant, no guild affiliation); B = the keep's garrison (thirty-one soldiers and the commander's household); T = blackroot toxin introduced into the keep's well water by the Gael warband besieging the outer wall, causing progressive paralysis within twelve hours.

**E1 (danger present scene).** The keep's great hall, the second morning of the siege. The garrison's quartermaster stood at the head of the table and delivered his report. Fourteen soldiers had reported numbness in their hands and feet overnight. Three could not grip a sword. Two had collapsed on the wall walk during the dawn watch and had to be carried down.

Commander Bryce sat in the high chair with her gauntlets on the table. "Illness," she said. "From the cold."

"It's not the cold," the quartermaster said. "The symptoms are identical across all fourteen. Numbness starts in the extremities, moves inward. The two who collapsed lost feeling in their legs entirely. It's not fever, it's not flux, and it's not cold."

The alchemist, Davan, had been sitting against the far wall since dawn, having been allowed into the hall only because the servants' quarters were full. He had arrived at the keep two days before the siege closed the gates and had been given a sleeping place in exchange for mending a cracked flue in the kitchen chimney.

He said nothing. He listened.

**E2 (danger escalation / identification scene).** The well yard, an hour later. Davan had asked the quartermaster's permission to examine the well. The quartermaster, a practical man who did not care where answers came from, had agreed.

Davan drew a bucket and set it on the stone rim. He cupped the water in his hands and smelled it. He tasted a drop and spat it out. Then he reached into his travelling pack and produced a small leather roll of glass vials. He pipetted a measure of well water into a vial containing a clear reagent and held it to the light.

The water turned the colour of old rust.

"Blackroot," he said.

The quartermaster looked at him.

"It's a tuber. Grows in the marshes east of here. The Gael burn it to ash and pack it into clay balls. Drop a dozen into a well and the water carries the toxin within hours. It paralyses the peripheral nerves first, then the diaphragm. Twelve hours from first exposure to full respiratory arrest."

"Can you treat it?" the quartermaster said.

"I can purge it. The counter-agent is simple -- calcined calcium from chalk or limestone, mixed with lye water and administered as a draught. It binds the blackroot alkaloid in the gut before it reaches the nerves. The keep's storeroom should have lime for mortar. Lye I can make from the wood ash in the kitchen."

"How long?"

"To make the preparation, two hours. But it has to be given to every person who drank the water, and it has to be given within ten hours of first exposure. The first cases are already eight hours in."

**E3 (failed initial response / confrontation scene).** The great hall, thirty minutes later. The quartermaster brought Davan before the commander. The well yard, the test, the diagnosis, the proposed treatment.

Commander Bryce looked at Davan for a long time.

"You arrived two days before the siege," she said. "A foreigner with no guild seal and no letter of introduction, carrying a pack full of chemicals. And now you tell me the well is poisoned and only you know the cure."

"I didn't say only I know it," Davan said. "I said I can make it. Any competent apothecary could do the same. You don't have an apothecary. You have me."

"Or you're working with the warband outside my walls, and the draught you're proposing to give my garrison will finish what the well started."

"If I were working with the warband, Commander, I'd be outside your walls, not inside them. I'm trapped here the same as you. The difference is that I know what's in your water and you don't, and in four hours the first of your soldiers will stop breathing."

Bryce turned to the quartermaster. "Lock the well. No one drinks. We'll melt snow from the battlement cisterns."

"The damage is already done," Davan said. "Stopping the water stops new exposure. It does nothing for the thirty-one people who drank it yesterday and this morning. They need the purgative."

"I will not administer an unknown substance prepared by an unknown man to my entire garrison during a siege."

"Then test it," Davan said. "Give me two hours to make the preparation. Administer it to one volunteer. If the volunteer improves, treat the rest. If the volunteer worsens, you can lock me in the cellar and throw away the key."

Bryce was silent. The quartermaster said: "Commander, I drank the water this morning."

Bryce looked at him.

"Use me," the quartermaster said. "I'll take the first dose. If it works, we treat the garrison. If it doesn't, I was going to die of it anyway."

Bryce set her gauntlets on the table. "You have two hours," she said to Davan. "The quartermaster will observe you make it. If anything goes into that preparation that he can't account for, we stop."

**E4 (intervention / outcome scene).** The kitchen, four hours later. Davan had made the preparation under the quartermaster's watch -- lime calcium scraite from the mortar stores, dissolved in lye water from the kitchen's ash bucket, filtered through linen, and dosed into thirty-one portions. The quartermaster had watched every step and confirmed every ingredient.

The quartermaster drank first. Within twenty minutes, the numbness in his fingers -- which he had not mentioned to the commander -- began to recede. Within an hour, he could flex both hands fully. He reported to the commander.

Bryce ordered the garrison treated. Davan and the quartermaster administered the draught to all thirty-one affected soldiers over the next two hours, prioritising the earliest cases. The two soldiers who had collapsed on the wall walk were the worst -- their paralysis had advanced to the torso, and they could not swallow easily. Davan thinned their doses with boiled water and had them drink slowly, a sip at a time, with the quartermaster supporting their heads.

By nightfall, twenty-nine of the thirty-one had regained sensation in their extremities. The two critical cases were breathing on their own but could not yet walk. Davan told the commander they would need a second dose in the morning and a third the following day, but they would live.

Bryce came to the kitchen after the last dose was administered. Davan was washing the preparation vessels in the basin.

"The siege could last weeks," she said. "The well is still contaminated. Can you purify it?"

"Drain it, scrub the walls with lye, and refill from the cistern. It will take a day. The blackroot breaks down once the source is removed."

"I'll put four soldiers on it at first light." She paused. "I owe you a debt."

"You owe me a better sleeping arrangement than the servants' corridor," Davan said. "We can discuss debts when the gates open."

## Re-arc-Fa1-3

**6 beats, non-linear: E1 (aftermath) -> E2 (danger present) -> E3 (danger escalation) -> E4 (preparation) -> E5 (intervention) -> E6 (outcome), embedded document style**

**Scenario.** A temple order's youngest seer is struck by a curse-bolt during a raid on the temple by a rogue war-mage seeking to steal a relic. The order's archivist, who has spent decades studying counter-curse theory but has never performed one, must attempt the unbinding before the curse reaches the seer's heart. The temple's senior healers are dead or incapacitated from the raid.

> A = the archivist (Brother Edric); B = the seer (Aelith, age fourteen); T = a binding curse cast by the war-mage Torvan, which constricts progressively from the point of impact toward the heart, killing the subject when it reaches the chest cavity.

**Note on beat order.** Depicted order is non-linear. Chronological order: E2 -- E3 -- E4 -- E5 -- E6 -- E1. The sequence opens with the archivist's account written after the fact, then moves to the events in order.

**E1 (aftermath -- embedded document).** The archivist's account, entered into the temple's incident record three days after the raid:

> *I, Brother Edric, archivist of the Order of the Sighted, set down this account at the instruction of the Prior. I am aware that my actions on the night of the raid exceeded my sanctioned competence and that the Prior's review is ongoing. I record what I did and what I observed, without justification or omission.*
>
> *The account that follows covers the period from the second bell of night, when the war-mage Torvan breached the east cloister, to the seventh bell of morning, when the seer Aelith opened her eyes. I was present throughout. I had no assistance. What I did, I did from texts I had studied but never practised, and I will not claim certainty that I understood them correctly. The seer is alive. Whether she is undamaged, the healers will determine when they are able.*

**E2 (danger present scene).** The east cloister, the second bell of night. The archivist had been in the archive reading a treatise on ward-geometry when the first explosion shook the building. He reached the cloister in time to see the aftermath: the east door blown inward, the two senior healers on the flagstones -- one motionless, the other clutching her shoulder where a shard of the door had opened a wound to the bone. Three novices were pressed against the far wall. The war-mage Torvan stood in the centre of the cloister with the relic case in one hand and a fading glow on the other.

The seer, Aelith, was on the ground near the archive door. She had been crossing the cloister when Torvan entered. The curse-bolt had struck her left arm above the elbow. The archivist could see the binding's mark -- a black discolouration spreading visibly along the skin, moving toward the shoulder at the speed of a slow pour of oil. The binding-curse was a constriction: it tightened the tissue it passed through, crushing muscle and vessel as it advanced. When it reached the chest cavity, it would close around the heart.

Torvan was already moving toward the outer wall. He did not look back at Aelith. She had not been his target -- she had simply been in the path of a bolt he had aimed at the healer who was now motionless on the floor.

The archivist knelt beside Aelith. She was conscious. Her left arm below the mark was grey and rigid, the flesh already compressed. She was not screaming, which he took as a bad sign -- the nerve pathways in the affected area were already dead.

**E3 (danger escalation scene).** The east cloister, minutes later. The archivist checked the two senior healers. Sister Margaid was dead -- the curse-bolt that had missed Aelith's head had struck Margaid full in the chest before Torvan redirected it. Brother Aldous was alive but his shoulder wound was deep and his right arm was useless; he could not perform the fine gestures that unbinding required.

Aldous looked at Aelith from the floor. "The binding will reach her shoulder within the hour," he said through his teeth. "After the shoulder, the chest. She has perhaps ninety minutes."

"Can you talk me through the unbinding?" the archivist said.

Aldous shook his head. "The unbinding requires a practitioner's focus and a practitioner's hands. I can't guide someone who has never channelled a counter-curse. The tolerances are too fine. One error and you accelerate the binding instead of reversing it."

"What if I have the text?"

Aldous stared at him. "You're an archivist."

"I'm an archivist who has spent eleven years reading the texts you practise from. I know the theory. I know the geometry. I have never performed it, but I have read every account of it that exists in this archive, including Sister Margaid's case notes from the siege of Dunmar."

Aldous closed his eyes. The pain in his shoulder was pulling him under. "Get the Vossar Codex," he said. "Chapter nine. The diagrams are precise. Do not improvise. Do not deviate. And do it quickly."

**E4 (preparation scene).** The archive, five minutes later. The archivist retrieved the Vossar Codex from its shelf -- a leather-bound manuscript he had catalogued, repaired, and read more times than any other text in the collection. He opened it to chapter nine: *On the Unbinding of Constriction Curses, with Diagrams of the Six Counter-Geometries*.

He carried the codex to the cloister, set it on the flagstones beside Aelith, and read the preparatory requirements. He needed three things: a silver stylus for drawing the counter-geometry on the skin, a source of focused will -- which the text described as "the practitioner's trained intention" -- and chalk of consecrated limestone to anchor the geometry's boundary.

He had the chalk. Every room in the temple kept consecrated chalk in a wall niche for ward-drawing. He had a silver stylus -- he used one for manuscript annotation. The focused will was the problem. The text assumed a trained healer. The archivist had no training in channelling intention. He had only the theoretical understanding of what intention was supposed to do: provide the directional force that the counter-geometry shaped into a reversal.

He looked at the codex's diagram. The counter-geometry was intricate -- a six-pointed figure drawn around the wound site, with precise angular relationships that determined the direction of the unbinding. If the angles were correct, the geometry would channel whatever intention the practitioner could muster into a reversal of the curse's direction. If the angles were wrong, the geometry would amplify the curse.

He measured the angles with a protractor from his annotation kit. He practised the figure on the flagstones twice.

**E5 (intervention scene).** The east cloister. The binding had reached Aelith's shoulder. The blackened skin extended from her elbow to the top of the deltoid muscle. She was shaking, her eyes open and fixed on the ceiling.

The archivist knelt beside her and drew the counter-geometry on her upper arm with the silver stylus, placing each line according to the codex's diagram. The chalk boundary went around the geometry's outer edge. His hands were steady -- he had spent eleven years making precise marks on fragile parchment, and the muscle memory served him now.

When the geometry was complete, he placed his palm at its centre, over the wound site, and did the only thing the text instructed: he held the intention to reverse. Not a spell. Not a formula. The text described it as *the practitioner's clear and sustained will that the binding retreat along its own path*. The archivist thought of it in the only terms he understood -- the same focused attention he brought to restoring a damaged manuscript, the steady insistence that the thing be made whole again, line by line, working backward from the damage to the original state.

The counter-geometry warmed under his hand. The binding-mark stopped advancing. For a long moment nothing happened. Then the black edge at Aelith's shoulder began to recede -- slowly, unevenly, pulling back along the same path it had travelled. The archivist held his hand in place and did not move. The recession took forty minutes. He did not shift his position. His hand cramped. He ignored it.

The binding retreated past the elbow, past the forearm, and collected at the point of original impact. When it reached the entry wound, the counter-geometry flared once and the black mark dissolved into the chalk boundary, which cracked and fell away as powder.

**E6 (outcome scene).** The east cloister, the seventh bell of morning. Aelith opened her eyes. Her left arm was livid with bruising where the binding had compressed the tissue, and she could not move her fingers, but the flesh was warm and the pulse at her wrist was present. Brother Aldous, conscious now and propped against the wall with his shoulder bound in linen, examined her from across the cloister and said the nerve damage might recover with time, or it might not.

The archivist sat on the floor with the Vossar Codex open beside him. He had not slept. His right hand, the one that had held the counter-geometry, was shaking and would not stop. He flexed it and watched the tremor.

The Prior arrived at the eighth bell, having ridden through the night from the southern chapter house. He surveyed the cloister -- the broken door, Sister Margaid's body covered with a blanket, the wounded Aldous, the bruised and half-conscious seer, and the archivist sitting among chalk dust with a manuscript open on the floor.

"You performed an unbinding," the Prior said. It was not a question.

"I performed what the codex described," the archivist said. "Whether it was an unbinding or something that merely resembled one, I cannot say. The seer is alive. My hand will not stop shaking. Beyond those two facts, I claim nothing."

## Re-arc-Fa1-4

**5 beats, chronological, present tense**

**Scenario.** A clan's grain stores are contaminated by a rival clan's saboteurs before the winter closing of the mountain passes. The clan's aged herbalist, unable to travel, sends her granddaughter down the mountain to the lowland market to trade for uncontaminated seed grain before the last caravan departs. The granddaughter must cross territory controlled by the rival clan to reach the market.

> A = the herbalist's granddaughter (Seren); B = the mountain clan (eighty-three people dependent on the winter grain supply); T = ergot contamination in the clan's entire grain store, introduced by saboteurs from the Blackthorn clan, which will cause convulsions, gangrene, and death if consumed over the winter.

**E1 (danger present scene).** The storehouse, first light. The elder opens the grain bins for the monthly inspection. She scoops a measure of rye from the nearest bin and pours it into a winnowing tray. The kernels are dark and swollen, several of them carrying the purple-black protrusions she recognises at once: ergot. She checks the second bin. The same. The third. Every bin in the storehouse is infested.

She calls the herbalist. Old Maesil arrives with her stick and her granddaughter Seren, and the three of them work through the bins for an hour. The contamination is total. Not patchy, not partial -- every bin shows the same pattern of infection, distributed evenly, as if the spores were introduced deliberately and thoroughly.

Maesil breaks one of the purple bodies between her fingers and smells it. "This didn't come from the field," she says. "Ergot from bad harvest concentrates in the bins closest to the walls, where the moisture gathers. This is uniform. Someone opened these bins and seeded them."

The elder looks at the storehouse door. The lock is intact. But the rear shutter -- the one used for ventilation during summer -- shows fresh scratches on its latch. The Blackthorn clan's territory begins three ridges south. Their saboteurs know these mountains as well as anyone in the clan.

The pass closes in nine days. The clan has no other food source for winter. Eighty-three people, including eleven children, will eat from these bins or starve.

**E2 (danger escalation scene).** The herbalist's cottage, midday. Maesil sits at her worktable with samples from each bin spread on cloth. She has confirmed the ergot in all twelve bins. The grain cannot be treated -- ergot alkaloids survive boiling, survive grinding, survive baking. Any bread made from this grain will poison whoever eats it. Convulsions first, then the burning sensation in the extremities, then the blackening of fingers and toes as the blood vessels constrict.

"We need clean grain," Maesil says. "Enough for eighty-three people through a six-month winter. That's roughly two thousand pounds."

The elder says the nearest source of grain in that quantity is the lowland market at Carden's Ford, five days' travel down the eastern trail. The clan has trade goods -- cured hides, dried herbs, copper wire -- sufficient to purchase the grain. But the last caravan of the season departs Carden's Ford in seven days. After that, the lowland merchants close their stalls and the ford becomes impassable with the autumn rains.

Maesil cannot make the journey. Her knees have not allowed her to walk the eastern trail in three years. The elder is needed in the village. The clan's two strongest hunters are on the western ridge, three days out and unreachable.

Seren stands in the doorway, listening. She is seventeen, strong, and she knows the eastern trail. She also knows that the trail passes through the Blackthorn clan's hunting grounds for two days of the five-day journey.

"I can go," she says.

**E3 (preparation scene).** The herbalist's cottage, that evening. Maesil packs Seren's provisions: five days of dried meat and hard biscuit, a water skin, a fire kit. She adds a pouch of dried betony and a vial of woundwort salve. She writes a trade-note on birch bark, sealed with the elder's mark, authorising Seren to negotiate on the clan's behalf for grain purchase at Carden's Ford.

"The Blackthorn grounds start at the second river crossing," Maesil says. "Their patrols run the ridgeline. Stay in the valley bottom and travel at night through their territory. Two nights. On the third morning you'll be past their boundary stones and in the lowland foothills."

"And if their patrols find me?"

"You're a girl carrying trade goods, not a war party. Tell them you're going to market. It has the advantage of being true."

"They'll know which clan I'm from. They sabotaged our grain. They don't want us buying more."

Maesil is quiet for a moment. "Then don't let them find you."

She presses something into Seren's hand -- a small wooden token carved with the sign of the Ash-root Guild, the herbalists' neutral fellowship that operates across all clan boundaries. "If you are caught," Maesil says, "show this. The Blackthorn herbalist is a woman named Fionn. She will not help you, but she will prevent them from killing you. Professional courtesy. It's thin, but it's something."

Seren leaves before dawn.

**E4 (intervention scene).** Carden's Ford, the sixth day. Seren arrives at the market at midday, having crossed the Blackthorn territory in two nights without detection. Her boots are worn through and she has eaten nothing since the previous morning -- she rationed her provisions for five days and the journey took six because she detoured wide around a Blackthorn patrol camp she spotted from the ridge on the second night.

The market is half-dismantled. The last caravan is loading. She finds the grain merchant, a lowlander named Hadewick, and presents the elder's trade-note. Hadewick reads it and looks at her -- filthy, exhausted, a mountain girl alone in a lowland market, trying to buy two thousand pounds of grain.

"I've got the grain," he says. "But my caravan leaves at dawn tomorrow and it goes south, not east. I don't freight up the mountain."

"I don't need you to freight it. I need you to sell it. I have trade goods worth the purchase." She opens her pack and sets out the hides, the copper wire, the dried herbs. Hadewick examines each item. He names a price. Seren counters. They negotiate for twenty minutes. The price settles at sixteen cured hides, forty arm-lengths of copper wire, and twenty bundles of dried mountain sage.

"Two thousand pounds is forty sacks," Hadewick says. "You can't carry forty sacks up a mountain."

"I'll hire a mule train."

"The last mule train left yesterday."

Seren stands in the market for a long moment. Then she walks to the caravan loading ground and speaks with the caravan master. She trades her last four bundles of sage -- Maesil's personal stock, which Seren was not supposed to sell -- for the hire of two mules and a handler for five days. The handler, a boy not much older than Seren, says he knows the eastern trail as far as the second river crossing but will not enter Blackthorn territory.

Seren tells him that will be far enough. She will manage the mules alone from there.

**E5 (outcome scene).** The mountain village, eleven days after Seren's departure. The pass has been closed for two days. Snow sits on the ridgeline. The elder stands at the storehouse door and watches the eastern trail, which is now buried under six inches of new snow.

Seren appears at the tree line in the late afternoon, leading two mules loaded with grain sacks. She is alone. The mule handler turned back at the second crossing as agreed. She has led the mules through Blackthorn territory by herself, travelling at night, resting the animals in concealed hollows during the day. She lost four sacks to a stream crossing where one mule stumbled -- thirty-six sacks remain, eighteen hundred pounds. Not the full two thousand, but enough.

The elder and three villagers unload the mules. The grain is clean -- Seren checked every sack at the point of purchase, breaking kernels and inspecting for the purple-black bodies. She found none. Hadewick's stock came from the southern lowlands, nowhere near the mountain ergot belt.

Maesil meets Seren at the cottage door. She sees the girl's face -- hollow-eyed, wind-burned, a bruise on her cheekbone where she walked into a branch in the dark -- and she sees the empty space in Seren's pack where the sage bundles had been.

"You sold my sage," Maesil says.

"I needed the mules."

Maesil looks at her for a long time. "I would have sold the sage, too," she says. "Come inside. You need to eat something that isn't dried meat."
